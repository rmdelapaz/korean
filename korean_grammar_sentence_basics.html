🇰🇷 Lesson 5: Sentence Basics: 이에요, 은/는 & 이/가

Welcome to your first real grammar lesson! In Lesson 4 you memorised phrases like 이름이 뭐예요? and 저는 학생이에요 as whole chunks. Today we open those chunks up and look at the moving parts. By the end, you'll be able to build your own sentences instead of just repeating ready-made ones, and that is the moment a phrasebook turns into a language.

Don't worry if the particles feel slippery at first. Every Korean learner goes through a stage of mixing up 은/는 and 이/가. Each mix-up is feedback, not failure: it shows you exactly which part of the pattern your brain is still building.

🧠 The Big Picture: Verb Last, Labels Everywhere

English relies on word order to show who does what: "The dog bites the man" and "The man bites the dog" mean very different things. Korean works differently. It has two big habits:

  1. The verb (or "to be") comes last. Korean is an SOV language: Subject, Object, Verb. Where English says "I am a student", Korean says "I student am".
  2. Nouns wear little labels called particles. A particle sticks to the end of a noun and tells you its job in the sentence: "this is the topic", "this is the subject", "this is the object". Think of them like luggage tags. Because every bag is tagged, the bags can move around and still reach the right destination.
KoreanWord by wordEnglish
저는 학생이에요.I (topic) + student + amI am a student.
이름이 뭐예요?name (subject) + what + isWhat is (your) name?
이것은 책이에요.this (topic) + book + isThis is a book.

Notice two things. First, Korean has no words for "a" or "the". Second, "is/am/are" is not a separate word; it glues directly onto the noun. Let's start there.

🔗 Noun + 이에요 / 예요: "is / am / are"

To say "X is Y" about a noun, attach 이에요 or 예요 to the end of the noun, with no space. The choice depends on one simple question: does the noun end in a final consonant (받침)? You learned about 받침 in Lessons 2 and 3, and here is the first place it really matters for grammar.

💡 Formula

Noun ending in a consonant (받침) + 이에요 → 학생 + 이에요 = 학생이에요

Noun ending in a vowel (no 받침) + 예요 → 의사 + 예요 = 의사예요

Why two forms? Try saying 의사이에요 quickly: the two vowels collide, so Korean smooths them into 예요. After a consonant there's no collision, so the full 이에요 stays, and the consonant slides over into it (책이에요 sounds like 채기에요).

NounEnds in…+ isRomanizationEnglish
학생ㅇ (받침)학생이에요haksaengieyo(I'm / it's) a student
선생님ㅁ (받침)선생님이에요seonsaengnimieyo(he's / she's) a teacher
회사원ㄴ (받침)회사원이에요hoesawonieyoan office worker

The same ending works for "am", "is" and "are", for statements and for questions. You simply raise your voice at the end to ask: 학생이에요? = "Are you a student?"

💡 Connecting to Lesson 4: In your self-introduction you met 입니다 (저는 레이입니다). 입니다 is the formal version of 이에요/예요 and never changes shape, whatever the noun ends in. 이에요/예요 is the warm, everyday polite version you'll hear most in conversation. Both are correct; you'll study the speech levels properly in Lesson 13.

🚫 "Is not": 이/가 아니에요

The negative of "X is Y" is Y이/가 아니에요. Notice the noun now takes the particle 이 or 가 (more about this particle below), and 아니에요 follows as a separate word.

💡 Formula

Noun with 받침 + 이 아니에요 → 학생이 아니에요 (I'm not a student)

Noun without 받침 + 가 아니에요 → 의사가 아니에요 (I'm not a doctor)

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
저는 학생이 아니에요.jeoneun haksaengi anieyo.I'm not a student.
저는 의사가 아니에요.jeoneun uisaga anieyo.I'm not a doctor.
이것은 물이 아니에요.igeoseun muri anieyo.This isn't water.
아니요, 커피가 아니에요. 차예요.aniyo, keopiga anieyo. chayeyo.No, it's not coffee. It's tea.

🏷️ The Topic Marker 은 / 는: "As for…"

The topic marker announces what you're talking about. The most useful English comparison is the phrase "As for…": 저는 학생이에요 is literally "As for me, (I'm a) student." It sets the stage, then the rest of the sentence comments on it.

💡 Formula

Noun with 받침 + 은 → 이름은, 선생님은, 이것은

Noun without 받침 + 는 → 저는, 친구는, 의사는

Topic markers have a second, very important job: contrast. When you set two things side by side, 은/는 means "X, as opposed to other things".

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
저는 학생이에요.jeoneun haksaengieyo.I'm a student.
제 친구는 선생님이에요.je chinguneun seonsaengnimieyo.My friend (on the other hand) is a teacher.
이것은 제 가방이에요.igeoseun je gabangieyo.This is my bag.
민수 씨는 한국 사람이에요.minsu ssineun hanguk saramieyo.Minsu is Korean.

You just met 제 ("my", polite). It is simply 저 + the possessive idea, shortened. You'll use it constantly: 제 이름, 제 친구, 제 책.

🎯 The Subject Marker 이 / 가: "It's THIS one"

The subject marker points at the thing that does the action or has the quality, and it tends to put a little spotlight on it: "it's this one (and not something else)". You've already seen it in 이름이 뭐예요? and in the negative 학생이 아니에요.

💡 Formula

Noun with 받침 + 이 → 이름이, 화장실이, 선생님이

Noun without 받침 + 가 → 친구가, 커피가, 의사가

Three special fusions: 저 + 가 → 제가 (I), 나 + 가 → 내가 (I, casual), 누구 + 가 → 누가 (who). You never say 저가 or 누구가.

⚖️ 은/는 or 이/가? The Honest Answer

This is the most famous puzzle in beginner Korean, and it's honest to say: there is no single rule that covers 100% of cases, even advanced learners keep refining their feel for it. But three guidelines will make you right the vast majority of the time.

Use 은/는 when…Use 이/가 when…
introducing yourself or the topic of conversation: 저는 알렉스예요.the noun is new information or the answer to "who / which / what?": 제가 알렉스예요. ("I'm Alex", e.g. when someone calls the name)
you are contrasting: 저는 학생이에요. 동생은 회사원이에요.the subject is a question word: 누가 선생님이에요? 어디가 화장실이에요?
you're making a general statement about something already known: 이름은 제니예요.set patterns demand it: N이/가 아니에요, 이름이 뭐예요?, 화장실이 어디예요?

Watch how the question shapes the answer:

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
A누가 민지 씨예요?Who is Minji? (which person here?)
B제가 민지예요.I'm Minji. (It's me.)
A민지 씨는 학생이에요?(As for you,) Minji, are you a student?
B아니요, 저는 회사원이에요.No, I'm an office worker.

✅ A Handy Rule of Thumb

A question word takes 이/가 (누가, 뭐가, 어디가), and the answer replaces the question word, so it keeps 이/가 too. If the question asks "what about X?", reply with 은/는.

👉 This, That & That Over There: 이 / 그 / 저

Korean splits "this / that" into three distances instead of two. The key is where the thing is relative to both speakers.

PrefixMeaningThingCasual speechPlace
이near me (the speaker)이것 (this thing)이거여기 (here)
그near you (the listener), or something already mentioned그것 (that thing)그거거기 (there)
저far from both of us저것 (that thing over there)저거저기 (over there)

The same 이/그/저 go in front of nouns: 이 사람 (this person), 그 책 (that book), 저 가방 (that bag over there). Careful: the 저 in 저것 ("that over there") is a different word from 저 meaning "I". Context always makes it clear.

In speech, Koreans shorten these constantly. You will hear the short forms far more than the textbook ones:

FullSpokenRomanizationMeaning
이것은이건igeonthis (topic)
이것이이게igethis (subject)
그것은그건geugeonthat (topic)
저것이저게jeogethat over there (subject)

❓ Asking: 뭐예요? 누구예요? 어디예요?

Question words slot into the same place as the answer. In English you move "what" to the front; in Korean you just swap the noun for the question word and leave everything else alone. 뭐 (what), 누구 (who) and 어디 (where) all end in a vowel, so they take 예요.

QuestionAnswerEnglish
이게 뭐예요?그건 우산이에요.What's this? · That's an umbrella.
저분은 누구예요?저분은 제 선생님이에요.Who is that (person over there)? · That's my teacher.
화장실이 어디예요?저기예요.Where is the restroom? · It's over there.
여기가 어디예요?여기는 서울역이에요.Where is this (place)? · This is Seoul Station.
이거 누구 가방이에요?제 가방이에요.Whose bag is this? · It's my bag.

Notice 누구 가방 = "whose bag": just put 누구 in front of the noun. And 저분 is a politer way to say 저 사람 ("that person") when the person might hear you.

💡 Two Set Phrases for Now: 있어요 / 없어요

You'll learn the grammar properly in Lessons 7 and 9, but these two are too useful to wait: 있어요 = "there is / I have" and 없어요 = "there isn't / I don't have". 물 있어요? (Do you have water?) · 네, 있어요. · 시간 없어요. (I don't have time.) Note they are not the same as 이에요: 이에요 says what something is; 있어요 says that something exists or is had.

Spot the grammar: 김민지예요 (ends in a vowel), 캐나다 사람이에요 (ends in ㅁ), 학생이 아니에요, 저분은 누구예요, and 그건 (= 그것은) because the book is near Alex, the listener.

🌏 Cultural Note — Leave "you" out: Korean has a word for "you" (당신), but in everyday conversation it can sound cold, confrontational or overly intimate. Koreans instead use the person's name + 씨 (알렉스 씨는요?), a title (선생님), or simply drop the word entirely because context makes it obvious. That's why 이름이 뭐예요? has no "your" in it. Dropping the topic is normal too: once you've said 저는 once, you rarely need to repeat it.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Spelling 아니에요 as 아니예요. Very common, even among Koreans online, but the standard spelling is 아니에요.
  • Choosing by the English word. 이에요/예요, 은/는 and 이/가 depend on how the Korean noun ends: 커피예요 (vowel), 물이에요 (consonant).
  • Saying 저가 or 누구가. These fuse: 제가, 누가.
  • Putting a space before 이에요. It's glued on: 학생이에요, not 학생 이에요. But 아니에요 is a separate word: 학생이 아니에요.
  • Using 은/는 on a question word. 누구는 선생님이에요? is wrong; say 누가 선생님이에요?

❓ Common Questions

Is 이에요 a verb meaning "to be"?

Almost. Grammarians call its dictionary form 이다, the "copula". It only links a noun to another noun ("A is B"). It does not mean "to be located" or "to exist"; that job belongs to 있어요. So "The book is on the desk" won't use 이에요. You'll build those sentences in Lesson 9.

I've heard both 이름이 뭐예요? and 이름은 뭐예요? Which is right?

Both are grammatical. 이름이 뭐예요? is the standard, neutral way to ask. 이름은 뭐예요? adds a light "and as for your name…?" feel, for example after you've already asked someone's age or job. When in doubt, use 이.

Can I just leave the particles out?

In casual speech Koreans often drop them, as in 이거 뭐예요? or 물 있어요?. But dropping them well is a skill that depends on context, and it's easy to sound odd. Practise with particles now; you'll drop them naturally later, the way native speakers do.

Why does 예요 sometimes sound like "에요"?

In relaxed speech, 의사예요 often sounds like 의사에요. It's still spelled 예요 after a vowel. Pronunciation shortcuts like this are normal; the spelling rule stays the same.

🌟 Going Further

  1. Write a five-sentence self-introduction using 저는, one 이에요 and one 예요 sentence, one 아니에요 sentence, and one contrast with 제 친구는. Read it aloud.
  2. Walk around your room pointing at objects. Ask 이게 뭐예요? and answer with the Korean name if you know it (책이에요, 물이에요, 가방이에요). For anything far away, switch to 저게 / 저기.
