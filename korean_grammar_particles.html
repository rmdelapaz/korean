🇰🇷 Lesson 9: Particles of Place, Time & People

🧭 The Big Idea: Prepositions That Come After

In English, little words like to, at, from and with go before the noun: "to school", "at the library", "with a friend". Korean does the same job with particles that stick to the end of the noun. That's why linguists call them postpositions. So "to school" is literally "school-to": 학교에. "With a friend" is "friend-with": 친구하고.

You have already met four particles. In Lesson 5 you learned the topic particle 은/는 and the subject particle 이/가, and in Lesson 7 the object particle 을/를. Those tell you who does what. The particles in this lesson answer the other big questions of a sentence: where? when? to whom? with whom? from when until when? Once you have them, your sentences stop being "I eat rice" and start being "I eat rice with my sister at a restaurant at 7."

💡 Mental Model: Tags on Luggage

Think of every noun in a Korean sentence as a suitcase and every particle as the luggage tag. The tag tells the verb what role that suitcase plays. Because the tags do the work, Korean word order is fairly flexible — as long as the verb comes last, 친구하고 도서관에서 and 도서관에서 친구하고 mean the same thing.

📍 에 — To, At, In (Destination, Location, Time)

에 is the "point" particle. It marks a point you move toward, a point where something simply is, or a point in time. It never changes shape — 에 attaches after both vowels and consonants.

📐 Formula

Place + 에 + 가다 / 오다 → go / come to a place

Place + 에 + 있다 / 없다 → be / not be at a place

Time + 에 → at / on / in a time

1. Destination (with movement verbs)

저는 학교에 가요.jeoneun hakgyoe gayo.I go to school.

주말에 카페에 가요.jumare kapee gayo.I go to a café on the weekend.

3. Time

⚠️ Time Words That Refuse 에

Clock times, days, months, seasons and parts of the day take 에 (세 시에, 월요일에, 여름에, 저녁에). But words that are already "relative to now" do not: 오늘 (today), 내일 (tomorrow), 어제 (yesterday), 지금 (now), 매일 (every day). English has the same quirk — you say "on Monday" but never "on today".

🏃 에서 — At (Where Action Happens) & From

에서 also attaches to places, but it has a different personality. 에 is a static point; 에서 is a stage where something happens. If someone is doing something at a place — studying, eating, working, meeting — use 에서. Like 에, it never changes shape.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
도서관에서 공부해요.doseogwaneseo gongbuhaeyo.I study at the library.
식당에서 밥을 먹어요.sikdangeseo babeul meogeoyo.I eat at a restaurant.
회사에서 일해요.hoesaeseo ilhaeyo.I work at a company.
카페에서 친구를 만나요.kapeeseo chingureul mannayo.I meet a friend at a café.
집에서 텔레비전을 봐요.jibeseo tellebijeoneul bwayo.I watch TV at home.

에서 has a second job: "from" a place — the starting point of movement.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
학교에서 집에 와요.hakgyoeseo jibe wayo.I come home from school.
회사에서 전화해요.hoesaeseo jeonhwahaeyo.I'm calling from the office. (or: at the office — context decides)

💡 Honest Nuance: 살다

With 살다 (to live), both 서울에 살아요 and 서울에서 살아요 are correct and natural. "Living" sits on the line between being somewhere and doing something somewhere, so Korean allows either. Don't let it shake your rule — for nearly every other verb the choice is clear.

🎁 에게 / 한테 / 께 — To a Person

When the target is a person or animal (giving to, calling, sending to), Korean switches from 에 to a people particle. 에 is for places and things; 에게 is for living beings.

ParticleStyleUse
에게neutral, common in writingthe textbook form
한테spoken, everydaywhat you'll hear most in conversation
께honorificto someone you honour: grandparents, teachers, bosses
KoreanRomanizationEnglish
친구에게 선물을 줘요.chinguege seonmureul jwoyo.I give a present to my friend.
동생한테 전화해요.dongsaenghante jeonhwahaeyo.I call my younger sibling.
엄마한테 메시지를 보내요.eommahante mesijireul bonaeyo.I send Mom a message.
할머니께 선물을 드려요.halmeonikke seonmureul deuryeoyo.I give Grandma a present. (드리다 = humble "give", Lesson 13)
회사에 전화해요.hoesae jeonhwahaeyo.I call the company. (not a person → 에)

에게서 / 한테서 — From a Person

Add 서 and the direction flips: from someone. Compare 에서 (from a place) — the 서 is the same "from" idea.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
친구한테서 선물을 받아요.chinguhanteseo seonmureul badayo.I get a present from a friend.
선생님에게서 한국어를 배워요.seonsaengnimegeseo hangugeoreul baewoyo.I learn Korean from the teacher.

💡 You'll Hear the 서 Dropped

With verbs like 받다 (receive) and 배우다 (learn), the verb already makes the direction obvious, so people often drop 서: 친구한테 선물을 받아요 is perfectly natural. Keep 서 while you're learning; just don't be surprised when native speakers leave it out.

➕ 도 (Also) & 만 (Only)

도 means "also / too" and 만 means "only / just". Both attach directly to the noun they focus on.

📐 The Replacement Rule

도 replaces 은/는, 이/가 and 을/를: 저는 → 저도, 커피를 → 커피도.
But it stacks after other particles: 집에서 → 집에서도, 서울에 → 서울에도.
만 usually replaces 이/가 and 을/를 too (물을 → 물만), and also stacks after place/time particles (주말에만).

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
저도 학생이에요.jeodo haksaengieyo.I'm a student too.
저는 커피도 마셔요.jeoneun keopido masyeoyo.I drink coffee too.
집에서도 공부해요.jibeseodo gongbuhaeyo.I study at home too.
물만 마셔요.mulman masyeoyo.I only drink water.
주말에만 일해요.jumareman ilhaeyo.I only work on weekends.
한국어만 공부해요.hangugeoman gongbuhaeyo.I study only Korean.

🤝 With & And: 하고 / 와·과 / (이)랑

Korean has three ways to say "with" (someone) or "and" (between two nouns). They mean the same thing; they differ in register, like "and" vs "as well as" in English. Add 같이 (together) for extra clarity: 친구하고 같이.

ParticleAfter a vowelAfter a consonant (받침)Register

와 / 과커피와빵과writing, formal speech
랑 / 이랑언니랑동생이랑casual, friendly speech
KoreanRomanizationEnglish
친구하고 영화를 봐요.chinguhago yeonghwareul bwayo.I watch a movie with a friend.
빵과 우유를 사요.ppanggwa uyureul sayo.I buy bread and milk.
커피와 차를 마셔요.keopiwa chareul masyeoyo.I drink coffee and tea.
동생이랑 같이 가요.dongsaengirang gachi gayo.I go together with my younger sibling.
언니랑 밥을 먹어요.eonirang babeul meogeoyo.I eat with my older sister.

⚠️ Watch the Backwards Pattern

와/과 is the one pair where the vowel form looks "longer": 와 after a vowel, 과 after a consonant (커피와, 빵과). Compare 가 after a vowel / 이 after a consonant — the logic is the same (smooth sound), but the shapes trip people up.

⏱️ 부터 … 까지 & 에서 … 까지 — From … Until

까지 means "until / up to / as far as". Pair it with a starting particle: 부터 for a starting time, 에서 for a starting place.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
아홉 시부터 여섯 시까지 일해요.ahop sibuteo yeoseot sikkaji ilhaeyo.I work from 9 until 6.
월요일부터 금요일까지 학교에 가요.woryoilbuteo geumyoilkkaji hakgyoe gayo.I go to school Monday to Friday.
서울에서 부산까지 멀어요.seoureseo busankkaji meoreoyo.It's far from Seoul to Busan.
집에서 학교까지 버스를 타요.jibeseo hakgyokkaji beoseureul tayo.I take the bus from home to school.

📦 있어요 / 없어요 — There Is, I Have

있다 is one of the most useful verbs in Korean because it covers two English ideas: "there is / to be somewhere" and "to have". Its opposite is a separate word, 없다. The thing that exists (or that you have) takes 이/가.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
시간이 있어요?sigani isseoyo?Do you have time?
돈이 없어요.doni eopseoyo.I don't have money.
저는 형이 있어요.jeoneun hyeongi isseoyo.I have an older brother.
근처에 편의점이 있어요?geuncheoe pyeonuijeomi isseoyo?Is there a convenience store nearby?

You'll soon notice 있다 hiding inside common words: 맛있어요 (tasty — "flavour exists") and 재미있어요 (fun — "fun exists"). Their opposites use 없다: 맛없어요, 재미없어요.

🗺️ Position Words + 에

English says "on the desk", "under the chair". Korean builds it as Noun + position word + 에 — literally "desk's top-at". The position words are nouns, so the order is always: the reference object first, then the position, then 에.

✅ The Same 에 / 에서 Rule Applies

Position words obey the rule you already know: 앞에 있어요 (is in front), but 앞에서 만나요 (meet in front — an action). One rule, used everywhere.

💬 Dialogue: Where Are You?

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
민지수진 씨, 지금 어디에 있어요?Sujin, where are you now?
수진도서관에 있어요. 도서관에서 한국어를 공부해요.I'm at the library. I'm studying Korean at the library.
민지저도 한국어를 공부해요! 오늘 저녁에 시간 있어요?I study Korean too! Do you have time this evening?
수진네, 있어요. 여섯 시까지 공부해요.Yes, I do. I'm studying until six.
민지그럼 여섯 시에 도서관 앞에서 만나요.Then let's meet in front of the library at six.
수진좋아요. 지훈 씨도 와요?Great. Is Jihun coming too?
민지아니요, 지훈 씨는 회사에 있어요. 아홉 시까지 일해요.No, Jihun is at the office. He works until nine.
수진그럼 우리 둘만 만나요!Then it's just the two of us!

Spot the particles: 에 for being somewhere and for a clock time, 에서 for studying and meeting, 까지 for "until", 도 for "too", and 만 for "only". Notice also that 만나요 here works like "let's meet" — Korean often uses the plain polite present as a suggestion (more in Lesson 17).

🇰🇷 Cultural note — "Meet me at Exit 11." Seoul runs on its subway, and a classic way to set a meeting point is a numbered station exit: 강남역 11번 출구 앞에서 만나요 ("Let's meet in front of Gangnam Station Exit 11"). Korean addresses and directions also move from big to small — country, city, district, building — so you'll hear strings of place nouns joined by position words and 에 all the time.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 도서관에 공부해요 ✗ → 도서관에서 공부해요. Studying is an action, so the place needs 에서.
  • 오늘에 가요 ✗ → 오늘 가요. 오늘, 내일, 어제, 지금, 매일 never take 에.
  • 친구에 전화해요 ✗ → 친구한테 (or 에게) 전화해요. People take 에게/한테; places take 에.
  • 저는도 ✗ → 저도. 도 replaces 은/는 — you don't stack them.
  • 빵와 ✗ → 빵과. After a consonant, use 과 (or just use 하고 and avoid the question!).
  • 위에 책상 ✗ → 책상 위에. The object comes first, then the position word.

If these feel like a lot of tiny tags to juggle, that's normal. Every "wrong" particle you produce is feedback that sharpens your ear — learners who make lots of particle mistakes early usually get them right sooner, because they're actually using them.

❓ Common Questions

Can I just leave particles out?

In casual speech Koreans often drop 은/는, 이/가 and 을/를 (시간 있어요? instead of 시간이 있어요?). Place, time and people particles like 에서, 에게, 부터 and 까지 are dropped far less, because they carry meaning the listener can't guess. As a learner, keep them all — dropping them naturally is something you'll pick up by listening.

Should I use 에게 or 한테?

They mean the same. 한테 is what you'll say and hear in conversation; 에게 appears in writing and slightly more formal speech. Use 께 only when the person deserves honorific treatment — you'll learn the matching honorific verbs in Lesson 13.

Why are there three words for "with/and"?

Register. 하고 is the everyday all-rounder, 와/과 feels written or formal, and (이)랑 feels warm and casual. If you're unsure, 하고 is never wrong in conversation.

Is it 부터 or 에서 for "from"?

For a beginner: 부터 for time (아홉 시부터), 에서 for places (서울에서). You will occasionally hear 부터 with a place when it means "starting with" (e.g. "let's clean starting from the kitchen"), but 에서 is the normal "from a place".
