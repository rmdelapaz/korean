🇰🇷 Lesson 26: Technology & Communication

Korea is one of the most connected countries on earth: fast internet almost everywhere, one messaging app nearly everyone shares, and a texting culture full of playful abbreviations. This lesson gives you the vocabulary for phones, apps and the internet, the verbs that go with them, and a first look at chat slang. Then it puts the modifiers and comparisons from Lesson 25 straight to work — "the app I use most", "the photo I took yesterday", "faster than my old phone".

💡 노트북 and 핸드폰 — Korean-made "English"

Some tech words look English but aren't used that way in English. 노트북 ("notebook") means a laptop, and 핸드폰 ("hand phone") means a mobile phone. English speakers won't understand them, and Koreans may not understand "laptop" — so learn them as Korean words.

💬 KakaoTalk (카카오톡)

KakaoTalk (카톡 for short) is the messaging app in Korea — well over 90% of people use it. It handles personal chats, group chats, work messages, payments (카카오페이), taxis (카카오택시) and more. "카톡 해!" even works as a verb phrase: "message me on Kakao!"

⚠️ 여보세요 Is a Phone Word

여보세요 is how you answer or start a phone call. Don't use it as a face-to-face "hello" — that's 안녕하세요. (In person, 여보세요 is only used to call out sharply to get a stranger's attention, which sounds rather abrupt.)

✅ How ㅋㅋㅋ Works

More ㅋ's = more laughter: ㅋㅋ (mild chuckle), ㅋㅋㅋ (laughing), ㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋㅋ (really funny). A single ㅋ can read as dry or even sarcastic, so most people send at least two. ㅎㅎ feels gentler and is common with people you're less close to.

🚫 Know It, Don't Send It: ㅁㅊ

You'll also see ㅁㅊ (미친, "crazy"). Among close friends it can mean "insane(ly good)", but 미친 is also a rude insult. It's worth recognising; it's not worth using.

💡 Culture Note — Naver (네이버): Naver is Korea's biggest portal — search, maps, news, blogs, shopping, webtoons and a very good dictionary. Google is widely used too, but for Korean restaurants, local reviews and directions, Koreans usually reach for Naver (or Kakao Map). Google Maps directions work poorly in Korea, so travellers should install Naver Map or Kakao Map.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 친구를 전화했어요 ✗ → 친구한테 전화했어요 ✓. You call to a person in Korean, so use 한테/에게 (께 for elders).
  • 사진을 만들어요 ✗ → 사진을 찍어요 ✓. Photos are 찍다.
  • 끄어요 ✗ → 꺼요 ✓. 끄다 is a ㅡ-verb (Lesson 21).
  • ㅋㅋ or ㅇㅋ in a message to your teacher — too casual. Write 네, 알겠습니다 or 감사합니다 in full.
  • 여보세요 as an in-person greeting — use 안녕하세요.

Tech vocabulary changes fast even for native speakers — nobody knows it all. If you guess a word and it's wrong, you've just found the next word worth learning.

❓ Common Questions

Is it 핸드폰 or 휴대폰?

Both mean mobile phone. 핸드폰 is the everyday word; 휴대폰 (and the fuller 휴대 전화) sounds a little more formal and appears in writing and ads. Younger people often just say 폰.

Why is the chat dialogue in 반말?

Close friends of the same age use casual speech, especially in texts. You learned in Lesson 13 that 반말 drops the 요 (보낼게요 → 보낼게). Stay with 해요체 until a Korean friend suggests you speak comfortably (말 놓아도 돼요).

What's the difference between 문자 and 카톡?

문자 is a regular SMS text; 카톡 is a KakaoTalk message. In practice most personal messaging happens on KakaoTalk, so 카톡 할게요 is often more natural than 문자 보낼게요. 문자 is still used for verification codes, deliveries and official notices.

How do I read ㅋㅋㅋ aloud?

You don't really — it's laughter on screen. If you had to, people say 크크크 or just laugh. The same goes for ㅠㅠ, which is a picture of tears, not a word.
