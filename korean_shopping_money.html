🇰🇷 Lesson 14: Shopping & Money

Shopping is the perfect place to test your Korean, because every exchange is short and predictable: a greeting, a question about price or size, a decision, a payment. It's also where you'll hear the most honorific speech — shop staff speak to customers very politely — so this lesson puts Lesson 13's speech levels to work straight away, alongside the Sino-Korean numbers you need for prices.

💰 Korean Won (원)

Korean currency is the won (₩). There are no decimals — prices are whole numbers. Bills: ₩1,000 · ₩5,000 · ₩10,000 · ₩50,000. Coins: ₩10 · ₩50 · ₩100 · ₩500. Money always uses Sino-Korean numbers (Lesson 6).

AmountKoreanRoughly (USD)
₩1,000천 원~$0.70
₩5,000오천 원~$3.50
₩10,000만 원~$7
₩15,000만 오천 원~$10.50

₩100,000십만 원~$70

(Exchange rates move; think "about ₩1,400 to the dollar" as a rough guide.)

💡 Reading Prices: Think in 만

Split the number into groups of four digits from the right. ₩23,500 = 2|3500 → 이만 삼천오백 원. ₩128,000 = 12|8000 → 십이만 팔천 원. And remember: no 일 before 만 or 천 — ₩11,000 is 만 천 원.

🌏 Culture Note — Over-Polite Service Korean: You will often hear staff say things like 커피 나오셨습니다 ("your coffee has come out", with the honorific -시-) or 이 가방은 오만 원이세요. Strictly speaking this is incorrect — honorifics are for people, not coffee or prices — and grammar guides criticise it, but it's so common in service settings that you should expect it. Don't copy it; just understand it as "extra politeness".

🌏 Culture Note — Where to Bargain: Bargaining (좀 깎아 주세요) is normal at traditional markets such as Namdaemun or Dongdaemun, especially when buying several items, but not in department stores, convenience stores or chain shops. Korea is extremely card-friendly — cards and phone payments work almost everywhere, even for a ₩1,000 snack — though small market stalls may prefer cash. Shops charge for plastic bags, so 봉투 필요 없어요 is a useful sentence.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 일만 원 / 십천 원 → 만 원.
  • 열다섯 천 원 → money is Sino: 만 오천 원.
  • 천 원 한 개 for "1,000 won each" → the unit comes first, marked with 에: 한 개에 천 원.
  • 저는 찾으세요 → never put the honorific -시- on your own actions: 저는 … 찾아요.
  • 작은 있어요? → 작은 needs a noun after it: 작은 거 있어요?
  • 깎아 주세요 at a department store → prices are fixed there; ask about 할인 instead.

If a clerk's fast honorific speech goes right past you, that's normal — even advanced learners hear it as a blur at first. Catch one word (얼마, 카드, 봉투), answer that, and you're shopping in Korean.

❓ Common Questions

Do I have to use honorifics with shop staff?

No. Polite 해요 style (찾아요, 주세요, 살게요) is exactly right for a customer. Staff use honorifics because you are the customer; you return the courtesy with polite speech and a 감사합니다, not with -시-.

What's the difference between 이거 and 이것?

They are the same word. 이것 (this thing) is shortened to 이거 in speech, and 이것은/이것을 become 이건/이걸. Use 이거 when shopping; you'll see 이것 in writing.

Is tax included in the price?

Yes. Korean price tags include VAT, so the number on the tag is what you pay. Tourists can often claim a VAT refund on larger purchases at shops marked "Tax Free".

Why do some prices end in 입니다 and others in 이에요?

Same meaning, different speech level (Lesson 13): 입니다 is formal 합니다 style, common from staff and in announcements; 이에요 is polite 해요 style, common at markets and between customers.
