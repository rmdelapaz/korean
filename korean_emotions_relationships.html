🇰🇷 Lesson 27: Emotions & Relationships

Being able to say how you feel — and to respond kindly when someone else is struggling — is what turns a language you've studied into one you can live in. This lesson gives you the core feeling words, the vocabulary of friendship and dating, and the uniquely Korean idea of 정. It also shows you one grammar point that surprises every learner: Korean treats your feelings and other people's feelings differently.

😊 Emotion Vocabulary

💡 Why "I'm angry" is 화났어요 (past)

화나다 literally means "anger comes out" — it's an event. Once it has happened, you are angry, so Korean uses the past: 화났어요. ("I got angry" → "I'm angry"). 놀랐어요 ("I was startled") works the same way. English does this too: "I'm surprised" is really a completed past participle.

🪞 My Feelings vs. Their Feelings: -아/어하다

Here is the surprise. In Korean, you can only state a feeling directly when it's your own (or when you ask the listener about theirs). You can't see inside another person, so to describe how someone else feels, you turn the adjective into a verb with -아/어하다 — "shows sadness", "acts happy". You met the same idea in Lesson 17 with 가고 싶어요 (me) vs 가고 싶어해요 (him/her).

✅ Formula: adjective stem + -아/어하다

슬프다 → 슬퍼 + 하다 → 슬퍼하다 · 기쁘다 → 기뻐하다 · 무섭다 → 무서워하다 · 외롭다 → 외로워하다

Build the 해요-form first (슬퍼요), drop 요, add 하다. The new verb then conjugates like any 하다-verb: 슬퍼해요, 슬퍼했어요.

KoreanEnglish
저는 슬퍼요.I'm sad.
친구가 슬퍼해요.My friend is sad (seems sad).
저는 개가 무서워요.I'm scared of dogs.
동생은 개를 무서워해요.My younger sibling is scared of dogs.
할머니께서 선물을 받고 기뻐하셨어요.Grandma was happy to get the present.

Notice the particle shift: with the adjective, the thing you feel about is the subject (개가 무서워요); with the -하다 verb it becomes the object (개를 무서워해요). The same thing happens with 좋다 and 좋아하다: 저는 고양이가 좋아요 = 저는 고양이를 좋아해요.

⚠️ "Marry him" ≠ 그를 결혼해요

In English, marry, date and break up take a direct object or "with". In Korean, 결혼하다, 사귀다 and 헤어지다 all take 하고 / 와·과 / (이)랑 ("with"): 민수 씨하고 결혼했어요. · 여자친구랑 헤어졌어요. Also note 사귀다 → 사귀어요 (no contraction is possible).

💫 Understanding 정 (Jeong)

정 is one of the most important ideas in Korean culture, and there's no exact English translation. It's the deep attachment that builds between people over time — a mix of loyalty, warmth and caring that goes beyond simple friendship or love.

정 applies to family, friends, coworkers, even your favourite restaurant owner. Koreans say 정이 들었어요. ("jeong has settled in") when that bond has formed. It's why friends share food from the same dish, why leaving a job feels so emotional, and why your Korean friend's grandmother may feed you like her own grandchild.

💡 Culture Note — comfort through food: Notice Sujin's instinct: when a friend is down, Koreans often answer with food and company rather than long speeches. "제가 살게요" (I'll buy) is a small act of 정. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 제 친구는 슬퍼요 sounds like you can read their mind → 제 친구는 슬퍼해요.
  • 저는 고양이를 좋아요 ✗ → 고양이가 좋아요 or 고양이를 좋아해요 ✓. Adjective ↔ 이/가; -하다 verb ↔ 을/를.
  • 그 사람을 결혼했어요 ✗ → 그 사람하고 결혼했어요 ✓.
  • 화나요 for "I'm angry (right now)" → usually 화났어요.
  • 무섭어요 / 슬프어요 ✗ → 무서워요 / 슬퍼요 ✓. Remember the irregulars.

Feelings are personal, so it's natural to feel a little shy using these words at first — 부끄러워요 is allowed! Every sentence you try is practice, and a gentle mistake about feelings almost always gets a warm reaction from Korean speakers.

❓ Common Questions

What's the difference between 기쁘다 and 행복하다?

기쁘다 is gladness about a specific event (합격해서 기뻐요). 행복하다 is a broader, deeper state of happiness with your life or situation (요즘 행복해요). Both translate as "happy", but 행복하다 feels bigger.

Can I ask "Are you sad?" with 슬퍼요?

Yes. The -아/어하다 rule is about statements about a third person. When you ask the listener directly (슬퍼요?), you're asking them to report their own feeling, which is fine. Likewise, in a story or novel the narrator may describe characters' feelings directly.

Do Koreans say 사랑해요 often?

Between couples and to children, yes, and it's increasingly common within families. Among friends it's less usual than in some cultures, though playful 사랑해 between close friends exists. To show affection to friends, Koreans more often say 보고 싶어요 or simply share food and time — that's 정 in action.

What's the difference between 걱정돼요 and 걱정해요?

걱정돼요 describes the state of being worried (시험이 걱정돼요 — "I'm worried about the exam"). 걱정해요 is the act of worrying, often about someone (엄마가 제 걱정을 많이 해요). In the negative command, only 걱정하지 마세요 is used.
