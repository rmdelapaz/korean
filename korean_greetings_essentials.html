🇰🇷 Lesson 4: Greetings & Essential Phrases

You can read Hangul and you know why Korean words sound the way they do. Now let's use them with people. In this lesson you'll learn to say hello and goodbye (Korean has two goodbyes), introduce yourself, thank and apologise, and ask the questions every beginner needs. You'll also get a first look at something English doesn't have: speech levels, where the ending of a sentence shows how polite you are being.

📖 Speech Levels in Korean

Korean has multiple speech levels based on formality and politeness. As a beginner, focus on two:

LevelNameWhen to UseEnding
Formal Polite합쇼체 (hapsyo-che)Business, presentations, news, formal first meetings-습니다 / -ㅂ니다
Informal Polite해요체 (haeyo-che)Everyday conversations, most situations — including with strangers-아요 / -어요

Think of it like the difference between "Good afternoon, how may I help you?" and "Hi, what can I get you?" — both are polite, one is more formal. Many phrases in this lesson come in both versions. You'll learn to build these endings yourself in Lesson 7 and study the whole system in Lesson 13; for now, learn each phrase as a whole.

⚠️ Important: 존댓말 (Polite Speech)

Always use polite speech (존댓말) with people you've just met, people older than you, or in formal situations. Using casual speech (반말) with the wrong person is considered very rude in Korean culture. When in doubt, stay polite — nobody is offended by too much politeness.

✅ Two Ways to Say Goodbye!

Korean has two goodbyes depending on who's leaving. 안녕히 가세요 literally means "go in peace" and 안녕히 계세요 means "stay in peace". If you're both leaving (say, parting on the street), you both say 안녕히 가세요.

💡 Culture Note — 씨 (ssi): 씨 is an honorific like "Mr./Ms." attached to a person's full name or first name. Never use 씨 with just a last name — that's rude. Say "수진 씨" or "김수진 씨", not "김 씨." Never use 씨 about yourself, and don't use it for someone clearly senior to you — a teacher is 선생님, not "[name] 씨".
🇰🇷 Culture Note — bows, modesty and "Have you eaten?": A greeting usually comes with a small bow of the head; the more formal the moment, the deeper the bow. When someone compliments your Korean, the natural reply is modest — 아니에요 ("not at all"), exactly as Ray does above — rather than "thank you". And between people who know each other, 밥 먹었어요? ("Have you eaten?") often works as a friendly "how are you?" rather than a real question about lunch.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up the two goodbyes. The one who leaves hears 가세요 ("go"); the one who stays hears 계세요 ("stay"). If you're walking out of a shop, say 안녕히 계세요.
  • Using 안녕 with strangers or elders. It's 반말 (casual). Default to 안녕하세요.
  • Putting 씨 after a surname or after your own name. Use a first or full name, and only for other people.
  • Saying 나 instead of 저 in polite speech. In a polite sentence, "I" is 저: 저는 레이예요.
  • Saying 감사합니다 exactly as spelled. Remember Lesson 3: it's [감사함니다].

❓ Common Questions

What does 안녕하세요 literally mean?

안녕 means "peace" or "well-being", and 하세요 is a polite, respectful form of "do/be". So 안녕하세요 is roughly "Are you at peace?" — which is why you can say it at any time of day, to anyone, and why the goodbyes 안녕히 가세요 and 안녕히 계세요 ("go / stay in peace") use the same word.

감사합니다 or 고맙습니다 — which is more polite?

Both are formal and both are fine in almost any situation. 감사 is a Sino-Korean (Chinese-origin) word; 고맙다 is native Korean. 감사합니다 can feel slightly more formal, so it's the safe choice in shops, offices and with strangers. 고마워요 is the softer informal polite version for people you know.

Should I say 네 or 예 for "yes"?

Both are correct. 네 is more common in everyday speech; 예 sounds a little more formal or old-fashioned and is often heard from older speakers and in very polite settings. Note that 네 is also used simply to show you're listening — "mm-hm, I'm following" — not always to agree.

How do I choose between the 합니다 style and the 해요 style?

For now, use the phrases exactly as you learned them: formal (합니다) for introductions in formal settings, presentations and service situations, informal polite (해요) for everyday conversation. Both are polite. Lesson 13 lays out the whole system, including casual 반말.
