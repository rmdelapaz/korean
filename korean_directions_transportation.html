🇰🇷 Lesson 16: Directions & Transportation

Korea's public transport is one of the best in the world: fast, clean, cheap and signposted in Korean and English. But the moment you need to ask a stranger where exit 5 is, or tell a taxi driver where to stop, you need Korean. The good news is that you already have almost all the grammar you need. This lesson is mostly about vocabulary and a few set phrases, and then putting your particles and your new past tense to work.

📍 Location Words

Remember the position nouns from Lesson 9? They work like "the top", "the front" in English: you say the thing + position + 에. So "in front of the station" is literally "station's front at": 역 앞에.

💡 Why 가세요 and 도세요?

Directions are given with the honorific -(으)세요 you met in Lesson 13. It's a polite "please do" when addressed to a stranger. 가다 → 가세요, 건너다 → 건너세요. 돌다 (to turn) is an ㄹ-verb, and its ㄹ drops before 세: 도세요. You'll get the full story on requests in Lesson 19 and on ㄹ-drop in Lesson 21. For now, learn 도세요 as a chunk.

💡 A few chunks to learn whole

가까워요 (it's close) comes from 가깝다, an irregular verb (Lesson 21 explains why the ㅂ turns into 우). 걸어서 (on foot) comes from 걷다 plus a connector you'll learn in Lesson 23. Until then, just treat both as words.

✅ T-money Card (교통카드)

Get a T-money card at any convenience store. It works on subways and buses nationwide, in most taxis and in convenience stores. Tap on when you board and tap off when you get off the bus too: that's how you get the free transfer discount. Recharge at convenience stores or subway machines.

🧩 Grammar in Action

No new grammar in this lesson, only new places to use what you already know. Here are four patterns that do nearly all of the work in transport talk.

1. The particle (으)로: "by" and "toward"

This lesson's one new particle. (으)로 marks the means (by bus) and the direction (to the left). The rule matches the others you know: after a 받침 add 으로, after a vowel 로, and after ㄹ it's also just 로.

Formula: noun + (으)로

vowel ending → 로: 버스로, 택시로, 기차로, 출구로
ㄹ ending → 로: 지하철로, 길로
other 받침 → 으로: 왼쪽으로, 오른쪽으로, 2호선으로, 공항으로

Why the special ㄹ rule? ㄹ already flows smoothly into ㄹ, so no buffer vowel 으 is needed.

KoreanEnglish
회사에 지하철로 가요.I go to work by subway.
공항에 택시로 갔어요.I went to the airport by taxi.
부산에 KTX로 가요.I go to Busan by KTX.
시청역에서 2호선으로 갈아타세요.Change to Line 2 at City Hall Station.
3번 출구로 나가세요.Go out through exit 3.

2. 에 vs 에서 (from Lesson 9)

You go to a place with 에, but you get on, get off and change at a place with 에서, because those are actions happening there. And 에서 … 까지 gives you "from … to".

KoreanEnglish
강남역에 가요.I'm going to Gangnam Station.
강남역에서 내려요.I get off at Gangnam Station.
어디에서 버스를 타요?Where do I catch the bus?
서울에서 부산까지 두 시간 반 걸려요.It takes two and a half hours from Seoul to Busan.
집에서 학교까지 멀어요.It's far from home to school.

Notice that 타다 takes the object particle: 버스를 타요, 지하철을 타요. English says "get on the bus", but Korean treats the bus as the thing you ride.

The -아/어 주세요 ("please do it for me") and -아도 돼요 ("is it OK if…") patterns are taught properly in Lesson 19. For now these are phrases to use as they are, and they'll get you anywhere in Seoul.

💡 Culture Note: Korean taxis are safe, metered and relatively affordable. Regular taxis (일반 택시) are usually orange, silver or white; deluxe taxis (모범 택시) are black, cost more and have more experienced drivers. Drivers often navigate by landmarks, not street addresses, so "Lotte Department Store near City Hall" works better than a numbered address. Late at night many people book through the Kakao T app. On the subway, the seats at the ends of each car (노약자석) are reserved for the elderly, pregnant and disabled. Leave them free even when the train is empty.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 역에 내려요 ❌ → 역에서 내려요 ✅. Getting off is an action at a place: 에서.
  • 버스에 타요 is heard in speech, but the textbook form is 버스를 타요. Use 을/를 with 타다.
  • 왼쪽로 ❌ → 왼쪽으로 ✅. 쪽 ends in a 받침, so it takes 으로.
  • 지하철으로 ❌ → 지하철로 ✅. ㄹ-final nouns take plain 로.
  • 발로 가요 for "on foot" ❌ → 걸어서 가요 ✅.
  • 시간이 걸려요 is "it takes time". Don't use 해요 for duration: "30분 해요" ❌ → 30분 걸려요 ✅.

Getting lost is part of learning a city, and so is fumbling your particles. Every time someone corrects you, you've just been given free, personalised feedback. Take it!

❓ Common Questions

Is 역 only for the subway?

No. 역 is any rail station: subway, train or KTX. 서울역 is both a subway stop and the main train station. Buses stop at a 정류장, and long-distance buses leave from a 터미널 (terminal).

Why do people say 이번 역 and 다음 역?

이번 means "this (coming) one" and 다음 means "next". Subway announcements say 이번 역은 … for the station you're arriving at now, and 다음 역은 … for the one after. If you hear your stop after 이번, get ready to get off.

Can I just say the place name to a taxi driver?

Yes. "명동이요" (Myeongdong, please) with a polite 요 attached to the noun is completely natural. 명동까지 가 주세요 is the full, slightly more polite version.

How do I read line numbers like 2호선?

호선 uses Sino-Korean numbers: 일호선, 이호선, 삼호선, 사호선… Exit numbers use them too: 3번 출구 = 삼 번 출구. You learned this number system in Lesson 6.

🌟 Going Further

  1. Open a Seoul subway map (the Kakao Metro or Naver Map app works well) and plan a real route, for example 홍대입구 to 잠실. Write it out in Korean: which line you take, where you change and where you get off, using 타요, 갈아타요 and 내려요.
  2. Write a five-sentence past-tense story of your last trip anywhere, even to the supermarket: how you went, how long it took, and one thing that went wrong (버스가 안 왔어요…).
