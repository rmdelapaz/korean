🇰🇷 Lesson 5: Numbers, Time & Dates

🎯 What You'll Learn

Korean uses TWO number systems — Sino-Korean (from Chinese) and Native Korean. You'll learn both, plus how to tell time, say dates, and use counters (the words between numbers and nouns).

Estimated Time: 55–65 minutes

⚠️ Numbers 1-4 Change Before Counters!

하나 → 한 · 둘 → 두 · 셋 → 세 · 넷 → 네 · 스물 → 스무

Example: 한 명 (one person), 두 개 (two items), 세 시 (three o'clock)

📅 Days, Months & Dates

Days and months use Sino-Korean numbers.

DayKoreanMonthKorean
Monday월요일January일월
Tuesday화요일February이월
Wednesday수요일March삼월
Thursday목요일June유월 (not 육월)
Friday금요일October시월 (not 십월)
Saturday토요일November십일월
Sunday일요일December십이월

Date format: Year 년 Month 월 Day 일 — 2026년 4월 14일 = April 14, 2026

✅ Easy Pattern

Months = Sino number + 월. Days of the week all end in 요일. Dates = Sino number + 일.

Exception: June is 유월 (not 육월) and October is 시월 (not 십월) for easier pronunciation.

🎯 Summary

🎉 Key Takeaways

  • Korean uses two number systems — learn when to use each
  • Native Korean: hours, counting with counters, age (1–99)
  • Sino-Korean: dates, money, minutes, phone numbers, 100+
  • Time: Native hours + Sino minutes (두 시 삼십 분)
  • Numbers 1–4 shorten before counters (하나→한, 둘→두, 셋→세, 넷→네)
  • Counters are required — you can't just say "three books," it's "책 세 권"
