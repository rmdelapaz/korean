🤒 Describing Symptoms

The pattern: [body part]이/가 아파요 = "My [body part] hurts."

KoreanMeaning
머리가 아파요.I have a headache.
배가 아파요.My stomach hurts.
목이 아파요.My throat hurts.
열이 나요.I have a fever.
기침이 나요.I have a cough.
콧물이 나요.I have a runny nose.
어지러워요.I'm dizzy.
토할 것 같아요.I feel like I'm going to throw up.
감기에 걸렸어요.I caught a cold.
알레르기가 있어요.I have allergies.
다쳤어요.I'm hurt/injured.

💊 At the Pharmacy (약국)

KoreanMeaning
약국 어디예요?Where is the pharmacy?
두통약 있어요?Do you have headache medicine?
감기약 주세요.Cold medicine, please.
소화제 주세요.Digestive medicine, please.
반창고 있어요?Do you have band-aids?
하루에 몇 번 먹어요?How many times a day do I take it?
식후에 드세요.Take it after meals.

✅ Korean Pharmacies

Korean pharmacies (약국) are everywhere and pharmacists are very helpful. Many common medicines that require prescriptions elsewhere are available over the counter. Look for the green cross sign. Pharmacists often speak some English in tourist areas.

KoreanMeaning

보험 있어요?Do you have insurance?
어디가 아프세요?Where does it hurt? (doctor asks)
언제부터 아팠어요?Since when has it hurt?
구급차ambulance
💡 Emergency: Dial 119 for ambulance/fire in Korea, or 112 for police. The 1339 medical helpline has English-speaking operators who can help foreigners find medical care.

🎯 Summary

🎉 Key Takeaways

  • [body part]이/가 아파요 = "My [body part] hurts" — most useful health phrase
  • Korean pharmacies (약국) are accessible and pharmacists are helpful
  • Emergency numbers: 119 (ambulance), 112 (police), 1339 (medical help)
  • Common medicine: 두통약 (headache), 감기약 (cold), 소화제 (digestion)
