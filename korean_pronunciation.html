🇰🇷 Lesson 3: Pronunciation Rules

🎯 What You'll Learn

Korean spelling is consistent, but pronunciation has predictable sound changes. Master these rules and you'll sound much more natural. This lesson covers 받침 (batchim), linking, nasalization, aspiration, and tensification.

Estimated Time: 50–60 minutes

📖 받침 (Batchim) — Final Consonants

받침 literally means "support" — it's the consonant at the bottom of a syllable block. Korean has 27 possible 받침 combinations, but they reduce to only 7 sounds when pronounced:

Pronounced As받침 LettersExampleReading
ㄱ [k]ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ국, 부엌, 밖guk, bueok, bak
ㄴ [n]ㄴ눈, 산nun, san
ㄷ [t]ㄷ, ㅌ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅎ맛, 있다, 꽃mat, itda, kkot
ㄹ [l]ㄹ달, 물dal, mul
ㅁ [m]ㅁ밤, 김bam, gim
ㅂ [p]ㅂ, ㅍ밥, 앞bap, ap
ㅇ [ng]ㅇ강, 방gang, bang

⚠️ Key Insight

Seven 받침 letters (ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅌ, ㅎ, ㄷ) ALL sound like [t] when at the end of a syllable. This is why 맛 (taste) and 맞 (correct) sound the same in isolation!

🧪 Try It: The 받침 Reducer

Type any Korean word to see how each syllable's final consonant is actually pronounced, and which rule collapses it. Try 꽃밭, 값, 읽다, 없다, or 부엌.

🔗 Linking (연음, Yeon-eum)

When a syllable ending in a 받침 is followed by a syllable starting with ㅇ, the 받침 "links" forward and becomes the initial consonant of the next syllable:

WrittenLinkingPronouncedMeaning
먹어ㄱ links → 거머거 [meo-geo]eat (casual)
읽어ㄱ links → 거일거 [il-geo]read (casual)
한국어ㄱ links → 거한구거 [han-gu-geo]Korean language
음악(no ㅇ follows)음악 [eu-mak]music

✅ Linking Rule

받침 + ㅇ = 받침 moves to become the initial consonant of the next syllable. This is the most common and most useful pronunciation rule. It makes Korean flow smoothly between syllables.

👃 Nasalization (비음화)

When certain consonants meet nasal consonants (ㄴ, ㅁ), they change to match. The stop consonants become their nasal counterparts:

받침Before ㄴ or ㅁBecomesExamplePronounced
ㄱ [k]+ ㄴ/ㅁㅇ [ng]한국말한궁말 [han-gung-mal]
ㄷ [t]+ ㄴ/ㅁㄴ [n]받는반는 [ban-neun]
ㅂ [p]+ ㄴ/ㅁㅁ [m]합니다함니다 [ham-ni-da]

💨 Aspiration (격음화)

When ㅎ meets certain consonants (or vice versa), they combine into an aspirated consonant:

CombinationResultExamplePronounced
ㄱ + ㅎㅋ축하추카 [chu-ka]
ㄷ + ㅎㅌ못해모태 [mo-tae]
ㅂ + ㅎㅍ답하다다파다 [da-pa-da]
ㅈ + ㅎㅊ좋다조타 [jo-ta]

💪 Tensification (경음화)

After a 받침 pronounced as [k], [t], or [p], the following plain consonant becomes tense (doubled):

WrittenChangePronouncedMeaning
학교ㄱ + ㄱ → ㄱ + ㄲ학꾜 [hak-kkyo]school
식당ㄱ + ㄷ → ㄱ + ㄸ식땅 [sik-ttang]restaurant
입구ㅂ + ㄱ → ㅂ + ㄲ입꾸 [ip-kku]entrance

🎵 Putting It All Together

Let's practice reading real sentences with these rules applied:

감사합니다 (thank you, formal)

Written: 감-사-합-니-다

Rules: 합 + 니 → nasalization → 함니다

Pronounced: 감-사-함-니-다 [gam-sa-ham-ni-da]

좋아요 (it's good / I like it)

Written: 좋-아-요

Rules: ㅎ 받침 + ㅇ → ㅎ becomes silent, linking

Pronounced: 조-아-요 [jo-a-yo]

먹는 (eating)

Written: 먹-는

Rules: ㄱ + ㄴ → nasalization → ㅇ + ㄴ

Pronounced: 멍-는 [meong-neun]

🎯 Summary

🎉 Key Takeaways

  • All 27 받침 reduce to just 7 sounds: ㄱ ㄴ ㄷ ㄹ ㅁ ㅂ ㅇ
  • Linking: 받침 + ㅇ → consonant moves forward
  • Nasalization: stops become nasals before ㄴ/ㅁ (ㄱ→ㅇ, ㄷ→ㄴ, ㅂ→ㅁ)
  • Aspiration: consonant + ㅎ → aspirated version (ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅍ, ㅊ)
  • Tensification: after [k/t/p] 받침, plain consonants become tense
