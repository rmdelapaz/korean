🇰🇷 Lesson 15: Passive Verbs

💡 Welcome to B2 (≈ TOPIK II Level 4)

This lesson opens the second half of the tier. Level 4 on TOPIK II (roughly 150 of 300 points) describes someone who can handle public and work situations with confidence, follow news and general-interest articles, talk about social and abstract topics in connected paragraphs, and tell formal and casual styles apart and use each where it fits. The grammar gets finer from here. You already have most of the everyday tools. B2 is about perspective: who acted, who made whom act, and what you personally saw. Passive, causative and the retrospective -더- (Lessons 15–17) are exactly that kind of grammar.

Think about the last time your morning went wrong. The alarm didn't go off. The road was jammed. Somebody's elbow got you on the subway, and your coffee got spilled. In stories like that, things happen to you. Often you don't even know who did them. Korean has a whole family of verbs for these moments: 길이 막혔어요 ("the road was jammed"), 문이 닫혔어요 ("the door closed"), 발을 밟혔어요 ("I got my foot stepped on"). This lesson covers them.

🧠 The Mental Model: A Different Verb, Not a Different Construction

English makes the passive with a helper verb: close → be closed, catch → be caught. The verb itself doesn't change. Korean mostly works like the English pair raise / rise: the passive is a separate verb, built by inserting a short suffix into the stem. 닫다 "to close (something)" becomes 닫히다 "to be closed / to close (by itself)". 열다 "to open (something)" becomes 열리다 "to be opened / to open".

✅ The Formula

transitive stem + -이- / -히- / -리- / -기- + 다

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject (이/가). The original doer disappears, or shows up with 에게/한테 (a person or animal) or 에 (a thing or force).

경찰이 도둑을 잡았어요. → 도둑이 경찰에게 잡혔어요.
"The police caught the thief." → "The thief was caught by the police."

English often uses the passive to sound formal or objective. Korean mostly uses it when the following is true, and the natural English translation is often not passive at all:

UseKoreanEnglish
Things happen by themselves (no doer in view)바람 때문에 창문이 저절로 열렸어요.The window blew open because of the wind.
Perception: what reaches your eyes and ears창밖으로 한강이 보이고 기차 소리도 들려요.You can see the Han River from the window, and you can hear the trains too.
Something unpleasant happens to you캠핑 가서 모기한테 온몸을 물렸어요.I went camping and got bitten all over by mosquitoes.
The result or state matters, not the action회의실 문이 잠겨 있어서 들어갈 수 없었어요.The meeting-room door was locked, so we couldn't get in.
Written or news style (the doer isn't important)사고 원인이 아직 밝혀지지 않았다.The cause of the accident has not yet been determined.

🧩 The Four Suffixes: -이-, -히-, -리-, -기-

Here is the honest truth about these suffixes: they are lexical. No rule tells you with certainty which suffix a verb takes, or whether it has a suffix passive at all. You learn 닫히다 the way you learn any other word. There are useful tendencies based on the last sound of the stem, and they will help you guess and remember. They are tendencies, though, not laws.

-이- (mostly after a vowel, or ㅎ / ㄲ)

ActivePassiveExampleEnglish
보다보이다날씨가 맑아서 멀리 산이 보여요.It's clear, so you can see the mountains far away.
놓다놓이다식탁 위에 꽃병이 놓여 있어요.There's a vase (placed) on the table.
쓰다쓰이다이 표현은 뉴스에서 자주 쓰여요.This expression is often used in the news.
쌓다쌓이다휴가 동안 일이 산더미처럼 쌓였어요.Work piled up like a mountain while I was on holiday.
바꾸다바뀌다회사 비밀번호가 또 바뀌었어요.The company password has been changed again.
잠그다잠기다문이 잠겨서 못 들어가요.The door's locked, so I can't get in.

Watch the contractions: 바꾸 + 이 → 바뀌, 나누 + 이 → 나뉘, and 잠그 + 이 → 잠기 (the ㅡ drops). The contracted forms are the standard spelling.

잡다잡히다범인이 사흘 만에 잡혔어요.The culprit was caught after three days.

⚠️ Not Every Verb Has a Suffix Passive

A large group of verbs has no -이/히/리/기- passive at all. These include 주다, 받다, 만나다, 돕다, 배우다, 가르치다, 던지다, 느끼다, 알다, and every 하다-verb. ✗ 주이다, ✗ 만나히다, ✗ 공부하이다 don't exist. For these verbs Korean uses -아/어지다, 되다, 받다 or 당하다 (below). Before you invent a suffix passive, check that it's a real word. A dictionary marks true passives with 피동.

🔁 Double Duty: Verbs That Are Both Passive and Causative

Next lesson you'll meet causative verbs ("make someone do"). They use the same -이/히/리/기- suffixes, so some verbs have identical passive and causative forms. The sentence frame tells you which one you're looking at. A passive has the "receiver" as its subject and no object. A causative has a new "causer" as its subject and usually an object with 을/를.

VerbPassive (it happens to the subject)Causative (the subject makes it happen)
보이다창밖으로 바다가 보여요. — "The sea can be seen."사진 좀 보여 주세요. — "Please show me the photos."
업히다아이가 아빠 등에 업혔어요. — "The child was carried on Dad's back."엄마가 아기를 언니에게 업혔어요. — "Mom had the older sister carry the baby on her back."
안기다아기가 엄마 품에 안겼어요. — "The baby was held in Mom's arms."할머니가 아기를 아빠에게 안겼어요. — "Grandma put the baby in Dad's arms."
읽히다이 책은 쉽게 읽혀요. — "This book reads easily."선생님이 학생들에게 시를 읽혔어요. — "The teacher had the students read a poem."
감기다눈이 저절로 감겨요. — "My eyes close by themselves."엄마가 아이 머리를 감겼어요. — "Mom washed the child's hair."
들리다음악 소리가 들려요. — "I can hear music." (from 듣다)아이에게 가방을 들렸어요. — "I had the child carry the bag." (from 들다)
걸리다벽에 시계가 걸려 있어요. — "A clock hangs on the wall." (from 걸다)엄마가 아이를 걸려서 유치원에 데려갔어요. — "Mom made the child walk to kindergarten." (from 걷다)

The last two rows come from different base verbs (듣다/들다, 걸다/걷다). Their passive readings are far more common, so just recognise the causative ones.

🌀 -아/어지다: The All-Purpose Passive

You met -아/어지다 in Lesson 12 on adjectives, where it means "become": 추워지다, 예뻐지다. On a verb it works as a passive: the action "gets done" or "comes about". It fills the gaps the suffix passives leave, and it's very common in written Korean.

Active-아/어지다ExampleEnglish
만들다만들어지다이 절은 천 년 전에 만들어졌다.This temple was built a thousand years ago.
이루다이루어지다드디어 꿈이 이루어졌어요.My dream has finally come true.
지우다지워지다실수로 파일이 다 지워졌어요.All the files got deleted by mistake.
켜다 / 끄다켜지다 / 꺼지다갑자기 불이 꺼졌다가 다시 켜졌어요.The lights suddenly went off and then came back on.
알리다알려지다그 식당은 방송에 나온 후 널리 알려졌어요.That restaurant became widely known after it was on TV.
느끼다느껴지다그 사람의 진심이 느껴졌어요.I could feel that person's sincerity.
깨다깨지다컵이 떨어져서 깨졌어요.The cup fell and broke.

-아/어지다 often carries a feeling of "it happens on its own / it works". Compare two passives of 쓰다:

KoreanEnglishNuance
이 단어는 요즘 잘 안 쓰여요.This word isn't used much these days.쓰이다 — "be used", a plain passive
이 펜은 부드럽게 잘 써져요.This pen writes smoothly.써지다 — "writing comes out easily", how well the action works

⚠️ The Double-Passive Trap

Stacking both passives, suffix + -아/어지다, gives forms like 쓰여지다, 잊혀지다, 닫혀지다 and 보여지다. They're extremely common in speech and song lyrics (think of the classic song 잊혀진 계절). Even so, the National Institute of Korean Language and school grammar treat them as double passives (이중 피동) and mark them as non-standard. In TOPIK writing and formal texts, use the single form: 쓰이다, 잊히다, 닫히다, 보이다.

🏷️ 하다-Verbs: 되다, 받다, 당하다

하다-verbs can't take a suffix, so they swap 하다 for another light verb. Each one has its own flavour:

Swap 하다 for…Meaning / feelExamples
되다Neutral: "get done". The default, and everywhere in news.사용되다, 발견되다, 결정되다, 취소되다, 연기되다 — 비 때문에 경기가 취소됐어요.
받다You receive something, often something welcome.사랑받다, 존경받다, 초대받다, 칭찬받다 — 그 선생님은 학생들에게 존경받아요.
당하다You suffer something unwelcome.무시당하다, 해고당하다, 거절당하다, 사기를 당하다 — 인터넷 중고 거래에서 사기를 당했어요.

A related everyday verb: 도둑맞다 "to have something stolen": 카페에서 노트북을 도둑맞았어요 ("I had my laptop stolen at a café"). 맞다 here works like 당하다: something bad lands on you.

🎯 -게 되다: "It Turned Out That…"

-게 되다 is the passive of situations. It means "come to / end up / it's been arranged that". The change happened through circumstances, not simply through your own decision. Attach -게 되다 to any verb stem.

KoreanEnglish
회사 일 때문에 다음 달부터 부산에서 살게 됐어요.Because of work, I'll be living in Busan from next month.
처음엔 싫었는데 지금은 김치를 좋아하게 됐어요.I didn't like kimchi at first, but I've come to love it.
이번에 저희 팀이 새 프로젝트를 맡게 되었습니다.Our team has been given the new project this time.
🌏 Culture Note — the modest announcement: Koreans often announce good news with -게 되다 even when they chose it themselves: 저희 다음 달에 결혼하게 됐어요 ("We're getting married next month"), 이번에 승진하게 되었습니다 ("I've been promoted"). Framing the news as something that came about sounds humble and gracious. 결혼해요 is fine too, just plainer. The opposite happens with blame. A child who knocks over a cup says 컵이 깨졌어요 ("the cup broke"), but an adult apologising at work takes ownership with the active form: 제가 실수로 컵을 깼습니다. 죄송합니다. Choosing between the passive and the active is partly a choice about responsibility.

👤 Who Did It? Marking the Agent

Agent is…ParticleExample
A person or animal에게 / 한테 (speech)도둑이 경찰에게 잡혔다. / 개한테 물렸어요.
A thing, force or cause에바람에 문이 닫혔어요. / 가방에 눌렸어요.
Anyone, in formal or written style에 의해(서)이 건물은 유명한 건축가에 의해 설계되었다.

에 의해(서) belongs to news, textbooks and reports, and it goes most naturally with 되다 and -아/어지다. In conversation it sounds stiff. Korean speakers usually either drop the agent or switch to an active sentence. "The window was broken by Minsu" is most naturally 민수가 창문을 깼어요. Heavy use of 에 의해 is a well-known sign of translated English.

🦶 "I Got My Foot Stepped On": Passive With an Object

Here is a very Korean pattern with no neat English equivalent. When something happens to your body part or belonging, you are the subject and the body part or belonging keeps its object particle 을/를:

KoreanEnglish
(저는) 지하철에서 발을 밟혔어요.I got my foot stepped on in the subway.
모기한테 팔을 물렸어요.I got bitten on the arm by a mosquito.
소매치기한테 지갑을 빼앗겼어요.I had my wallet taken by a pickpocket.

It's an "adversity" passive. The subject is the person affected, and the pattern usually describes something unpleasant.

Passive verbs combine with everything you already know, and -아/어 있다 (Lesson 12) is a favourite partner for describing resulting states: 문이 열려 있어요 ("the door is open"), 벽에 그림이 걸려 있어요 ("a picture is hanging on the wall"), 이름이 적혀 있어요 ("a name is written on it").

Count the passives: 꺼져, 들렸어요, 막혀서, 밀려서, 밟혔고요, 잠겨, 준비됐어요, 안 됐어요. Junho never says who turned off the phone or locked the door, because it doesn't matter to the story.

📰 Reading: The Lost-and-Found Office

A short news-style item (made up for this lesson) in 한다체. Notice how many verbs are passive, and how rarely an agent is named.

기사
서울의 한 지하철 유실물센터에 따르면 지난달에만 천 건이 넘는 분실물이 접수되었다. 가장 많이 발견된 물건은 휴대폰이었고, 지갑과 우산이 그 뒤를 이었다. 출근 시간에는 사람들에게 밀려 급하게 내리다가 선반에 올려놓은 가방을 두고 내리는 경우가 많다. 문이 닫히는 순간 물건이 떨어지는 일도 있다. 주인이 찾아가지 않은 물건은 일주일 동안 보관된 뒤 경찰서로 넘겨진다. 센터 직원은 "물건을 잃어버렸다면 내린 역과 시간, 열차 칸 번호를 알려 주는 것이 좋다"고 말했다.
English translation

According to a subway lost-and-found centre in Seoul, more than a thousand lost items were handed in last month alone. The item found most often was the mobile phone, followed by wallets and umbrellas. At rush hour, many people get pushed by the crowd, get off in a hurry, and leave behind the bag they put on the luggage rack. Things also fall out at the moment the doors close. Items that their owners don't collect are kept for one week and then handed over to the police station. A centre employee said, "If you've lost something, it helps to tell us the station you got off at, the time, and the carriage number."

  4. 그 가수는 많은 사람들에게 _____ (사랑하다 → "receive", present 해요) (Answer: 사랑받아요)
  5. 친구가 중고 거래를 하다가 사기를 _____ ("suffer", past 해요) (Answer: 당했어요)
  6. 이 펜은 부드럽게 잘 _____ (쓰다 → "writes well", -아/어지다, present 해요) (Answer: 써져요)

Exercise 3 — -게 되다, adversity passives and agents

  1. 회사 일 때문에 다음 달에 부산으로 _____ (이사하다 + -게 되다, past 해요) (Answer: 이사하게 됐어요|이사하게 되었어요)
  2. 처음에는 싫었는데 지금은 김치를 _____ (좋아하다 + -게 되다, past 해요) (Answer: 좋아하게 됐어요|좋아하게 되었어요)
  3. 지하철에서 발을 _____ 너무 아팠어요. (밟다, passive, -아서/어서) (Answer: 밟혀서)
  4. 캠핑장에서 모기한테 팔을 많이 _____ (물다, passive, past 해요) (Answer: 물렸어요)
  5. 이 그림은 조선 시대의 한 화가에 _____ 그려졌다. (formal agent marker) (Answer: 의해)
  6. 바람_____ 문이 쾅 닫혔어요. (particle for a non-living cause) (Answer: 에)

Exercise 4 — Complete the dialogue

  1. A: 왜 이렇게 늦었어요? B: 차가 너무 _____ (막다, passive, -아서요/어서요) (Answer: 막혀서요)
  2. A: 지갑은 찾았어요? B: 아니요, 지하철에서 _____ (had it stolen, 도둑 + 맞다, past 해요) (Answer: 도둑맞았어요)
  3. A: 문이 안 열려요. B: 아, 문이 _____ 있어요. 비밀번호를 눌러 보세요. (잠그다, passive, -아/어 form) (Answer: 잠겨)
  4. A: 어제 그 문제는 어떻게 됐어요? B: 다행히 잘 _____ (풀다, passive, past 해요) (Answer: 풀렸어요)
  5. A: 이 사진 언제 찍었어요? B: 저도 모르게 _____ 사진이에요. (찍다, passive, past modifier) (Answer: 찍힌)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension

  1. 지난달 천 건이 넘는 분실물이 _____ (the 되다-passive used in the article, past 한다체) (Answer: 접수되었다|접수됐다)
  2. 가장 많이 발견된 물건은 _____이었다. (Answer: 휴대폰)
  3. 출근 시간에 사람들은 다른 사람들에게 _____ 급하게 내린다. (밀다, passive, the linking form used in the article) (Answer: 밀려)
  4. 주인이 찾아가지 않은 물건은 _____ 동안 보관된다. (Answer: 일주일)
  5. 그 뒤에 물건은 _____로 넘겨진다. (Answer: 경찰서)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 문이 닫았어요. → ✓ 문이 닫혔어요. (If the door is the subject, you need the passive verb.)

✗ 가방이 도둑맞혔어요. → ✓ 가방을 도둑맞았어요. (도둑맞다 is already "have stolen", and the item takes 을/를.)

✗ 사용해지다, 결정해졌어요 → ✓ 사용되다, 결정됐어요. (For 하다-verbs, use 되다.)

✗ 쓰여진 글, 보여지는 모습 (in formal writing) → ✓ 쓰인 글, 보이는 모습. (Avoid double passives.)

✗ 민수 씨에 의해 창문이 깨졌어요. (in conversation) → ✓ 민수 씨가 창문을 깼어요. (에 의해 is for written style. In speech, use an active sentence.)

✗ 만나히다, 주이다 → these don't exist. (Not every verb has a suffix passive.)

If the four suffixes feel like a list of random exceptions right now, that's normal. It's the same for Korean children, who learn 닫히다 and 열리다 one word at a time. Every passive you meet in a real sentence stays with you better than ten you memorise from a list, so collect them from what you read and hear.

❓ Common Questions

Is there any rule for which suffix a verb takes?

Only tendencies: -이- after vowels and ㅎ (보이다, 놓이다), -히- after ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ/ㅈ (먹히다, 닫히다, 잡히다), -리- after ㄹ and 르/ㄷ-irregulars (열리다, 눌리다, 들리다), -기- after ㄴ/ㅁ/ㅅ/ㅊ (안기다, 감기다, 쫓기다). There are exceptions (잠그다 → 잠기다), and many verbs have no suffix passive at all. Treat each passive as a vocabulary item and use the tendencies as a memory aid.

Is 걸리다 in 감기에 걸리다 or 시간이 걸리다 a passive?

Historically it comes from the passive of 걸다 ("hang, hook"), and you can still feel the image of being "caught" by a cold. In modern Korean, though, 감기에 걸리다 ("catch a cold"), 시간이 걸리다 ("take time") and 경찰에 걸리다 ("get caught by the police") are fixed expressions. Learn them as vocabulary.
