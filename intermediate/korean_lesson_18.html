🇰🇷 Lesson 18: Hypotheticals & Regret

Ask a group of Korean friends 로또에 당첨된다면 뭐 할 거야? ("If you won the lottery, what would you do?") and the conversation will run for an hour. Ask them about their twenties and you'll hear a different kind of sentence: "If only I had studied harder back then…" Wishes, what-ifs and regrets are some of the most human things we say — and until now your Korean has had only one tool for them, -(으)면. This lesson gives you the rest of the toolkit: imagining, wishing, regretting, and setting practical conditions ("if you want to…", "only if…", "when it happens, do this").

🧠 The Mental Model: A Reality Dial

English marks how real a condition is by shifting tenses: "If it rains" (real) → "If it rained" (imaginary) → "If it had rained" (it didn't). Korean uses different endings instead. Think of a dial that turns from real to impossible:

Dial settingEndingKoreanEnglish
Real / likely-(으)면비가 오면 집에 있을 거예요.If it rains, I'll stay home.
Hypothetical-다면내일 세상이 끝난다면 뭘 하겠어요?If the world ended tomorrow, what would you do?
Counterfactual past-았/었더라면그때 알았더라면 말렸을 거예요.If I had known then, I would have stopped you.

The result half has its own tools: -(으)ㄹ 텐데 ("it would…"), -았으면 좋겠다 ("I wish") and -았으면 좋았을 텐데 ("it would have been nice if…"). Three practical conditions round things out: -(으)려면, -아야 and -거든.

🌈 -다면: "If (Hypothetically)…"

-다면 is built exactly like the reported-speech forms from Lesson 2: take the plain (한다체) form of the verb and add -면. If you can say 간다고 했어요, you can already say 간다면.

💡 The Formula

Action verb: -ㄴ다면 / -는다면 → 간다면, 먹는다면, 산다면 (살다)

Adjective, 있다/없다: -다면 → 크다면, 바쁘다면, 있다면, 없다면

Noun: (이)라면 → 나라면, 학생이라면

Past: -았/었다면 → 갔다면, 먹었다면, 학생이었다면

KoreanEnglish
로또에 당첨된다면 부모님께 집을 사 드리고 싶어요.If I won the lottery, I'd like to buy my parents a house.
만약 시간을 되돌릴 수 있다면 대학교 1학년 때로 돌아가고 싶어요.If I could turn back time, I'd like to go back to my first year of university.
내가 너라면 그 회사에는 안 갈 거야.If I were you, I wouldn't go to that company.
그 소문이 사실이라면 정말 큰 문제예요.If that rumour is true, it's a really big problem.
제가 그 자리에 있었다면 똑같이 했을 거예요.If I had been in that position, I would have done the same.

만약(에) ("if, supposing") and 혹시 ("by any chance") often open the sentence as a flag: "a hypothetical is coming". They work with -(으)면 too, but they are especially at home with -다면.

Which one? -(으)면 or -다면

SituationNatural choiceWhy
General truth: "When spring comes, flowers bloom."봄이 오면 꽃이 피어요.Spring certainly comes — no hypothesis. 봄이 온다면 sounds as if spring might not come.
Real plan: "If I go to Seoul, I'll call you."서울에 가면 연락할게요.A likely event. 서울에 간다면 would suggest you doubt you'll ever go.
Imaginary identity: "If I were a bird…"내가 새라면 너한테 날아갈 텐데.Impossible situations strongly prefer (이)라면.
Accepting someone's premise: "If that's really what you want…"그게 정말 네가 원하는 거라면 응원할게.-다면/(이)라면 can mean "if it's true that…" — you take the other person's words as the premise.

The line is soft: in casual chat 로또에 당첨되면 뭐 할 거야? is perfectly natural. -다면 just makes "imagine this" explicit, which is why it dominates in songs, essays and TOPIK writing.

🌫️ -(으)ㄹ 텐데: "It Would…, So / But…"

텐데 is 터 (an old noun for "expectation") + 이다 + -ㄴ데. So -(으)ㄹ 텐데 literally says "given my expectation that…" — the speaker makes a confident guess, then either builds on it or leaves it hanging with a sigh.

💡 The Formula

Stem + -(으)ㄹ 텐데: 갈 텐데, 먹을 텐데, 추울 텐데 (춥다), 알 텐데 (알다), 학생일 텐데

Past guess: -았/었을 텐데 → 도착했을 텐데, 먹었을 텐데

Sentence-final: -(으)ㄹ 텐데(요)

Use 1 — Background for advice or a question

KoreanEnglish
밖에 많이 추울 텐데 따뜻하게 입고 나가세요.It'll be really cold out, so dress warmly.
지금 출발하면 길이 막힐 텐데 조금 이따가 가는 게 어때요?The roads will be jammed if you leave now — how about going a bit later?
하루 종일 일하셔서 피곤하실 텐데 먼저 들어가세요.You must be tired after working all day — please go home first.
벌써 도착했을 텐데 왜 연락이 없지?He should have arrived by now — why hasn't he been in touch?

Use 2 — Wishes and regrets (hanging endings)

KoreanEnglish
돈만 있으면 당장 여행을 갈 텐데.If only I had the money, I'd go travelling right now.
민수 씨도 같이 왔으면 좋았을 텐데요.It would have been nice if Minsu had come too.
비가 와야 가뭄이 끝날 텐데요.It has to rain for the drought to end… (and it isn't raining).

Which one? -(으)ㄹ 텐데 or -(으)ㄹ 테니까 (Lesson 4)

-(으)ㄹ 텐데-(으)ㄹ 테니까
Core feelGuess as background ("…, so / but…")Guess or intention as a firm reason
Speaker's own promise✗ 제가 할 텐데 쉬세요.✓ 제가 할 테니까 쉬세요. (I'll do it, so rest.)
Sentence-final sigh✓ 시간이 있으면 좋을 텐데.✗ not used this way
Concern for the listener✓ 피곤할 텐데 쉬어. (softer, sympathetic)✓ 피곤할 테니까 쉬어. (more matter-of-fact)

⏪ -았/었더라면: "If (Only) … Had…"

Here is the -더- from Lesson 17 again, this time looking back at a past that didn't happen. -았더라면 is the strongest counterfactual Korean has: it can only describe a past that is already closed. It almost always pairs with a "would have" result: -았을 텐데, -았을 거예요 or -지 않았을까요?

💡 The Formula

[Past stem]-더라면 + [past guess]: 출발했더라면 … 놓치지 않았을 텐데

알다 → 알았더라면 · 듣다 → 들었더라면 · 하다 → 했더라면 · 학생이다 → 학생이었더라면

KoreanEnglish
조금만 일찍 출발했더라면 기차를 놓치지 않았을 텐데.If I'd left just a little earlier, I wouldn't have missed the train.
그때 네 말을 들었더라면 이런 일은 없었을 거야.If I'd listened to you then, this wouldn't have happened.
미리 알았더라면 제가 도와드렸을 거예요.If I'd known in advance, I would have helped you.
대학 때 한국어를 시작했더라면 지금쯤 훨씬 잘할 텐데.If I'd started Korean at university, I'd be much better by now.

Look at the last example: a past condition with a present result (지금쯤 … 잘할 텐데). Korean mixes them just as English does — choose the tense of the result by when the result would be true.

Which one? Three ways to say "if … had"

FormFeelExample
-았으면Everyday speech; context makes it counterfactual일찍 왔으면 만났을 거예요.
-았다면Clearly hypothetical; neutral, common in writing일찍 왔다면 만났을 거예요.
-았더라면Strongest "but it didn't happen"; carries regret일찍 왔더라면 만났을 텐데.

🌠 Wishing: -았/었으면 좋겠다

This is the most common way to say "I wish" or "I hope" in Korean — and it confuses everyone at first because it uses a past form to talk about the present or future. Think of it as "It would be good if it had (already) happened": you picture the wish as fulfilled.

KoreanEnglish
주말에 날씨가 좋았으면 좋겠어요.I hope the weather is nice this weekend.
이번에는 꼭 합격했으면 좋겠어요.I really hope I pass this time.
부모님이 늘 건강하셨으면 좋겠어요.I hope my parents always stay healthy.
다음 주까지 자료를 보내 주셨으면 합니다.I would appreciate it if you could send the materials by next week.

-(으)면 좋겠다 (without the past) also exists and means the same, a little more neutrally: 날씨가 좋으면 좋겠어요. -았으면 하다 is a softer, more formal variant — perfect for polite requests at work, as in the last example.

Regret: -았으면 좋았을 텐데 and -(으)ㄹ 걸 그랬다

KoreanEnglish
너도 같이 갔으면 좋았을 텐데.It would have been great if you'd come too.
미리 예약했으면 좋았을 텐데요.We should have booked in advance (it would have been good).
우산을 가지고 올 걸 그랬어요.I should have brought an umbrella.

-(으)ㄹ 걸 그랬다 is the everyday "should have" for your own choices; in chat it shrinks to just -(으)ㄹ걸: 살걸! ("Should've bought it!").

📌 -거든: "If/When That Happens, Then Do This"

You know -거든요 as a sentence ending that gives a reason (Lesson 5). -거든 in the middle of a sentence is a different animal: a condition that sets up an instruction, suggestion or promise.

💡 The Formula and the Rule

Stem + -거든 + [command / suggestion / promise / intention]

도착하거든 전화하세요 · 시간이 있거든 놀러 오세요 · 결과가 나오거든 알려 드릴게요

The second clause cannot be a plain statement of fact or a past event.

✓ Natural✗ Wrong
집에 도착하거든 문자 보내. (command)봄이 오거든 꽃이 핀다. (general fact → 봄이 오면)
궁금한 점이 있거든 언제든지 물어보세요. (request)시간이 있거든 영화를 봤어요. (past event → 시간이 있어서 / 있을 때)
비가 그치거든 출발합시다. (suggestion)배가 고프거든 밥을 먹어요. (habit statement → 고프면)
그 사람을 만나거든 안부 좀 전해 줘. (request)

Honestly, in casual speech -(으)면 covers all of these too (도착하면 문자 보내). -거든 adds a slightly deliberate, caring "when that moment comes…" tone — it's common from parents, teachers and older speakers, and in advice columns.

🎯 -(으)려면: "If You Want To…"

-(으)려면 is a shortening of -(으)려고 하면 ("if you intend to"). It states a goal; the second clause says what the goal requires — typically -아야 하다/되다, -(으)면 되다, 필요하다 or a command.

KoreanEnglish
명동에 가려면 4호선을 타야 해요.To get to Myeongdong, you need to take Line 4.
좋은 자리에 앉으려면 일찍 가는 게 좋아요.If you want a good seat, it's best to go early.
김치찌개를 맛있게 만들려면 잘 익은 김치를 써야 돼요.If you want to make tasty kimchi stew, you have to use well-fermented kimchi.
한국어를 잘하려면 매일 조금씩이라도 들어야 해요.To get good at Korean, you should listen every day, even a little.

Stem rules are the usual ones: ㄹ-stems take -려면 directly (만들려면, 살려면), ㄷ-irregulars change (듣다 → 들으려면). Because it expresses intention, -(으)려면 goes with action verbs; for adjectives, add -아지다: 건강해지려면 ("to become healthy").

🔑 -아/어야: "Only If…"

You know -아야 하다 ("must"). Take away 하다 and -아야 becomes a connector meaning "only when X, Y" — X is the necessary condition, the key that unlocks Y.

KoreanEnglish
표가 있어야 들어갈 수 있어요.You can only go in if you have a ticket.
직접 먹어 봐야 맛을 알 수 있죠.You only know the taste once you try it yourself.
사람은 잠을 충분히 자야 건강해요.People are only healthy if they get enough sleep.
이 서류에 서명하셔야 처리가 됩니다.This can only be processed once you sign the form.
열심히 해야만 성공할 수 있다.Only by working hard can you succeed. (-아야만 = emphatic)

⚠️ No commands after -아야

The second clause of -아야 cannot be a command or a "let's": ✗ 표가 있어야 들어가세요. Say ✓ 표가 있어야 들어갈 수 있어요 (fact) or ✓ 표가 있으면 들어가세요 (command with -(으)면).

💬 Dialogue: The Missed Deadline

Jihun and Sua are university friends, so they use 반말 (Lesson 1). Jihun has just found out he missed the exchange-programme deadline.

📰 Reading: 십 년 전의 나에게

A thirty-year-old writes a letter to their twenty-year-old self. Letters to oneself are a popular essay genre in Korean, and this one is in 한다체, the written style you'll study properly in Lesson 21.

Korean
십 년 전의 나에게. 그때 너는 매일 바쁘다는 말만 했다. 그래서 할머니께 전화도 자주 드리지 못했다. 그때 조금만 더 자주 연락했더라면 지금 이렇게 후회하지는 않을 텐데. 그리고 대학교 때 그렇게 좋아하던 음악을 포기하지 않았더라면 내 삶은 어떻게 달라졌을까? 하지만 후회만 하고 있을 수는 없다. 만약 다시 스무 살로 돌아간다면 나는 더 많이 실패해 보고 싶다. 실패를 해 봐야 무엇이 정말 중요한지 알 수 있기 때문이다. 십 년 뒤의 나는 오늘을 후회하지 않았으면 좋겠다. 기타를 다시 배우려면 바로 오늘 시작해야 한다. 그래서 나는 내일 기타 학원에 등록하기로 했다.
English translation

To me, ten years ago. Back then all you ever said was that you were busy. So you didn't call Grandma often either. If I had kept in touch just a little more often back then, I wouldn't regret it like this now. And if I hadn't given up the music I loved so much at university, how would my life have turned out differently? But I can't just keep regretting. If I went back to being twenty, I would want to fail more. Because only by failing can you find out what really matters. I hope that the me of ten years from now doesn't regret today. If I want to learn guitar again, I have to start today. So I've decided to sign up at a guitar school tomorrow.

Culture note — 소 잃고 외양간 고친다. "Fixing the cowshed after losing the cow" is the Korean proverb for acting too late, and you'll hear it whenever someone says -았더라면. The internet has its own version: 껄무새 (-ㄹ걸 + 앵무새 "parrot"), a jokey name for investors who keep squawking 그때 살걸! 그때 팔걸! ("Should've bought! Should've sold!"). Regret is universal — Korean just has very good grammar for it.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Wrong -다면 base: ✗ 가다면, ✗ 먹다면 → ✓ 간다면, ✓ 먹는다면. Action verbs need the -ㄴ다/-는다 form, just like reported speech. (Adjectives are fine: 크다면.)
  • -거든 with a plain statement: ✗ 봄이 오거든 꽃이 핀다 → ✓ 봄이 오면 꽃이 핀다.
  • Commands after -아야: ✗ 표가 있어야 들어가세요 → ✓ 표가 있어야 들어갈 수 있어요.
  • 텐데 for your own promise: ✗ 제가 할 텐데 쉬세요 → ✓ 제가 할 테니까 쉬세요.
  • -았더라면 about the future: ✗ 내일 비가 왔더라면… → ✓ 내일 비가 온다면… (-았더라면 is only for a past that didn't happen).
  • Reading -았으면 좋겠다 as past: 합격했으면 좋겠어요 is a hope about the future, not "I wanted to have passed".

  1. 춥다 → 밖에 많이 _____ 코트 입고 나가세요. (-(으)ㄹ 텐데) (Answer: 추울 텐데)
  2. 피곤하다 → 하루 종일 일하셔서 _____ 먼저 들어가세요. (honorific + 텐데) (Answer: 피곤하실 텐데)
  3. 막히다 → 지금 출발하면 길이 _____ 조금 이따가 가요. (-(으)ㄹ 텐데) (Answer: 막힐 텐데)
  4. 출발하다 → 조금만 일찍 _____ 기차를 놓치지 않았을 텐데. (-았더라면) (Answer: 출발했더라면)
  5. 듣다 → 그때 네 말을 _____ 이런 일은 없었을 거야. (-었더라면) (Answer: 들었더라면)
  6. 도와드리다 → 미리 알았더라면 제가 _____ 거예요. (would have helped) (Answer: 도와드렸을)
  7. 도착하다 → 벌써 _____ 왜 연락이 없지? (past guess + 텐데) (Answer: 도착했을 텐데)

Exercise 3 — Wishes and regrets

  1. 좋다 → 주말에 날씨가 _____ 좋겠어요. (-았으면) (Answer: 좋았으면)
  2. 합격하다 → 이번에는 꼭 _____ 좋겠어요. (Answer: 합격했으면)
  3. 주다 → 다음 주까지 자료를 보내 _____ 합니다. (honorific polite request) (Answer: 주셨으면)
  4. 가다 → 너도 같이 _____ 좋았을 텐데. (Answer: 갔으면)
  5. 좋다 → 미리 예약했으면 _____ 텐데요. (regret) (Answer: 좋았을)
  6. 예약하다 → 미리 _____ 걸 그랬어요. ("should have") (Answer: 예약할)

Exercise 4 — -거든, -(으)려면, -아야

  1. 도착하다 → 집에 _____ 문자 보내. (-거든) (Answer: 도착하거든)
  2. 있다 → 궁금한 점이 _____ 언제든지 물어보세요. (-거든) (Answer: 있거든)
  3. 가다 → 명동에 _____ 4호선을 타야 해요. (goal) (Answer: 가려면)
  4. 만들다 → 김치찌개를 맛있게 _____ 잘 익은 김치를 써야 돼요. (goal) (Answer: 만들려면)
  5. 있다 → 표가 _____ 들어갈 수 있어요. (only if) (Answer: 있어야)
  6. 직접 먹어 _____ 맛을 알 수 있죠. (보다 + "only if") (Answer: 봐야)
  7. 오다 → 봄이 _____ 꽃이 핀다. (general truth — -거든 or -(으)면?) (Answer: 오면)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension: 십 년 전의 나에게

  1. 글쓴이는 _____께 자주 연락하지 못해서 후회한다. (Answer: 할머니)
  2. 글쓴이가 대학교 때 포기한 것은 _____이다. (Answer: 음악)
  3. 스무 살로 돌아간다면 글쓴이는 더 많이 _____ 보고 싶다. (실패하다) (Answer: 실패해)
  4. 실패를 해 _____ 무엇이 중요한지 알 수 있다. (only if) (Answer: 봐야)
  5. 기타를 다시 배우려면 바로 _____ 시작해야 한다. (when?) (Answer: 오늘)
  6. 글쓴이는 내일 기타 _____에 등록하기로 했다. (Answer: 학원)

❓ Common Questions

Is -았다면 the same as -았더라면?

Very close. Both describe a past that didn't happen, and in many sentences you can swap them. -았더라면 is stronger and more emotional — it leans toward regret ("if only…"), while -았다면 is more neutral and can also mean "if it's true that it happened": 그가 정말 그렇게 말했다면 사과해야 해요 ("If he really said that, he should apologise"). -았더라면 can't do that second job.

Why does -았으면 좋겠다 use the past tense?

Korean imagines the wish as already completed — "it would be good if it had happened". English does something similar with "I wish it were Friday". You can also say -(으)면 좋겠다 without the past; the meaning is the same, and -았으면 is simply more common and a little more heartfelt.

Can I use -거든 in formal situations?

Yes, but it's rarer. You may see 문의 사항이 있으시거든 연락 주십시오 in older or very polished writing, but most modern notices use -(으)시면: 문의 사항이 있으시면 연락 주십시오. In speech, -거든 feels personal and warm; in business writing, -(으)면 is the safe default.

What's the difference between -(으)려면 and -(으)면?

-(으)면 states a condition; -(으)려면 states a goal. 서울역에 가면 사람이 많아요 = "When you go to Seoul Station, there are lots of people." 서울역에 가려면 1호선을 타세요 = "If you want to get to Seoul Station, take Line 1." If the second half tells someone how to achieve the first half, you want -(으)려면.

🌟 Going Further

  • TOPIK-style writing (200–300 자): 「만약 한 달 동안 아무 일도 하지 않아도 된다면 무엇을 하고 싶습니까?」 Answer in 한다체, using -다면, -(으)ㄹ 텐데 and -았으면 좋겠다 at least once each.
  • Your own letter: Write five sentences to yourself five years ago using -았더라면 … -았을 텐데, then three sentences to yourself five years from now using -았으면 좋겠다.

If these forms feel like a crowd right now, that's normal — B2 is where Korean starts offering three ways to say one thing, and choosing between them grows with exposure. Notice them in dramas and songs, and they'll sort themselves out.
