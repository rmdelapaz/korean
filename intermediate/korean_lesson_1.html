🇰🇷 Lesson 1: Speaking Casually: 반말 & Switching Styles

🎉 Welcome to B1 (≈ TOPIK 3)

You finished the beginner tier: you can introduce yourself, order, shop, give directions, talk about your day and connect clauses with -아서, -(으)니까 and -(으)면. That is real Korean. The intermediate tier builds on all of it. Here is what changes:

  • Longer speech. You'll move from single sentences to paragraphs: telling a story, passing on a message, giving reasons and weighing pros and cons.
  • Nuance. Beginner Korean says what happened. B1 Korean also shows how you feel about it: surprised, guessing, reminding, hearing it second-hand. Most of that lives in endings.
  • Real registers. You'll produce 반말, report what others said, and by B2 read news-style written Korean (한다체), which TOPIK II's writing paper requires.
  • A reading in every lesson. Each lesson has a 150–400-character Korean passage with comprehension questions.
  • Less scaffolding. Romanization now appears only for new vocabulary. Explanations stay in English, but the examples get longer and more natural.

One honest note: many learners hit a plateau around B1. Beginner progress is fast because every lesson adds something new and visible, while intermediate progress is about control and nuance. It feels slower, but it's what makes you sound like yourself in Korean. Keep going.

You've been meeting 지수 at a language exchange for a month. You get along, you're the same age, and the polite -아요/어요 endings are starting to feel like a glass wall between you. Then one evening she says: 우리 동갑인데 말 놓을까요? ("We're the same age, so shall we drop the formality?") This is a real moment in Korean friendships. If you say yes, you need to be able to speak 반말, not just recognise it. That's this lesson.

🧠 The Mental Model: Distance, Not Rudeness

In the beginner tier you learned to recognise 반말 as "해요 minus 요". That rule still covers most of it. What's new is the social logic. 반말 isn't rude in itself. It signals closeness and, sometimes, hierarchy. Korean speech styles work like a distance dial:

StyleExampleWhat it signals
하십시오체 (formal polite)식사하셨습니까?Public, official, ceremonial distance
해요체 (polite)밥 먹었어요?Everyday respect: the safe default with adults
반말 / 해체 (casual)밥 먹었어?Closeness: friends, siblings, partners, and older-to-younger
해라체 (plain)밥 먹었니? / 밥 먹어라.Parent-to-child, teacher-to-pupil; also the base of written style

The danger isn't 반말 itself. It's using it uninvited, because that claims a closeness or a rank the other person never agreed to. 뭐 해? is warm from a friend and insulting from a stranger.

💡 The Core Formula (review + one new piece)

해요체 − 요 = 반말: 가요 → 가, 먹었어요 → 먹었어, 할 거예요 → 할 거야, 좋아요 → 좋아.

Nouns: 이에요 → 이야, 예요 → 야: 학생이야, 친구야, 뭐야?

New: 반말 has a few dedicated endings with no 해요 twin: -자 (let's), -니?/-냐? (questions), -아라/어라 and -(으)렴 (commands). You'll learn these below.

🗣️ Statements and Questions: Same Form, Different Tune

As in 해요체, a 반말 statement and question are the same string. Only your intonation changes. Rising pitch at the end makes it a question.

해요체반말English

저녁 먹었어요?저녁 먹었어?Did you have dinner?
그 사람 누구예요?그 사람 누구야?Who's that person?

아니요, 몰라요.아니, 몰라.No, I don't know.

Casual reactions matter as much as full sentences: 응 or 어 (yeah), 아니 (no), 그래 (okay / sure), 맞아 (right), 진짜? (really?), 고마워, 미안해, 잘 가, 내일 봐.

The two dedicated question endings: -니? and -냐?

Alongside the plain 먹었어?, 반말 has two more question endings. They're optional, but you will hear them constantly, and they carry a different feel.

EndingKoreanEnglishFeel
-니?밥 먹었니?Have you eaten?Soft, caring; typical from parents, teachers, older people; also between female friends
-니?요즘 많이 바쁘니?Are you very busy these days?Gentle concern
-니?이게 네 동생이니?Is this your little brother?Noun: 이니 / 니 (after a vowel)

Both attach straight to the stem, and the past works as usual: 갔니?, 먹었냐?. As a learner, default to plain -아/어? and let -니 and -냐 come gradually. In the traditional written standard, adjectives take -(으)냐 (좋으냐?) and verbs take -느냐 (먹느냐?). In real speech almost everyone says 좋냐? and 먹냐?. You'll meet this again in Lesson 2, where -냐 turns into the reported-question ending -냐고.

🤝 Let's: -자

The 해요체 "let's" form (가요, 먹어요) is ambiguous. 반말 has a dedicated suggestion ending, -자, attached straight to any stem:

KoreanEnglish
배고파. 뭐 먹자.I'm hungry. Let's eat something.
이번 주말에 같이 한강 가자!Let's go to the Han River together this weekend!
오늘은 그냥 집에서 쉬자.Let's just rest at home today.
우리 사진 찍자. 하나, 둘, 셋!Let's take a photo. One, two, three!
그 얘기는 이제 하지 말자.Let's not talk about that anymore.

"Let's not" is -지 말자. 먹지 말자 means "let's not eat", never 안 먹자. There's no past or future here: -자 is always about now or later, like English "let's".

"Don't" is -지 마 (반말) or -지 마라 (해라체): 걱정하지 마 ("Don't worry"), 울지 마 ("Don't cry"), 거짓말하지 마라 ("Don't lie", from a parent).

⚠️ -아라/어라 between adults

Between adult friends, -아라 can be playful (잘 먹어라~, 잘 자라~), but in a flat tone it sounds bossy, like an older sibling giving orders. With peers, the plain -아/어 command is always the safe casual choice. As a learner you'll mostly hear -(으)렴 in dramas, songs and picture books, and rarely need to produce it.

💡 Why everyone says 니가

In standard spelling, "I" is 내가 and "you" is 네가. In modern Seoul speech, ㅐ and ㅔ sound almost the same, so 내가 and 네가 are hard to tell apart. Speakers solve this by saying 니가 for "you" (and 니 for "your"). It's technically non-standard but completely normal in speech. You'll see it in subtitles and song lyrics. Write 네가 in formal writing, and say whichever feels natural.

Names in 반말

When you call a friend, add -아 after a consonant and -야 after a vowel: 지훈아!, 민지야!. When a consonant-final name is in the middle of a sentence, casual speech inserts 이 before particles: 지훈이가, 지훈이는, 지훈이랑 ("with Jihun"). Vowel-final names don't need it: 민지가, 민지는. These are given names only. You don't add -아/-야 to a surname, and you'd never use them with someone older than you.

KoreanEnglish
지훈아, 너 어제 왜 연락 안 했어?Jihun, why didn't you get in touch yesterday?
지훈이는 요즘 아르바이트 때문에 바빠.Jihun's busy with his part-time job these days.
걔 진짜 웃겨. 너도 한번 만나 봐.That guy is so funny. You should meet him too.
쟤 누구야? 처음 보는데.Who's that over there? I've never seen them before.
너네 오늘 몇 시에 끝나?What time do you guys finish today?

🔑 Getting the Key: 말 놓다

Switching to 반말 is usually a small explicit negotiation, and the phrases for it are worth memorising as chunks.

KoreanEnglishWhen
우리 말 놓을까요?Shall we drop the formalities?Between near-equals; usually the older one asks
말 편하게 하세요.Please speak comfortably (casually) to me.Older/senior giving a younger person permission, or inviting mutual 반말
말 놓으셔도 돼요.You can speak casually to me.Younger person inviting an older one to drop the politeness
우리 동갑이니까 말 트자.We're the same age, so let's go casual.말(을) 트다: both switch to 반말
몇 년생이에요?What year were you born?The polite way to find the age relationship
저보다 한 살 많으시네요. 말 편하게 하세요.You're a year older than me. Please talk casually to me.The younger person offers; the older one then uses 반말, the younger often stays polite

Notice the last row. Switching isn't always mutual. Between people of different ages, the most common result is that the older person uses 반말 and the younger one keeps 해요체, often with 형/오빠/누나/언니 instead of a name. Same-age friends (동갑) usually switch both ways.

🇰🇷 Cultural note: age and 년생. Since June 2023, Korean law counts age the international way (만 나이). Socially, though, hierarchy still follows birth year: that's why people ask 몇 년생이에요? rather than 몇 살이에요?. Classmates are treated as equals even if one was born in a different year, and people born in January or February who started school early (빠른 년생) can scramble the system. Nobody expects a foreigner to master this. Being willing to ask, and waiting to be invited, is what earns trust.

🔀 Switching Styles in Real Life

Speech style follows the situation as well as the relationship. The same two people can switch back and forth within an hour.

SituationWhat happens
Two same-age coworkers who are friends반말 over lunch, 해요체 (or even 하십시오체) in a meeting in front of their manager
Your friend's parentsAlways 해요체 or higher, even though you use 반말 with their child
A friend's friend you just met, same ageStart polite. Wait for someone to suggest 말 놓자
ChildrenAdults use 반말 with children right away; that's expected, not rude

You'll also hear 반존대, a deliberate mix of 반말 and 존댓말, for example a younger person who is close to an older friend: 형, 이거 먹어요. 진짜 맛있어. It's friendly but hard to calibrate, so notice it before you try it. Native usage here genuinely varies by family, region and generation, so observe the people around you.

💬 Dialogue: 말 놓을까요?

수진 (28) and 준호 (25) have been in the same hiking club for two months. After a hike, they sit down for a drink.

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
수진준호 씨는 몇 년생이에요? 저보다 어려 보이는데.What year were you born, Junho? You look younger than me.
준호저는 2001년생이에요. 수진 씨는요?I was born in 2001. How about you, Sujin?
수진저는 98년생이에요. 제가 세 살 많네요.I was born in '98. So I'm three years older.
준호아, 그럼 말 편하게 하세요. 저도 누나라고 불러도 돼요?Oh, then please speak casually to me. Can I call you 누나?
수진그래, 좋아! 그럼 너도 말 놓을래? 우리 이제 친구잖아.Sure, great! Then do you want to go casual too? We're friends now.
준호진짜요? 아… 아직 좀 어색한데. 알았어, 누나!Really? Ah… it still feels a bit awkward. Okay, 누나!
수진하하, 잘했어. 다음 주 토요일에 북한산 가자. 시간 돼?Haha, well done. Let's do Bukhansan next Saturday. Are you free?
준호응, 돼. 근데 나 등산화 없어. 네… 아니, 누나 거 빌려줄 수 있어?Yeah, I'm free. But I don't have hiking boots. Can you… I mean, can you lend me yours, 누나?

준호알았어. 늦지 말고 일찍 와, 누나!Got it. Don't be late, come early, 누나!

Notice how 준호 wobbles: 진짜요? is still polite, and he catches himself about to say 네 ("your") to an older person. That hesitation is exactly what switching feels like at first, even for Koreans.

English translation

It's already been six months since I came to Korea. At first I used polite speech with everyone, because I worried 반말 would sound rude. Then I met Jisu at a language exchange meetup. Jisu was the same age as me. The third time we met, she asked, "We're the same age, so shall we drop the formalities?" I was a little nervous, but I answered, "Sure!" Since that day we've talked in 반말. Amazingly, using 반말 made us closer. But when I met Jisu's parents last month, of course I used polite speech. 반말 is language you use depending on the relationship, not on the person.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. Using 반말 before you're invited. Being the same age doesn't mean you're automatically casual with each other. Wait for 말 놓을까요? or 말 편하게 하세요, or ask politely yourself.

2. Forgetting the noun change. ✗ 나는 학생이에 / 학생 → ✓ 나는 학생이야. ✗ 친구예 → ✓ 친구야.

3. Mixing pronouns across styles. ✗ 저는 배고파 → ✓ 나는 배고파. ✗ 제가 할게 → ✓ 내가 할게. The pronoun has to match the ending.

4. Using 너 with someone older. Even in a mutual-반말 friendship with someone older, many people prefer 형/누나/오빠/언니 to 너. When in doubt, use the kinship term.

5. "Let's not" with 안. ✗ 안 가자 → ✓ 가지 말자. And "don't" is ✓ 가지 마, not ✗ 안 가.

6. Adding -아/-야 to a surname or to an older person's name. ✗ 김아! ✗ (to your boss) 민수야! Vocative -아/-야 is for given names of friends and people younger than you.

❓ Common Questions

If someone uses 반말 with me, can I automatically use it back?

Not automatically. An older person, a boss or a shop owner in their sixties may use 반말 with a younger person without expecting it back. If the speaker is clearly your age and in a friendly setting, it usually means they're open to it, but asking (우리 말 놓을까요?) is never wrong.

Is 반말 the same as 해라체?

They overlap. 반말 (해체) is the 해요 form without 요. 해라체 is the plain style with -다, -니?/-냐?, -아라, -자, and it's also the base of written Korean (한다체), which you'll study in Lesson 21. In casual speech people mix them freely: 밥 먹었어? and 밥 먹었니? are both "casual". -자 technically belongs to 해라체, but everyone uses it in everyday 반말.

Can I use -(으)시- in 반말?

Yes, when you talk about someone honoured: 할머니 지금 주무셔 ("Grandma's sleeping now"), 사장님 오셨어? ("Is the boss here?"). The ending is casual because of your listener, and -(으)시- honours the person you're talking about. That's the two-dial system from the beginner tier at work.

🌟 Going Further

Challenge 1: Watch five minutes of a Korean drama or variety show with Korean subtitles. Write down every 반말 ending you catch (-자, -니, -냐, -아라, -지 마…) and who said it to whom. What does the choice tell you about their relationship?

Challenge 2: Write a short text message exchange (8–10 lines) between two same-age friends planning a weekend, entirely in 반말. Then rewrite the first three lines as if one of them were texting an older coworker.

🔮 Looking Ahead

Now that you can speak casually, you'll start hearing casual reports: 민수가 내일 온대, 엄마가 일찍 오래. Those are contractions of reported speech. First you need the full forms: in Lesson 2: Reported Speech I: Passing On What People Said you'll learn to pass on statements, questions, suggestions and commands with -다고, -냐고, -자고 and -(으)라고. The 반말 endings you learned today (-냐, -자, -아라) will turn out to be the building blocks.
