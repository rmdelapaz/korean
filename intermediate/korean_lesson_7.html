🇰🇷 Lesson 7: Contrast & Concession

Every real decision has two sides. The new job pays more but the hours are long. The apartment is cheap because it's far from the station — and you'll take it even if the commute is annoying. At A2 you could say most of this with one tool: -지만. It works, but it makes every trade-off sound the same. Korean speakers weigh pros and cons with a whole set of endings, each with its own flavour: "even if", "even though (and it's annoying)", "sure, it's X, but…", "whereas", "in exchange for".

This lesson gives you that set. By the end you'll be able to talk through a decision — a move, a job offer, a purchase — the way a Korean friend would, and you'll understand why 좋긴 한데… is one of the most useful half-sentences in the language.

🧠 The Mental Model: Two Families

All the patterns in this lesson fall into two families. Sort them this way and most confusion disappears.

FamilyWhat it doesPatterns
Concession ("expectation broken")Clause A should lead to a certain result, but clause B goes the other way anyway.-아/어도, -더라도, -(으)ㄴ/는데도, -기는 하지만
Contrast ("two sides of the scale")A and B are put side by side and compared; neither is a surprise.-(으)ㄴ/는 반면에, -는 대신에, -(으)ㄴ/는데

Concession is about surprise: "I took medicine, and still my head hurts." Contrast is about balance: "Seoul is convenient; the countryside, on the other hand, is quiet." When you're choosing between two endings, first ask: is there a broken expectation here, or just two sides?

🛡️ -아/어도: "Even If / Even Though"

-아/어도 is the workhorse of concession. You already know it from -아도 되다 ("it's okay even if you…" = "you may"). On its own, it simply means "even if" or "even though".

💡 The Formula

Take the polite 해요 form, drop 요, add 도: 가요 → 가도, 먹어요 → 먹어도, 해요 → 해도.

Irregulars follow the -아요 form: 듣다 → 들어도, 춥다 → 추워도, 모르다 → 몰라도, 낫다 → 나아도.

Nouns: 학생이어도 (after 받침), 친구여도 (after a vowel). In speech 학생이라도 / 친구라도 is also very common.

Past: -았/었어도 — 열심히 공부했어도 ("even if I had studied hard / even though I studied hard").

KoreanEnglish
이 김치찌개는 식어도 맛있어요.This kimchi stew is tasty even when it's cold.
비가 와도 내일 등산은 갈 거예요.Even if it rains, we're still going hiking tomorrow.
저는 커피를 마셔도 잠을 잘 자요.Even if I drink coffee, I sleep fine.
길을 몰라도 걱정하지 마세요. 지도 앱이 있잖아요.Don't worry even if you don't know the way. You've got a map app.
그 사람은 백 번 물어봐도 대답을 안 할 거예요.You could ask him a hundred times and he still wouldn't answer.

아무리 … -아도: "No matter how…"

Put 아무리 ("however much") in front and -아도 becomes "no matter how". This combination is so frequent it's worth learning as one chunk.

KoreanEnglish
아무리 바빠도 아침은 꼭 먹어요.No matter how busy I am, I always eat breakfast.
아무리 생각해도 이해가 안 돼요.No matter how much I think about it, I don't get it.
아무리 비싸도 이 가방은 사고 싶어요.However expensive it is, I want this bag.

Because -아/어도 describes a condition that doesn't change the outcome, it is happy before anything: statements, questions, commands (-(으)세요), suggestions (-자, -(으)ㅂ시다). Hold on to that — it's exactly where -는데도 will differ.

🌫️ -더라도: "Even If (Supposing That…)"

-더라도 attaches straight to any stem (가더라도, 먹더라도, 바쁘더라도, 학생이더라도) and also means "even if". The difference from -아도 is a matter of weight: -더라도 leans towards a hypothetical — something that hasn't happened, may well not happen, or that the speaker is imagining for the sake of argument. It sounds a little more emphatic and a little more careful, which is why it turns up in advice, promises and formal writing.

KoreanEnglish
혹시 시험에 떨어지더라도 너무 실망하지 마세요.Even if you should fail the exam, don't be too disappointed.
힘들더라도 포기하지 마.Even if it gets hard, don't give up.
무슨 일이 있더라도 약속은 꼭 지킬게요.Whatever happens, I'll keep my promise.
가격이 조금 오르더라도 품질이 좋으면 사람들은 살 것이다.Even if the price goes up a little, people will buy it if the quality is good.

💡 -아도 vs -더라도: How Different Are They Really?

Honestly: they overlap a lot, and in many sentences native speakers use either. The tendency is this:

비가 와도 갈 거예요 — neutral "even if it rains". Rain is a normal possibility.

비가 오더라도 갈 거예요 — "even supposing it rains", with a touch more determination.

For a situation that is already a fact (it is raining right now, it did rain), -더라도 sounds off. Use -아도 or, better, -는데도.

😤 -(으)ㄴ/는데도: "Even Though (It Actually Happened)"

This one combines the background ending -(으)ㄴ/는데 with 도 ("even"). Because -는데 always describes something real, -는데도 is only for actual facts: something that is true or did happen, followed by a result that goes against expectation. It often carries a feeling of surprise, frustration or complaint.

💡 The Formula

Action verbs, 있다/없다: stem + -는데도 → 먹는데도, 가는데도, 맛있는데도. ㄹ drops: 살다 → 사는데도.

Adjectives: stem + -(으)ㄴ데도 → 싼데도, 작은데도, 추운데도, 먼데도 (멀다).

Past (both): -았/었는데도 → 먹었는데도, 비쌌는데도.

Nouns: N인데도 → 주말인데도, 중학생인데도.

KoreanEnglish
약을 먹었는데도 아직 머리가 아파요.I took medicine, but my head still hurts.
주말인데도 회사에 나가야 해요.Even though it's the weekend, I have to go into the office.
이 가방은 싼데도 품질이 정말 좋아요.This bag is cheap, and yet the quality is really good.
한국에 10년 넘게 사는데도 아직 매운 음식을 못 먹어요.Even though I've lived in Korea over ten years, I still can't eat spicy food.
몇 번이나 전화했는데도 안 받았어.I called several times and he still didn't pick up.

⚠️ No Commands or Suggestions After -는데도

A fact that has already frustrated you can't be turned into an instruction. After -(으)ㄴ/는데도, the second clause must be a statement (or a question about a fact).

✗ 피곤한데도 조금만 더 참으세요. → ✓ 피곤해도 조금만 더 참으세요 / 피곤하더라도 조금만 더 참으세요.

✗ 비가 오는데도 나가자. → ✓ 비가 와도 나가자.

Which "even" do I need?

🤝 -기는 하지만: "Sure, It's X, But…"

This is concession with a shrug. The speaker admits the first point is true, then adds the "but" that really matters. It's the Korean way of saying "Yeah, it's delicious and all, but…"

💡 The Formula

stem + -기는 하지만 → 맛있기는 하지만, 가기는 하지만. Past goes on 하다: 가기는 했지만.

Everyday forms: -긴 하지만, -긴 한데, and the "echo" version that repeats the verb: 맛있기는 맛있는데…, 좋긴 좋은데….

KoreanEnglish
그 식당은 맛있기는 하지만 너무 멀어요.That restaurant is tasty, sure, but it's too far.
파티에 가기는 했지만 금방 나왔어요.I did go to the party, but I left soon.
한국어가 어렵긴 한데 재미있어.Korean is hard, yeah, but it's fun.
이 노트북, 좋기는 좋은데 좀 무거워요.This laptop is good, it really is, but it's a bit heavy.

Compare the plain version: 맛있지만 멀어요 just reports two facts. 맛있기는 하지만 멀어요 tells the listener "I grant you it's tasty (maybe you mentioned it), but my real point is the distance." It's conversational, and it's often a gentle way of disagreeing.

⚖️ -(으)ㄴ/는 반면(에): "Whereas / On the Other Hand"

Now the contrast family. 반면 literally means "the opposite side". -(으)ㄴ/는 반면에 puts two facts on the scales: one side is like this, the other side is like that. It's slightly formal — very common in news, essays and presentations, and in careful speech when you're weighing pros and cons.

💡 The Formula

Action verbs, 있다/없다: -는 반면에 → 좋아하는 반면에, 많이 있는 반면에.

Adjectives: -(으)ㄴ 반면에 → 편리한 반면에, 많은 반면에, 가까운 반면에.

Past action verbs: -(으)ㄴ 반면에 → 늘어난 반면에.

Nouns: N인 반면에. 에 is optional: 편리한 반면 works too.

KoreanEnglish
도시 생활은 편리한 반면에 스트레스가 많아요.City life is convenient; on the other hand, it's stressful.
형은 운동을 좋아하는 반면에 동생은 책 읽기를 좋아해요.My older brother likes sport, whereas my younger brother likes reading.
수도권 인구는 늘어난 반면 지방 인구는 크게 줄었다.The population of the capital region grew, while the regional population fell sharply.
이 휴대폰은 화면이 큰 반면에 배터리가 빨리 닳아요.This phone has a big screen, but on the other hand the battery runs down quickly.

Tip: -반면에 is not a "surprise" ending. You would not say 약을 먹은 반면에 머리가 아파요 — nothing is being weighed there; it's a broken expectation, so it needs -는데도.

🔄 -는 대신(에): "Instead" and "In Exchange"

대신 means "substitute". As a noun it's simple: 커피 대신에 차를 마실게요 ("I'll have tea instead of coffee"). Attached to a verb or adjective it has two meanings, and the second is the one that makes it perfect for pros and cons.

MeaningKoreanEnglish
1. Replacement ("instead of doing")주말에 놀러 가는 대신에 집에서 푹 쉬었어요.Instead of going out at the weekend, I rested at home.
1. Replacement택시를 타는 대신 걸어갔어요.I walked instead of taking a taxi.
2. Trade-off ("…but to make up for it")이 회사는 월급이 적은 대신에 휴가가 길어요.This company pays less, but in return the holidays are long.
2. Trade-off그 집은 역에서 먼 대신에 월세가 싸요.That place is far from the station, but it makes up for it with cheap rent.
2. Exchange ("I'll do X if you do Y")오늘은 내가 요리하는 대신에 설거지는 네가 해.I'll cook today — in exchange, you do the dishes.

Form: action verbs -는 대신에 (past -(으)ㄴ 대신에: 점심을 굶은 대신 저녁을 많이 먹었어요), adjectives -(으)ㄴ 대신에. With the trade-off meaning, one side is a minus and the other is a plus that compensates for it. That's the key difference from 반면에, which just puts two sides next to each other without implying that one "pays for" the other.

🎬 -(으)ㄴ/는데: Setting the Scene

You met -는데요 as a sentence ending in Lesson 5. In the middle of a sentence, -(으)ㄴ/는데 is the great all-purpose connector of spoken Korean. It gives background for what comes next — and sometimes that background is a contrast.

UseKoreanEnglish
Background → request하늘이 흐린데 우산 가지고 가.The sky's cloudy — take an umbrella.
Background → question이번 주말에 시간 있는데 영화 볼래요?I'm free this weekend — want to see a movie?
Contrast언니는 키가 큰데 저는 작아요.My sister is tall, but I'm short.
Soft contrast옷은 예쁜데 좀 비싸네요.The clothes are pretty, but a bit pricey.

Form is identical to -는데도 without 도: 가는데, 먹는데, 있는데; 비싼데, 추운데; 갔는데; 학생인데. Unlike -는데도, plain -는데 has no restriction on what follows — commands and suggestions are fine, as the first two examples show.

How does it differ from -지만? -지만 is a clear, even "but". -는데 is softer and more open: it sets something down and lets the next clause do the work. That softness is why Koreans so often end a sentence on -는데… and leave the rest unsaid.

PatternCore ideaBest forExample

-(으)ㄴ/는데background / soft "but"conversation, setting up requests싼데 맛없어요.

The last two rows look almost identical in English. The nuance: 싼 반면에 품질이 낮아요 lists two sides; 싼 대신에 품질이 낮아요 says the low quality is the price you pay for the low price.

💬 Dialogue: The Job Offer

Su-a has been offered a new job and talks it over with her friend Ji-hun. They're close friends, so it's all 반말 (Lesson 1) — and listen for the -대 hearsay endings from Lesson 3.

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
지훈수아야, 이직 제안 받았다며? 어떻게 할 거야?Su-a, I heard you got a job offer? What are you going to do?
수아아직 고민 중이야. 연봉은 지금보다 훨씬 높은 반면에 야근이 많대.I'm still thinking. The salary is way higher than now, but apparently there's a lot of overtime.
지훈야근이 좀 많아도 연봉이 높으면 괜찮지 않아?Isn't it fine, even with a bit more overtime, if the pay is high?
수아돈이 중요하기는 하지만 저녁 시간도 중요하잖아.Money's important, sure, but my evenings matter too, you know.
지훈하긴. 회사 위치는 어때?True. What about the location?
수아집에서 좀 먼 대신에 재택근무를 일주일에 두 번 할 수 있대.It's a bit far from home, but to make up for it I could work from home twice a week.
지훈오, 그건 좋다. 그럼 출퇴근 스트레스는 좀 줄겠네.Oh, that's good. Then the commuting stress would go down a bit.
수아응. 근데 지금 회사 사람들이 너무 좋아서 떠나기가 아쉬워.Yeah. But the people at my current company are so nice, it's hard to leave.
지훈이해해. 나도 전 회사에서 월급이 적었는데도 동료들 때문에 3년이나 다녔어.I get it. At my last company the pay was low, and I still stayed three years because of my coworkers.
수아그러니까. 아무리 조건이 좋더라도 결국 사람이 제일 중요한 것 같아.Exactly. However good the conditions are, in the end people seem to matter most.
지훈그럼 이번 주말에 장단점을 다 적어 보고 결정해.Then write down all the pros and cons this weekend and decide.
수아그래야겠다. 고마워!I guess I should. Thanks!

Notice how each ending does a different job: 높은 반면에 weighs two sides, 많아도 dismisses a condition, 중요하기는 하지만 grants a point, 먼 대신에 offers a trade-off, 적었는데도 reports a real surprise, and 좋더라도 imagines a hypothetical.

📰 Reading: 시골로 이사한 지 1년

A blogger writes about a year of living in the countryside. It's in 해요체, like most personal blog posts.

Korean
저는 작년 봄에 서울을 떠나 강원도에 있는 작은 마을로 이사했어요. 시골 생활은 조용하고 공기가 맑은 반면에 교통이 정말 불편해요. 버스가 한 시간에 한 대밖에 없어서 결국 중고차를 샀어요. 월세는 서울의 절반인 대신에 가까운 곳에 병원이나 큰 마트가 없어요. 처음 몇 달은 아는 사람이 없어서 많이 외로웠어요. 그런데 그렇게 외로웠는데도 서울로 돌아가고 싶지는 않았어요. 마을 사람들은 친절하기는 하지만 가끔 제 생활에 관심이 너무 많아요. 제가 늦게 일어나면 옆집 할머니가 "어디 아파?" 하고 문을 두드리세요. 그래도 저는 앞으로 조금 불편하더라도 여기에서 계속 살 생각이에요.
English translation

Last spring I left Seoul and moved to a small village in Gangwon Province. Country life is quiet and the air is clean, but on the other hand transport is really inconvenient. There's only one bus an hour, so in the end I bought a used car. The rent is half of what it is in Seoul, but in exchange there's no hospital or big supermarket nearby. For the first few months I didn't know anyone and I was very lonely. And yet, even though I was that lonely, I didn't want to go back to Seoul. The villagers are kind, it's true, but sometimes they're too interested in my life. If I get up late, the grandmother next door knocks on my door asking "Are you sick?" Still, even if it's a bit inconvenient, I plan to keep living here.

🏡 Cultural note — 귀촌 and the "yes, but" culture. Moving from the city to the countryside (귀촌, or 귀농 if you move to farm) has become a real trend among Koreans tired of Seoul's prices and pace, and blog posts like this one are a genre of their own. Notice, too, how the writer never simply complains: every negative is softened with a positive (친절하기는 하지만…). Korean conversation strongly prefers this "yes, but" shape. When a Korean friend answers your invitation with 좋긴 한데… and trails off, they are very often saying no — politely. Learn to hear it, and learn to use it: it's far gentler than a flat 싫어요.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. -는데도 before a command. ✗ 바쁜데도 꼭 오세요. → ✓ 바빠도 꼭 오세요 / 바쁘더라도 꼭 오세요.

2. -더라도 for something already true. ✗ (It is raining right now) 비가 오더라도 우산이 없어요. → ✓ 비가 오는데 우산이 없어요.

3. Using -는 for adjectives. ✗ 작는데도, 많는 반면에 → ✓ 작은데도, 많은 반면에. (있다/없다 are the exception: 맛있는데도, 재미없는 반면에.)

4. Putting the past on the wrong verb in -기는 하지만. ✗ 갔기는 하지만 → ✓ 가기는 했지만. The tense goes on 하다, not on the main verb.

5. 반면에 for a broken expectation. ✗ 열심히 공부한 반면에 시험을 못 봤어요. → ✓ 열심히 공부했는데도 시험을 못 봤어요. 반면에 compares; it doesn't express surprise.

6. Forgetting 대신 has two meanings. 일하는 대신에 쉬었어요 = "rested instead of working", but 월급이 적은 대신에 휴가가 길어요 = "low pay, but long holidays to compensate". Read the context.

Exercise 2 — Fix the mistake

  1. ✗ 피곤한데도 조금만 더 참으세요. → _____ 조금만 더 참으세요. (피곤하다; a command follows) (Answer: 피곤해도|피곤하더라도)
  2. ✗ 내일 비가 오는데도 우리 등산 가자. → 내일 비가 _____ 우리 등산 가자. (오다; hypothetical) (Answer: 와도|오더라도)
  3. ✗ 서울은 인구가 많는 반면에 지방은 줄고 있어요. → 서울은 인구가 _____ 지방은 줄고 있어요. (많다) (Answer: 많은 반면에|많은 반면)
  4. ✗ 방이 작는데도 월세가 비싸요. → 방이 _____ 월세가 비싸요. (작다) (Answer: 작은데도)
  5. ✗ 제가 청소를 할 대신에 네가 요리해. → 제가 청소를 _____ 네가 요리해. (하다) (Answer: 하는 대신에|하는 대신)
  6. ✗ 열심히 공부한 반면에 시험을 못 봤어요. → 열심히 _____ 시험을 못 봤어요. (공부하다; a broken expectation) (Answer: 공부했는데도)

Exercise 3 — Weighing pros and cons

  1. 이 노트북은 성능이 _____ 좀 무거워요. (좋다, -기는 하지만) (Answer: 좋기는 하지만|좋긴 하지만)
  2. 도시 생활은 _____ 스트레스가 많아요. (편리하다, -(으)ㄴ 반면에) (Answer: 편리한 반면에|편리한 반면)
  3. 이 회사는 월급이 _____ 휴가가 길어요. (적다, trade-off) (Answer: 적은 대신에|적은 대신)
  4. 오늘은 제가 저녁을 _____ 내일은 네가 사. (사다, in exchange) (Answer: 사는 대신에|사는 대신)
  5. 형은 운동을 _____ 동생은 책 읽기를 좋아해요. (좋아하다, whereas) (Answer: 좋아하는 반면에|좋아하는 반면)
  6. 그 영화를 _____ 내용이 잘 기억 안 나요. (보다, "I did see it, but…") (Answer: 보기는 했지만|보긴 했지만)
  7. 하늘이 _____ 우산 가지고 가세요. (흐리다, background -(으)ㄴ데) (Answer: 흐린데)

Exercise 4 — Complete the conversation

  1. 가: 이 옷 어때? 나: 예쁘긴 _____ 너한테는 좀 작은 것 같아. (하다, background -(으)ㄴ데) (Answer: 한데)
  2. 가: 내일 소풍 취소할까요? 나: 아니요, 비가 _____ 가요. 실내에서 놀면 돼요. (오다, -아/어도) (Answer: 와도)
  3. 가: 민수 씨 아직 연락 없어요? 나: 네, 몇 번이나 _____ 안 받아요. (전화하다, -는데도, past) (Answer: 전화했는데도)
  4. 가: 점심 뭐 먹을까요? 나: 오늘은 밥 _____ 국수 어때요? (instead of) (Answer: 대신에|대신)
  5. 가: 그 일 힘들지 않아요? 나: _____ 보람이 있어요. (힘들다, -기는 하지만) (Answer: 힘들기는 하지만|힘들긴 하지만)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension (시골로 이사한 지 1년)

  1. 글쓴이는 _____ 에 있는 작은 마을로 이사했어요. (province) (Answer: 강원도)
  2. 교통이 불편해서 글쓴이는 중고_____ 를 샀어요. (one syllable) (Answer: 차)
  3. 월세는 서울의 _____ 이에요. (how much?) (Answer: 절반)
  4. 가까운 곳에 _____ 이나 큰 마트가 없어요. (Answer: 병원)
  5. 글쓴이는 앞으로 조금 _____ 여기에서 계속 살 생각이에요. (불편하다, form in the text) (Answer: 불편하더라도)

❓ Common Questions

Can I just use -지만 for everything?

You'll be understood — -지만 is never wrong as "but". But you'll miss nuance: you can't say "even if" with -지만 at all, and a string of -지만 sentences sounds like a textbook. Aim to recognise all of these patterns now and to use -아도, -는데도 and -는데 actively; -반면에 and -더라도 will come naturally as you read more.

Is -아도 되다 the same -아도?

Yes. 먹어도 돼요 is literally "even if you eat, it's fine" → "you may eat". Likewise 안 가도 돼요 ("even if you don't go, it's fine") → "you don't have to go". Once you see the literal meaning, permission and "even if" are the same grammar.

What's the difference between -는데도 and -는데 도?

There isn't a spaced version — -는데도 is always written together. What you may be thinking of is -는 데도, with a space, where 데 is a noun meaning "place/situation" (Lesson 10 will introduce -는 데). In speech they sound the same; context tells you which one it is.

Is 반면에 only for writing?

It's more common in writing, news and presentations, but perfectly normal in careful speech, especially when you're explicitly comparing: 장점이 있는 반면에 단점도 있어요. In very casual chat, people more often use -는데 or 근데 for the same job.

🌟 Going Further

Challenge 1: Pick a real decision you're facing (or once faced): a move, a phone, a course. Write six sentences about it, one each with -아도, -더라도, -는데도, -기는 하지만, -반면에 and -대신에. Then read them aloud.

Challenge 2 (TOPIK-style): In 200–300 characters of 한다체, answer: 도시 생활과 시골 생활의 장단점은 무엇인가? Use at least two contrast patterns. (Tip: 한다체 verbs end in -ㄴ다/-는다, adjectives in -다: 편리하다, 불편하다, 줄어든다.)

If these endings blur together right now, that's normal. B1 is exactly the stage where the grammar you know outruns the grammar you use, and many learners feel stuck on a plateau here. The way through isn't memorising more tables — it's noticing these patterns in dramas and articles until one day 싼데도 comes out of your mouth before you think about it.
