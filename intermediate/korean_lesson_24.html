🇰🇷 Lesson 24: Idioms, Proverbs & 사자성어

Imagine a Korean coworker says about your manager: 그분은 발이 정말 넓어요. You know every word — 발 is "foot", 넓다 is "wide" — and yet the sentence makes no sense. Does your manager have wide feet? No: it means she knows a lot of people. This is the moment every B2 learner reaches, when the grammar is under control but the expressions still trip you up. Korean is full of them, and native speakers use them constantly — in chat, in dramas, in news headlines and in TOPIK reading passages.

This lesson gives you the three layers of fixed expression in Korean, the most useful items in each, and — just as important — a sense of when each one fits. An idiom used in the right place makes you sound fluent; a proverb used at the wrong moment makes you sound like a fortune cookie.

🧠 The Mental Model: Three Layers of Fixed Expression

LayerWhat it isExampleWhere you meet it
관용어 (idioms)A phrase whose meaning isn't the sum of its words; usually a body part + verb발이 넓다 — to know many peopleEverywhere: chat, work, dramas, news
속담 (proverbs)A complete folk saying with a lesson or observation티끌 모아 태산 — every little bit adds upConversation, speeches, columns, advice
사자성어 (four-character idioms)Four Sino-Korean syllables, usually from Classical Chinese작심삼일 — resolutions that last three daysEssays, news, speeches — and jokes

The good news: idioms conjugate like any other verb phrase. Once you know 발이 넓다, you can say 발이 넓은 사람 (a well-connected person), 발이 넓어서 (because she's well connected) or 발이 넓으시네요 (you really know everyone!). What you can't do is swap the words: 발이 크다 ("big feet") is just a comment about shoe size. The fixed part is fixed; the ending is free.

💡 The Formula

[fixed idiom stem] + [any ending you already know]

눈이 높다 → 눈이 너무 높아서 아직 결혼을 못 했대요 — "They say he's too picky, so he still hasn't married."

You can usually add adverbs (정말, 너무, 좀) and change the tense, but you should keep the noun, the particle and the verb exactly as learned.

🦶 관용어: Body-Part Idioms

Korean builds a huge number of idioms from body parts, and they follow a loose logic: 눈 (eyes) is about standards and attention, 입 (mouth) is about talking and taste, 귀 (ears) is about listening and being persuaded, 손 (hands) is about effort and generosity, 발 (feet) is about going places and social reach, and 배/간 (belly, liver) are about gut feelings and courage.

⚠️ Honorifics Still Apply

An idiom about a senior person takes -(으)시- like any other predicate: 팀장님은 발이 넓으세요, 할머니는 손이 크세요. Grammatically the subject is the body part, but the honour goes to its owner — the same logic as 할아버지는 키가 크세요 from the beginner tier.

📜 속담: Proverbs

Proverbs are complete sentences, usually in plain (한다체) form, so they sound a bit like quotations. That is exactly how Koreans use them: you cite a proverb to comment on a situation. Here are the ones you will hear most.

속담LiteralMeaning / English equivalent
가는 말이 고와야 오는 말이 곱다Outgoing words must be kind for incoming words to be kindTreat others as you want to be treated
낮말은 새가 듣고 밤말은 쥐가 듣는다Birds hear daytime words, mice hear night wordsThe walls have ears
원숭이도 나무에서 떨어진다Even monkeys fall from treesEven experts make mistakes
소 잃고 외양간 고친다Fixing the barn after losing the cowClosing the stable door after the horse has bolted
호랑이도 제 말 하면 온다Even the tiger comes if you talk about itSpeak of the devil
티끌 모아 태산Gather dust and you get a great mountainEvery little bit adds up
백지장도 맞들면 낫다Even a sheet of paper is easier lifted by twoMany hands make light work
사공이 많으면 배가 산으로 간다Too many boatmen and the boat goes up the mountainToo many cooks spoil the broth
발 없는 말이 천 리 간다A word without feet travels a thousand liNews (and gossip) travels fast
누워서 떡 먹기Eating rice cake lying downA piece of cake

Notice the pun in 말: in 가는 말이 고와야… and 발 없는 말이… 말 means "words", but it is spelled the same as 말 "horse" — which is why a "word without feet" travelling a thousand li is funny. A 리 (里) is an old unit of distance of roughly 400 metres, so 천 리 is simply "very far".

How to Drop a Proverb Into Conversation

You rarely just blurt out a proverb. Koreans wrap it in a frame that says "as the saying goes". These frames use grammar you have already met in the reported-speech and -더- lessons:

FrameKoreanEnglish
-다는 말이 있잖아요티끌 모아 태산이라는 말이 있잖아요. 조금씩이라도 저축하세요.You know the saying "every little bit adds up". Save even a little at a time.
속담에 -다고 하잖아요속담에 백지장도 맞들면 낫다고 하잖아요. 같이 해요.The proverb says two can lift a sheet of paper more easily. Let's do it together.
-다더니 (… and sure enough)호랑이도 제 말 하면 온다더니, 저기 민수 씨 오네요!Speak of the devil — here comes Minsu!
-(이)라더니그림의 떡이라더니, 백화점 옷은 보기만 해요.They say "a rice cake in a picture" — I just look at the department-store clothes.
딱/그야말로 N(이)네요이 문제는 그야말로 누워서 떡 먹기네요.This problem is truly a piece of cake.

-다더니 is a contraction of -다고 하더니: "they said X, and (now I see) it's true". It is the single most natural way to apply a proverb to something that just happened. For noun-ending proverbs (그림의 떡, 티끌 모아 태산) use -(이)라더니 or -(이)라는 말이 있잖아요.

🌏 Cultural note: Many proverbs sound like advice, so be careful who you aim them at. Telling your boss 소 잃고 외양간 고치네요 about a failed project is a criticism of how they managed it. With seniors, proverbs are safest when they are about yourself (원숭이도 나무에서 떨어진다더니, 제가 실수했네요) or about a shared, neutral situation (금강산도 식후경이니까 밥부터 먹을까요?).

🀄 사자성어: Four-Character Idioms

사자성어 (四字成語) are four Sino-Korean syllables, each one a hanja morpheme — so last lesson's work on Sino-Korean roots pays off immediately. Many come from Chinese classics; some are Korean-made. They are compact, a little learned-sounding, and loved by news writers, speech-givers and anyone writing an essay.

사자성어HanjaRomanizationBreakdown → meaning
작심삼일作心三日jaksimsamilmake + mind + three + days → a resolution that lasts three days
일석이조一石二鳥ilseogijoone + stone + two + birds → two birds with one stone
금시초문今始初聞geumsichomunnow + begin + first + hear → first I've heard of it
대기만성大器晩成daegimanseonggreat + vessel + late + complete → a late bloomer
설상가상雪上加霜seolsanggasangsnow + on + add + frost → adding insult to injury
역지사지易地思之yeokjisajiswap + place + think → put yourself in their shoes
동문서답東問西答dongmunseodapeast question, west answer → an irrelevant answer
고진감래苦盡甘來gojingamnaebitter ends, sweet comes → no pain, no gain
막상막하莫上莫下maksangmakhanone above, none below → neck and neck
우왕좌왕右往左往uwangjwawanggo right, go left → running around in confusion

Grammar Around 사자성어

Most 사자성어 are nouns, so they take 이다, particles and noun frames. A few describe an action and take 하다.

PatternKoreanEnglish
N이에요/예요그 얘기는 금시초문이에요.That's the first I've heard of it.
N(으)로 끝나다올해 다이어트도 작심삼일로 끝났어요.My diet this year also ended after three days.
N의 N이 경기는 막상막하의 승부였다.This match was a neck-and-neck contest.
N하다갑자기 불이 꺼지자 사람들이 우왕좌왕했다.When the lights suddenly went out, people ran around in confusion.
N형 (type)그는 전형적인 대기만성형 배우다.He is a classic late-blooming actor.
N(이)라고 하다이런 걸 일석이조라고 하죠.This is what you'd call two birds with one stone.

🔀 Which One? Choosing the Right Layer

Several ideas can be said in two or three layers. Choosing between them is mostly about register.

IdeaCasual / spokenProverb사자성어 (formal / written)

✅ Register Rule of Thumb

관용어 are safe almost everywhere, including polite conversation with seniors and most writing.

속담 are warm and folksy; great in conversation, speeches and opinion columns, but be careful using them to lecture someone older.

사자성어 sound educated. In an essay (like TOPIK 쓰기 54) one well-chosen 사자성어 adds polish; three in a paragraph sounds forced. In casual talk, people often use them jokingly: 또 작심삼일이야? ("Three-day resolution again?")

Look at how naturally the two speakers slide 사자성어 into casual 반말 — 완전 작심삼일이지 뭐, 일석이조잖아. In speech, four-character idioms are often used with a wink, which is part of their charm.

📰 Reading: 작심삼일을 이기는 법

Korean
새해가 되면 많은 사람들이 운동이나 외국어 공부 같은 계획을 세운다. 그러나 그 결심이 사흘을 넘기지 못하는 경우가 많아서 '작심삼일'이라는 말까지 생겼다. 그렇다면 작심삼일을 막는 방법은 없을까? 전문가들은 목표를 작게 나누라고 조언한다. '티끌 모아 태산'이라는 속담처럼 하루 십 분의 공부도 일 년이 지나면 큰 차이를 만든다. 또 계획이 한 번 무너졌다고 해서 포기할 필요는 없다. 작심삼일을 백 번 하면 삼백 일이 되기 때문이다. 고생 끝에 낙이 온다는 말을 믿고 오늘 다시 시작해 보자.
English translation

When the new year comes, many people make plans such as exercising or studying a foreign language. However, those resolutions often fail to last past three days — so much so that the expression "jaksimsamil" (a three-day resolution) was born. Is there no way to prevent it, then? Experts advise breaking goals into small pieces. As the proverb "gather dust and you get a mountain" says, even ten minutes of study a day makes a big difference after a year. Also, just because a plan falls apart once doesn't mean you need to give up. After all, if you make a three-day resolution a hundred times, that's three hundred days. Let's trust the saying that joy comes after hardship and start again today.

Notice -다고 해서 in 무너졌다고 해서 포기할 필요는 없다 — "just because X doesn't mean Y". It is a common written-style frame worth stealing for your own essays.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out For

Changing the words. ✗ 발이 커요 / ✗ 눈이 비싸요. Idioms only work in their fixed form: 발이 넓어요, 눈이 높아요.

Translating English idioms word for word. "Break a leg" is not 다리를 부러뜨리세요 (that is a threat!). Say 잘하세요 or 파이팅! "It's raining cats and dogs" is 비가 억수같이 와요.

Forgetting 이다 after a 사자성어. ✗ 그건 일석이조. (only acceptable as a headline or a clipped casual remark) → ✓ 그건 일석이조예요 / 일석이조네요.

Choosing 이에요 vs 예요 by the hanja instead of the sound. It is the last syllable that counts: 작심삼일이에요 (ends in ㄹ), 일석이조예요 (ends in a vowel).

Using 배가 아프다 literally in the wrong place. 사촌이 땅을 사면 배가 아프다 is about jealousy. If you really have a stomachache, add context: 점심을 잘못 먹었는지 배가 아파요.

Overloading an essay. Three 사자성어 in one paragraph sounds like you memorised a list. One apt expression per essay is plenty.

❓ Common Questions

Do I need to learn the hanja for 사자성어?

You don't need to write them, but knowing what each syllable means is the best memory aid: once you know 일 = one, 석 = stone, 이 = two, 조 = bird, you will never forget 일석이조. It also lets you guess unfamiliar ones in a reading passage, which is exactly the skill Lesson 23 trained.

Will Koreans find it strange if a foreigner uses proverbs?

Usually they are delighted — a well-timed 호랑이도 제 말 하면 온다더니! often gets a laugh and a compliment. The risk is not strangeness but tone: a proverb aimed at someone older can sound like a lecture. Use them about yourself or shared situations first.

Are these expressions old-fashioned?

Some are (you will rarely hear 시치미 explained, even though everyone says 시치미를 떼다). But the body-part idioms are thoroughly modern, and news headlines use 사자성어 every day — for example 설상가상 in stories about bad weather after an earthquake, or 막상막하 in election coverage.

How many should I learn?

For TOPIK 4, being able to recognise the forty or so in this lesson and the vocabulary list is a solid base. Active use can be much smaller: pick five you genuinely like and use them this week until they feel like yours.

🌟 Going Further

Challenge 1: Write a 200–300 character paragraph in 한다체 about a time a plan of yours failed and what you learned. Use exactly one 사자성어 and one 속담, each in a natural frame (-다는 말처럼, -다더니).

Challenge 2: Watch one episode of a Korean drama or variety show and note every idiom you hear. Many variety-show captions (자막) put idioms in big letters — they are easy to spot and pause on.

If idioms feel endless, that's because they are — even native speakers keep learning new ones into adulthood. You don't need all of them; you need the common core and the confidence to ask 그게 무슨 뜻이에요? when a new one appears. Every one you collect makes the next one easier to guess.
