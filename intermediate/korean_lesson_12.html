🇰🇷 Lesson 12: Helper Verbs

It's an hour before your housewarming party. You check the flat: the food is ready (you made it in advance), the front door is standing open, the lights are on — and your roommate has eaten half the cake. In Korean, every one of those facts is expressed with a small verb bolted onto the end of a big one:

KoreanEnglishHelper
음식은 미리 만들어 놨어요.I made the food in advance (and it's ready).-아 놓다
현관문이 열려 있어요.The front door is open.-아 있다
룸메이트가 케이크를 반이나 먹어 버렸어요.My roommate went and ate half the cake.-아 버리다
집이 정말 깨끗해졌어요.The place has become really clean.-아지다

These are helper verbs (보조 용언). They add attitude (regret, relief), aftermath (the state left behind) and time (change up to now or from now on). Our theme is the household — moving, cleaning, a flat changing over time.

🧠 The Mental Model: Main Verb + Faded Verb

A helper verb is an ordinary verb that has partly faded: 버리다 "throw away" → "completely, gone for good"; 놓다 "put down" → "do and leave ready"; 있다 "be (located)" → "stay in the state". The main verb carries the action; the helper carries the colour.

💡 The Formula

Most helpers: take the 해요 form, drop 요, add the helper → 먹어요 → 먹어 버리다, 해요 → 해 놓다, 와요 → 와 있다

-고 helpers: stem + 고 → 먹고 말다, 입고 있다

Tense and politeness go on the helper: 먹어 버렸어요, 만들어 놓을게요, 앉아 계세요

Spacing: standard spelling allows both 먹어 버리다 and 먹어버리다. But 잊어버리다 (forget) and 잃어버리다 (lose) are dictionary words and always one word. -아지다 is always attached: 추워지다.

🗑️ -아/어 버리다: "Done and Gone"

-아 버리다 says an action is completely finished and can't be undone — and it adds the speaker's feeling about that. The feeling can go either way:

NuanceKoreanEnglish
Regret — it's gone배가 고파서 동생 케이크까지 다 먹어 버렸어요.I was hungry, so I ate my sister's cake too — all of it.
Regret — can't take it back비밀이었는데 친구한테 말해 버렸어요.It was a secret, but I went and told my friend.
Relief — burden gone밀린 빨래를 오늘 다 해 버려서 속이 시원해요.I got all the piled-up laundry done today — what a relief.
Decisive — "just do it"안 입는 옷은 이번 기회에 다 정리해 버리자.Let's just get rid of all the clothes we don't wear while we're at it.

-아 버리다 attaches to verbs, not adjectives, and it happily appears in plans and commands: 그냥 오늘 다 끝내 버릴게요 ("I'll just finish it all today").

🎬 -고 말다: "In the End, It Happened"

-고 말다 tells the end of a story: after a process, often after effort to prevent it, an outcome finally happens — usually one the speaker didn't want. It loves words like 결국 (in the end), 끝내 (ultimately) and 그만 (without meaning to).

KoreanEnglish
다이어트 중이었는데 결국 밤 열한 시에 치킨을 시키고 말았어요.I was on a diet, but in the end I ordered fried chicken at 11 p.m.
늦지 않으려고 뛰었지만 버스를 놓치고 말았어요.I ran so as not to be late, but I missed the bus anyway.
꾹 참으려고 했는데 그만 웃고 말았어요.I tried hard to hold it in, but I burst out laughing.

-고 말다 has a second, very different use: with -겠다 (or -(으)ㄹ 거예요) it expresses fierce determination — "I will, no matter what": 올해는 꼭 합격하고 말겠어요. ("This year I'm going to pass, whatever it takes.")

⚠️ Not the "don't" 말다

The 말다 in -고 말다 has nothing to do with -지 말다 ("don't"). 먹지 마세요 = "Don't eat it." 먹고 말았어요 = "I ended up eating it." Watch the connector: -지 is prohibition, -고 is "ended up".

Which one? -아 버리다 vs -고 말다

-아/어 버리다-고 말다
Core pictureThe action is complete — done and goneAfter a process, the outcome finally arrived
FeelingsRegret or reliefMostly regret or pity (or determination with -겠다)

Relief: "got my homework out of the way"✓ 숙제를 빨리 해 버렸어요.✗ 숙제를 하고 말았어요 sounds like "sadly, I ended up doing my homework"
Suggestions and commands✓ 오늘 다 치워 버리자!✗ not used

In many regret sentences both are fine, and they can even combine: 약속을 잊어버리고 말았어요 ("I ended up forgetting the appointment"). A rule of thumb: if you can add "argh!" or "phew!", reach for -아 버리다; if you're telling how a story ended, reach for -고 말다.

📌 -아/어 놓다 and -아/어 두다: "Do Now, For Later"

Both mean do something in advance and leave the result in place for later. They're the grammar of preparation. In speech they contract: 놓았어요 → 놨어요, 두었어요 → 뒀어요.

KoreanEnglish
손님들이 오기 전에 음식을 미리 만들어 놓았어요.I made the food before the guests arrived (so it's ready).
토요일 저녁 식당은 제가 예약해 뒀어요.I've booked the restaurant for Saturday evening.
에어컨을 켜 놓고 나와 버렸어요.I went out and left the air conditioner on.
이 표현은 시험에 자주 나오니까 꼭 알아 두세요.This expression often comes up in exams, so make sure you remember it.
계약서 같은 중요한 서류는 복사해 두는 게 좋아요.It's a good idea to keep copies of important documents like contracts.

Are 놓다 and 두다 different? Mostly they're interchangeable. 놓다 is more colloquial and often describes a physical state left behind (켜 놓다, 열어 놓다, 틀어 놓다). 두다 leans toward keeping something for a longer time and sounds a touch tidier; it's the usual choice in instructions like 알아 두세요 and 기억해 두세요.

🪑 -아/어 있다 vs -고 있다: State vs Action

This is the most important contrast in the lesson, so let's be precise.

💡 The Two Pictures

-고 있다 = the action is in progress. The film is still running: 친구가 오고 있어요 — "My friend is on the way."

-아/어 있다 = a change has already happened, and its result is still there. The film has stopped on the last frame: 친구가 와 있어요 — "My friend has come and is here now."

Rule 1: -아 있다 needs an intransitive verb

-아 있다 only attaches to verbs with no object — verbs of motion and posture, verbs of change, and passive verbs (the full passive system comes in Lesson 15; for now, learn 열리다, 닫히다, 켜지다, 꺼지다, 걸리다, 놓이다 as words).

Verb typeKoreanEnglish
Posture할머니께서 소파에 앉아 계세요.Grandma is sitting on the sofa.
Posture아이가 너무 피곤해서 침대에 누워 있어요.The child is so tired he's lying on the bed.
Motion남편은 출장 때문에 지금 부산에 가 있어요.My husband is away in Busan on a business trip.
Change베란다에 꽃이 예쁘게 피어 있어요.Flowers are blooming prettily on the balcony.
Passive현관문이 열려 있어요.The front door is open.
Passive거실 벽에 가족사진이 걸려 있어요.A family photo is hanging on the living-room wall.

With an honoured subject, 있다 becomes 계시다: 앉아 계세요, 와 계세요.

Rule 2: transitive verbs can't take -아 있다

✗ 창문을 열어 있어요 is wrong, because 열다 takes an object. You have two correct options, and they mean different things:

KoreanWhat it tells youEnglish
창문이 열려 있어요.The state only (passive 열리다 + 있다)The window is open.
창문을 열어 놓았어요.Someone opened it on purpose and left itI've left the window open.
창문을 열고 있어요.The action in progressSomeone is opening the window.

Side by side: in progress vs resulting state

-고 있다 (in progress)English-아 있다 (resulting state)English
손님들이 오고 있어요.The guests are on their way.손님들이 벌써 와 있어요.The guests are already here.
동생이 학교에 가고 있어요.My brother is on his way to school.동생이 학교에 가 있어요.My brother is at school (he's gone there).
문이 천천히 열리고 있어요.The door is slowly opening.문이 열려 있어요.The door is open.
꽃이 하나둘 피고 있어요.The flowers are starting to bloom one by one.꽃이 활짝 피어 있어요.The flowers are in full bloom.

Rule 3: wearing verbs use -고 있다 for both

Verbs of wearing and carrying — 입다 (clothes), 신다 (shoes), 쓰다 (hat, glasses), 끼다 (gloves, ring), 매다 (tie), 차다 (watch), 메다 (backpack), 들다 (hold) — are transitive, so -아 있다 is impossible. Instead, -고 있다 does double duty:

KoreanUsual meaning
민지 씨는 오늘 빨간 코트를 입고 있어요.Minji is wearing a red coat today. (state — the normal reading)
잠깐만요, 지금 옷을 입는 중이에요.Hang on, I'm getting dressed right now. (action — use -는 중이다 to be clear)
안경을 쓴 사람이 우리 팀장님이에요.The person wearing glasses is our team leader. (past modifier = wearing)

So ✗ 코트를 입어 있어요 and ✗ 안경을 써 있어요 are both wrong. And for posture verbs, the reverse: to say someone is seated, use 앉아 있어요, not 앉고 있어요 (which could only describe the slow-motion act of sitting down).

📈 -아/어지다: "Become…"

Attach -아/어지다 to an adjective and it becomes a verb of change: "get / become ADJ". It is always written as one word.

Adjective+ -아/어지다KoreanEnglish
춥다추워지다11월이 되니까 날씨가 갑자기 추워졌어요.Now it's November, the weather has suddenly got cold.
깨끗하다깨끗해지다대청소를 해서 집이 정말 깨끗해졌어요.We did a big clean, so the house has got really clean.
좋다좋아지다요즘 한국어 실력이 점점 좋아지고 있어요.My Korean is gradually getting better these days.
길다길어지다봄이 되면 낮이 길어질 거예요.When spring comes, the days will get longer.

Notice the tenses: -아졌어요 reports the new state (it has got cold), -아지고 있어요 the ongoing change (it's getting better), -아질 거예요 a future change. A useful pair: 날씨가 추워요 ("it's cold") vs 날씨가 추워졌어요 ("it has turned cold").

💡 좋아지다 vs 좋아하게 되다

-아지다 is for adjectives; for verbs, "come to (do)" is -게 되다, which you'll study properly in Lesson 15: 한국 음식을 좋아하게 됐어요 ("I've come to like Korean food"). Honest note: in everyday speech Koreans also say 그 사람이 좋아졌어요 to mean "I've started to like him/her", using the adjective 좋다. And when -아지다 attaches to a verb (이 펜은 잘 써져요, "this pen writes well"), it's the passive-like use — also Lesson 15.

⏳ -아/어 오다 and -아/어 가다: Change Along the Timeline

Picture yourself standing at now. -아 오다 is an action that has been coming toward now from the past. -아 가다 is an action moving away from now into the future, or moving toward its finish.

HelperKoreanEnglish
-아 오다 (past → now)이 식당은 삼십 년 동안 같은 맛을 지켜 왔어요.This restaurant has kept the same taste for thirty years.
-아 가다 (now → future)앞으로도 이웃들과 좋은 관계를 만들어 가고 싶어요.I want to keep building good relationships with the neighbours from now on.
-아 가다 (nearing the end)밥이 거의 다 돼 가요.The rice is almost ready.
-아 가다 (gradual decline)물을 안 줘서 화분의 꽃이 죽어 가요.I didn't water it, so the plant is dying.
-아져 가다 (combined)한국 생활에 조금씩 익숙해져 가요.I'm gradually getting used to life in Korea.

Don't confuse these with literal movement compounds like 사 오다 ("buy and bring") or 가져가다 ("take along"), where 오다/가다 still mean physical movement.

🌏 Culture Note — 집들이 gifts: When Koreans move house, they hold a 집들이 (housewarming) and guests bring practical gifts with a pun attached. The classic is 두루마리 휴지 (toilet roll): may everything in the new home "unroll" smoothly. Another is 세제 (laundry detergent): may good fortune "bubble up" like foam. A giant pack of toilet paper still gets a knowing laugh at the door.

📰 Reading: 우리 동네 이야기

A diary entry in plain written style (한다체, Lesson 21) — only past -았다 and adjective forms you can already read.

Korean
이 동네에 산 지 벌써 이십 년이 되었다. 그동안 동네는 많이 변해 왔다. 내가 처음 이사 왔을 때 이 길에는 작은 가게들이 모여 있었다. 떡집도 있었고, 오래된 책방도 있었다. 하지만 큰 마트가 생기면서 손님이 줄었고, 책방은 결국 문을 닫고 말았다. 요즘은 길이 넓어지고 높은 아파트가 많아졌다. 생활이 편해진 것은 좋지만 가끔은 옛날 모습이 그립다. 다행히 떡집은 아직도 그 자리에 남아 있다. 주인 할머니는 오십 년 동안 같은 방법으로 떡을 만들어 오셨다. 할머니는 "힘이 있을 때까지 이 가게를 지켜 갈 거야."라고 말씀하셨다. 나도 이 동네가 변해 가는 모습을 계속 기록해 가고 싶다.
English translation

It has already been twenty years since I started living in this neighbourhood. In that time, the neighbourhood has changed a lot. When I first moved here, small shops were clustered along this street. There was a rice-cake shop, and an old bookshop too. But as a big supermarket opened, customers dwindled, and in the end the bookshop closed its doors. These days the roads have got wider and there are many more tall apartment blocks. It's good that life has become more convenient, but sometimes I miss how things used to look. Fortunately, the rice-cake shop is still there in the same spot. The elderly owner has made rice cakes the same way for fifty years. She said, "I'll keep this shop going as long as I have the strength." I, too, want to keep recording how this neighbourhood changes.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 창문을 열어 있어요. → ✓ 창문이 열려 있어요 (state) / 창문을 열어 놓았어요 (left it open). (No -아 있다 with transitive verbs.)

✗ 오늘 청바지를 입어 있어요. → ✓ 청바지를 입고 있어요. (Wearing verbs use -고 있다.)

✗ 할머니가 의자에 앉고 계세요. (meaning "is seated") → ✓ 앉아 계세요.

✗ 드디어 숙제를 끝내고 말았어요! (happy) → ✓ 숙제를 끝내 버렸어요 / 드디어 끝냈어요. (-고 말다 sounds regretful.)

✗ 지갑을 잃어 버렸어요. → ✓ 잃어버렸어요. (One dictionary word.)

✗ 날씨가 추워 졌어요. → ✓ 추워졌어요. (-아지다 is always attached.)

If -아 있다 and -고 있다 keep tangling in your head, you're in excellent company — this is one of the contrasts that takes intermediate learners the longest to feel. Each time you stop and ask "is it moving, or has it stopped?", you're building the instinct. It will come.

❓ Common Questions

Is -아 버리다 always negative?

No. It's about finality; the speaker's feeling fills in the rest. 다 먹어 버렸어요 can be a guilty confession or a satisfied "I polished it off", and 확 말해 버릴까? ("Should I just come out and say it?") is decisiveness.

Should I say 놓다 or 두다?

In most sentences either works: 예약해 놨어요 / 예약해 뒀어요. 놓다 is a little more casual and common for leaving things switched on or open (켜 놓다, 열어 놓다). 두다 feels more like "keep for later" and is the standard choice in 알아 두세요, 기억해 두세요 and 저장해 두세요 (save it).

Why is "I'm married" 결혼했어요 and not 결혼하고 있어요 or 결혼해 있어요?

Some Korean verbs describe a change whose result simply lasts, and Korean uses the plain past to express the resulting state: 결혼했어요 ("I'm married"), 아빠를 닮았어요 ("I look like my dad"), 잘생겼어요 ("he's handsome"), 늦었어요 ("I'm late"). 결혼하고 있어요 would suggest the wedding ceremony is happening right now, and 결혼해 있어요 isn't used. Learn these as a small set.
