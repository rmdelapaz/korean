🇰🇷 Lesson 8: Time & Sequence

Think about the last good travel story you told. It probably wasn't "I went to Jeju. I ate fish. I came home." It was more like: "As soon as we landed, we rented a car. While we were driving along the coast, the GPS died. We were heading for the beach when we got completely lost — so we went into a café, and on the way back we found the best restaurant of the trip."

Stories live on timing words. At A2 you learned -고, -아서, -기 전에, -(으)ㄴ 후에 and -(으)면서. This lesson adds the rest of the storyteller's kit: -자마자, -고 나서, -는 동안, -는 중이다, -(으)ㄹ 때까지, -는 길에, -(으)ㄴ 지 N 되다 — and the pair that every Korean learner has to wrestle with at some point, -다가 and -았/었다가.

🧠 The Mental Model: A Timeline

Picture one action (A) as a bar on a timeline. Each pattern points to a different place on or around that bar.

Where on the timeline?PatternExample
The instant A ends → B-자마자 ("as soon as")도착하자마자 전화했어요.
A is completely finished → B-고 나서 ("after finishing")숙제를 하고 나서 놀았어요.
B happens during the whole of A-는 동안 ("while / during")제가 자는 동안 비가 왔어요.
Right in the middle of A-는 중이다 / -는 중에지금 회의하는 중이에요.
B continues up to the point A happens-(으)ㄹ 때까지 ("until")비가 그칠 때까지 기다렸어요.
B happens along the route of A-는 길에 ("on the way")집에 가는 길에 빵을 샀어요.
A is cut off partway, and B takes over-다가 ("was doing A, then")공부하다가 잠이 들었어요.
A is completed, then B (often reverses it)-았/었다가 ("did A, then")창문을 열었다가 닫았어요.
How long since A started-(으)ㄴ 지 N 되다한국에 온 지 2년 됐어요.

Keep this table in mind; it's the map for everything below. A useful general rule: in almost all of these patterns, the first clause carries no tense. The final verb decides whether the whole story is past, present or future: 도착하자마자, not ✗ 도착했자마자.

⚡ -자마자: "As Soon As"

Attach -자마자 to any action-verb stem — no vowel harmony, no 받침 changes: 가자마자, 먹자마자, 듣자마자, 만들자마자. The second action follows immediately, often with a sense of urgency or "couldn't wait".

KoreanEnglish
공항에 내리자마자 엄마한테 전화했어요.As soon as I got off the plane, I called my mum.
호텔에 들어가자마자 침대에 누웠어요.The moment I got into the hotel, I lay down on the bed.
영화가 끝나자마자 사람들이 다 나갔어요.As soon as the film ended, everyone left.
저는 아침에 일어나자마자 휴대폰부터 확인해요.I check my phone the moment I wake up.
집에 도착하자마자 연락해.Call me as soon as you get home.

The two clauses can have different subjects (영화가 끝나자마자 사람들이 나갔어요), and a command at the end is fine. Adjectives don't take -자마자 — "as soon as it's cold" would be 추워지자마자 (becomes cold).

✅ -고 나서: "After Finishing"

You know -(으)ㄴ 후에 ("after"). -고 나서 adds an emphasis on completion: A is done and dusted, and then B. It's very common in instructions and in step-by-step narration.

KoreanEnglish
밥을 먹고 나서 이 약을 드세요.Take this medicine after you've eaten.
짐을 풀고 나서 좀 쉬자.Let's rest once we've unpacked.
그 영화를 보고 나서 부산에 가고 싶어졌어요.After watching that film, I started wanting to go to Busan.
일을 다 끝내고 나서 퇴근했어요.I left work after finishing everything.

💡 -고 나서 vs -(으)ㄴ 후에 vs -아서

-(으)ㄴ 후에 is neutral "after", common in writing: 졸업한 후에 취직했어요.

-고 나서 stresses that A was finished first: 청소를 다 하고 나서 친구를 불렀어요.

-아서 (sequence) is for "go somewhere / do something and then use it there": 부산에 가서 회를 먹었어요. Saying 부산에 가고 나서 회를 먹었어요 sounds as if the going and the eating were two unrelated events.

⏳ -는 동안(에): "While / During"

동안 means "period". After a noun it gives duration (3년 동안, 방학 동안). After an action verb with -는, it means "during the time that A is going on".

KoreanEnglish
제가 운전하는 동안 친구는 계속 잤어요.While I drove, my friend slept the whole time.
여행하는 동안 매일 일기를 썼어요.I kept a diary every day while I was travelling.
제가 없는 동안 고양이 좀 봐 줄래요?Could you look after my cat while I'm away?
한국에 사는 동안 이사를 다섯 번 했어요.I moved house five times while I lived in Korea.

Note: it's -는 동안 even for past events (여행하는 동안 … 썼어요). 없다 and 있다 take -는 as usual.

💡 -는 동안 vs -(으)면서

-(으)면서 = one person doing two things at once: 음악을 들으면서 운전해요.

-는 동안 = a stretch of time; the other clause can be a different person: 제가 운전하는 동안 친구는 잤어요. You can't say ✗ 제가 운전하면서 친구는 잤어요.

🔧 -는 중이다: "In the Middle Of"

중 means "middle". -는 중이다 says someone is right in the middle of an activity — often as an explanation of why they can't do something else. With nouns that are activities, just add 중: 회의 중, 통화 중, 수업 중, 공사 중 ("under construction").

KoreanEnglish
지금 운전하는 중이라서 이따가 전화할게.I'm driving right now, so I'll call you later.
김 과장님은 지금 회의 중이세요.Manager Kim is in a meeting right now.
다음 여행 계획을 세우는 중이에요.I'm in the middle of planning my next trip.
여행하는 중에 여권을 잃어버렸어요.I lost my passport while (in the middle of) travelling.

How is it different from -고 있다? Often both work: 밥을 먹고 있어요 / 밥을 먹는 중이에요. -는 중이다 stresses "busy with it right now, not yet finished". It sounds odd with long-term states or things nobody does on purpose: ✗ 서울에 사는 중이에요, ✗ 그 사람을 아는 중이에요 — use -고 있다 or the plain present instead.

🏁 -(으)ㄹ 때까지: "Until"

You know -(으)ㄹ 때 ("when") and 까지 ("until/up to"). Put them together and you get "until the time that…". After a 받침 add -을 때까지; after a vowel or ㄹ-stem, -ㄹ 때까지 (ㄹ-stems don't double: 알 때까지).

KoreanEnglish
비가 그칠 때까지 카페에서 기다렸어요.We waited in a café until the rain stopped.
제가 돌아올 때까지 여기 있어요.Stay here until I get back.
이해할 때까지 계속 물어보세요.Keep asking until you understand.
해가 질 때까지 해변에 앉아 있었어요.We sat on the beach until the sun went down.

🛣️ -는 길에: "On the Way"

길 is "road, way". -는 길에 attaches to movement verbs (가다, 오다, 나가다, 들어오다, 돌아가다, 출근하다, 퇴근하다…) and means "on the way (to/from)". It's typically used for doing something along the route — especially 들르다, "to drop by".

KoreanEnglish
퇴근하는 길에 마트에 들렀어요.I stopped by the supermarket on my way home from work.
학교 가는 길에 우체국에 좀 들러 줄래?Could you drop by the post office on your way to school?
숙소로 돌아오는 길에 야시장을 구경했어요.On the way back to our lodgings we looked around the night market.
가: 어디야? 나: 지금 집에 가는 길이야.A: Where are you? B: I'm on my way home now.

The last example shows the handy sentence-final form -는 길이다: "I'm on my way". It's used constantly on the phone.

↪️ -다가: "Was Doing A, Then…"

This is the big one. -다가 (often shortened to -다 in speech) attaches to any stem: 가다가, 먹다가, 공부하다가, 자다가. It means that A was in progress and had not finished when the subject switched to B, or when B happened.

💡 The -다가 Picture

A ─────── ✂ → B

A is cut off in the middle. The same person who was doing A moves on to B (or B happens to them).

UseKoreanEnglish
Interruption / switch공부하다가 잠이 들었어요.I was studying and fell asleep.
Interruption / switch밥을 먹다가 전화를 받으러 나갔어요.I was eating when I went out to take a call.
Something happens along the way해안 도로를 달리다가 길을 잃었어요.We were driving along the coast road and got lost.
Something happens along the way학교에 가다가 선생님을 만났어요.On my way to school I ran into my teacher.
Cause of a (bad) result계단에서 뛰다가 넘어졌어요.I was running on the stairs and fell over.
Cause of a (bad) result요리하다가 손을 베었어요.I cut my hand while cooking.
State switch날씨가 따뜻하다가 갑자기 추워졌어요.It had been warm, then suddenly it got cold.
Spoken short form자다 깼어요.I woke up in my sleep (lit. was sleeping, then woke).

⚠️ The Same-Subject Rule

-다가 follows one subject through a change. The two clauses can't be two different people each doing their own thing:

✗ 제가 공부하다가 룸메이트가 청소했어요. → ✓ 제가 공부하는 동안 룸메이트가 청소했어요 / 제가 공부하는데 룸메이트가 청소했어요.

An honest footnote: in real speech you'll hear sentences like 공부하다가 전화가 왔어요 or 올라가다가 비가 와서 내려왔어요, where the second clause is an event that happens to the first subject. These are natural and fine. What's wrong is two different people acting on purpose.

🔁 -았/었다가: "Did A (Completely), Then…"

Add the past tense before -다가 and the picture changes completely. With -았/었다가, action A was finished — you arrived, you put it on, you bought it — and then B follows. B is very often the reverse of A, or something that happened while you were in the state A created.

💡 The -았다가 Picture

A ───────● → B (often ↩ undoing A)

A reached its end point. Same subject throughout, as with -다가.

UseKoreanEnglish
Reversal창문을 열었다가 너무 추워서 닫았어요.I opened the window, but it was too cold so I shut it again.
Reversal바지를 샀다가 너무 커서 환불했어요.I bought some trousers, but they were too big so I returned them.
Reversal (go and come back)편의점에 잠깐 갔다가 올게.I'll pop to the convenience store and come back.
Something happens once there시장에 갔다가 옛날 친구를 만났어요.I went to the market and ran into an old friend there.
Something happens once there도서관에 갔다가 문이 닫혀 있어서 그냥 왔어요.I went to the library, but it was closed, so I just came back.
Do A, then B (after waiting)비가 그칠 때까지 기다렸다가 출발하자.Let's wait until the rain stops and then set off.

Did you notice 갔다 오다 — "to go and come back"? It's so common it's practically one verb: 화장실 갔다 올게요 ("I'll just go to the bathroom"), 여행 잘 갔다 왔어요? ("Did you have a good trip?"). Its close cousin 다녀오다 means the same and sounds a little more polished.

Minimal pairs: feel the difference

📅 -(으)ㄴ 지 N 되다: "It's Been N Since…"

To say how much time has passed since something started or happened, use the past modifier -(으)ㄴ + 지 + a length of time + 되다 (most often past: 됐어요). 지 is written with a space — it's a separate noun meaning "time since".

KoreanEnglish
한국에 온 지 3년 됐어요.It's been three years since I came to Korea.
한국어를 배운 지 얼마나 됐어요?How long have you been learning Korean?
이 동네에서 산 지 벌써 10년이 넘었어요.I've already lived in this neighbourhood for over ten years.
그 친구를 안 지 오래됐어요.I've known that friend for a long time.
밥 먹은 지 한 시간도 안 됐는데 또 배고파.It hasn't even been an hour since I ate and I'm hungry again.

ㄹ-stems drop ㄹ: 살다 → 산 지, 알다 → 안 지. Instead of 되다 you'll also hear 넘다 ("be over") and 지나다 ("pass"): 졸업한 지 5년이 지났어요. English uses "have been -ing" here; Korean always counts from the starting event.

📰 Reading: 경주 여행 일기

Mina keeps a travel diary. Korean diaries are written in plain 한다체 — past-tense verbs end in -았다/-었다 instead of -았어요. You'll study this style properly in Lesson 21; for now, read -았다 as -았어요.

Korean
10월 3일, 맑음. 오늘은 경주 여행 첫날이었다. 서울역에서 KTX를 타고 두 시간쯤 걸려서 경주에 도착했다. 시내에 도착하자마자 자전거를 빌려서 대릉원으로 향했다. 자전거를 타고 가다가 예쁜 한옥 카페가 보여서 잠깐 쉬었다. 그런데 카페에 있는 동안 갑자기 소나기가 쏟아졌다. 비가 그칠 때까지 기다렸다가 다시 출발했다. 대릉원과 첨성대를 구경하고 나서 숙소로 돌아가는 길에 유명한 경주빵을 샀다. 한국에 온 지 벌써 2년이 됐는데 이렇게 혼자 여행한 것은 처음이다. 다리는 아프지만 정말 행복한 하루였다.
English translation

October 3rd, sunny. Today was the first day of my trip to Gyeongju. I took the KTX from Seoul Station and arrived in Gyeongju after about two hours. As soon as I reached the town centre, I rented a bike and headed for Daereungwon (the royal tomb park). I was cycling along when I spotted a pretty hanok café, so I took a short break. But while I was in the café, a sudden shower poured down. I waited until the rain stopped and then set off again. After seeing Daereungwon and Cheomseongdae (the ancient observatory), I bought the famous Gyeongju bread on the way back to my lodgings. It's already been two years since I came to Korea, but this is the first time I've travelled alone like this. My legs hurt, but it was a really happy day.

🚄 Cultural note — travel talk in Korea. Koreans greet someone back from a trip with 잘 갔다 왔어요? or 여행 잘 다녀왔어요? and it's polite to bring a small edible souvenir for coworkers — Gyeongju bread, Jeju tangerine chocolate, and so on. Destinations like Gyeongju, the ancient Silla capital, are also classic school-trip spots (수학여행), so almost every Korean adult has a story that starts 고등학교 때 수학여행 갔다가…

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. Tense on the first clause. ✗ 도착했자마자, ✗ 여행했는 동안 → ✓ 도착하자마자, 여행하는 동안. The final verb carries the tense.

2. Different people with -다가. ✗ 엄마가 요리하다가 제가 설거지했어요. → ✓ 엄마가 요리하는 동안 제가 설거지했어요.

3. -다가 when A was finished. ✗ 옷을 입다가 벗었어요 (you can't take it off if you never got it on) → ✓ 옷을 입었다가 벗었어요.

4. -는 길에 with non-movement verbs. ✗ 공부하는 길에 → ✓ 공부하다가 / 공부하는 중에. 길에 needs a journey: 가다, 오다, 출근하다, 퇴근하다.

5. The wrong tense form before 지. ✗ 한국에 오는 지 3년 됐어요 → ✓ 온 지. It's always the past modifier -(으)ㄴ.

6. -는 중이다 for long-term states. ✗ 서울에 사는 중이에요 → ✓ 서울에 살고 있어요 / 서울에 살아요.

Exercise 1 — Choose the time pattern

  7. 이 동네에서 _____ 얼마나 됐어요? (살다, since) (Answer: 산 지)

  1. 학교에 _____ 길에서 선생님을 만났어요. (가다; hadn't arrived yet) (Answer: 가다가)
  2. 도서관에 _____ 문이 닫혀 있어서 그냥 왔어요. (가다; got there) (Answer: 갔다가)
  3. 편지를 _____ 마음에 안 들어서 찢었어요. (쓰다; finished writing it) (Answer: 썼다가)
  4. 공부를 _____ 잠이 들었어요. (하다; interrupted) (Answer: 하다가)
  5. 창문을 _____ 너무 추워서 다시 닫았어요. (열다) (Answer: 열었다가)
  6. 계단에서 _____ 넘어졌어요. (뛰다) (Answer: 뛰다가)
  7. 그 바지를 _____ 너무 커서 환불했어요. (사다) (Answer: 샀다가)
  8. 날씨가 _____ 오후에 갑자기 추워졌어요. (따뜻하다; state switch) (Answer: 따뜻하다가)

Exercise 3 — Combine or correct

  1. 영화가 끝났어요. 곧바로 사람들이 나갔어요. → 영화가 _____ 사람들이 나갔어요. (as soon as) (Answer: 끝나자마자)
  2. 저는 잤어요. 그동안 남편이 운전했어요. → 제가 _____ 남편이 운전했어요. (자다, while) (Answer: 자는 동안|자는 동안에)
  3. 출근했어요. 그 길에 커피를 샀어요. → _____ 커피를 샀어요. (출근하다, on the way) (Answer: 출근하는 길에)
  4. ✗ 제가 공부하다가 룸메이트가 청소했어요. → 제가 _____ 룸메이트가 청소했어요. (-는 동안) (Answer: 공부하는 동안|공부하는 동안에)
  5. ✗ 부산에 가고 나서 회를 먹었어요. → 부산에 _____ 회를 먹었어요. (went there and ate there) (Answer: 가서)
  6. 가: 어디예요? 나: 지금 집에 _____ 이에요. (가다, on my way) (Answer: 가는 길)

Exercise 4 — Complete the conversation

  1. 가: 제주도에서 뭐 했어요? 나: 공항에 _____ 바다부터 보러 갔어요. (내리다, as soon as) (Answer: 내리자마자)
  2. 가: 한국어 공부 오래 했어요? 나: 이제 _____ 2년 됐어요. (시작하다, since) (Answer: 시작한 지)
  3. 가: 왜 이렇게 늦었어? 나: 버스를 _____ 잠이 들어서 두 정거장을 지나쳤어. (타고 가다; interrupted) (Answer: 타고 가다가)
  4. 가: 김 과장님 계세요? 나: 지금 _____ 조금 후에 다시 전화해 주세요. (회의, in the middle of; polite) (Answer: 회의 중이세요|회의 중이에요)
  5. 가: 여행 사진 언제 보여 줄 거야? 나: 사진 정리를 다 _____ 보여 줄게. (하다, after finishing) (Answer: 하고 나서)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension (경주 여행 일기)

  1. 미나는 서울역에서 _____ 를 타고 경주에 갔어요. (Answer: KTX)
  2. 시내에 도착하자마자 _____ 를 빌렸어요. (Answer: 자전거)
  3. 카페에 있는 동안 갑자기 _____ 가 쏟아졌어요. (a sudden shower) (Answer: 소나기)
  4. 숙소로 돌아가는 길에 _____ 을 샀어요. (a famous food) (Answer: 경주빵)
  5. 미나는 한국에 온 지 _____ 이 됐어요. (how long?) (Answer: 2년|이 년|이년)

❓ Common Questions

What's the difference between -자마자 and -는 대로?

You may meet -는 대로 in workplace Korean: 도착하는 대로 연락드리겠습니다 ("I'll contact you as soon as I arrive"). It's used for a deliberate next action, usually in the future, and sounds a little more formal and businesslike. -자마자 is the everyday "the moment that", and works for past events too.

Is -다가 the same as the 다가 in 다가오다?

Treat them as different things. 다가오다 ("to approach") and 다가가다 ("to go up to") are ordinary dictionary verbs written as one word, whatever their history. The connector -다가 always attaches to the stem of another verb or adjective: 가다가, 먹다가.

Can -다가 be used for the future or with commands?

Yes. The -다가 clause itself has no tense, so the end of the sentence decides: 가다가 배고프면 이거 먹어 ("If you get hungry on the way, eat this"), 조금 기다렸다가 드세요 ("Wait a moment and then eat"). -다가 and -았다가 are both happy before commands.

Why is it 됐어요 and not 돼요 in -(으)ㄴ 지 N 됐어요?

되다 here means "to come to be". Three years have come to be, so the past form describes the current total. You'll occasionally hear 이제 1년 돼요 ("it's now coming to a year"), but 됐어요 is the standard, safe choice.

🌟 Going Further

Challenge 1: Tell the story of a real trip in eight sentences, using each of these once: -자마자, -는 동안, -다가, -았다가, -는 길에, -(으)ㄹ 때까지, -고 나서, -(으)ㄴ 지. Record yourself reading it aloud.

Challenge 2 (TOPIK-style): Write a diary entry (200–300 characters, in 한다체 past like Mina's) about a day when something didn't go to plan. Include at least one -다가 and one -았다가, and check that each has only one subject.

-다가 and -았다가 trip up learners for months, even advanced ones — so if you still have to stop and think "was it finished?", you're doing exactly what you should. That pause is your brain building the distinction; with enough stories, it disappears.
