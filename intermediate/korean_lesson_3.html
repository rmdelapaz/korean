🇰🇷 Lesson 3: Reported Speech II: -대요, -래요 & Hearsay

Stand by any office water cooler in Seoul and you'll hear it: 박 대리님 결혼하신대요! 진짜요? 누구랑 한대요? 회사 그만두신다면서요? Gossip, news and "apparently…" in Korean come at speed, and almost none of it uses the full -다고 했어요 forms from Lesson 2. It uses contractions. In this lesson you'll learn to understand and produce them, confirm things you've heard, get 달라고 vs 주라고 right, and pick the right verb for saying, asking, requesting and passing on.

🧠 The Mental Model: Squeeze Out -고 하-

Every contraction in this lesson comes from one sound change. In fast speech, -고 해요 collapses: the 고 and the 하 disappear, and the vowel of 해 fuses onto the ending before it.

💡 The Formula

-다고 해요 → -대요 · -라고 해요 → -래요 · -자고 해요 → -재요 · -냐고 해요 → -냬요

So: take the Lesson 2 form, drop 고 하-, and change the last vowel ㅏ to ㅐ. 간다고 해요 → 간대요. 오라고 해요 → 오래요.

You don't need a single new rule for choosing the form. If you can make the Lesson 2 version, you can make the contraction.

ㄹ-stems follow Lesson 2 exactly: 살다 → 산대요 ("they say she lives…"), 열다 → 연대요 ("they say it opens…"), 만들다 → 만들래요 ("they told me to make it"). And the honorific survives inside: 사장님이 곧 오신대요 ("The boss says he's coming soon").

⚠️ -래요 has a look-alike twin

You've probably already heard -(으)ㄹ래요 ("I want to / will you?"): 뭐 먹을래요? (Lesson 5 covers it.) That's a different ending. Look at the consonant before 래: 먹을래요 (with ㄹ, "want to eat") vs 먹으래요 (no ㄹ, "they told me to eat"). Similarly, 갈래요 = "I'll go / want to go", 가래요 = "they told me to go".

👂 What -대요 Really Means: "They Say" / "Apparently"

-대요 is Korean's everyday hearsay marker. Sometimes a specific person said it. Just as often the source is vague: the news, a friend of a friend, "people". English uses "apparently", "I heard", "they say" or "supposedly".

내일 비가 많이 온대요. 우산 꼭 챙기세요.Apparently it's going to rain a lot tomorrow. Make sure you bring an umbrella.

Two small things. First, 뭐래(요)? is short for 뭐라고 해(요)?, "What did they say?" Between friends, 뭐래? with a certain tone also means "What nonsense is that?" Second, 그렇대요 ("so they say") is the all-purpose way to pass something on without committing to it.

💡 Tense: -대요 vs -댔어요

-대요 (from 해요) is used both for "they say" and "they said", and context tells you which. If you want to stress that it was said in the past, use the full form -다고 했어요, or the spoken contraction -댔어요 / -랬어요: 민수가 온댔어요 ("Minsu said he'd come"), 엄마가 일찍 오랬어요 ("Mom told me to come early"). -댔어요 often has a "but that's what they said…" flavour: 분명히 온댔는데… ("He definitely said he'd come, but…").

⚠️ -대요 or -데요? A spelling trap even Koreans hit

They sound identical. -대요 (ㅐ) = hearsay, "someone said". -데요 (ㅔ) is a different ending from the -더- family: "I saw/experienced this myself", which you'll study in Lesson 17. 그 식당 맛있대요 = "People say it's good." 그 식당 맛있데요 = "I went and it was good." A useful trick: if you can expand it to -다고 해요, write ㅐ.

🤔 Checking What You Heard: -다면서요?

When you've heard something and want the other person to confirm it, especially someone who would know, add -면서요? to the reported form: -다면서요?, -(이)라면서요?, -(으)라면서요?, -자면서요?. It means "I heard that…, is that right?" or "Didn't you say…?" In 반말 it's -다면서? or, even shorter, -다며?

KoreanEnglish
다음 달에 결혼한다면서요? 축하해요!I heard you're getting married next month? Congratulations!
지난주에 제주도에 갔다면서요? 어땠어요?I heard you went to Jeju last week. How was it?
민호 씨 동생이 의사라면서요?Your younger sibling is a doctor, right, Minho?
요즘 회사 일이 많이 바쁘다면서요?I hear work's really busy for you these days?
너 이사 간다며? 어디로 가?I heard you're moving? Where to? (반말)
일찍 오라면서요! 왜 아무도 없어요?You told me to come early! Why isn't anyone here?

The last example shows a second use: throwing someone's own words back at them, often as a complaint. And as a connector, -다면서 means "saying that…": 바쁘다면서 먼저 나갔어요 ("Saying he was busy, he left first"). For now, just recognise this.

-다고 했어요Full, explicit: "X said…"; safest in careful speech민수 씨가 못 온다고 했어요.
-대요Everyday spoken hearsay: "X says / apparently…"민수 씨 못 온대요.

The -다는 in the fifth row is itself a contraction: -다고 하는 → -다는. It works with all four types: 온다는 소식 ("the news that he's coming"), 오라는 연락 ("a message telling me to come"), 가자는 제안 ("a suggestion to go"), 뭐냐는 질문 ("the question of what it is"). It's common in writing and news, and you'll build on it at B2.

🎁 달라고 vs 주라고: Who Gets the Favour?

Lesson 2 left one puzzle open: requests with 주다 ("give" / "do for"). When someone says -아/어 주세요, the reported form depends on who receives the favour.

💡 The Rule

If the favour is for the person who said it → -아/어 달라고 (from the special verb 달다, which only exists in this use).

If the favour is for someone else → -아/어 주라고 (or 드리라고 if that someone deserves honour).

Contracted: 달래요 / 주래요 / 드리래요.

OriginalWho benefits?ReportedEnglish
민수: "저 좀 도와주세요."민수 (the speaker)민수 씨가 좀 도와 달라고 했어요.Minsu asked (me) to help him.
선생님: "민수 좀 도와주세요."민수 (someone else)선생님이 민수를 좀 도와주라고 하셨어요.The teacher told me to help Minsu.
친구: "나한테 전화해 줘."친구 (the speaker)친구가 전화해 달래요.My friend wants me to call her.

엄마: "할머니께 이것 좀 갖다 드려."할머니 (honoured)엄마가 할머니께 이걸 갖다 드리라고 했어요.Mom told me to take this to Grandma.

Why does Korean care? Because 주다 in a command always points away from the speaker, toward someone else. When you report "give me", the "me" is no longer the person talking. 달다 exists to say "give it back toward the one who asked". English hides this, since "she asked me to help her" and "she told me to help him" use the same verb, so learners must build the habit consciously. Before reporting any -아/어 주세요, ask yourself: for whom?

🗣️ Choosing the Reporting Verb

하다 works for everything, but choosing a more specific verb makes your Korean clearer and more precise. Here are the core verbs and the quote types they naturally take.

Count the contractions: 결혼하신대요 (with honorific -시-), 한대요 (question about a report), 분이래요 / 소문이래요 (nouns), 넘었대요 (past), 아니래요, 하래요 (command), 사재요 (suggestion), 되냬요 (question). Then notice the last line switches to a full form with 전하다, because 도현 is sending a message, not reporting one. (여쭤보다 is the humble form of 물어보다. You'll meet it properly in Lesson 20.)

🇰🇷 Cultural note: 카더라. Koreans have a joke word for unverified gossip: 카더라 (from the Gyeongsang dialect for "they say"), as in 카더라 통신 ("the they-say news agency"). Piling on -대요 without a source can sound exactly like that. In the workplace, the more careful -다고 들었어요 and naming your source (팀장님 말씀으로는…) come across as more trustworthy. The grammar of hearsay is also the grammar of social responsibility.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. Contracting from the wrong base. ✗ 먹대요 (for a present verb) → ✓ 먹는대요. ✗ 학생대요 → ✓ 학생이래요. The contraction keeps every Lesson 2 rule.

2. -대요 vs -데요. ✗ 내일 비가 온데요 (for "they say") → ✓ 온대요.

3. -으래요 vs -을래요. ✗ 선생님이 책을 읽을래요 → ✓ 읽으래요 ("told us to read").

4. 주라고 for "give me". ✗ 친구가 (자기) 책을 주라고 했어요 → ✓ 책을 달라고 했어요. Ask "for whom?"

5. Using -다면서요? about yourself or for new information. -다면서요? checks something you already heard, usually with the person it concerns. To announce news, use -대요.

6. Asking with 말하다. ✗ 어디 가냐고 말했어요 sounds odd → ✓ 어디 가냐고 물었어요 / 물어봤어요 (or just 했어요).

  4. 소문 들었어요? 그 가수가 _____. (은퇴하다, hearsay — ㅐ or ㅔ?) (Answer: 은퇴한대요)
  5. 동생이 저한테 뭐 먹고 싶냐고 해요. → 동생이 저한테 뭐 먹고 _____. (Answer: 싶냬요)

  1. 빵집은 _____ 달에 문을 닫는대요. (Answer: 다음)
  2. 사장님은 나이가 벌써 _____이 넘으셨대요. (seventy, native Korean) (Answer: 일흔)
  3. 사장님 딸은 _____에서 빵을 배웠대요. (Answer: 프랑스)
  4. 딸 이야기를 해 준 사람은 _____ 아주머니예요. (Answer: 옆집)
  5. 사장님은 아직 아무것도 _____ 않았다고 하셨어요. (Answer: 결정하지)
  6. 사장님은 동네 소문을 너무 믿지 _____ 하셨어요. (Answer: 말라고)

❓ Common Questions

Is -대요 less polite than -다고 했어요?

Not less polite, since it's still 해요체, but it's more conversational. In a report to your boss or in writing, the full form (or -다고 들었습니다) sounds more careful. With coworkers and friends, -대요 is completely natural.

Can -대요 be a question?

Yes, with rising intonation: 민수 씨 온대요? means "Did Minsu say he's coming?" or "Is Minsu coming, did you hear?" Likewise 뭐래요? ("What did they say?") and 언제 오래요? ("When did they tell you to come?").

Why is 달라고 never 달아 or 달아요?

달다 in this sense is a defective verb. It survives only in reported and a few set forms: 달라고, 달래요, 달라는. Its old imperative 달라 even appears in written requests and signs ("…해 달라"). You don't need to conjugate it any other way.

What's the difference between -다면서요? and -지요/-죠?

-지요? checks something you believe or know ("It's cold, isn't it?"). -다면서요? checks something you heard from someone else. The source is the whole point. You'll meet -지요 again among the nuance endings in Lesson 5.

If contractions still make you freeze mid-sentence, fall back on the full -다고 해요 form. It's always correct, and the contractions will come with listening. Most learners understand -대요 months before they produce it smoothly.

Hearsay is one way to talk about things you're not sure of. The other is guessing from what you see. In Lesson 4: Guessing & Inferring you'll learn Korean's "how sure am I?" scale: -(으)ㄴ/는 것 같다, -나 보다, -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다, -는 모양이다 and -(으)ㄹ 테니까.
