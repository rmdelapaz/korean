🇰🇷 Lesson 11: Embedded Questions & Choices

You're standing at a bus stop in a city you don't know. You could ask a stranger 이 버스 시청 가요? — "Does this bus go to City Hall?" It's correct, and people will answer. But listen to how Koreans themselves ask:

KoreanEnglish
저기요, 혹시 이 버스가 시청에 가는지 아세요?Excuse me, do you happen to know whether this bus goes to City Hall?

The question has been folded inside another sentence: "Do you know whether this bus goes to City Hall?" That folding is gentler, more polite, and absolutely everywhere in Korean — at information desks, on the phone, in emails, in your own head (I wonder what I should eat…). This lesson teaches you how to fold questions with -(으)ㄴ/는지 and -(으)ㄹ지, and then how to offer and make choices: -거나, -든지, N(이)나 and 아무 N(이)나. Our theme: asking for information.

🧠 The Mental Model: A Question in a Pocket

Think of -지 as a pocket that holds a question. You take any question — 어디 살아요?, 비싸요?, 왜 늦었어요? — replace its ending with the right -지 form, and slip the pocket into a bigger sentence whose verb is about knowing, asking or wondering.

💡 The Formula

[question clause + -(으)ㄴ/는지] + 알다 / 모르다 / 궁금하다 / 물어보다 …

어디 살아요? → 민수 씨가 어디 사는지 알아요? — "Do you know where Minsu lives?"

비싸요? → 그 호텔이 비싼지 모르겠어요. — "I'm not sure whether that hotel is expensive."

The shape of -지 comes straight from the noun modifiers you already know: verbs take -는, adjectives take -(으)ㄴ, and the future/uncertain version takes -(으)ㄹ.

TypePresentPastFuture / uncertain
Verb-는지: 가는지, 먹는지, 사는지, 만드는지-았/었는지: 갔는지, 먹었는지-(으)ㄹ지: 갈지, 먹을지, 살지
Adjective-(으)ㄴ지: 비싼지, 좋은지, 추운지, 먼지-았/었는지: 비쌌는지, 추웠는지-(으)ㄹ지: 비쌀지, 추울지
이다인지: 학생인지, 누구인지 (누군지)이었는지 / 였는지일지
있다 / 없다있는지, 없는지있었는지있을지

Watch the usual suspects. ㄹ-stems drop ㄹ before ㄴ: 살다 → 사는지, 멀다 → 먼지, 길다 → 긴지. ㅎ-adjectives lose ㅎ: 어떻다 → 어떤지, 그렇다 → 그런지. ㅂ-adjectives change: 춥다 → 추운지, 어렵다 → 어려운지. And in speech, 뭐인지 → 뭔지, 누구인지 → 누군지.

🔍 Two Kinds of Pocket: "What/Where…" and "Whether…"

If the original question had a question word (어디, 뭐, 언제, 왜, 누구, 얼마, 어떻게), the pocket means "where / what / why …". If it was a yes/no question, the pocket means "whether …".

Original questionEmbeddedEnglish
회의가 몇 시에 시작해요?회의가 몇 시에 시작하는지 알려 주세요.Please let me know what time the meeting starts.
이 근처에 약국이 있어요?이 근처에 약국이 있는지 좀 알아봐 주시겠어요?Could you check whether there's a pharmacy near here?
왜 늦었어요?선생님이 제가 왜 늦었는지 물어보셨어요.The teacher asked me why I was late.
그 사람 누구예요?아까 인사한 사람이 누군지 기억이 안 나요.I can't remember who that person I greeted earlier was.
부산 날씨가 어때요?이번 주말에 부산 날씨가 어떨지 궁금해요.I wonder what the weather in Busan will be like this weekend.

To make "whether or not" explicit, put both halves side by side:

PatternKoreanEnglish
-는지 안 -는지나가기 전에 문이 잠겼는지 안 잠겼는지 확인하세요.Check whether the door is locked or not before you go out.
N인지 아닌지이 정보가 사실인지 아닌지 알아봐야 해요.We need to find out whether this information is true or not.
-(으)ㄹ지 말지모임에 갈지 말지 아직 못 정했어요.I still haven't decided whether to go to the gathering or not.

The verbs that hold the pocket

The most common partners are 알다, 모르다, 궁금하다, 물어보다, 알아보다 (look into), 확인하다 (check), 기억하다 / 기억이 나다, 알려 주다, 고민하다 / 고민이다 (agonise over) and 정하다 / 결정하다 (decide). A particle can follow the pocket (어디 사는지를 물어봤어요, 뭐가 문제인지가 궁금해요), but in speech it's usually dropped.

🌏 Culture Note — 혹시 and the indirect request: Korean politeness often works by not asking directly. Compare the ladder: 화장실 어디예요? → 화장실이 어디에 있는지 아세요? → 혹시 화장실이 어디에 있는지 아세요? Each step adds distance and makes it easier for the other person to say "I don't know". 혹시 ("by any chance") is the magic softener; 실례지만 ("excuse me, but") opens the conversation with a stranger. The polite "no" in return is 잘 모르겠어요. 저도 여기 처음이라서요.

🔮 -(으)ㄹ지: Questions About the Future and Decisions

Use -(으)ㄹ지 when the question is about something that hasn't happened yet, or about a choice you still have to make. It's the ending of indecision:

KoreanEnglish
내일 비가 올지 모르겠어요.I don't know whether it'll rain tomorrow.
면접 때 뭘 입을지 며칠째 고민 중이에요.I've been agonising for days over what to wear to the interview.
휴가 때 제주도에 갈지 부산에 갈지 아직 못 정했어요.I haven't decided yet whether to go to Jeju or Busan for my holiday.
이 계획이 잘될지 걱정이에요.I'm worried about whether this plan will work out.

⚠️ -(으)ㄹ지 모르다 vs -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다

In Lesson 4 you learned -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다, "might": 비가 올지도 몰라요 = "It might rain." That 도 makes it a guess, and it's the safe way to say "might".

Without 도, 비가 올지 모르겠어요 = "I don't know whether it will rain" — a statement about your knowledge. Honest caveat: in natural speech the 도 is sometimes dropped when the meaning is "might", especially before -(으)니까: 비가 올지 모르니까 우산 챙기세요 ("It might rain, so take an umbrella"). Context decides; when you mean "might", keep the 도.

😲 Two Special Uses You'll Hear Daily

1. 얼마나 -는지 몰라요: "You have no idea how…!"

Literally "I don't know how much it is…", but it actually means the opposite: it's incredibly so. It's one of the most expressive patterns in everyday Korean.

KoreanEnglish
이 집 떡볶이가 얼마나 매운지 몰라요.You wouldn't believe how spicy this place's tteokbokki is.
연락이 안 돼서 얼마나 걱정했는지 몰라요.I couldn't reach you — you have no idea how worried I was.
가을 하늘이 얼마나 예쁜지 몰라요.The autumn sky is so incredibly beautiful.

2. -는지 as a guessed cause

A -지 clause can also sit at the start of a sentence as the speaker's guess about the reason for what follows: "maybe because…, I guess".

KoreanEnglish
감기에 걸렸는지 아침부터 목이 아파요.I must have caught a cold — my throat's been sore since this morning.
뭘 잘못 먹었는지 배가 계속 아파요.I must have eaten something bad; my stomach keeps hurting.
피곤했는지 아이가 차에서 바로 잠들었어요.The child must have been tired — they fell asleep in the car right away.

🔀 Choices I: "Or" — N(이)나, -거나, 또는, 아니면

English has one "or". Korean picks a different word depending on what it's connecting:

FormJoinsKoreanEnglish
N(이)나two nouns버스나 지하철을 타세요.Take the bus or the subway.
-거나two verbs / clauses주말에는 집에서 쉬거나 친구를 만나요.At weekends I rest at home or meet friends.
또는nouns or clauses, formal / written여권 또는 외국인등록증을 가져오십시오.Please bring your passport or residence card.
아니면two sentences; A-or-B questions커피 드릴까요, 아니면 차 드릴까요?Would you like coffee, or would you like tea?

Two points trip learners up. First, when you ask someone to choose between two options, use 아니면 between two full questions, not -거나 — -거나 describes alternatives, it doesn't ask for a decision. Second, -거나 하다 means "do things like…": 심심하면 게임을 하거나 해요 ("When I'm bored I play games or something").

The other two jobs of (이)나

UseKoreanEnglish
"…or something" (softens an offer)우리 잠깐 커피나 한잔할까요?Shall we grab a coffee or something?
"as much as…!" (surprise at a large amount)병원에서 두 시간이나 기다렸어요.I waited at the hospital for a whole two hours!

🎲 Choices II: -든지 — "Whichever, Whatever"

-든지 (also -든가, and short -든) presents options as equally acceptable — the speaker doesn't mind which. That makes it perfect for offering freedom, and for "no matter what/where/who".

UseKoreanEnglish
Either… or… (your choice)전화하시든지 문자를 보내시든지 편하신 방법으로 연락 주세요.Call or text — contact us whichever way suits you.
Do it or don't (indifferent, a bit blunt)가든지 말든지 네가 알아서 해.Go or don't — it's up to you.
Question word + -든지 = "-ever"무슨 일을 하든지 최선을 다하세요.Whatever you do, do your best.
Question word + 든지궁금한 게 있으면 언제든지 물어보세요.If you have any questions, ask any time.

Handy set words: 언제든지 (any time), 어디든지 (anywhere), 누구든지 (anyone), 뭐든지 (anything).

⚠️ -든지 vs -던지 (spelling!)

Choice is always spelled with 든. -던지 is a different ending built on the retrospective -더- (Lesson 17), as in 얼마나 춥던지 ("it was so cold that…"). Native writers mix these up all the time online, and it's a classic correction in Korean spelling guides. Write 가든지 말든지, never ✗ 가던지 말던지.

🤷 아무 N(이)나: "Any N at All"

아무 means "any (at all), no particular one". With (이)나 it means "any … you like"; with 도 plus a negative it means "no … at all" (nobody, nothing, nowhere):

Positive: 아무 N(이)나EnglishNegative: 아무 N도 + 안/못/없다English
아무거나 괜찮아요.Anything's fine.아무것도 안 먹었어요.I didn't eat anything.
아무 데나 앉으세요.Sit anywhere you like.주말에 아무 데도 안 갔어요.I didn't go anywhere at the weekend.
아무 때나 오세요.Come any time.교실에 아무도 없어요.There's nobody in the classroom.
아무나 들어오세요.Anyone, come on in.아무도 몰라요.Nobody knows.

Note the spellings: 아무거나 (from 아무것이나) and 아무것도 are written as one word; 아무 데나, 아무 때나 have a space.

누구나 vs 아무나, 언제나 vs 아무 때나

FormMeaningKoreanEnglish
누구나everyone, without exception (welcoming)이 강좌는 누구나 들을 수 있어요.Anyone can take this course.
아무나just anyone, indiscriminately이 일은 아무나 할 수 있는 게 아니에요.Not just anybody can do this job.
언제나always (not "any time"!)그 식당은 언제나 사람이 많아요.That restaurant is always crowded.
아무 때나 / 언제든지any time (you like)언제든지 연락 주세요.Get in touch any time.
🌏 Culture Note — the "아무거나" problem: Ask a Korean friend 뭐 먹을래? and there's a good chance they'll answer 아무거나. ("Anything.") Then you suggest something and they say, "Hmm, not that…" This is such a well-known joke that some restaurants have put a dish called 아무거나 on the menu, and drinks companies have sold a drink by that name. If you want to be helpful rather than polite-but-vague, give a hint: 매운 것만 아니면 아무거나 괜찮아요 ("Anything's fine, as long as it's not spicy").

Notice that almost every one of Maria's questions is embedded: 언제까지 해야 하는지 알고 싶어서, 어디에서 만드는지 아세요?, 뭐뭐 필요한지도, 언제 시작하는지 알 수 있을까요? That's what makes her sound polite and fluent. The 뭐뭐 in 뭐뭐 필요한지 is a common spoken way of saying "which things (plural)".

Korean
한국 직장인들이 매일 하는 고민이 있어요. 바로 "오늘 점심은 뭘 먹을지"예요. 동료에게 뭘 먹고 싶은지 물어보면 대부분 "아무거나요."라고 대답해요. 그런데 막상 김치찌개를 먹자고 하면 "어제 먹었는데요."라는 대답이 돌아와요. 이렇게 쉽게 결정하지 못하는 것을 농담으로 '햄릿 증후군'이라고 불러요. 짜장면을 먹을지 짬뽕을 먹을지 고민하는 사람들을 위해 '짬짜면'이라는 메뉴도 생겼어요. 한 그릇에 짜장면과 짬뽕을 반씩 담아 줘요. 요즘은 점심 메뉴를 대신 골라 주는 앱도 있어요. 버튼만 누르면 아무 메뉴나 하나를 골라 주니까 고민할 필요가 없어요. 하지만 앱이 고른 메뉴가 마음에 안 들면 사람들은 또 버튼을 누른대요.
English translation

There's a dilemma Korean office workers face every day. It's "what to eat for lunch today". If you ask a colleague what they want to eat, most of them answer, "Anything." But when you actually suggest kimchi stew, the answer comes back: "I had that yesterday." Jokingly, this inability to make easy decisions is called "Hamlet syndrome". For people agonising over whether to eat jajangmyeon or jjamppong, a dish called jjamjjamyeon has even appeared: one bowl holds half jajangmyeon and half jjamppong. These days there are also apps that choose your lunch menu for you. You just press a button and it picks any dish at random, so there's no need to agonise. But apparently, if people don't like the dish the app picked, they press the button again.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 이 버스가 시청에 가요 알아요? → ✓ 이 버스가 시청에 가는지 알아요? (Fold the question with -지.)

✗ 어디 살는지, 회사가 멀은지 → ✓ 사는지, 먼지. (ㄹ drops before ㄴ.)

✗ 그 식당이 좋는지 → ✓ 좋은지. (Adjectives take -(으)ㄴ지, not -는지.)

✗ 교실에 아무나 없어요. → ✓ 교실에 아무도 없어요. ("Nobody / nothing / nowhere" = 아무 N도 + negative.)

✗ 커피거나 차 → ✓ 커피나 차. (Nouns take (이)나; -거나 is for verbs.)

✗ 가던지 말던지 → ✓ 가든지 말든지. (Choice = 든.)

✗ 언제나 오세요 (meaning "come any time") → ✓ 언제든지 오세요. (언제나 = always.)

Embedded questions can feel like a lot of machinery for something English does with one word. Give it a week of use and they'll start coming out on their own — every learner who now says 혹시 … 아세요? without thinking once had to build it piece by piece.

✏️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Fold the question

  1. 민수 씨가 어디 살아요? → 민수 씨가 어디 _____ 알아요? (Answer: 사는지)
  2. 이 가방 얼마예요? → 이 가방이 _____ 몰라요. (Answer: 얼마인지|얼만지)
  3. 그 식당 맛있어요? → 그 식당이 _____ 궁금해요. (Answer: 맛있는지)
  4. 왜 늦었어요? → 선생님이 제가 왜 _____ 물어보셨어요. (Answer: 늦었는지)
  5. 회사가 멀어요? → 회사가 얼마나 _____ 확인해 보세요. (Answer: 먼지)
  6. 오늘 부산 날씨가 어때요? → 오늘 부산 날씨가 _____ 알려 주세요. (Answer: 어떤지)
  7. 저 사람 누구예요? → 저 사람이 _____ 기억이 안 나요. (Answer: 누구인지|누군지)
  8. 요즘 서울 추워요? → 요즘 서울이 _____ 궁금해요. (Answer: 추운지)

Exercise 2 — Future, decisions and "whether or not"

  1. 내일 비가 _____ 모르겠어요. (오다, future) (Answer: 올지)
  2. 면접 때 뭘 _____ 고민이에요. (입다) (Answer: 입을지)
  3. 모임에 갈지 _____ 아직 못 정했어요. (Answer: 말지)
  4. 이 가방이 _____ 가짜인지 모르겠어요. (진짜) (Answer: 진짜인지)
  5. 이 정보가 사실인지 _____ 알아봐야 해요. (Answer: 아닌지)
  6. 비가 _____ 몰라요. 우산을 챙기세요. (오다 — "might", the unambiguous form) (Answer: 올지도)

Exercise 3 — "You have no idea how…" and guessed causes

  1. 이 떡볶이가 얼마나 _____ 몰라요! (맵다) (Answer: 매운지)
  2. 연락이 안 돼서 얼마나 _____ 몰라요. (걱정하다, past) (Answer: 걱정했는지)
  3. 가을 하늘이 얼마나 _____ 몰라요. (예쁘다) (Answer: 예쁜지)
  4. 감기에 _____ 아침부터 목이 아파요. (걸리다 — guessed cause, past) (Answer: 걸렸는지)

❓ Common Questions

Why not just ask the question directly?

You can, and among friends you usually will. But with strangers, staff and seniors, an embedded question (혹시 … 아세요?, … 알 수 있을까요?) gives the other person room to say they don't know, which is more considerate. It's also the only way to report what someone asked you: 선생님이 제가 왜 늦었는지 물어보셨어요.

Can I use -는지 with verbs other than know/ask/wonder?

Yes — any verb or phrase about finding out, checking, remembering, worrying or deciding: 알아보다, 확인하다, 기억하다, 조사하다, 걱정이다, 고민이다, 정하다. You'll also see it before 보다 in "let's see whether": 맛있는지 한번 먹어 볼게요 ("I'll taste it to see if it's good").

Are -든지, -든가 and -든 different?

They mean the same thing. -든지 is the neutral, standard choice for writing; -든가 feels a little more colloquial; -든 is the shortest and most casual (뭘 하든 상관없어). In question-word set phrases, 언제든지 and 뭐든지 are the most common, with 언제든, 뭐든 in casual speech.

What's the difference between 누구든지 and 누구나?

They're very close and often interchangeable: 누구든지 올 수 있어요 / 누구나 올 수 있어요 both mean "anyone can come". 누구나 leans toward "everyone, all people" (누구나 실수를 해요 — "everyone makes mistakes"), while 누구든지 stresses "no matter who". 아무나 is the one to be careful with: it can sound like "just any random person".

🌟 Going Further

1. The information email: Write a short, polite email (150–250 characters, 해요체) to a Korean language school asking about their courses. Use at least three embedded questions: when the course starts, how much it costs, whether there's a level test, and so on. Start with 안녕하세요. 한국어 수업에 대해 몇 가지 여쭤보고 싶어서 연락드려요.

2. Indecision diary: Write four sentences about decisions you're currently stuck on using -(으)ㄹ지 말지 or -(으)ㄹ지 -(으)ㄹ지 (e.g. 이번 방학에 한국에 갈지 말지 고민이에요).
