🇰🇷 Lesson 2: Reported Speech I: Passing On What People Said

Your phone buzzes while you're in class. Afterwards, your roommate tells you everything: the landlord called and said he's coming tomorrow, your friend asked whether you're free on Saturday, your mom told you to call her back. Half of adult conversation is passing on what someone else said, and at A2 you could only do it clumsily, with a direct quote or not at all. Since beginner level you've already used one piece of this grammar without knowing it: 한국어로 뭐라고 해요? ("How do you say it in Korean?"). That 라고 is reported speech. Now you'll learn the whole system.

🧠 The Mental Model: Plain Style + 고 + 하다

English reports speech by changing words around: "I'm busy" becomes "She said she was busy." Korean does something more mechanical and, once you see it, easier:

💡 The Formula

[What was said, in plain style] + 고 + 하다 (or another speech verb)

"바빠요." → 바쁘다 + 고 + 했어요 → 바쁘다고 했어요 — "(She) said she's busy."

The ending you put before 고 depends on the type of sentence that was said. There are only four types:

Statement → -다고 · Question → -냐고 · Suggestion → -자고 · Command → -(으)라고

Look at those four endings: -다, -냐, -자, -(으)라. You met three of them in Lesson 1 as 해라체/반말 endings (먹냐?, 먹자, 먹어라). Reported speech is basically "turn it into plain style, then add 고". The polite 요, the formal -습니다 and the listener-directed -(으)세요 all disappear, because you aren't talking to the original listener anymore.

✅ Two things that make Korean easier than English here

1. No tense backshift. English says "he said he was tired" even if he's still tired. Korean keeps the original tense: 민수가 "피곤해요" → 민수가 피곤하다고 했어요. Only if Minsu said "I was tired" (피곤했어요) do you use 피곤했다고.

2. One flexible main verb. 하다 covers "say", "ask", "suggest" and "tell someone to". Its tense shows when it was said: 했어요 (said), 해요 (says / they say), 할 거예요 (will say). In speech you'll also hear 그랬어요 in place of 했어요: 바쁘다고 그랬어요.

Look at the grandmother row. When the person you're quoting deserves respect, the honorific -(으)시- can go inside the quote (드신다고) and on the main verb (하셨어요, often with 께서). Plain 먹는다고 했어요 would be understandable, but it sounds careless about Grandma.

⚠️ The two classic traps

Verbs need -ㄴ/는다고 in the present. ✗ 먹다고 ✗ 가다고 → ✓ 먹는다고, ✓ 간다고. Adjectives never take it: ✗ 좋는다고 → ✓ 좋다고.

Nouns need (이)라고, not 다고. ✗ 학생다고 → ✓ 학생이라고. And 있다/없다 take plain -다고 (있다고), even though they sometimes behave like verbs elsewhere. (있는다고 exists only in the "stay" sense: 집에 있는다고 했어요, "He said he'll stay home.")

❓ 2. Questions: -냐고

For a reported question, take the question in plain style (-냐) and add 고. Today, the simplest and most common spoken form attaches -냐고 to everything. You'll also see the traditional written forms, where verbs (and 있다/없다) take -느냐고 and consonant-final adjectives take -으냐고. Both are correct. Use -냐고 in speech, and recognise -느냐고/-으냐고 in writing and formal speech.

🤝 3. Suggestions: -자고

Anything that meant "let's" (-아요/어요 used as a suggestion, -(으)ㅂ시다, -(으)ㄹ까요?, 반말 -자) is reported with -자고, attached straight to the stem. "Let's not" becomes -지 말자고.

OriginalReportedEnglish
민지: "같이 영화 봐요!"민지 씨가 같이 영화 보자고 했어요.Minji suggested we watch a movie together.
팀장님: "5분만 쉽시다."팀장님께서 5분만 쉬자고 하셨어요.The team leader suggested we take a five-minute break.

One subtlety: -(으)ㄹ까요? can be a real question about opinion ("Shall I…?" / "Do you think…?") and not a suggestion. Only report it with -자고 when it clearly meant "let's".

📣 4. Commands and Requests: -(으)라고

Orders, instructions and polite requests (-(으)세요, -(으)십시오, -아/어라, 반말 -아/어) all become -(으)라고: -라고 after a vowel or ㄹ-stem, -으라고 after a consonant. "Don't" becomes -지 말라고.

Notice what happens to -(으)세요: the -시- disappears, because it honoured the original listener, who is usually you. 쉬세요 becomes 쉬라고, not 쉬시라고. (The honorific stays only when the person being told to act deserves respect from your point of view. For example, 사장님께 먼저 가시라고 했어요 means "I told the boss to go ahead first.")

💡 What about -아/어 주세요?

Requests with 주다 ("please do it for me") have their own twist: they split into 달라고 and 주라고 depending on who benefits. That's the centrepiece of Lesson 3, so for now just recognise that 도와주세요 isn't reported as 도와주라고 in every case.

🔄 Shifting the Point of View

When you turn a direct quote into reported speech, words that point to the speaker, the listener, the place or the time may need to shift, just as in English.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. Keeping the polite ending inside the quote. ✗ 바빠요고 했어요 ✗ 바쁩니다고 → ✓ 바쁘다고 했어요. Everything inside becomes plain style.

2. Verb vs adjective. ✗ 먹다고 → ✓ 먹는다고. ✗ 크는다고 → ✓ 크다고.

3. Nouns. ✗ 학생다고 → ✓ 학생이라고. ✗ 의사이라고 → ✓ 의사라고.

4. "Don't" in reports. ✗ 가지 마라고 → ✓ 가지 말라고. The 말다 stem keeps its ㄹ here.

5. Backshifting like English. If she said "I'm tired", it's ✓ 피곤하다고 했어요, not ✗ 피곤했다고 했어요. That would mean she said she had been tired.

6. Mixing up 자고 and 라고. 가자고 했어요 = suggested going (together). 가라고 했어요 = told me to go. Check who is doing the action.

  2. 의사: "푹 쉬세요." → 의사 선생님이 푹 _____ 하셨어요. (Answer: 쉬라고)
  3. 선생님: "책을 읽으세요." → 선생님께서 책을 _____ 하셨어요. (Answer: 읽으라고)
  4. 친구: "술 마시지 맙시다." → 친구가 술을 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 마시지 말자고)
  5. 엄마: "늦게 들어오지 마." → 엄마가 늦게 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 들어오지 말라고)
  6. 코치: "천천히 걸으세요." → 코치가 천천히 _____ 했어요. (걷다) (Answer: 걸으라고)
  7. 요리사: "케이크를 만드세요." → 요리사가 케이크를 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 만들라고)
  8. 팀장님: "회의실로 오세요." → 팀장님께서 회의실로 _____ 하셨어요. (Answer: 오라고)

Exercise 4 — Tense, point of view and direct quotes

  1. 민수: "제가 할게요." → 민수 씨가 자기가 _____ 했어요. (promise) (Answer: 하겠다고)
  2. 수지: "어제 너무 피곤했어요." → 수지 씨가 어제 너무 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 피곤했다고)
  3. 하준: "그때는 학생이었어요." → 하준 씨가 그때는 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 학생이었다고)
  4. 아이가 "엄마!"_____ 불렀어요. (direct-quote marker) (Answer: 라고|하고)
  5. 사장님: "내일은 가게 문을 안 열 거예요." → 사장님께서 내일은 가게 문을 안 _____ 하셨어요. (Answer: 열 거라고)
  6. 제니: "한국 음식이 맛있어요." → 제니 씨가 한국 음식이 _____ 했어요. (Answer: 맛있다고)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension

  1. 점심시간에 전화가 _____ 통 왔어요. (Answer: 세)
  2. 내일 회의는 오후 _____시로 바뀌었어요. (Answer: 3)
  3. 팀장님께서 회의 자료를 _____까지 보내라고 하셨어요. (Answer: 오늘)
  4. 치과에서 목요일 예약을 _____로 바꿀 수 있냐고 물어봤어요. (Answer: 금요일)
  5. 수진 씨가 퇴근 후에 같이 _____을 먹자고 했어요. (Answer: 저녁)
  6. 수진 씨는 7시까지 _____ 앞에서 기다리겠다고 했어요. (Answer: 회사)

❓ Common Questions

Should I say -냐고 or -느냐고?

In conversation, -냐고 for everything is normal and accepted. -느냐고 (verbs, 있다/없다) and -(으)냐고 (adjectives: 좋으냐고) are the traditional forms. You'll see them in older textbooks, formal writing and news. Recognise them, but don't worry about producing them.

Why does 이다 become 이라고 and not 이다고?

Historically, the plain statement form of 이다 before a quotation became -(이)라. You can see the same 라 in the direct-quote marker 라고 and in 뭐라고?. Just treat N + (이)라고 as its own rule. After a vowel, 이 usually drops: 의사라고, 친구라고.

Can the main verb be something other than 하다?

Yes: 말하다, 묻다/물어보다, 부탁하다, 듣다 and more. Each fits certain quote types. 묻다 goes with -냐고, and -다고 들었어요 means "I heard that…". That's a big part of Lesson 3.

How do I report something someone said in 반말 or 하십시오체?

Exactly the same way. The original speech style disappears when you convert to plain style + 고. "밥 먹었어?", "밥 먹었어요?" and "식사하셨습니까?" all become 밥 먹었냐고 (or, respectfully, 식사하셨냐고). You only choose the politeness of your own main verb (했어/했어요/했습니다) for your current listener.

This is a lot of new machinery, and it won't all be automatic by tomorrow. That's normal. Reported speech usually takes a few weeks of noticing before it flows. Try reporting one thing someone said to you each day, and it will come.
