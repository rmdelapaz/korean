🇰🇷 Lesson 10: Turning Verbs into Nouns

It's the first week of January, and every Korean social feed is full of lists like this one:

새해 결심 (New Year's resolutions)English
1. 매일 만 보 걷기1. Walk 10,000 steps every day
2. 밤 열두 시 전에 자기2. Go to bed before midnight
3. 커피는 하루에 한 잔만 마시기3. Only one coffee a day
4. 한 달에 책 두 권 읽기4. Read two books a month

By March, the same people are writing notes to themselves that look like this: 운동 못 감. 너무 피곤했음. ("Didn't make it to the gym. Was too tired.") Two different noun-endings, two different moods — the hopeful plan and the settled fact. And in between, in ordinary conversation, people say 저는 일찍 자는 게 제일 어려워요. ("Going to bed early is the hardest thing for me.")

This lesson is about those three ways of turning a verb into a noun, plus two very useful frames built on them: -는 데 ("in doing…") and -는 편이다 ("tend to…"). Our theme is habits and lifestyle — perfect, because talking about habits means talking about actions as things.

🧠 The Mental Model: Why Korean Needs Noun-Makers

Korean particles — 이/가, 을/를, 은/는, 도, 에 — only attach to nouns. So whenever you want an action to be the subject or object of a sentence ("Swimming is fun", "I hate waking up early"), you must first package the verb as a noun. English does this with "-ing" or "to…"; Korean has three packages, each with its own personality:

Noun-makerPersonalityTypical homeExample
-는 것 (-는 거)The all-rounder: "the act / the fact of…"; carries tenseEveryday speech and writing요리하는 게 재미있어요.
-기The activity itself, often in general or not yet doneLists, rules, goals, and many fixed frames요리하기 쉬워요.
-(으)ㅁA settled fact, stated brieflyNotices, memos, forms, formal writing, text messages요리 수업 취소됨.

You have actually been using two of these for a long time. You met -는 것을 좋아해요 in the beginner tier, and every -기 전에, -기 때문에, -기 위해서 and -기로 하다 you've learned contains a -기 noun. This lesson simply opens the box and shows you what's inside.

📦 -는 것: The All-Rounder

-는 것 is just a noun modifier + 것 ("thing, fact"). Because you already know the modifiers, you already know how to put tense inside it:

💡 Formula: modifier + 것

Verb, present / habit: -는 것 → 걷는 것, 사는 것 (ㄹ drops), 만드는 것

Verb, completed: -(으)ㄴ 것 → 걸은 것, 산 것, 시작한 것

Verb, not yet / future: -(으)ㄹ 것 → 걸을 것, 살 것, 할 것

Adjective: -(으)ㄴ 것 → 건강한 것, 바쁜 것, 추운 것  ·  이다: 인 것

Speech contractions: 것이 → 게, 것을 → 걸, 것은 → 건, 것이에요 → 거예요

KoreanEnglish
제 취미는 주말마다 오래된 영화를 보는 거예요.My hobby is watching old movies every weekend.
혼자 여행하는 게 생각보다 훨씬 재미있어요.Travelling alone is much more fun than I thought.
저는 아침에 일찍 일어나는 걸 정말 싫어해요.I really hate getting up early in the morning.
제가 운동을 시작한 건 작년 봄이었어요.It was last spring that I started exercising.
나이가 드니까 건강한 게 제일 중요한 것 같아요.Now that I'm getting older, being healthy seems like the most important thing.
제가 전에 한국에서 산 거 알았어요?Did you know I used to live in Korea?

One small warning: 것 can also mean a plain thing. 오늘 할 게 너무 많아요 means "I have too many things to do today", not "doing is too much". Context always makes it clear, and the grammar is identical, so you don't need to worry about which is which — just enjoy that it works both ways.

🏷️ -기: The Activity Itself

-기 attaches straight to any stem, with no 받침 or irregular changes: 걷기, 자기, 듣기, 살기, 만들기. It names the activity as a general idea — the thing you could put on a to-do list — rather than a particular event with a time stamp. That's why it's the natural choice for resolutions, rules, instructions and headings:

손 씻기Washing handsPoster in a school bathroom

The -기 frames

More importantly, a whole family of expressions is built on -기. These are fixed partnerships: the frame chooses -기, and -는 것 usually sounds unnatural in them.

FrameMeaningKoreanEnglish
-기 쉽다 / 어렵다easy / hard to do이 단어는 발음하기 어려워요.This word is hard to pronounce.
-기 쉽다prone to, likely to (something bad)겨울에는 감기에 걸리기 쉬워요.You catch colds easily in winter.
-기 좋다 / 편하다good / convenient for doing이 동네는 조용해서 살기 좋아요.This neighbourhood is quiet, so it's a good place to live.
-기 힘들다tough to do퇴근하고 나서 요리하기 힘들어요.It's tough to cook after work.
-기(가) 싫다(I) don't feel like doing오늘은 비가 와서 나가기 싫어요.It's raining, so I don't feel like going out.
-기 시작하다start doing작년부터 요가를 배우기 시작했어요.I started learning yoga last year.
-기(를) 바라다hope that (formal wish)좋은 결과가 있기를 바랍니다.I hope you get a good result.

Notice the double life of -기 쉽다. With something you do on purpose, it means "easy to do" (이 가방은 들고 다니기 쉬워요 — "this bag is easy to carry around"). With something that happens to you, it means "prone to" (급하게 쓰면 실수하기 쉬워요 — "if you write in a hurry, you're likely to make mistakes"). English splits these into two expressions; Korean doesn't.

⚠️ 싫다 vs 싫어하다 (and 좋다 vs 좋아하다)

-기 싫다 is an adjective describing my feeling right now: 나가기 싫어요 ("I don't feel like going out"). For someone else, switch to the verb: 동생이 학교에 가기 싫어해요 ("My brother doesn't want to go to school"). For general likes and dislikes, the most common pattern in speech is -는 걸 좋아하다 / 싫어하다: 사람 많은 곳에 가는 걸 싫어해요 ("I dislike going to crowded places").

✍️ -(으)ㅁ: The Settled Fact

-(으)ㅁ is the most "written" of the three. It turns a whole clause into a compact fact, and it's the ending of choice for notices, forms, meeting notes, certificates — and, playfully, for casual text messages.

💡 Formula: -(으)ㅁ

Vowel stem + ㅁ: 가다 → 감, 하다 → 함, 자다 → 잠, 아프다 → 아픔

Consonant stem + 음: 먹다 → 먹음, 있다 → 있음, 없다 → 없음, 좋다 → 좋음

ㄹ stem: keep ㄹ, add ㅁ underneath: 살다 → 삶, 만들다 → 만듦, 알다 → 앎

Past: -았음/었음 → 갔음, 먹었음, 했음  ·  이다: 임 → 학생임

Irregulars wake up (vowel-initial ending): 춥다 → 추움, 듣다 → 들음, 낫다 → 나음

UseKoreanEnglish
Office notice내일 오전 회의 없음.No meeting tomorrow morning.
Class announcement오늘 수업은 10분 늦게 시작함.Today's class will start ten minutes late.
Certificate wording위 사람은 본 과정을 수료하였음을 증명함.This certifies that the above person has completed the course.
Formal writing그때 나는 그 소문이 사실임을 알았다.At that point I realised the rumour was true.

🌏 Culture Note — 음슴체, the "memo style": Korean internet users write whole posts in -음 endings (음슴체, "the -eum-seum style"): 어제 친구랑 부산 감. 바다 진짜 예뻤음. It started as a joke imitation of office memos and is now standard in online reviews, community posts and texts between friends. It sounds brisk and a little humorous. It is not for seniors, and it sounds odd out loud in face-to-face speech — say it and people will hear you "reading a memo".

⏱️ -는 데: "In Doing…" (Time, Money, Usefulness)

데 is a small noun meaning "place, case, situation". After a modifier it means "in / for doing X", and it pairs with a handful of verbs about cost, effort and benefit:

💡 Formula: V-는 데 + 걸리다 / 들다 / 쓰다 / 필요하다 / 좋다 / 도움이 되다

회사에 가다 + -는 데 + 한 시간 걸리다 → 회사에 가는 데 한 시간 걸려요. — "It takes an hour to get to work."

KoreanEnglish
집에서 회사까지 가는 데 지하철로 한 시간쯤 걸려요.It takes about an hour by subway to get from home to work.
작년에 이사하는 데 돈이 생각보다 많이 들었어요.Moving last year cost more than I expected.
저는 월급의 삼분의 일을 월세 내는 데 써요.I spend a third of my salary on paying rent.
자기 전에 따뜻한 물로 샤워하는 건 잠을 잘 자는 데 도움이 돼요.A warm shower before bed helps you sleep well.
한국 드라마는 듣기 실력을 키우는 데 좋아요.Korean dramas are good for building listening skills.

⚠️ -는 데 (with a space) vs -는데 (no space)

They sound identical, but they are different grammar. -는데 is the connector from Lesson 7 ("…and / …but / background"): 회사에 가는데 비가 왔어요 ("I was going to work and it rained"). -는 데 is modifier + noun: 회사에 가는 데 한 시간 걸려요. Quick test: if you can slip in 에 (가는 데에 한 시간…), it's the noun, so write the space.

📏 -는 편이다: "I Tend To…", "Fairly…"

편 means "side" (as in "on my side"). -는 편이다 says which side of a scale you fall on: "I'm on the early-sleeper side", "she's on the tall side". It is the Korean way to describe yourself without sounding absolute — very handy in a culture that values modesty.

💡 Formula: -는 편이다

Verb (habit): -는 편이다 → 저는 일찍 자는 편이에요.

Adjective: -(으)ㄴ 편이다 → 제 동생은 키가 큰 편이에요.

Past habit: -는 편이었다 → 어렸을 때는 책을 많이 읽는 편이었어요.

Verb + -(으)ㄴ 편이다: judges one finished event against expectation → 이번 시험은 잘 본 편이에요 ("I did fairly well on this exam").

KoreanEnglish
저는 매운 음식을 잘 먹는 편이에요.I'm pretty good with spicy food.
남편은 주말에도 일찍 일어나는 편이에요.My husband tends to get up early even at weekends.
제가 소리에 좀 예민한 편이라서 귀마개를 하고 자요.I'm a bit sensitive to noise, so I sleep with earplugs.
이 동네는 다른 동네보다 조용한 편이에요.This neighbourhood is fairly quiet compared with others.

Because -는 편이다 places you on a scale, there has to be a scale. With verbs it almost always needs a degree word — 잘, 많이, 자주, 일찍, 늦게, 빨리. 저는 밥을 먹는 편이에요 ("I tend to eat rice") sounds strange because everyone eats; 저는 밥을 빨리 먹는 편이에요 ("I'm a fast eater") is perfect. Pure facts with no degree can never take it: ✗ 저는 한국 사람인 편이에요.

Look at how the three shapes divide the work. In flowing conversation the speakers use -는 것 (하는 게, 걷는 건, 먹는 걸, 보는 것). The moment they write a goal list, they switch to -기 (걷기, 안 보기, 끊기). And the frames pick their own partner: 운동하기 싫어요, 시작하기 쉽잖아요, 잠드는 데 걸려요, 안 하는 편이라서.

English translation

These days the "miracle morning" is popular among Korean office workers. The miracle morning means getting up early, at dawn, and having some time of your own before going to work. In this time people read, exercise and plan their day. People who have tried it say that using the morning well changes the whole day. But it isn't easy for everyone. People who work until late at night find it hard to get up at dawn. Experts say that getting enough sleep is more important than forcing yourself to get up early, because cutting down on sleep actually makes it easy to damage your health. So these days, more and more people are looking for the time that suits them.

Spot the noun-makers: 갖는 것, 쓰는 것, 일어나는 것, 자는 것 (neutral facts), and two -기 frames, 일어나기 힘들어요 and 해치기 쉽기 때문이에요 — the second one is "prone to" -기 쉽다 wrapped inside -기 때문에!

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 겨울에는 감기에 걸리는 것 쉬워요. → ✓ 감기에 걸리기 쉬워요. (쉽다/어렵다 take -기.)

✗ 회사까지 가는데 한 시간 걸려요. → ✓ 가는 데 한 시간 걸려요. (Noun 데 — write the space.)

✗ 저는 한국 사람인 편이에요. → ✓ 저는 한국 사람이에요. (No scale, no 편.)

✗ 만들음, 살음 → ✓ 만듦, 삶. (ㄹ stays and ㅁ sits under it.)

✗ 동생이 학교에 가기 싫어요. → ✓ 동생이 학교에 가기 싫어해요. (싫다 is for your own feelings.)

✗ 합격하는 것을 바랍니다. → ✓ 합격하시기를 바랍니다. (바라다 takes -기를.)

✗ (to your manager, face to face) 보고서 다 했음. → ✓ 보고서 다 했습니다. (-음 is for notes and texts, not speech.)

If you find yourself hesitating between 먹는 거 and 먹기 in the middle of a sentence, that's a good sign, not a bad one: at B1 the hesitation is the learning. Every time you check which frame you're in, the choice becomes a little more automatic.

  6. 제 동생은 키가 _____ 편이에요. (크다) (Answer: 큰)

❓ Common Questions

Is -기를 좋아하다 wrong? My textbook had 수영하기를 좋아해요.

It isn't wrong. Both 수영하는 것을 좋아해요 and 수영하기를 좋아해요 are grammatical. In everyday speech, 수영하는 걸 좋아해요 is by far the most common; -기를 좋아하다 sounds slightly more written or textbook-like. Use -는 걸 in conversation and don't worry if you hear the other.

Can -기 ever carry the past tense?

Only inside a few frames. -았기 때문에 is very common (늦게 잤기 때문에 피곤해요), and you may meet -았기를 바라다 in writing (무사히 도착했기를 바랍니다, "I hope you arrived safely"). But in lists and in frames like -기 쉽다 or -기 시작하다, -기 has no tense of its own; the main verb carries it.

Is it 바라요 or 바래요?

The standard form of 바라다 is 바라요 (and 바랍니다). In speech and texts you'll constantly see and hear 바래요, which is technically nonstandard. In writing — especially formal writing and TOPIK — use 바라요 / 바랍니다.

When would I actually use -(으)ㅁ in my own Korean?

Three places: writing notices or meeting notes (회의 없음, 자료 제출 완료함), filling in forms and short reports, and — once you're comfortable — casual online posts or texts to friends in 음슴체. In formal essays you'll mostly meet it after verbs of knowing and reporting: 사실임을 알 수 있다, 중요함을 강조했다.

Is -는 편이다 just a way of being vague?

Partly, and that's the point. Korean speakers prefer not to make sweeping statements about themselves. 저는 요리를 잘해요 can sound like bragging; 저는 요리를 잘하는 편이에요 ("I'm a decent cook") sounds confident but modest. It's equally useful for gentle criticism: 가격이 좀 비싼 편이네요 ("It's on the pricey side").

🌟 Going Further

1. Lifestyle paragraph (TOPIK 쓰기 style): In 200–300 characters of 해요체, describe your daily habits. Use at least one -는 편이다, one -기 쉽다/어렵다, one -는 데 걸리다 and one -는 것. Example start: 저는 아침에 일찍 일어나는 편이에요…

2. Two notes, two moods: Write your own five resolutions in -기 style. Then write a three-line "diary note" about yesterday in -(으)ㅁ style (어제 운동 못 감…). Read both aloud and notice how different they feel.
