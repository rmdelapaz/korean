🇰🇷 Lesson 5: Endings That Carry Feeling

Many learners hit the same wall around B1. Their grammar is correct, their vocabulary is decent, and yet Korean friends' eyes glaze over slightly during conversation. The culprit is often one tiny habit: every sentence ends in plain -아요/어요. It rains. It's cold. I ate already. I'm busy. Correct, but flat, like a news ticker.

Listen to Koreans talk and you'll hear a constant sprinkle of other endings: 비가 오네요, 춥잖아요, 벌써 먹었거든요, 좀 바쁜데요… Each one takes the same fact and adds an attitude: surprise, "you know this", "let me explain", "hmm, well…". This lesson hands you those endings. Master even three of them and your Korean will instantly sound more like a person talking and less like a textbook reading itself aloud.

🧠 The Mental Model: Who Knows What?

Almost all of these endings answer one hidden question: what is the state of knowledge between you and the listener? Did you just find this out? Does the listener already know it? Are you telling them something new that explains things? Are you guessing?

-네요I'm noticing this right now"Oh, …!" / "Wow, …"비가 오네요.
-군요 / -구나I've just realised or learned this"Ah, so …" / "I see"비가 오는군요.
-잖아요You already know this"…, you know" / "Come on, …"비가 오잖아요.
-거든요You didn't know this; here's the background"…, you see"비가 오거든요.

All of these have 반말 versions: drop the 요 (오네, 오잖아, 오거든, 오는데, 오지, 올걸, 있을래), with -구나 as the casual partner of -군요. You learned 반말 in Lesson 1, so you'll hear these everywhere in dramas.

😮 -네요: "Oh, look!"

-네요 marks a fresh discovery through your own senses: you see, taste, hear or feel something and share your reaction. It invites the listener to react too, which makes it a great conversation starter.

💡 The Formula

Any verb or adjective stem + 네요 → 오네요, 맵네요, 잘하시네요

ㄹ-stems drop ㄹ: 멀다 → 머네요, 알다 → 아네요, 만들다 → 만드네요

Past: 왔네요, 늦었네요 · Nouns: 학생이네요, 친구네요

With inference -겠-: 힘들겠네요 ("That must be hard")

KoreanEnglish
와, 눈이 정말 많이 오네요.Wow, it's really snowing a lot.
이 김치 생각보다 안 맵네요.This kimchi is less spicy than I expected.
한국어를 정말 잘하시네요!Your Korean is really good!
벌써 열두 시가 됐네요. 시간이 빨리 가네요.It's already twelve. Time flies.
A: 여기 좀 추운 것 같아요. B: 그렇네요. 창문 닫을까요?A: It's a bit cold in here. B: You're right, it is. Shall I close the window?

Because -네요 is about perceiving, it sounds odd with facts you have always known. Nobody says 제 이름은 민수네요 unless they have amnesia.

💡 -군요 / -구나: "Ah, I see!"

-군요 marks a realisation: a new piece of information clicking into place. It overlaps with -네요, but it also works for things you learn from someone's explanation, not just from your senses.

💡 The Formula

Action verbs: -는군요 (가는군요, 먹는군요) · Adjectives: -군요 (예쁘군요, 춥군요)

Nouns: 학생이군요, 의사군요 · Past: 갔군요, 바빴군요

반말: -(는)구나: 가는구나, 예쁘구나, 그렇구나

KoreanEnglish
A: 저 다음 달에 결혼해요. B: 아, 그렇군요! 축하해요.A: I'm getting married next month. B: Oh, I see! Congratulations.
아, 민수 씨가 요즘 운동을 하는군요. 그래서 건강해 보였군요.Ah, so Minsu is working out these days. That's why he looked so healthy.
(반말, to a child) 벌써 숙제를 다 했구나! 잘했어.You've already finished your homework! Well done.
(to yourself) 아, 이 버튼을 누르는 거구나.Ah, so you press this button.

✅ Which one? -네요 or -군요

Situation-네요-군요 / -구나

An honest note: native usage of -군요 in polite speech varies by age and region. Many younger speakers rarely say -군요 in conversation apart from 그렇군요, but everyone uses -구나 in 반말 and in thinking aloud.

🤝 -잖아요: "…, you know"

-잖아요 is a contraction of -지 않아요 ("isn't it?"). It reminds the listener of something they already know, usually as a reason or a point in your favour. Your tone decides whether it sounds friendly or reproachful.

💡 The Formula

Stem + 잖아요 (no 는, no 으): 가잖아요, 예쁘잖아요, 있잖아요

Past: 했잖아요 · Nouns: 학생이잖아요, 친구잖아요

UseKoreanEnglish
Shared reason오늘은 일찍 자야 해요. 내일 시험이잖아요.I need to sleep early. The exam is tomorrow, remember.
Point in your favour민지 씨는 파란색이 잘 어울리잖아요.You know blue really suits you, Minji.
Mild reproach그러니까 제가 조심하라고 했잖아요!See, I told you to be careful!
Pointing something out저기 있잖아요, 빨간 간판!It's right there, see? The red sign!
Conversation opener (있잖아)있잖아, 나 어제 진짜 웃긴 일 있었어.You know what? Something really funny happened yesterday.

⚠️ Careful upwards

Because -잖아요 implies "you should know this", it can sound like you are correcting a senior. 부장님, 그건 어제 말씀드렸잖아요 may come across as "I already told you that!" With superiors, a softer choice is 어제 말씀드렸는데요… or 어제 말씀드린 것 같은데요.

📖 -거든요: "…, you see"

-거든요 is the opposite of -잖아요: it gives background the listener doesn't know. It usually answers an implied "why?", or sets up a request or story that comes next.

💡 The Formula

Stem + 거든요: 가거든요, 바쁘거든요, 있거든요

Past: 먹었거든요 · Nouns: 학생이거든요, 친구거든요

UseKoreanEnglish
Answering "why?"A: 왜 안 먹어요? B: 아까 많이 먹었거든요.A: Why aren't you eating? B: I ate a lot earlier, you see.
Setting up a request제가 다음 주에 이사하거든요. 그래서 좀 도와줄 수 있어요?I'm moving next week, you see. So could you give me a hand?
Setting up a story저 어제 명동에 갔거든요. 거기서 누구를 만났는지 알아요?So I went to Myeongdong yesterday. Guess who I ran into there?
Defiant (반말)나도 할 수 있거든!Excuse me, I can do it too!

✅ Which one? -잖아요 or -거든요

KoreanWhat the speaker assumes
내일 시험이잖아요."You know perfectly well the exam is tomorrow."
내일 시험이거든요."You may not know this, but I have an exam tomorrow."

If you're not sure the listener knows, -거든요 is the safer choice. Overusing it with seniors, though, can sound like you're making excuses, so a plain -아서요 (시험이 있어서요) is often the most humble reason.

Don't confuse the sentence-final -거든요 with the connecting ending -거든 ("if/when…, then do…"), as in 도착하거든 전화해. That one comes in Lesson 18.

🌫️ -(으)ㄴ/는데요: "Well…" and the Art of Trailing Off

You know -는데 as a connector ("…, and / but"). Put 요 on it and stop there, and you get a sentence that deliberately leaves the door open. The unspoken second half ("…so what do you think?" / "…so I can't") is the whole point.

💡 The Formula

Action verbs, 있다/없다: -는데요 (가는데요, 맛있는데요) · Adjectives: -(으)ㄴ데요 (바쁜데요, 좋은데요)

Nouns: -인데요 (민수인데요, 학생인데요) · Past: -았/었는데요 (갔는데요)

ㄹ-stems: 살다 → 사는데요, 멀다 → 먼데요

UseKoreanEnglish
Soft opening저, 이거 좀 여쭤보고 싶은데요…Um, I'd like to ask you something…
Phone / introducing yourself여보세요, 저 김민수인데요. 수진 씨 계세요?Hello, this is Kim Minsu. Is Sujin there?
Polite contradictionA: 이거 수진 씨 가방이에요? B: 아니요, 제 가방이 아닌데요.A: Is this your bag, Sujin? B: No, it's not mine, actually.
Soft refusalA: 오늘 저녁에 시간 있어요? B: 오늘은 좀 바쁜데요…A: Are you free this evening? B: Today's a bit busy, though…
Mild surprise or praise와, 생각보다 노래를 잘 부르는데요!Wow, you sing better than I expected!

The soft refusal is one of the most useful sentences in Korean life. Saying 바빠요 is a flat fact; saying 좀 바쁜데요… lets the other person understand the "no" without you having to say it.

🤲 -지요 / -죠: "…, right?" and "Of course!"

-지요, almost always shortened to -죠 in speech, assumes shared ground. Intonation does a lot of the work here.

UseKoreanEnglish
Confirming (rising ↗)내일 회의가 3시에 시작하죠?The meeting starts at 3 tomorrow, right?
Strong agreement (falling ↘)A: 떡볶이 좋아해요? B: 그럼요, 당연히 좋아하죠!A: Do you like tteokbokki? B: Of course I do!
Explaining the obvious매일 연습하니까 잘하죠.She practises every day, so of course she's good.
Polite suggestion이쪽으로 앉으시죠.Please, have a seat over here.
Hopeful assumption (-겠죠)지금 출발하면 괜찮겠죠?If we leave now it'll be fine, right?
Softened question죄송한데, 화장실이 어디죠?Sorry, where's the restroom again?

Formation is simple: stem + 지요/죠 (가죠, 좋죠), past 갔죠, nouns 학생이죠 / 친구죠. With nouns and question words, -죠 sounds gentler than -예요: 성함이 어떻게 되시죠? is a warm, professional way to ask someone's name.

🎯 -(으)ㄹ걸요: "I bet… / Probably, actually"

-(으)ㄹ걸요 offers the speaker's guess, often gently correcting the listener's assumption. It is said with a slight rise at the end, but it is a statement, not a question.

KoreanEnglish
A: 은행에 지금 가도 돼요? B: 지금은 벌써 문을 닫았을걸요.A: Can I go to the bank now? B: I think they'll have closed already.
그 식당은 월요일에 쉴걸요.I'm pretty sure that restaurant is closed on Mondays.
민수 씨는 아마 모를걸요.Minsu probably doesn't know.
저 사람 아마 학생일걸요.That person is probably a student.

Two restrictions: the guess is always the speaker's, and you cannot use it to ask a real question (use -(으)ㄹ까요? for that). Past guesses take -았/었을걸요.

⚠️ The other -(으)ㄹ걸: regret

Said with a falling tone, usually in 반말 or to yourself, -(으)ㄹ걸 means "I should have…": 아, 우산을 가져올걸. ("Ugh, I should have brought an umbrella.") The full form is -(으)ㄹ걸 그랬다: 좀 더 일찍 출발할걸 그랬어요. Context and tone keep the two apart: a guess about the world vs regret about your own past action.

🙋 -(으)ㄹ래요: "I'll… / Do you want to…?"

-(으)ㄹ래요 expresses will or preference: what I feel like doing, or what you feel like doing. It is friendly, casual-polite and very common among friends and colleagues of similar rank.

KoreanEnglish
A: 주말에 같이 등산 갈래요? B: 좋아요, 갈래요!A: Want to go hiking this weekend? B: Sure, I'm in!
저는 비빔밥 먹을래요.I'll have bibimbap.
피곤해서 오늘은 집에 있을래요.I'm tired, so I think I'll stay home today.
(to a senior) 뭐 드실래요?What would you like to have?

Subject rule: statements are about I, questions are about you. You cannot say *민수 씨가 갈래요 to mean "Minsu wants to go". With someone senior, ask with -(으)실래요? (드실래요?, 앉으실래요?). A flat refusal like 안 먹을래요 can sound childish or stubborn, so adults soften it: 저는 괜찮아요.

✅ Which one? -(으)ㄹ래요, -(으)ㄹ게요 or -(으)ㄹ까요?

Trace the knowledge: 잘랐네요 (Haeun notices it now), 있거든요 (news to her), 그렇군요 (she's just learned it), 좋아하잖아요 (she already knows), 그렇죠 (agreeing on shared ground), 있는데요… (trailing off), 잘할걸요 (her confident guess), 힘이 나네요 (his feeling right now), 마실래요 (her preference) and 살게요 (his promise).

🌏 Culture Note: 맞장구 (back-channelling) — Korean listeners keep up a steady stream of small responses called 맞장구: 네, 네… · 아, 그렇구나 · 진짜요? · 그러니까요! (“Exactly!”) · 그렇죠. Staying silent while someone talks can feel cold or disapproving. Many of today's endings are the raw material of good 맞장구, so practise reacting, not just answering.

📰 Reading: 톰의 한국어가 달라졌어요

Korean
제 친구 톰은 한국어를 3년 동안 공부했어요. 문법 시험은 항상 잘 보는데, 한국 사람하고 이야기하면 대화가 자꾸 끊어져요. 왜 그럴까요? 톰은 모든 문장을 '-아요/어요'로만 끝내거든요. 한국어에는 말하는 사람의 마음을 보여 주는 종결 어미가 많잖아요. 새로운 것을 발견했을 때는 '-네요', 이유를 설명할 때는 '-거든요', 상대방도 아는 이야기를 할 때는 '-잖아요'를 써요. 그래서 요즘 톰은 이 어미들을 하나씩 연습하고 있어요. 지난주에 톰이 제 요리를 먹고 "와, 진짜 맛있네요!"라고 했어요. 짧은 한마디였지만 정말 자연스러웠어요. 톰도 웃으면서 말했어요. "이제 한국어가 더 재미있어질 것 같은데요?"
English translation

My friend Tom has studied Korean for three years. He always does well on grammar tests, but when he talks with Korean people, the conversation keeps stalling. Why? Because Tom ends every sentence with plain -아요/어요. Korean, as you know, has lots of sentence endings that show the speaker's feelings. When you discover something new you use -네요, when you explain a reason you use -거든요, and when you talk about something the listener already knows you use -잖아요. So these days Tom is practising these endings one by one. Last week Tom tried my cooking and said, "Wow, this is really delicious!" It was a short remark, but it sounded completely natural. Tom smiled and said, "I think Korean is going to get even more fun now, don't you?"

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 여기서 역까지 멀네요. → ✓ 머네요. (ㄹ drops before 네.)

✗ 매일 가는잖아요. → ✓ 매일 가잖아요. (-잖아요 attaches straight to the stem.)

✗ 날씨가 예쁘는군요. → ✓ 예쁘군요. (-는군요 is for action verbs only.)

✗ 저 대학생거든요. → ✓ 저 대학생이거든요. (A noun ending in a consonant needs 이.)

✗ 민수 씨가 영화 볼래요. → ✓ 민수 씨가 영화 보고 싶대요. (-(으)ㄹ래요 statements are only about "I".)

✗ 내일 비가 올걸요? (as a genuine question) → ✓ 내일 비가 올까요? (-(으)ㄹ걸요 is your guess, not a question.)

✗ (telling your boss something new) 제가 어제 보고서를 보냈잖아요. → ✓ 어제 보고서를 보냈는데요… (-잖아요 implies they should already know.)

Don't expect to use all eight endings by the end of today. Native speakers acquire these through thousands of hours of listening, and many learners find they understand them long before they produce them. That gap is normal, and it closes. Pick two endings for this week, perhaps -네요 and -거든요, and use them on purpose until they feel like yours.

  4. 제가 다음 주에 _____. 그래서 좀 도와줄 수 있어요? (이사하다, background) (Answer: 이사하거든요)
  5. 그러니까 제가 조심하라고 _____! (말하다, past, reproach) (Answer: 말했잖아요)
  6. A: 왜 이렇게 한국 음식을 잘 알아요? B: 어머니가 한국 _____. (사람이다, background) (Answer: 사람이거든요)
  7. A: 내일 파티에 올 수 있어요? B: 미안해요. 내일 시험이 _____. (있다, your reason, new to them) (Answer: 있거든요)

Exercise 3 — -(으)ㄴ/는데요 or -지요/-죠?

  1. A: 이거 수진 씨 가방이에요? B: 아니요, 제 가방이 _____. (아니다, polite contradiction) (Answer: 아닌데요)
  2. (on the phone) 여보세요, 저 김민수_____. 수진 씨 계세요? (이다, -는데요) (Answer: 인데요)
  3. A: 오늘 저녁에 같이 영화 볼래요? B: 오늘은 좀 _____… (바쁘다, soft refusal) (Answer: 바쁜데요)
  4. 내일 회의가 3시에 _____? (시작하다, confirming, short form) (Answer: 시작하죠|시작하지요)
  5. A: 떡볶이 좋아해요? B: 그럼요, 당연히 _____! (좋아하다, agreement, short form) (Answer: 좋아하죠|좋아하지요)
  6. 와, 생각보다 노래를 잘 _____! (부르다, -는데요, praise) (Answer: 부르는데요)
  7. 지금 출발하면 _____? (괜찮다, -겠- + short form, "it'll be fine, right?") (Answer: 괜찮겠죠|괜찮겠지요)

Exercise 4 — -(으)ㄹ걸요 and -(으)ㄹ래요

  1. A: 은행에 지금 가도 돼요? B: 여섯 시니까 벌써 문을 _____. (닫다, past guess) (Answer: 닫았을걸요)
  2. A: 민수 씨도 그 영화 봤을까요? B: 아마 _____. 영화를 정말 좋아하거든요. (보다, past guess) (Answer: 봤을걸요)
  3. A: 그 식당 월요일에도 해요? B: 아니요, 월요일에는 _____. (쉬다, guess) (Answer: 쉴걸요)
  4. A: 뭐 드실래요? B: 저는 녹차 _____. (마시다, my choice) (Answer: 마실래요)
  5. A: 주말에 같이 등산 _____? (가다, asking a friend's wish) (Answer: 갈래요)
  6. (반말, to yourself) 아, 배고파. 아까 점심을 많이 _____. (먹다, "I should have") (Answer: 먹을걸)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension: 톰의 한국어가 달라졌어요

  1. 톰은 한국어를 _____ 동안 공부했어요. (Answer: 3년|삼 년)
  2. 한국 사람하고 이야기하면 대화가 자꾸 _____. (is cut off) (Answer: 끊어져요)
  3. 톰의 문제는 모든 문장을 '-아요/어요'로만 _____ 거였어요. (끝내다, modifier) (Answer: 끝내는)
  4. 이유를 설명할 때는 '-_____'를 써요. (the ending) (Answer: 거든요)
  5. 상대방도 아는 이야기를 할 때는 '-_____'를 써요. (the ending) (Answer: 잖아요)
  6. 톰은 요리를 먹고 "와, 진짜 _____!"라고 했어요. (Answer: 맛있네요)

❓ Common Questions

Is -군요 old-fashioned?

Not old-fashioned, but in polite speech it sounds a little formal or bookish, and you'll hear it more from older speakers, presenters and teachers. 그렇군요 is an exception that everyone uses. In 반말 and when talking to yourself, -구나 is completely everyday.

Can I use -잖아요 with my boss?

You can, carefully. For a reason that is genuinely shared and positive, it's fine: 오늘 금요일이잖아요! But whenever it could mean "you should have known that", switch to -는데요 or add a softener. Tone of voice matters as much as grammar here.

Why do Koreans answer the phone with -는데요?

Because the sentence isn't finished. 저 민수인데요… means "It's Minsu, and (I'm calling because…)". The -는데요 signals "there's more; I'm handing it over to you", which feels less abrupt than a flat 민수예요.

Can I stack these endings?

A few combinations are common: -겠네요 (inference + discovery: 힘들겠네요), -겠죠 (inference + shared assumption: 괜찮겠죠?), -았네요, -았잖아요, -았거든요 (past + anything). You can't stack two sentence-final attitudes like *-네잖아요.
