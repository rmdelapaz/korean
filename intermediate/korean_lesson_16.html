🇰🇷 Lesson 16: Causative Verbs

Ask any parent of a toddler to describe their evening and you'll hear a list of things done to and for a small person: I fed her, bathed her, put her in pajamas, read to her, and put her to sleep. In Korean, almost every verb in that sentence is a special kind of verb:

KoreanEnglish
저녁에 아기한테 이유식을 먹이고, 목욕을 시키고, 잠옷을 입히고, 책을 읽어 주고, 아홉 시에 재웠어요.In the evening I fed the baby her baby food, gave her a bath, put her pajamas on, read her a book, and put her to sleep at nine.

먹이다, 입히다 and 재우다 are causative verbs: "make or help someone do (or be) something". Last lesson, the passive took the doer away. The causative adds a new doer, the causer. Parents, teachers and bosses use these verbs all day, and learning them turns a whole domain of family life into natural Korean.

🧠 The Mental Model: Adding a Puppeteer

Picture the original sentence as a small scene: 아기가 자요 ("the baby sleeps"). The causative puts a puppeteer above the scene: 엄마가 아기를 재워요 ("Mom puts the baby to sleep"). The new causer becomes the subject, and everyone who was already in the scene moves down one slot.

✅ The Formula

stem + -이- / -히- / -리- / -기- / -우- / -구- / -추- + 다

Intransitive or adjective base → the old subject becomes the object (을/를):
아기가 자요 → 엄마가 아기를 재워요.

Transitive base → the old subject takes 에게/한테, and the object stays 을/를:
아이가 밥을 먹어요 → 아빠가 아이에게 밥을 먹여요.

Compare the passive: 문이 닫혔어요 has one participant fewer than 문을 닫았어요. The causative 재우다 has one participant more than 자다. Passive and causative move in opposite directions, even though they often share the same suffixes.

🧩 The Suffixes

As with the passive, causative forms are lexical. You learn them as words, and many verbs have no suffix causative at all (가다, 오다, 만나다, 배우다, 하다-verbs). There are more suffixes than for the passive, and some of them attach to adjectives too: 높다 → 높이다 "raise".

The -우- forms mostly hide an -이- as well: 자 + 이 + 우 → 재우다, 타 + 이 + 우 → 태우다, 서 + 이 + 우 → 세우다, 크 + 이 + 우 → 키우다. That's why the vowel changes. -구- is rare (달구다 and 일구다 "to cultivate land" are about the only ones you'll meet). One more irregular pair is worth knowing: 없다 → 없애다 "get rid of" (벌레를 없애다, 스트레스를 없애다).

🔁 Double Duty (Again): Is It Passive or Causative?

In Lesson 15 you saw that 보이다, 업히다, 안기다, 읽히다, 감기다, 씻기다 (and the look-alikes 들리다, 걸리다) can be either passive or causative. The test is simple: count the participants and look for 을/를.

SentenceReadingWhy
아기가 엄마 품에 안겼어요.Passive: "The baby was held in Mom's arms."The baby is the subject, and there's no object.
할머니가 아기를 엄마 품에 안겼어요.Causative: "Grandma put the baby in Mom's arms."A new causer, with the baby as 를 object.
아이가 아빠 등에 업혔어요.Passive: "The child rode on Dad's back."Subject only, no object.
엄마가 아기를 언니에게 업혔어요.Causative: "Mom had the older sister carry the baby on her back."Causer + object (아기를) + causee (언니에게).
이 책은 잘 안 읽혀요.Passive: "This book doesn't read easily."No causer, no object.
선생님이 학생들에게 소설을 읽혔어요.Causative: "The teacher had the students read a novel."Causer + causee + object.

💡 맞히다 or 맞추다?

These two are famous for confusing native speakers too. 맞히다 means "get it right / hit it": 정답을 맞히다, 과녁을 맞히다. 맞추다 means "fit, match, adjust or compare": 친구와 답을 맞춰 봤어요 ("I compared answers with a friend"), 시간을 맞추다, 퍼즐을 맞추다, 입을 맞추다. You'll hear 정답을 맞추다 all the time, but the standard form (and the one TOPIK expects) is 정답을 맞히다.

🧑‍🍼 Lexical Causative vs -게 하다

Any verb, even one with no suffix causative, can be made causative with -게 하다: stem + 게 하다. So many verbs have two causatives, and they don't mean quite the same thing.

KoreanEnglishWhat the causer does
엄마가 아이에게 옷을 입혔어요.Mom dressed the child.Direct: Mom's own hands put the clothes on.
엄마가 아이에게 옷을 입게 했어요.Mom had the child get dressed.Indirect: Mom told him (or let him), and the child did it himself.
간호사가 환자에게 약을 먹였어요.The nurse gave the patient the medicine.Direct, e.g. put it in his mouth, helped him swallow.
의사가 환자에게 약을 먹게 했어요.The doctor had the patient take the medicine.Indirect: an instruction.

This is a strong tendency, not an absolute rule. 선생님이 학생들에게 책을 읽혔어요 is a lexical causative, yet it's clearly indirect, because the students do the reading. The more physical the help, the more likely you'll hear the suffix form. The more it's an instruction or permission, the more likely -게 하다.

The many faces of -게 하다

MeaningKoreanEnglish
Make (require)아버지는 우리에게 매일 일기를 쓰게 하셨어요.My father made us write a diary every day.
Let (allow), often with -아 주다부모님이 친구 집에서 자고 오게 해 주셨어요.My parents let me sleep over at a friend's house.
Prevent / not allow의사 선생님이 아버지에게 술을 못 마시게 하셨어요.The doctor didn't allow my father to drink.
Prevent (fuller form)아이들이 밖에 나가지 못하게 했어요.We didn't let the children go outside.
Stronger, often emotional result: -게 만들다그 사람의 말투가 나를 화나게 만들었어요.His tone of voice made me angry.

The causee with -게 하다 is flexible: 아이에게 공부하게 했어요, 아이를 공부하게 했어요 and 아이가 공부하게 했어요 all occur. 에게/한테 and 을/를 are the most common in speech.

📋 시키다: "Have Someone Do It"

시키다 is the causative partner of 하다. It works in two ways:

UseKoreanEnglish
N을/를 시키다: tell someone to do a task형이 동생한테 심부름을 시켰어요.The big brother sent his little brother on an errand.
Order food오늘 저녁은 치킨 시켜 먹자.Let's order chicken for dinner tonight.
N + 시키다 (replacing 하다)엄마가 동생을 매일 한 시간씩 공부시켜요.Mom makes my little brother study for an hour every day.

⚠️ 소개시키다: The Causative Nobody Needs

친구 좀 소개시켜 줘 ("Introduce me to a friend") is everywhere in everyday speech. Strictly speaking, though, the person introducing is the speaker's friend, who isn't making anyone else do the introducing. Usage guides therefore call 소개시키다 an unnecessary causative and recommend 소개해 주다. The same goes for 교육시키다 when you teach someone yourself. Use -시키다 in speech if you like, because everybody does. In formal writing and on TOPIK, use 소개하다 and 교육하다.

💡 Not for Your Elders

먹이다, 입히다, 재우다 and 씻기다 describe caring for someone who depends on you: a child, a patient, a pet. Using them for your grandmother or a guest sounds as if you're treating them like a baby. For elders, use honorific and humble verbs: 할머니께 진지를 차려 드렸어요 ("I prepared a meal for Grandma"), 손님께 차를 대접했어요 ("I served the guest tea"). You'll study that vocabulary in Lesson 20.

Look at 신게 했어 against 신기다. Grandma didn't put Minho's shoes on (that would be 신겼어). She let him do it himself, and that's why the shoes ended up on the wrong feet. The choice of causative tells the whole story. Notice also 좌우가 바뀌다, a passive from last lesson.

🌏 Culture Note — "뭐 시켜요?": Among Korean parents, one of the most common questions is 애들 뭐 시켜요?, literally "What do you make your kids do?", meaning "What classes are your kids taking?" Typical answers are 피아노 시켜요, 태권도 시켜요, 수영 시켜요. The phrase reflects how central after-school academies (학원) are to Korean childhood, a system often discussed under the name 교육열 ("education fever"). Another causative runs through Korean family life: 먹이다. Grandparents measure love in how much they feed you, and "더 먹어" never really means "if you want".

  2. 의사 선생님이 아버지에게 담배를 _____ 하셨어요. (피우다 + the "prevent" pattern with 못) (Answer: 피우지 못하게|못 피우게)

  4. 형이 동생한테 편의점 심부름을 _____ (assign a task, past 해요) (Answer: 시켰어요)
  5. 그 영화는 전 세계 관객들을 _____ (감동하다 → causative, past 한다체) (Answer: 감동시켰다)
  6. 부모님을 _____ 싶지 않아서 열심히 준비했어요. (실망하다 → causative, -고) (Answer: 실망시키고)

Exercise 4 — Favours and double-duty verbs

  1. 휴대폰에 있는 가족사진 좀 _____ 주세요. (보다 → causative, -아/어) (Answer: 보여)
  2. 비가 많이 오니까 역까지 차로 _____ 줄게요. (타다 → causative, -아/어) (Answer: 태워)
  3. 내일 아침 여섯 시에 저 좀 _____ 줄래요? (깨다 → causative, -아/어) (Answer: 깨워)
  4. 할머니가 우는 아기를 엄마에게 _____ (안다 → causative "put in someone's arms", past 해요) (Answer: 안겼어요)
  5. 결과가 나오면 바로 _____ 드릴게요. (알다 → causative, -아/어) (Answer: 알려)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension

  1. 엄마는 6시 반에 일어나서 아이들을 _____ (the causative from the blog, present 해요) (Answer: 깨워요)
  2. 엄마는 큰애에게 옷을 스스로 _____ 해요. (입다 + the indirect causative) (Answer: 입게)
  3. 작은애는 아직 어려서 엄마가 옷을 _____ 해요. (입다 → suffix causative + "must") (Answer: 입혀야)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 엄마가 아기에게 재웠어요. → ✓ 엄마가 아기를 재웠어요. (Intransitive base: the causee takes 을/를.)

✗ 아이를 밥을 먹였어요. → ✓ 아이에게 밥을 먹였어요. (Transitive base: causee 에게, object 을/를.)

✗ 할머니께 밥을 먹였어요. → ✓ 할머니께 진지를 차려 드렸어요. (먹이다 treats the person as a dependant.)

✗ 저를 역까지 타 주세요. → ✓ 역까지 태워 주세요. (타다 is "ride", 태우다 is "give a ride".)

✗ 정답을 맞췄어요 (in formal writing) → ✓ 정답을 맞혔어요.

✗ 가이다, 만나이다 → these don't exist. (Use -게 하다 for verbs without a suffix causative: 가게 하다.)

Two lessons in a row of lexical verb lists is a lot. If you catch yourself mixing up 입혔어요 and 입었어요, take it as progress: you're noticing a difference that didn't exist for you a week ago. Put the family verbs (먹이다, 입히다, 씻기다, 재우다, 깨우다, 태우다) into your SRS reviews first. They are the high-frequency core.

❓ Common Questions

What's the difference between -게 하다 and -게 만들다?

-게 만들다 is stronger and more "forced", and it's often used for emotional or unintended results: 그 일이 나를 더 강하게 만들었다 ("That experience made me stronger"), 사람을 기다리게 만들다 ("keep someone waiting"). -게 하다 is the neutral, all-purpose form, and it covers "let" as well as "make".

Is 웃기다 a verb or an adjective?

Grammatically it's a verb (the causative of 웃다), so the plain written form is 웃긴다. In everyday use, though, it acts like an adjective meaning "funny": 그 사람 진짜 웃겨, 웃긴 영상. It can also mean "ridiculous": 웃기지 마! ("Don't be ridiculous!").

If a verb is both passive and causative, how do I know which one I'm hearing?

Look at the frame. Passive: the affected person or thing is the subject and there's no 을/를 object (아이가 업혔어요). Causative: there's an extra causer, and usually an 을/를 object (엄마가 아이를 업혔어요). Context almost always settles it.
