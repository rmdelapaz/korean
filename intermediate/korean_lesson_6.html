🇰🇷 Lesson 6: Reasons & Causes

You're twenty minutes late for work. Your team leader looks up. In English you have one word to start your explanation: because. Everything else (whose fault it was, whether it was bad luck, whether you were simply busy) has to be added with extra words.

Korean builds that attitude into the connector itself. "I overslept, and wham, I was late" (-는 바람에). "I was busy finishing the report, so I couldn't…" (-느라고). "Thanks to you…" (덕분에). "It's the weather's fault…" (탓에). "Seeing that it was on sale, I bought two" (-길래). At A2 you learned -아서 and -(으)니까, the neutral workhorses. This lesson gives you the specialist tools, and with them the grammar of excuses, explanations, gratitude and blame.

🧠 The Mental Model: What Kind of Story Is Your Reason?

Every reason connector answers two questions. What kind of cause was it? (a busy activity, a sudden accident, someone's help, someone's fault, something you noticed). What can come after it? (a command? a past result only? only your own action?). Get those two right and the rest is conjugation.

🏛️ -기 때문에 and N 때문에: The Emphatic "Because"

때문 means "reason" or "cause". -기 때문에 puts the reason in the spotlight, which makes it the natural choice in explanations, presentations and essays. Unlike -아서, it happily takes the past tense.

💡 The Formula

Verb/adjective stem + 기 때문에 → 가기 때문에, 바쁘기 때문에, 갔기 때문에

Noun + 때문에 → 비 때문에 ("because of the rain")

Noun + 이기 때문에 → 학생이기 때문에 ("because (someone) is a student")

At the end: 왜냐하면 … 기 때문이에요 / 때문입니다 ("That's because…")

KoreanEnglish
요즘 날씨가 춥기 때문에 감기에 걸린 사람이 많아요.Because it's cold these days, lots of people have caught colds.
그 사람은 학생이기 때문에 평일에는 바빠요.Because he's a student, he's busy on weekdays.
비 때문에 경기가 취소됐어요.The game was cancelled because of the rain.
미리 예약했기 때문에 기다리지 않았어요.We didn't wait, because we'd booked ahead.
제가 이 회사를 선택한 이유는 사람을 중요하게 생각하기 때문입니다.The reason I chose this company is that it values people.

⚠️ Two cautions

No commands or suggestions after it. 비가 오기 때문에 우산을 가져가세요 sounds unnatural. Use -(으)니까: 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요.

N 때문에 with a person often sounds like blame. 너 때문에 늦었어! = "I'm late because of you!" To give credit, use 덕분에 (below).

⏳ -느라고: "I Was Busy Doing…"

-느라고 (often shortened to -느라) says: my time and energy went into X, so Y suffered. It is the king of polite excuses, because it frames the problem as being busy with something, not as laziness.

💡 The Formula

Action verb stem + 느라고 → 공부하느라고, 보느라고, 준비하느라고

ㄹ-stems drop ㄹ: 놀다 → 노느라고, 만들다 → 만드느라고

No tense on -느라고, even for the past: 어제 공부하느라고 못 갔어요.

KoreanEnglish
어젯밤에 드라마 보느라고 잠을 못 잤어요.I didn't sleep last night because I was watching a drama.
요즘 이사 준비하느라고 정신이 없어요.I'm frantic these days getting ready to move.
친구하고 이야기하느라고 내릴 역을 지나쳤어요.I was busy talking with a friend and missed my stop.
친구들하고 노느라고 시간 가는 줄 몰랐어요.I was having so much fun with friends that I lost track of time.
보고서 쓰느라 점심도 못 먹었어요.I was writing a report, so I couldn't even eat lunch.

⚠️ The -느라고 rulebook

1. Action verbs only. *바쁘느라고 is impossible; use 바빠서.

2. Same subject in both clauses. The person who was busy is the person who couldn't.

3. No tense marker on it: *공부했느라고.

4. No commands or suggestions after it.

5. The result is usually negative or effortful: 못 하다, 늦다, 힘들다, 정신이 없다, 바쁘다.

One lovely exception to "negative": the set phrases of thanks for someone's effort. 오시느라 고생 많으셨어요. ("Thank you for coming all this way.") 준비하느라 수고하셨습니다. ("Thank you for all your work preparing this.") The result (hardship) is still effortful, but the sentence is a warm acknowledgement.

🌪️ -는 바람에: "And Then, Out of Nowhere…"

바람 is literally "wind". -는 바람에 describes a cause that blew in suddenly: an accident, a surprise, something you didn't plan. The result is almost always unwelcome and already happened.

💡 The Formula

Verb stem + 는 바람에, always -는, even for past events → 자는 바람에, 오는 바람에

ㄹ-stems: 밀다 → 미는 바람에, 열다 → 여는 바람에

The main clause carries the tense (usually past).

KoreanEnglish
늦잠을 자는 바람에 회사에 지각했어요.I overslept and ended up being late for work.
갑자기 비가 오는 바람에 옷이 다 젖었어요.It suddenly started raining and my clothes got soaked.
휴대폰이 고장 나는 바람에 연락을 못 드렸어요.My phone broke, so I couldn't get in touch with you.
옆 사람이 저를 미는 바람에 커피를 쏟았어요.The person next to me pushed me and I spilled my coffee.
버스를 잘못 타는 바람에 30분이나 늦었어요.I got on the wrong bus and was a whole 30 minutes late.

Unlike -느라고, the two clauses can have different subjects (옆 사람이 미는 바람에 제가 쏟았어요). Like -느라고, it takes no tense itself and cannot be followed by a command or suggestion. Adjectives don't take it.

🙏 덕분에 and 😤 탓에: Credit and Blame

These two are mirror images. 덕분 is "benefit, favour"; 탓 is "fault, blame". Both attach to nouns directly and to verbs through a modifier.

Form덕분에 (good)탓에 (bad)
Noun선생님 덕분에날씨 탓에
Verb, past (most common)친구가 도와준 덕분에늦게 잔 탓에
Verb, present / habit매일 운동하는 덕분에잠을 잘 못 자는 탓에
Adjective날씨가 좋은 덕분에길이 좁은 탓에
Sentence-final다 여러분 덕분입니다.다 제 탓이에요.
KoreanEnglish
친구가 도와준 덕분에 이사를 빨리 끝냈어요.Thanks to my friend's help, I finished moving quickly.
선생님 덕분에 시험에 합격했어요.I passed the exam thanks to my teacher.
어젯밤에 늦게 잔 탓에 하루 종일 피곤했어요.Because I went to bed late last night, I was tired all day.
제가 확인을 안 했어요. 다 제 탓이에요.I didn't check. It's all my fault.
남 탓하지 마세요.Don't blame other people.

덕분에 also lives on as a courtesy formula. When someone asks 잘 지내셨어요?, a classic reply is 네, 덕분에요. ("Yes, thanks to you."), even if the person did nothing in particular. It's a small bow in words.

In casual conversation, people often express blame with plain 때문에 (날씨 때문에); 탓에 sounds a little more formal and makes the blame explicit. 때문에 is neutral, 덕분에 is grateful, 탓에 points a finger.

👁️ -길래: "Seeing That…, I…"

-길래 explains why you did something by pointing to what you noticed or heard. It tells a tiny story: stimulus, then your reaction.

💡 The Formula

Any stem + 길래 → 하길래, 좋길래, 왔길래 (past)

Nouns: 세일이길래, 친구길래

Written equivalent: -기에 (날씨가 좋기에 산책을 나갔다)

KoreanEnglish
가게에서 세일을 하길래 두 개 샀어요.They were having a sale, so I bought two.
친구가 배고프다고 하길래 라면을 끓여 줬어요.My friend said he was hungry, so I made him some ramyeon.
날씨가 좋길래 잠깐 산책하고 왔어요.The weather was nice, so I went for a quick walk.
문이 열려 있길래 들어가 봤어요.The door was open, so I went in to have a look.

⚠️ -길래 restrictions

1. The main clause is the speaker's own action, usually in the past.

2. The first clause is normally something outside you: another person, the weather, a sign. For your own feelings, use -아서: 배가 고파서 라면을 먹었어요, not *제가 배고프길래.

3. No commands or suggestions after it.

-길래 has a second life in questions, where it expresses curiosity or disbelief about what could justify something: 도대체 무슨 일이길래 그렇게 웃어요? ("What on earth happened that you're laughing so much?") · 얼마나 맛있길래 줄이 이렇게 길어요? ("Just how good is it, that the line is this long?")

📺 (으)로 인해(서): The News Anchor's "Due To"

N(으)로 인해(서) is formal and written. You'll meet it in news, announcements and official notices, and you'll want it for TOPIK writing. Before a noun it becomes (으)로 인한.

KoreanEnglish
폭우로 인해 일부 도로가 통제되었습니다.Due to heavy rain, some roads have been closed.
공사로 인해 불편을 드려 죄송합니다.We apologise for the inconvenience caused by construction.
태풍으로 인한 피해가 컸다.The damage caused by the typhoon was severe.
스트레스로 인해 잠을 못 자는 사람이 늘고 있다.The number of people who can't sleep because of stress is growing.

Don't use it for everyday personal reasons (*늦잠으로 인해 늦었어); it would sound like you are reading your own news report.

🤷 -아서 그런지: "Maybe It's Because…"

Sometimes you notice a result and can only guess at the reason. -아서 그런지 (literally "whether it's so because…") hedges the cause, and it connects neatly with the guessing grammar from Lesson 4.

KoreanEnglish
날씨가 추워서 그런지 사람이 별로 없네요.Maybe because it's cold, there aren't many people around.
주말이라서 그런지 길이 많이 막혀요.Maybe because it's the weekend, the roads are really jammed.
어제 늦게 자서 그런지 머리가 아파요.My head hurts; maybe it's because I went to bed late.
A: 왜 이렇게 졸리지? B: 점심을 많이 먹어서 그런가 봐.A: Why am I so sleepy? B: Must be because you ate a big lunch.

Form it from the -아서 form you already know (추워서, 자서, 주말이라서) plus 그런지. The last example shows the related sentence-final -아서 그런가 보다: "it must be because…".

🌏 Culture Note: apologise first, explain second. In a Korean workplace, an explanation that comes before the apology can sound like 핑계 (an excuse). The usual order is 죄송합니다 first, then a short reason with -는 바람에 or -느라고, then what you'll do about it. People even flag it themselves: 핑계 같지만… ("This may sound like an excuse, but…"). Notice how Minho does exactly this with his team leader, while with his colleague 수아 he's more relaxed.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 요즘 바쁘느라고 연락을 못 했어요. → ✓ 요즘 바빠서 연락을 못 했어요. (-느라고 needs an action verb.)

✗ 숙제를 했느라고 못 갔어요. → ✓ 숙제를 하느라고 못 갔어요. (No tense on -느라고.)

✗ 늦잠을 잤는 바람에 늦었어요. → ✓ 늦잠을 자는 바람에 늦었어요. (Always -는 바람에.)

✗ 비가 오기 때문에 우산을 가져가세요. → ✓ 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요. (Commands need -(으)니까.)

✗ 선생님 탓에 합격했어요. → ✓ 선생님 덕분에 합격했어요. (탓 is for blame.)

✗ 제가 배고프길래 라면을 먹었어요. → ✓ 배가 고파서 라면을 먹었어요. (-길래's trigger is normally outside you.)

✗ 공부하느라고 같이 가요. → ✓ No -느라고 before a suggestion: 공부해야 하니까 다음에 같이 가요.

Seven "because" forms, each with its own rules, is a lot. If you catch yourself falling back on -아서 in conversation, that's fine: it is always understood. Growth at B1 often looks like this: you start noticing -느라고 and -는 바람에 in dramas weeks before you use them, and one day an excuse comes out of your mouth fully formed. Noticing is progress.

Exercise 3 — -길래, -아서 그런지 or (으)로 인해

❓ Common Questions

Is -느라고 always negative?

The result almost always involves something missed, delayed or effortful. The main exception is the thank-you formula (오시느라 고생하셨어요, 준비하느라 수고하셨어요), where you acknowledge the effort someone made. You won't hear *공부하느라고 합격했어요; for a good result use 덕분에 or -아서.

What's the difference between -기 때문에 and -아서?

Both give a cause. -아서 is light and neutral, and it can't take past tense (*갔어서). -기 때문에 emphasises the reason, takes the past (갔기 때문에) and sounds more deliberate, which is why it is popular in essays and presentations. In casual chat, -아서 is usually more natural.

Can -는 바람에 ever describe a good result?

Occasionally, for a pleasant surprise: 친구가 갑자기 한턱내는 바람에 공짜로 먹었어요 ("My friend suddenly treated everyone, so I ate for free"). Native judgements vary, and many speakers feel a slight "it happened to me" flavour even there. For exams and safe usage, treat it as the connector of unexpected, unwelcome events.

Is -길래 the same as -기에?

Yes in meaning. -기에 is the older, more written form (and appears in formal writing and news quotes); -길래 is its everyday spoken descendant. Both carry the same restrictions.

Why not just use 때문에 for everything?

You can be understood that way, but you'd lose a layer of meaning Koreans expect. 지하철 때문에 늦었어요 is fine; 지하철이 멈추는 바람에 늦었어요 adds "and it wasn't my fault, it just happened". That extra nuance is what makes an excuse sound sincere.
