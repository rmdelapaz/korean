🇰🇷 Lesson 17: Remembering & Witnessing: -더-

You've just come back from a weekend in Busan, and a friend asks how it was. In English you'd say "The sea was so blue, and the people were really friendly." A Korean speaker will very likely say:

KoreanEnglish
바다가 정말 파랗더라고요. 사람들도 친절하더라고요.The sea was really blue, I found. And the people were friendly, I noticed.

That little -더- doesn't really mean "was". It means "I saw this for myself back then, and I'm bringing it back to you." -더- is one of the hardest items at TOPIK level 4 because English has nothing like it. It's also one of the most useful for sounding natural when you share experiences, give reviews and tell stories. This lesson covers the whole -더- family: -더라고요, -던데요, the modifier -던, and the connectors -더니 and -았더니.

🧠 The Mental Model: Rewinding the Camera

Imagine you wore a body camera yesterday. -더- means: rewind to that moment and replay what the camera recorded. Three conditions come with it:

✅ The Three Conditions of -더-

1. You perceived it yourself. You saw, heard, tasted or felt it. It isn't hearsay (for that you have -대요, Lesson 3) and it isn't general knowledge.

2. The perceiving happened in the past. You're reporting from a memory. For something you're noticing right now, use -네요.

3. You took it in as an observer. The information came to you: something you noticed or found out, not something you deliberately did. This is where the famous first-person restriction comes from (below).

The tense before -더- is the tense at the moment you were watching, not the tense relative to now:

KoreanWhat the camera recordedEnglish
가게에 갔는데 직원들이 문을 닫더라고요.The action in progress: they were closing up as I watched.When I got to the shop, the staff were closing up.
가게에 갔는데 벌써 문을 닫았더라고요.The action already completed: the shop was already shut.When I got to the shop, it had already closed.
하늘을 보니까 곧 비가 오겠더라고요.What it looked like was coming.Looking at the sky, it seemed it would rain soon.

Formation is easy: -더- attaches straight to any stem, with no vowel harmony and no 받침 changes. 가더라고요, 먹더라고요, 춥더라고요, 만들더라고요. It also combines with -시-, -았- and -겠-: 가시더라고요, 갔더라고요, 가겠더라고요. With nouns, use 이더라고요 (학생이더라고요). After a vowel the 이 usually drops in speech: 의사더라고요.

⚠️ Don't Confuse -더라도 With -더-

-더라도 ("even if", Lesson 7) looks similar, but it's a separate ending with nothing to do with witnessing: 비가 오더라도 갈 거예요 ("I'll go even if it rains"). You'll see a similar look-alike next lesson: -았더라면 ("if … had") historically does contain -더-, but you can learn it as a whole unit.

👀 -더라고요: Sharing What You Found

-더라고요 is the conversational workhorse. It presents something you experienced as new information for the listener, and it invites them to react. It's the natural form for reviews, travel stories and reporting on people you've seen.

KoreanEnglish
새로 생긴 빵집에 가 봤는데 크루아상이 정말 맛있더라고요.I tried the new bakery, and the croissants were really good.
민수 씨를 오랜만에 봤는데 살이 많이 빠졌더라고요.I saw Minsu for the first time in ages, and he'd lost a lot of weight.
한국은 겨울에 생각보다 훨씬 춥더라고요.Winter in Korea turned out to be much colder than I expected.
회의실에 가 보니까 아무도 없더라고요.I went to the meeting room, and there was nobody there.
뉴스 보니까 내일부터 장마가 시작된다고 하더라고요.I saw on the news that the rainy season starts tomorrow.

The last example is interesting. A forecast is hearsay, so why is -더- allowed? Because -더- is attached to 하다: what you personally witnessed is the news saying it. -다고 하더라고요 ("I heard it said") is very common.

🙋 The First-Person Restriction, and Its Exceptions

Condition 3 (you take the information in as an observer) produces the most famous rule about -더-. Read this section carefully, because learners either ignore the rule or over-apply it.

🚫 The Rule

In a statement ending in -더- (-더라고요, -더라, -더군요, -던데요), the speaker can't be the subject of their own deliberate action.

✗ 저는 어제 도서관에 가더라고요. ✗ 제가 김치찌개를 만들던데요.

Why? You don't "observe" your own choices as news. You already knew you were going to the library. Just say 저는 어제 도서관에 갔어요.

💡 Exception 1: Your Own Feelings and Sensations

When the predicate describes your inner experience (an emotion, a bodily sensation, or how something struck you), first person is not just allowed, it's the natural subject. Your feelings are something you "take in".

저는 그 영화가 너무 슬프더라고요 — "I found that film so sad."

등산하고 나니까 다리가 아프더라고요 — "After the hike, my legs were aching."

저한테는 좀 맵더라고요 — "It was a bit spicy for me."

The mirror image: you can't directly perceive someone else's feelings, so ✗ 민수 씨가 슬프더라고요 sounds odd. Report what you could see instead: 민수 씨가 슬퍼하더라고요 ("Minsu was grieving") or 민수 씨가 슬퍼 보이더라고요 ("Minsu looked sad").

💡 Exception 2: Discovering Something About Yourself

When you see yourself from the outside, as something that happened without your deliberate control and that surprised you, first person becomes possible, because you really are an observer now:

꿈에서 제가 하늘을 날고 있더라고요 — "In my dream, I was flying."

사진을 보니까 제가 눈을 감고 있더라고요 — "Looking at the photo, I saw I had my eyes shut."

정신을 차려 보니 제가 병원에 누워 있더라고요 — "When I came to, I was lying in a hospital."

막상 해 보니까 제가 요리를 꽤 잘하더라고요 — "When I actually tried, it turned out I'm pretty good at cooking."

An honest note: linguists describe the exact edges of this rule in slightly different ways, and native speakers' judgments on borderline sentences vary. The core is solid, though. No -더- for your own deliberate actions. Yes for your feelings and sensations, and for surprising, uncontrolled discoveries about yourself. The restriction applies to sentence endings and to -더니. The modifier -던 is free of it: 제가 다니던 학교 ("the school I used to attend") is perfectly normal.

💭 -던데요: "I Noticed… (So?)"

-던데요 is -더- + -ㄴ데요 (Lesson 5). You report what you saw and leave the sentence open for the listener. That makes it perfect for gently disagreeing, offering an observation, or hinting.

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
A그 식당 비싸죠?That restaurant's expensive, right?
B아니요, 생각보다 싸던데요.No, it was cheaper than I expected, actually.
A민수 씨 오늘 안 왔죠?Minsu didn't come in today, did he?
B아까 사무실에 왔던데요?He was in the office earlier, though (I saw him).
A이 옷 좀 이상하지 않아요?Doesn't this outfit look a bit odd?
B저는 괜찮던데요.I thought it looked fine.

Notice 저는 괜찮던데요: first person, allowed, because it's your impression (Exception 1). In 반말 it's -던데: 아까 왔던데? The casual question 어떻던데? ("So how was it?") asks about the listener's experience. In questions, -더- points at what the listener witnessed.

🔗 The Modifier -던: -(으)ㄴ vs -던 vs -았던

You already know -(으)ㄴ as the past modifier for verbs (먹은 빵, "the bread I ate"). -던 and -았던 add the "rewind" feeling of -더-. Here's the three-way contrast with one verb:

FormKoreanEnglishMeaning
-(으)ㄴ먹은 빵the bread (I) ateA completed action, full stop.
-던먹던 빵the bread (I) was eatingInterrupted or unfinished: half-eaten bread left on the table.
-았던먹었던 빵the bread (I) ate (back then)Completed in the past and recalled: "that bread I had that time".
KoreanEnglishWhy this form
제가 마시던 커피 누가 버렸어요?Who threw away the coffee I was drinking?-던: unfinished
여기가 제가 매일 다니던 학교예요.This is the school I used to go to every day.-던: past habit
작년 여름에 갔던 바닷가에 다시 가고 싶어요.I want to go back to the beach we went to last summer.-았던: completed once, recalled
어제 산 책을 벌써 다 읽었어요.I already finished the book I bought yesterday.-(으)ㄴ: simple completed action
언니가 입던 옷을 물려받았어요.I got my sister's hand-me-downs (the clothes she used to wear).-던: worn repeatedly, used

With adjectives and 이다, -(으)ㄴ is present (예쁜 = "pretty"), so to talk about a past state you must use -던 or -았던: 그렇게 조용하던 / 조용했던 동네가 지금은 시끄러워요 ("The neighbourhood that used to be so quiet is noisy now"), 대학생이었던 시절 ("my university days"). For adjectives, -던 and -았던 are close to interchangeable. -았던 underlines a little more strongly that the state is over.

For habits, 자주 가던 곳 and 자주 갔던 곳 are both common, and native speakers often use them interchangeably. -던 paints the habit as an ongoing stretch of the past. -았던 looks back on it as finished. If you're unsure, remember the clear cases: unfinished → -던, one completed occasion recalled → -았던.

🎬 -더니 and -았더니: Stories With a Twist

These two connectors look almost the same but point in opposite directions. -더니 is about someone else you watched. -았더니 is about what you did and what you found as a result.

-더니: I watched A happen, then B

UseKoreanEnglish
Contrast or change아침에는 날씨가 맑더니 오후에는 비가 쏟아졌어요.It was clear in the morning, but in the afternoon it poured.
Cause → result민수 씨가 매일 연습하더니 결국 대회에서 1등을 했어요.Minsu practised every day, and in the end he won first prize.
One action, then another친구가 편지를 읽더니 갑자기 울기 시작했어요.My friend read the letter and suddenly started crying.

Restrictions: the first clause is normally about someone or something other than the speaker (✗ 제가 열심히 공부하더니 합격했어요). The two clauses usually share the same subject or topic. The second clause is a statement, never a command or suggestion.

-았더니: I did A, and found or got B

UseKoreanEnglish
Discovery집에 갔더니 아무도 없었어요.When I got home, nobody was there.
Result약을 먹었더니 열이 내렸어요.I took the medicine, and my fever went down.
Result오랜만에 운동했더니 온몸이 아파요.I exercised for the first time in ages, and now I ache all over.
Someone's response친구한테 물어봤더니 자기도 모른대요.I asked a friend, and she said she doesn't know either.

With -았더니, the first clause is normally the speaker's own action, or the viewpoint character's action in a story. The second clause is what that action led to or revealed.

-더니-았더니

Example민수가 창문을 열더니 소리를 질렀어요.
Minsu opened the window and shouted.		제가 창문을 열었더니 눈이 오고 있었어요.

You met a similar pattern at A2: 집에 가니까 아무도 없었어요. For discoveries, -(으)니까 and -았더니 overlap almost completely. -았더니 sounds a little more like storytelling, and it's the more natural choice for "I did X, and as a result Y" (약을 먹었더니 나았어요).

Every -더- in Eunji's lines is either something she observed (크더라고요, 예쁘더라고요, 많던데요), her own sensation (저한테는 좀 달더라고요, Exception 1), or a surprising discovery about herself (제가 눈을 감고 있더라고요, Exception 2). She never says ✗ 제가 카페에 들어가더라고요. For her own deliberate action she uses 들어갔더니, which is exactly the job of -았더니.

🌏 Culture Note — how do you know? Korean grammar keeps asking a question English leaves to context: how do you know this? Over this tier you've collected a whole toolkit for answering it. 비가 오더라고요 (I saw it), 비가 온대요 (I heard it), 비가 오나 봐요 (I infer it: people have umbrellas), 비가 오네요 (I'm noticing it right now), 비가 오잖아요 (you know it too). Choosing the right one isn't fussiness. It's part of being a reliable, polite speaker, and it's why -더라고요 is everywhere in Korean travel shows, food reviews and chats about coworkers.

📰 Reading: My Old Neighbourhood

A short personal essay in 한다체. Notice how -던, -았던, -더-, -더니 and -았더니 each do a different job in the memory.

수필
지난 주말, 10년 만에 어릴 때 살던 동네에 가 보았다. 내가 매일 다니던 초등학교는 그대로였지만, 친구들과 떡볶이를 사 먹던 분식집은 사라지고 그 자리에 카페가 들어서 있었다. 골목도 생각보다 훨씬 좁더라. 어릴 때는 그렇게 넓어 보이던 운동장이 지금 보니 작은 마당 같았다. 옛날 우리 집 앞에 갔더니 낯익은 할머니 한 분이 앉아 계셨다. 옆집 할머니였다. 할머니는 나를 한참 쳐다보시더니 "네가 그 꼬마구나!" 하며 웃으셨다. 할머니의 얼굴에는 주름이 많이 늘었지만 웃음소리는 예전 그대로였다.
English translation

Last weekend I went back to the neighbourhood where I lived as a child, for the first time in ten years. The elementary school I used to walk to every day was still the same, but the snack bar where my friends and I used to buy tteokbokki was gone, and a café stood in its place. The alleys were much narrower than I'd thought, too. The playground that had looked so huge when I was little seemed, now, like a small yard. When I went to the front of our old house, a familiar old woman was sitting there. It was the grandmother from next door. She looked at me for a long moment, then laughed: "You're that little kid!" Her face had many more wrinkles, but her laugh was just the same as before.

  5. A: 그 영화 재미없대요. B: 그래요? 저는 _____ (재미있다 — my impression) (Answer: 재미있던데요)

Exercise 4 — -더니 or -았더니?

  1. 아침에는 날씨가 _____ 오후에는 비가 왔어요. (맑다) (Answer: 맑더니)
  2. 민수 씨가 매일 _____ 결국 시험에 합격했어요. (연습하다 — I watched him do it) (Answer: 연습하더니)
  3. 제가 창문을 _____ 시원한 바람이 들어왔어요. (열다 — my own action) (Answer: 열었더니)
  4. 친구한테 _____ 자기도 모른대요. (물어보다 — my own action) (Answer: 물어봤더니)
  5. 동생이 편지를 _____ 갑자기 울기 시작했어요. (읽다 — I watched) (Answer: 읽더니)
  6. 오랜만에 등산을 _____ 다리가 너무 아파요. (하다 — my own action) (Answer: 했더니)
  7. 동생은 어릴 때 채소를 _____ 지금은 제일 좋아해요. (싫어하다 — observed change) (Answer: 싫어하더니)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension

  1. 글쓴이는 _____ 만에 어릴 때 살던 동네에 갔다. (Answer: 10년|십년)
  2. 친구들과 떡볶이를 사 먹던 분식집 자리에는 지금 _____가 있다. (Answer: 카페)
  3. 어릴 때 넓어 보이던 _____이 지금은 작은 마당 같았다. (Answer: 운동장)
  4. 옛날 집 앞에 _____ 옆집 할머니가 앉아 계셨다. (가다 — the connector used in the essay) (Answer: 갔더니)
  5. 할머니는 글쓴이를 한참 _____ 웃으셨다. (쳐다보다 — honorific + the connector used in the essay) (Answer: 쳐다보시더니)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 저는 주말에 친구를 만나더라고요. → ✓ 저는 주말에 친구를 만났어요. (No -더- for your own deliberate actions.)

✗ (only heard about it from a friend) 그 식당 맛있더라고요. → ✓ 그 식당 맛있대요. (-더- needs your own experience. Hearsay takes -대요.)

✗ 여자 친구가 기쁘더라고요. → ✓ 여자 친구가 기뻐하더라고요. (For other people's feelings, report what you could see.)

✗ 제가 열심히 공부하더니 합격했어요. → ✓ 열심히 공부했더니 합격했어요. (Your own action → -았더니.)

✗ 어렸을 때 예쁜 누나 → ✓ 어렸을 때 예뻤던 누나. (-(으)ㄴ on an adjective is present. A past state needs -던 or -았던.)

✗ Writing 맛있더라구요 in formal text → ✓ 맛있더라고요. (-구요 is only the spoken pronunciation.)

-더- is the item that makes even strong intermediate learners hesitate, and many plateau here for a while. That's normal, not a sign that you've hit your limit. The best way forward is listening: notice every -더라고요 in a drama or a YouTube vlog and ask "what did they see?" Before long the first-person rule will feel less like a rule and more like common sense.

❓ Common Questions

I hear "-더라구요" all the time. Is it wrong?

It's the normal spoken pronunciation, just as -고요 is often said -구요. In writing, including chat messages to anyone senior, spell it -더라고요.

How do I ask someone about their experience with -더-?

The everyday polite question is simply 어땠어요? Questions with -더- exist, but they're more limited: 어떻던가요? sounds rather formal or old-fashioned, while casual 어떻던데? and 재밌던데? are common between friends. In a question, -더- points at what the listener witnessed.

Can -더라고요 be used for things that are still true?

Yes. The perception is in the past, but the fact can still hold: 한국은 겨울이 춥더라고요 ("Korean winters are cold, I found"). You're sharing what you learned by experience, not claiming it has stopped being true.

Why is 제가 다니던 학교 fine if first person is restricted?

Because the restriction belongs to the sentence-final -더- endings (and -더니), which assert a witnessed fact. The modifier -던 only marks a past action as habitual or unfinished inside a noun phrase, so it works with any subject.
