🇰🇷 Lesson 22: Opinion & Argument

Sooner or later a Korean friend, colleague or teacher turns to you and asks 넌 어떻게 생각해? — about working from home, about plastic cups in cafés, about whether children should have smartphones. At A2 you could answer 좋아요 or 싫어요. At B2 you are expected to say what you think, why, give an example, concede a point to the other side and land on a conclusion. And at the end of the TOPIK II writing paper, question 54 asks you to do exactly that on paper, in 600–700 characters of 한다체.

This lesson gives you the building blocks for both: the spoken phrases for discussions and debates, the written connectors that hold an essay together, and a step-by-step plan for TOPIK writing questions 53 and 54.

🧠 The Mental Model: Four Bricks of an Argument

Whatever the language, a persuasive argument is built from the same four bricks. Korean simply gives each brick its own set of stock phrases, and examiners and listeners expect to hear them.

BrickJobTypical Korean signal
주장 (claim)What you think-다고 생각한다, -아야 한다
근거 (reason)Why you think it왜냐하면 … -기 때문이다, 첫째/둘째
예시 (example)Proof from real life예를 들어, 실제로
결론 (conclusion)Pull it together, say what should happen따라서, 결론적으로, -아야 할 것이다

One more brick makes an argument sound mature rather than stubborn: the concession (양보) — admitting the other side has a point before explaining why you still disagree. In Korean that is 물론 … -지만, or -(으)ㄴ/는 것은 사실이다. 그러나 …

🗣️ Stating an Opinion: From Soft to Firm

You already know -다고 from reported speech (Lessons 2–3). Opinions are simply reported speech about your own mind: 중요하다고 생각해요, 문제라고 생각해요, 줄여야 한다고 생각해요. What changes is how strongly you commit.

💡 Why Koreans Hedge So Much in Speech

In conversation, especially with someone older or higher-ranking, a bare 저는 반대합니다 can sound confrontational. Korean speakers often wrap opinions in 것 같아요 or -지 않을까요? so that the listener can disagree without anyone losing face. That's politeness, not uncertainty. But on paper the logic flips: an essay full of 것 같다 sounds as if the writer doesn't know what they think. Hedge in speech, commit in writing.

Every brick is there — claim, reason, concession, gentle challenge — plus 다만 ("only, however"), a softer "but" that adds a limitation rather than flatly contradicting.

Which one? The connectors learners mix up

PairThe difference
반면에 vs 그러나/하지만반면에 sets two sides side by side ("whereas"): 도시는 편리하다. 반면에 시골은 조용하다. It can't express "but, contrary to expectation": ✗ 비가 왔다. 반면에 소풍을 갔다. → ✓ 비가 왔다. 그러나 소풍을 갔다.
따라서 vs 그러므로 vs 그래서그래서 is everyday "so". 따라서 ("accordingly") and 그러므로 ("therefore") are written and formal; 그러므로 feels a little more like a logical deduction. In essays, 따라서 and 그러므로 are usually interchangeable.
게다가 vs 또한Both add a point. 게다가 ("on top of that") piles on something extra, often stronger, and is common in speech; 또한 is the neutral essay word. 게다가 is fine in essays too — just don't use it for every addition.
왜냐하면 … vs -기 때문에왜냐하면 opens a separate sentence and must close with -기 때문이다 (or -거든요 in speech). -기 때문에 joins the reason to the same sentence. Don't use both in one clause: ✗ 왜냐하면 비용이 크기 때문에 반대한다.

💡 -아야 한다 vs -아야 할 것이다

우리는 에너지를 아껴야 한다 — a direct statement of obligation: "We must save energy."

우리는 에너지를 아껴야 할 것이다 — the same obligation, framed as a considered conclusion: "We will need to / ought to save energy." It sounds measured and is a favourite for the last sentence of an essay. Use -아야 한다 in the body and save -아야 할 것이다 for the finish, so it keeps its weight.

📝 TOPIK II Writing: What Questions 51–54 Ask

The TOPIK II writing paper has four questions and 50 minutes. The first two test sentence-level accuracy; the last two test whether you can organise real written Korean. Questions 53 and 54 carry most of the writing score, and both must be written in 한다체.

QuestionTaskLengthPoints
51Fill two blanks in a practical text (notice, email, message) — match its styleone sentence per blank10
52Fill two blanks in an explanatory paragraph (usually 한다체)one sentence per blank10
53Describe a chart, graph or survey result200–300 characters30
54Opinion essay on a social topic, answering the guiding questions600–700 characters50

Answers to 53 and 54 are written on a 원고지 grid (manuscript paper): one character per square, spaces take a square, and each paragraph starts with one empty square. The character counts above include spaces. The official scoring looks at three broad areas — content and task completion (did you answer everything asked?), organisation (is it logically structured, with good connectors?) and language use (range and accuracy of grammar and vocabulary, appropriate written style). The exact marks inside each area are the examiners' business; your job is to hit all three.

Question 53: describe the data, don't judge it

Question 53 gives you a survey or trend chart. Your job is to report it objectively: who was surveyed, what the results were, and any causes or outlook the chart shows. Leave your own opinion out.

Question 54: plan before you write

Question 54 gives a short background statement and usually two or three guiding questions. The instructions tell you to write 600–700 characters and not to copy the prompt word for word — so rephrase the background in your own words. The single most reliable structure is one paragraph per guiding question, framed by an introduction and a conclusion. Spend five minutes on an outline; it saves you from running out of squares halfway through your argument.

그러나 스마트폰을 지나치게 사용하면 여러 가지 문제가 생긴다. 우선 늦은 밤까지 스마트폰을 보느라 잠이 부족해지고, 그 결과 수업 시간에 집중하기 어려워진다. 또한 온라인 대화가 늘어나는 반면에 친구나 가족과 직접 얼굴을 보며 이야기하는 시간은 줄어든다. 이는 청소년의 사회성 발달에 부정적인 영향을 미칠 수 있다. 게다가 익명성 때문에 사이버 폭력의 피해를 입는 경우도 적지 않다.
English translation

However, using a smartphone excessively causes several problems. First, teenagers lose sleep because they are on their phones until late at night, and as a result it becomes hard to concentrate in class. In addition, while online conversations increase, the time spent talking face to face with friends and family decreases. This can have a negative effect on teenagers' social development. On top of that, because of anonymity, quite a few suffer from cyberbullying.

그러나 turns from the positives of 본론 1, 우선 … 또한 … 게다가 orders three problems, and every sentence ends in 한다체. A matching conclusion might open: 따라서 스마트폰 사용을 무조건 막기보다는 올바르게 사용하는 방법을 가르쳐야 할 것이다.

✅ TOPIK 54 Checklist

☐ Every guiding question answered, each in its own paragraph

☐ Background rephrased, not copied from the prompt

☐ 한다체 from the first sentence to the last — no -요, no -습니다

☐ At least one connector at the start of each paragraph (그러나, 또한, 따라서…)

☐ A clear position in the conclusion (-아야 할 것이다)

☐ 600–700 characters including spaces; indent each paragraph by one square

🇰🇷 Culture note: Korean students practise structured argument in 논술 (essay writing), which some universities use in admissions, and in school 토론 (debate) classes. The expected shape — background, clear claim, reasons with examples, concession, conclusion — is exactly what TOPIK 54 rewards. In everyday conversation, though, Koreans usually signal disagreement indirectly, especially with seniors: 그것도 좋은데요… or 한번 더 생각해 보면 어떨까요? often means "I disagree."

📰 Reading: A Newspaper Column

칼럼

English translation

The four-day week: time to discuss it. Interest in the four-day working week has been growing recently as some companies introduce it on a trial basis. Supporters argue that when rest time increases, employees' satisfaction and work efficiency rise together. In fact, one company that introduced the system found that productivity did not fall. On the other hand, small and medium-sized businesses say that they are short of staff and find it hard to cut working days. ⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. 왜냐하면 with no ending to match. ✗ 왜냐하면 비용이 많이 든다. → ✓ 왜냐하면 비용이 많이 들기 때문이다.

2. 반면에 as a general "but". ✗ 열심히 공부했다. 반면에 시험에 떨어졌다. → ✓ 열심히 공부했다. 그러나 시험에 떨어졌다.

3. Speech habits in the essay. ✗ 제 생각에는 환경 문제가 심각한 것 같아요. → ✓ 환경 문제는 매우 심각하다.

4. Double hedging. ✗ 중요한 것 같다고 생각한다 ("I think it seems important"). Choose one: 중요하다고 생각한다.

5. Forgetting 이라고 after a noun. ✗ 스마트폰은 필수품다고 생각한다 → ✓ 필수품이라고 생각한다.

6. Unbalanced paragraphs in TOPIK 54. Spending 400 characters on guiding question ① leaves no room for ③. Plan the lengths first.

✏️ Practice

Exercise 1 — Turn it into an opinion with -다고

  1. 환경 보호가 중요하다 → 저는 환경 보호가 _____ 생각합니다. (Answer: 중요하다고)
  2. 정부가 지원을 늘려야 하다 → 저는 정부가 지원을 _____ 생각합니다. (Answer: 늘려야 한다고)
  3. 스마트폰은 필수품이다 → 나는 스마트폰이 _____ 생각한다. (Answer: 필수품이라고)
  4. 이 정책은 효과가 없다 → 나는 이 정책이 효과가 _____ 본다. (Answer: 없다고)
  5. 사람들이 운동을 충분히 하지 않다 → 요즘 사람들이 운동을 충분히 하지 _____ 생각한다. (Answer: 않는다고)
  6. 그 결정은 옳았다 → 나는 그 결정이 _____ 생각한다. (past) (Answer: 옳았다고)

Exercise 2 — Choose the connector

  1. 도시는 편리하다. _____ 시골은 조용하고 공기가 맑다. (whereas) (Answer: 반면에|반면)
  2. 운동은 건강에 좋다. _____ 스트레스를 줄이는 데에도 도움이 된다. (furthermore) (Answer: 또한|게다가)
  3. 출생률이 낮아지고 있다. _____ 학생 수도 줄고 있다. (therefore, formal) (Answer: 따라서|그러므로)
  4. 일회용품을 줄여야 한다. _____ 카페에서 개인 컵을 사용할 수 있다. (for example) (Answer: 예를 들어|예컨대)
  5. 나는 이 제도에 반대한다. _____ 비용이 너무 많이 들기 때문이다. (because — opens the sentence) (Answer: 왜냐하면)
  6. 1인 가구가 늘고 있다. _____ 혼자 사는 사람이 많아지고 있다는 뜻이다. (in other words) (Answer: 즉|다시 말해)
  7. _____ 이 문제를 해결하려면 우리 모두의 노력이 필요하다. (in conclusion) (Answer: 결론적으로|요컨대)

Exercise 3 — Essay grammar

  1. 문제를 해결하기 위해서는 정부와 시민이 함께 _____. (노력하다, formal conclusion "ought to") (Answer: 노력해야 할 것이다)
  2. 무엇보다 어릴 때부터 올바른 습관을 _____ 것이 중요하다. (기르다) (Answer: 기르는)
  3. 기술이 발전하면서 일부 일자리는 _____ 수밖에 없다. (to decrease) (Answer: 줄|줄어들|감소할)
  4. 온라인 수업이 편리한 것은 _____이다. 그러나 집중하기 어렵다는 단점도 있다. (fact — concession) (Answer: 사실)
  5. 수면 부족은 학업 성적에 나쁜 영향을 _____. (미치다, 한다체 present) (Answer: 미친다)
  6. 정부는 새로운 지원 방안을 마련할 _____ 있다. (need) (Answer: 필요가)

Exercise 4 — From conversation to essay style

  1. 제 생각에는 이 문제가 심각한 것 같아요. → 이 문제는 매우 _____. (no hedge, 한다체) (Answer: 심각하다)
  2. 그래서 우리가 에너지를 아껴야 돼요. → _____ 우리는 에너지를 아껴야 한다. (formal "therefore") (Answer: 따라서|그러므로)
  3. 사람들이 환경에 관심을 가져야 돼요. → 사람들은 환경에 관심을 _____. (한다체 "must") (Answer: 가져야 한다)
  4. 왜냐하면 돈이 너무 많이 들거든요. → 왜냐하면 비용이 너무 많이 _____. (들다 + -기 때문이다) (Answer: 들기 때문이다)
  5. 이런 문제를 어떻게 풀 수 있을까요? → 이러한 문제를 어떻게 해결할 수 _____? (written -는가 question) (Answer: 있는가)
  6. 작은 가게들은 사람이 부족하거든요. → 작은 가게들은 _____이 부족하다. (staff, workforce) (Answer: 인력)

Exercise 5 — Reading comprehension

  1. 일부 기업은 주 4일 근무제를 _____으로 도입했다. (on a trial basis) (Answer: 시범적)
  2. 찬성하는 쪽은 휴식이 늘면 만족도와 업무 _____이 높아진다고 주장한다. (Answer: 효율)
  3. 제도를 도입한 한 기업에서는 _____이 떨어지지 않았다. (Answer: 생산성)
  4. 중소기업들은 _____이 부족해서 일하는 날을 줄이기 어렵다고 말한다. (Answer: 인력)
  5. 글쓴이는 업종별 상황을 고려한 _____ 도입이 필요하다고 주장한다. (gradual) (Answer: 단계적인|단계적)
  6. 글쓴이에 따르면 중요한 것은 일하는 시간이 아니라 일하는 _____이다. (Answer: 방식)

❓ Common Questions

Can I write 나는 in a TOPIK essay?

Yes — 나는 (never 저는, which is a speech form) is acceptable in a 한다체 essay. Many good essays use it once or twice at most, because an opinion essay is already understood to be your opinion: 청소년의 스마트폰 사용을 무조건 막아서는 안 된다고 생각한다 needs no subject at all. 필자 ("the writer") is mainly for newspaper columns.

Is it bad to write fewer than 600 or more than 700 characters?

Stay inside the range. The answer grid only gives you roughly 700 squares, so running long is physically limited; the far more common problem is an unfinished essay that stops at 400 characters because the writer ran out of time. That's why the five-minute outline matters — and why many test-takers do question 54 before 53 to protect the bigger task. Try both orders in practice tests and see which suits you.

What if I have no strong opinion on the topic?

You don't need a sincere conviction — you need a clear, defensible one. Choose the side you can find the most reasons and examples for, even if you'd be more nuanced in real life. A balanced "both sides have merits, so a gradual approach is best" conclusion is fine too, as long as it is stated clearly.

Writing an argument in a second language is hard because you're juggling ideas, grammar and structure all at once — it's normal for your first practice essays to feel clumsy and take far too long. That's not a sign you've plateaued; it's what building a new skill feels like. Write one paragraph a day, and within a few weeks the connectors and sentence endings will start arriving on their own, leaving your attention free for the ideas.
