🇰🇷 Lesson 19: Degree, Extent & Near Misses

Korean conversation runs on friendly exaggeration. You're not just hungry, you're 배고파 죽겠어요 ("hungry enough to die"). The film wasn't funny, it was so funny I nearly died. The café isn't good, it's worth queuing for. This lesson gives you the grammar behind all of that: how to say how much, to what extent, what nearly happened, and what's worth doing — which also makes it the lesson for giving (and decoding) recommendations.

🧠 The Mental Model: A Measuring Kit

Think of these seven patterns as tools in a kit, each measuring something different:

ToolMeasuresKoreanEnglish
-(으)ㄹ수록a rising scale알면 알수록 재미있어요.The more you know, the more fun it is.
-(으)ㄹ 정도로how extreme눈물이 날 정도로 웃었어요.I laughed so much I cried.
-(으)ㄹ 만큼how much / in proportion먹을 만큼만 가져가세요.Take only as much as you'll eat.
-(으)ㄹ 뻔했다a near miss하마터면 넘어질 뻔했어요.I almost fell over.
-(으)ㄹ 만하다worth / good enough이 책은 읽을 만해요.This book is worth reading.
-다시피shared knowledge / "practically"아시다시피 요즘 바빠요.As you know, I'm busy these days.
-기는커녕"far from it"쉬기는커녕 잠도 못 잤어요.Far from resting, I couldn't even sleep.

Five of them are built on the future modifier -(으)ㄹ that you've used since A2 (갈 때, 먹을 거예요). That -(으)ㄹ means "not yet real / imagined", and you'll see why it suits most of these patterns.

📈 -(으)ㄹ수록: "The More…, The More…"

💡 The Formula

Stem + -(으)ㄹ수록 (written as one word): 갈수록, 먹을수록, 알수록 (알다), 더울수록 (덥다), 많을수록

Doubled for emphasis: -(으)면 -(으)ㄹ수록 → 보면 볼수록, 많으면 많을수록

No past tense before it — the final verb carries the tense.

KoreanEnglish
한국어는 배우면 배울수록 재미있어요.The more I learn Korean, the more fun it gets.
그 사람은 만날수록 좋은 사람인 것 같아요.The more I meet him, the more he seems like a good person.
날씨가 더울수록 물을 많이 마셔야 해요.The hotter it is, the more water you need to drink.
물건이 비쌀수록 무조건 좋은 건 아니에요.A pricier item isn't automatically a better one.
친구는 많으면 많을수록 좋다고 생각해요?Do you think the more friends, the better?
날씨가 갈수록 추워지네요.It's getting colder and colder.

갈수록 ("the further it goes") has become a fixed adverb meaning "as time goes on / increasingly". You'll hear it constantly: 갈수록 물가가 올라요 ("Prices keep going up"). It combines beautifully with -아/어지다 from Lesson 12.

🌡️ -(으)ㄹ 정도로: "So Much That…"

정도 is a noun meaning "degree, extent" — you already know it as "about" (30분 정도). With a modifier in front, it names the point something reached: "to the degree that I would cry". The -(으)ㄹ is there because the result is often imagined or exaggerated (you didn't literally die).

💡 The Formula

-(으)ㄹ 정도로 + verb/adjective: 눈물이 날 정도로 웃었어요

-(으)ㄹ 정도이다 (ending the sentence): 말도 못 할 정도예요

Negative: -(으)ㄹ 정도는 아니다 → "not to the point of…"

KoreanEnglish
다리가 아플 정도로 하루 종일 걸었어요.I walked all day, to the point that my legs hurt.
그 가게는 믿을 수 없을 정도로 싸요.That shop is unbelievably cheap.
어제 콘서트는 사람이 말도 못 할 정도로 많았어요.There were indescribably many people at the concert yesterday.
잠을 못 잘 정도로 걱정이 돼요.I'm so worried I can't sleep.
맛있긴 한데 한 시간씩 기다릴 정도는 아니에요.It's tasty, but not so good that it's worth waiting an hour.

⚖️ -(으)ㄹ 만큼 and -(으)ㄴ/는 만큼

You know 만큼 from comparisons: 형만큼 키가 커요 ("as tall as my brother"). After a verb modifier, 만큼 keeps that idea of matching amount. Which modifier you use changes the meaning:

FormMeaningKoreanEnglish
-(으)ㄹ 만큼as much as (would be needed) / to the extent that먹을 만큼만 가져가세요.Take only as much as you'll eat.
-(으)ㄹ 만큼(extreme degree, = 정도로)죽을 만큼 보고 싶었어.I missed you so much it hurt.
-(으)ㄴ 만큼 (past)in proportion to what was done노력한 만큼 결과가 나올 거예요.You'll get results in proportion to your effort.
-(으)ㄴ/는 만큼 (present)since…, correspondingly비싼 만큼 성능이 좋아요.It's expensive, but the performance matches.
N인 만큼since it is N…중요한 시험인 만큼 열심히 준비해야 해요.Since it's an important exam, you must prepare hard.

Which one? 정도로 or 만큼

Situation정도로만큼
Extreme degree (exaggeration)✓ 배가 터질 정도로 먹었어요.✓ 배가 터질 만큼 먹었어요. (same meaning)
A needed amount✗ unnatural✓ 필요한 만큼 쓰세요. (Use as much as you need.)
"In proportion to" / "since"✗✓ 노력한 만큼, 비싼 만큼
"Not to the point of…"✓ 그 정도는 아니에요. (most common)possible, less common

Rule of thumb: for exaggeration, either works and 정도로 is more frequent in speech; for amounts and proportion, only 만큼 will do.

😱 -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다: "I Almost…!"

-(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 describes something that very nearly happened but didn't. The -(으)ㄹ is the "unreal" modifier again — the event stayed imaginary. Because a near miss is always something that's already over, the pattern is essentially always used in the past: -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다. It loves the adverb 하마터면 ("very nearly, narrowly").

KoreanEnglish
하마터면 계단에서 넘어질 뻔했어요.I very nearly fell on the stairs.
늦잠을 자서 버스를 놓칠 뻔했어요.I overslept and almost missed the bus.
휴대폰을 택시에 두고 내릴 뻔했어요.I almost left my phone in the taxi.
큰일 날 뻔했네요!That was a close call! (Something big almost happened.)
너무 웃겨서 죽을 뻔했어.It was so funny I nearly died. (exaggeration)

Which one? -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 or 거의 -았다

KoreanWhat actually happened
케이크를 다 먹을 뻔했어요.I nearly ate the whole cake — but I stopped in time.
케이크를 거의 다 먹었어요.I ate almost all of it — a small piece is left.

뻔했다 = a line you didn't cross. 거의 = a real action that got close to 100%. Mixing them up can turn "I nearly crashed" into "I mostly crashed".

👍 -(으)ㄹ 만하다: "Worth It" or "Good Enough"

-(으)ㄹ 만하다 says something is worth doing, or at least doable / acceptable. It's the backbone of Korean recommendations — and it has a tone trap worth knowing.

KoreanEnglishTone
이 영화는 정말 볼 만해요.This film is really worth seeing.recommendation
서울에서 가 볼 만한 곳을 추천해 주세요.Please recommend places worth visiting in Seoul.recommendation
그 사람은 믿을 만한 사람이에요.He's a trustworthy person.fixed phrase
조금 아프지만 참을 만해요.It hurts a bit, but it's bearable."manageable"
요즘 어때요? — 그럭저럭 살 만해요.How are things? — Not bad, getting by."okay"
그렇게 오래 기다렸으니 화가 날 만하죠.After waiting that long, it's understandable to be angry."understandable"

⚠️ The review trap: 먹을 만해요 vs 먹어 볼 만해요

먹을 만해요 often means "it's edible / it's okay" — faint praise. 먹어 볼 만해요 ("worth trying") is a real recommendation. If a Korean friend says 음… 먹을 만해, don't book a table for your parents.

🤝 -다시피: "As You Know" and "Practically"

-다시피 has two jobs. The first appears in presentations and emails constantly; the second is everyday exaggeration.

Job 1 — "As you (can) see / know / heard"

It attaches to a small set of perception and knowledge verbs: 알다, 보다, 듣다, 느끼다, 말하다/말씀드리다, 배우다. Past forms are fine: 말씀드렸다시피.

KoreanEnglish
아시다시피 이번 주는 회의가 많습니다.As you know, there are a lot of meetings this week.
보시다시피 이번 달 매출이 크게 늘었습니다.As you can see, sales rose sharply this month.
지난번에 말씀드렸다시피 마감은 금요일입니다.As I mentioned last time, the deadline is Friday.
너도 알다시피 나 요즘 돈이 없잖아.As you know, I'm broke these days.

Note: 알다 keeps its ㄹ — 알다시피, not 아다시피.

Job 2 — -다시피 하다: "practically, as good as"

KoreanEnglish
시험 기간에는 도서관에서 살다시피 했어요.During exams I practically lived in the library.
역까지 뛰다시피 걸었어요.I half-ran to the station.
일이 너무 바빠서 일주일 동안 굶다시피 했어요.Work was so busy I basically starved for a week.

🙅 -기는커녕: "Far From…, Not Even…"

-기는커녕 (after nouns: 은/는커녕) knocks down an expectation: "forget X — not even the smaller Y happened", or "far from X, the opposite happened". The second clause usually has 도, 조차 or 만, and is often negative.

KoreanEnglish
주말에 쉬기는커녕 밥 먹을 시간도 없었어요.Far from resting at the weekend, I didn't even have time to eat.
칭찬은커녕 혼만 났어요.Far from being praised, I just got told off.
돈을 모으기는커녕 빚만 늘었어요.Far from saving money, all I did was pile up debt.
한국어를 잘하기는커녕 한글도 아직 못 읽어요.Far from speaking Korean well, I can't even read Hangul yet.
할인은커녕 오히려 더 비쌌어요.Forget a discount — it was actually more expensive.

The direction matters: the first part names something better or bigger that you might expect; the second part is lower or opposite. ✗ 쉬기는커녕 여행도 갔어요 ("far from resting, I even travelled") doesn't work as an addition — for "not only X but also Y", use -(으)ㄹ 뿐만 아니라.

💬 Dialogue: Back from Busan

Minji and Eric are colleagues catching up on Monday morning, in polite 해요체.

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
에릭민지 씨, 주말에 부산 잘 다녀왔어요?Minji, did you have a good trip to Busan at the weekend?
민지네, 근데 하마터면 기차를 놓칠 뻔했어요. 역까지 뛰다시피 했어요.Yes, but I very nearly missed the train. I practically ran to the station.
에릭아이고, 큰일 날 뻔했네요. 부산은 어땠어요?Oh dear, that was close. How was Busan?
민지보면 볼수록 매력적인 도시더라고요. 특히 감천문화마을은 꼭 가 볼 만해요.The more I saw, the more charming I found it. Gamcheon Culture Village in particular is really worth a visit.
에릭거기 사람 많지 않았어요?Wasn't it crowded?
민지말도 못 할 정도로 많았어요. 사진을 찍기는커녕 걸어 다니기도 힘들었어요.Unbelievably crowded. Forget taking photos — it was hard just to walk around.
에릭음식은요? 돼지국밥 먹어 봤어요?What about the food? Did you try pork-and-rice soup?
민지그럼요. 눈물이 날 만큼 맛있었어요. 서울에서 먹은 거랑은 비교가 안 돼요.Of course. It was so good I could have cried. No comparison with what I've had in Seoul.
에릭저도 다음 달에 가려고 하는데, 숙소는 어디가 좋아요?I'm planning to go next month — where's good to stay?
민지아시다시피 해운대 쪽은 비싸잖아요. 저는 서면에 묵었는데 교통도 편하고 지낼 만했어요.As you know, the Haeundae area is expensive. I stayed in Seomyeon — transport was convenient and it was perfectly decent.
에릭좋은 정보 고마워요. 얘기를 들을수록 빨리 가고 싶어지네요.Thanks for the tips. The more I hear, the more I want to go soon.

Notice 지낼 만했어요: Minji is saying the hotel was fine — not amazing. Compare 가 볼 만해요 for the village, which is a genuine recommendation.

📰 Reading: [솔직 후기] 회사 앞 칼국숫집

An office worker posts an "honest review" of a noodle restaurant on a blog. Reviews like this are full of degree expressions — and of careful, polite hedging.

Korean
회사 근처에 새로 생긴 칼국숫집에 다녀왔어요. 점심시간에는 줄을 설 정도로 인기가 많다고 해서 11시에 갔는데도 벌써 사람들이 기다리고 있었어요. 그래서 하마터면 오후 회의에 늦을 뻔했어요. 국물은 먹으면 먹을수록 깊은 맛이 났지만, 면이 좀 짰어요. 가격은 싸기는커녕 오히려 다른 집보다 이천 원 정도 비쌌어요. 사장님 말씀에 따르면 재료를 매일 아침 직접 준비한다고 해요. 사진에서 보시다시피 양은 정말 많아요. 결론적으로 맛은 기대만큼은 아니었지만 먹을 만했어요. 한 번쯤 가 볼 만하지만 한 시간씩 줄을 설 정도는 아니에요!
English translation

I went to the new kalguksu (knife-cut noodle) place near my office. I'd heard it's so popular that people queue at lunchtime, so I went at 11 — and even so, people were already waiting. So I very nearly ended up late for my afternoon meeting. The broth tasted deeper the more I ate, but the noodles were a bit salty. Far from being cheap, it was actually about 2,000 won more expensive than other places. According to the owner, they prepare the ingredients themselves every morning. As you can see in the photo, the portions are really big. In conclusion, the taste wasn't quite what I'd hoped, but it was decent. It's worth trying once, but not to the point of queuing for an hour!

Culture note — 강추, 비추 and 인생 맛집. Korean review culture has its own scale. At the top sit 강추 (강력 추천, "strongly recommend") and 인생 맛집 ("the best restaurant of my life"); at the bottom, 비추 (비추천, "not recommended"). In between, the -(으)ㄹ 만하다 family does the subtle work: 가 볼 만하다 is a yes, 먹을 만하다 is a polite shrug. And exaggeration with 죽겠다 or -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 isn't taken literally — using it yourself will make you sound far more natural than a strictly accurate "I was very hungry".

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Present-tense 뻔하다: ✗ 어제 넘어질 뻔해요 → ✓ 넘어질 뻔했어요. A near miss is already over.
  • Confusing -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 with the adjective 뻔하다: 뻔한 이야기 means "a predictable story". Same spelling, completely different word.
  • Past before -(으)ㄹ수록: ✗ 배웠을수록 → ✓ 배울수록 (the final verb carries the tense).
  • Praising with 먹을 만해요: to the chef, it sounds lukewarm. Say 정말 맛있어요 or 또 오고 싶어요.
  • 알다시피 to your boss: ✗ 부장님, 알다시피… → ✓ 부장님, 아시다시피…
  • -기는커녕 + a bigger thing: ✗ 쉬기는커녕 여행도 갔어요. The second part must be lower or opposite.

❓ Common Questions

Are -(으)ㄹ 정도로 and -(으)ㄹ 만큼 interchangeable?

For exaggerated degree, yes: 죽을 정도로 and 죽을 만큼 mean the same, and 정도로 is a bit more frequent in speech. But only 만큼 can express a needed amount (필요한 만큼) or proportion (노력한 만큼, 비싼 만큼), and "not to that extent" is almost always 그 정도는 아니에요.

Can I ever use -(으)ㄹ 뻔하다 in the present?

In practice, no — a near miss is always reported after it's over, so you'll see -(으)ㄹ 뻔했다 (뻔했어요, 뻔했습니다) almost exclusively. The one thing to watch out for is the unrelated adjective 뻔하다 ("obvious, predictable"), which is used in the present all the time: 결과가 뻔해요 ("The result is obvious").

Is 갈수록 always about time?

Mostly. 갈수록 on its own has become an adverb for "as time goes by / more and more": 갈수록 태산 is even a proverb ("the further you go, the higher the mountains" — things keep getting harder). When you mean literal distance, add the place: 남쪽으로 갈수록 따뜻해져요 ("the further south you go, the warmer it gets").

Can -기는커녕 attach to adjectives?

Yes: 싸기는커녕 오히려 비싸요 ("far from cheap, it's actually expensive"), 조용하기는커녕 너무 시끄러웠어요. The logic is the same — the first part is the expectation, the second knocks it down.

🌟 Going Further

  • Write your own review (200–300 자): Review a café, film or place you know. Use at least four patterns from this lesson, and make your final verdict clear with 가 볼 만하다, 먹을 만하다 or -(으)ㄹ 정도는 아니다.
  • Near-miss story: Tell a friend (in 반말) about a time something nearly went wrong, using 하마터면 … -(으)ㄹ 뻔했어 and one -다시피 하다.

Here's a pattern worth noticing about your own progress: 한국어는 배우면 배울수록 어려운 것 같지만, 알면 알수록 재미있어요. The feeling that Korean gets harder at B2 is usually a sign that you've started noticing nuance you couldn't see before — that's growth, not a wall.
