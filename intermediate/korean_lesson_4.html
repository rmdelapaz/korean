🇰🇷 Lesson 4: Guessing & Inferring

You walk into the office on a Monday morning. Nobody is chatting. The team leader's door is shut. A colleague's umbrella is dripping by the desk, and someone has left a half-eaten 김밥 next to the keyboard. Without a single word being spoken, you already know a lot: it's raining, someone skipped breakfast to get in early, and the meeting behind that door is probably not going well.

Koreans have a famous word for this skill: 눈치, the art of reading a situation. And Korean grammar has a whole toolkit for saying "I'm guessing…", with each tool quietly signalling how sure you are and what your guess is based on. English mostly gets by with "I think", "probably", "must" and "might". Korean makes you choose, and the choice tells the listener something. That is what this lesson is about.

🧠 The Mental Model: Two Questions Behind Every Guess

Before you pick a form, ask yourself two questions:

  1. What is my evidence? Something I can see or hear right now? Something I noticed about another person? Just general knowledge?
  2. How sure am I? Pretty confident, or only "it's possible"?

🔍 -(으)ㄴ/는/(으)ㄹ 것 같다: "It seems / I think"

You already know the three noun-modifier endings from the beginner course. 것 같다 simply hangs "it seems like the thing that…" off them, so the modifier carries the tense. That is the whole trick, and it is where most errors happen.

The pair that matters most: -는 것 같다 vs -(으)ㄹ 것 같다

Both can describe the present, but the evidence differs. -는 것 같다 leans on something you are perceiving or already know; -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 is a prediction or a guess from the outside.

⚠️ 것 같다 as a politeness cushion

Koreans constantly use 것 같다 about things they know perfectly well, just to sound less blunt: 이게 더 예쁜 것 같아요 ("I think this one is prettier"), 조금 비싼 것 같아요 ("It seems a bit expensive" = I'm not buying it). It is polite and very natural. But be aware that Korean commentators sometimes tease the habit when it is used for one's own feelings, as in 행복한 것 같아요 ("I think I'm happy"). If you are sure of your own feeling, you can simply say 행복해요.

👀 -나 보다 / -(으)ㄴ가 보다: "I guess… (judging by that)"

This is the detective's ending. You have seen a clue, and you draw a conclusion about someone or something else. The clue is often stated in the same breath: 불이 꺼져 있네요. 다들 퇴근했나 봐요. ("The lights are off. I guess everyone's gone home.")

💡 The Formula

Action verbs, 있다/없다: stem + -나 보다 → 가나 봐요, 먹나 봐요, 맛있나 봐요

Adjectives: stem + -(으)ㄴ가 보다 → 바쁜가 봐요, 좋은가 봐요, 추운가 봐요

Nouns: N + 인가 보다 → 학생인가 봐요, 주말인가 봐요

Past (everything): -았/었나 보다 → 갔나 봐요, 바빴나 봐요, 학생이었나 봐요

Watch the ㄹ-stems: ㄹ drops before -나 and before -ㄴ가, just as it does before -니까. 열다 → 여나 봐요, 살다 → 사나 봐요, 멀다 → 먼가 봐요, 길다 → 긴가 봐요.

⚠️ Three restrictions that make it sound native

1. Not for your own direct experience. If you tasted the soup yourself, you know it's good: say 맛있어요 or 맛있네요. 맛있나 봐요 is what you say while watching someone else wolf it down.

2. You may use it about yourself only as a discovery. 제가 많이 긴장했나 봐요 ("I guess I was really nervous") works because you are reading your own shaking hands like a clue.

3. Tense goes on the guessed verb, not on 보다. "It seems he was busy" is 바빴나 봐요, never 바쁜가 봤어요. For a future guess, speakers use -(으)려나 보다 or -(으)ㄹ 건가 보다: 비가 오려나 봐요 ("Looks like it's going to rain").

A note on real usage: textbooks and TOPIK teach -(으)ㄴ가 보다 for adjectives, but in everyday speech you will also hear 바쁘나 봐요, 좋나 봐요. Both are understood; use -(으)ㄴ가 in writing and exams.

🎲 -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다: "It might…"

Literally "(I) don't know whether it will…", which is exactly how English speakers hedge: "Who knows, it might rain." This is the low-certainty end of the scale, and it goes hand in hand with 어쩌면 ("perhaps"), 혹시 ("by any chance") and 아마 ("probably").

KoreanEnglish
금요일 저녁이라서 길이 막힐지도 몰라요.It's Friday evening, so the roads might be jammed.
그 사람은 벌써 집에 갔을지도 몰라요.He might already have gone home.
산 위는 추울지도 모르니까 겉옷을 챙기세요.It might be cold at the top, so take a jacket.
어쩌면 그 말이 거짓말일지도 몰라.Maybe what he said is a lie. (반말)
혹시 제가 틀렸을지도 모르지만, 한번 확인해 볼게요.I might be wrong, but I'll check once.

A close neighbour is -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 ("it's possible that"): 길이 막힐 수도 있어요. The two are often interchangeable. -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다 sounds a touch more like genuine uncertainty or worry; -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 sounds like a neutral statement of possibility. Remember 모르다 is 르-irregular: 몰라요, 몰랐어요.

⚡ -겠- of Inference: The Instant Reaction

You met -겠- as "will / intend" in 제가 하겠습니다. Its other job is an instant inference from what is in front of you, often with emotion. It is the ending of empathy and first impressions.

SituationKoreanEnglish
Seeing a friend's cake와, 맛있겠다!Wow, that looks delicious!
Friend worked a double shift많이 피곤하시겠어요.You must be exhausted.
Friend queued for three hours정말 힘들었겠어요.That must have been really hard.
Friend lost their wallet속상하겠어요.You must be so upset.
Looking at black clouds곧 비가 오겠어요.It's going to rain any minute.
Weather forecast내일은 전국에 비가 오겠습니다.Tomorrow it will rain nationwide.

Compare 맛있겠다! (a gut reaction, said on seeing the food) with 맛있을 것 같아요 (a considered opinion: "I think it'd be tasty"). When someone tells you their troubles, -겠어요 is the warmest thing you can say; it shows you are imagining their situation. The past form -았/었겠어요 reacts to something that already happened.

🧭 -(으)ㄹ 테니까: A Guess (or a Promise) as a Reason

테니까 is a contraction of 터 ("expectation") + 이다 + -니까. It has two uses, and the subject tells you which one you are looking at.

UseKoreanEnglish
Guess → advice (any subject)지금은 길이 막힐 테니까 지하철을 타세요.The roads will be jammed now, so take the subway.
Guess about the past → advice지금쯤 회의가 끝났을 테니까 전화해 보세요.The meeting should be over by now, so try calling.
Guess → advice밤에는 추울 테니까 따뜻하게 입고 가.It'll be cold at night, so dress warmly. (반말)
My intention → request (I/we)제가 청소를 할 테니까 민수 씨는 요리해 주세요.I'll do the cleaning, so you do the cooking, Minsu.
My intention → request내가 표를 살 테니까 너는 자리 좀 맡아 줘.I'll buy the tickets, so you save us some seats.

Unlike plain -(으)니까, the first clause is not a fact; it is what the speaker expects or intends. The second clause is usually a command, suggestion or request. There is no present-tense 는 version: *비가 오는 테니까 does not exist. In formal speech and writing you will also see the shorter -(으)ㄹ 테니: 곧 도착할 테니 잠시만 기다려 주십시오.

Notice how the certainty rises and falls: 생긴 것 같아요 (general sense), 때문인가 봐요 (clue: the boss's face), 길어질지도 모르겠네요 (only a possibility; 모르겠네요 is a softer, musing version of 몰라요), 나오실 테니까 (expectation used as advice), 쓴 모양이에요 (clue: the third coffee), 피곤하겠어요 (empathy).

🌏 Culture Note: 눈치 — 눈치, a word built on 눈 ("eye"), is the ability to sense unspoken moods and expectations. Someone who picks things up quickly is 눈치가 빠르다; someone oblivious is 눈치가 없다; and constantly checking others' reactions before acting is 눈치를 보다. In a culture where a lot goes unsaid, guessing grammar is not decoration. It is how people talk about the invisible layer of a conversation without making anyone lose face.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 친구가 벌써 갔는 것 같아요. → ✓ 친구가 벌써 간 것 같아요. (Past verbs take the modifier -(으)ㄴ, not 았는.)

✗ 날씨가 추워는 것 같아요. → ✓ 날씨가 추운 것 같아요. (Adjectives take -(으)ㄴ in the present.)

✗ 이 식당 음식이 맛있은가 봐요. → ✓ 맛있나 봐요. (있다/없다 words take -나.)

✗ 어제 많이 바쁜가 봤어요. → ✓ 어제 많이 바빴나 봐요. (Tense goes on the guessed verb.)

✗ (after tasting it yourself) 이 국 맛있나 봐요. → ✓ 이 국 맛있네요. (-나 보다 is for evidence about others.)

✗ 비가 오는 테니까 우산 가져가세요. → ✓ 비가 올 테니까… (테니까 only follows -(으)ㄹ.)

✗ 가게가 문을 안 열나 봐요. → ✓ 문을 안 여나 봐요. (ㄹ drops before -나.)

Six forms that all translate as "I think" or "it seems" can feel like a plateau: you understand them when you read them, but in conversation your mouth keeps choosing 것 같아요. That is completely normal at B1. Start by adding just one new form, perhaps -겠어요 for empathy, and let the others follow as you notice them in dramas and chats.

Exercise 3 — 모양이다, 지도 모르다, 테니까 or -겠-?

  1. 금요일 저녁이라서 길이 _____ 몰라요. (막히다, "might") (Answer: 막힐지도)
  2. 전화를 안 받아요. 벌써 집에 _____ 몰라요. (가다, "might have") (Answer: 갔을지도)
  3. 오후에 비가 _____ 테니까 우산을 가져가세요. (오다, guess as reason) (Answer: 올)
  4. 제가 청소를 _____ 테니까 민수 씨는 요리해 주세요. (하다, my intention) (Answer: 할)
  5. 지금쯤 회의가 _____ 테니까 전화해 보세요. (끝나다, already done by now) (Answer: 끝났을)
  6. 와, 이 케이크 정말 _____! (맛있다, 반말 reaction on seeing it) (Answer: 맛있겠다)
  7. 하루 종일 서서 일했어요? 다리가 많이 _____. (아프다, polite -겠- reaction) (Answer: 아프겠어요)
  8. 불이 다 꺼져 있어요. 모두 _____ 모양이에요. (퇴근하다, already happened) (Answer: 퇴근한)

Exercise 4 — Complete the dialogue

  1. A: 지훈 씨가 전화를 안 받아요. B: 지금 운전 _____ 봐요. (중이다) (Answer: 중인가)
  2. A: 수진 씨 얼굴이 빨개요. B: 열이 _____ 것 같아요. (있다, present) (Answer: 있는)
  3. A: 저녁에 공원에 갈까요? B: 좋아요. 그런데 저녁에는 _____ 테니까 겉옷을 챙기세요. (춥다) (Answer: 추울)
  4. A: 내일 소풍 갈 수 있을까요? B: 글쎄요, 비가 _____ 몰라요. (오다) (Answer: 올지도)
  5. A: 콘서트 표를 사려고 세 시간 동안 줄을 섰어요. B: 정말 _____! (힘들다, past -겠-, polite) (Answer: 힘들었겠어요)

  2. 동료는 "오늘 회의가 잘 안 _____ 봐요."라고 했어요. (Answer: 됐나)

❓ Common Questions

Is it bad to say 것 같아요 all the time?

No. It is the most common hedge in spoken Korean and always grammatical. The only caution is about your own clear feelings and sensations: 좋은 것 같아요 about a gift you love can sound oddly unsure. When you know, say it directly (좋아요, 맛있어요). When you want to soften an opinion or a refusal, 것 같아요 is perfect.

Can I ask a question with -나 보다?

Not naturally. It reports the speaker's own inference, so it is a statement. To ask, use 것 같다 (비가 올 것 같아요?) or ask directly.

What is the difference between 올지도 몰라요 and 올 수도 있어요?

They overlap heavily and both mean "might". -(으)ㄹ 수도 있다 simply states a possibility; -(으)ㄹ지도 모르다 adds a hint of "I really can't say" and is often used when you are slightly worried. In most everyday sentences you can use either.

How is the inference -겠- different from the intention -겠-?

Look at the subject and the verb. 제가 하겠습니다 (first person, action verb) is intention. 맛있겠다, 피곤하시겠어요, 비가 오겠어요 (an adjective, the listener, or the weather) are inference. Weather forecasts use -겠습니다 as a formal, polite prediction.
