🇰🇷 Lesson 14: Workplace Korean

Your first morning at a Korean office. Someone says 팀장님이 찾으세요, and you realise you don't know which of the five people in the room is the 팀장, whether the 과장 sitting next to you is higher or lower, or how to begin the email you've just been asked to send. In the beginner course you learned the basic ladder of titles and that 님 is for other people. This lesson turns that into working Korean: how to address people, how reports and approvals flow, how to write an email and take a call, how meetings run, and how to handle 회식.

Workplace Korean is also where your B1 grammar suddenly becomes essential. Passing on your boss's instructions is reported speech. Explaining a delay is -느라고 and -는 바람에. Asking whether something is possible is -는지. You already have the tools; this lesson gives you the setting.

🧠 The Mental Model: Rank, Role and Distance

Every time you speak to a colleague you are, consciously or not, reading three things:

AxisKoreanQuestion it answers
Rank직급Where is this person on the seniority ladder? (사원, 대리, 과장…)
Role직책What do they lead? (팀장, 본부장…)
Distance관계Same team? Same 입사 year? Another company?

The key B1 insight is that rank and role are different things. 과장 is a rank; 팀장 is a job. A 과장 who leads a team is usually addressed by the role, 팀장님, because the role is what matters in daily work. When in doubt, listen to what the others in your team call that person and copy them.

English equivalents are rough; every company translates them differently. The ladder above (사원 → 대리 → 과장 → 차장 → 부장) is the traditional core, but in recent years many companies have flattened it: some use one or two levels such as 매니저 or 프로, some call everyone by name + 님 (민수님), and some use English names. The rule of thumb is the same everywhere: observe first, then copy.

💡 선배 and 동기

Alongside official titles, relationships run on 입사 year. People who joined the same year are 입사 동기 and often speak 반말 with each other in private (Lesson 1). Someone who joined before you may be your 선배 even at the same rank. 부하 직원 ("subordinate") exists, but people usually say 팀원 ("team member") or 후배.

📋 보고 and 결재: How Decisions Travel

Korean offices distinguish two things English often lumps together as "running it by the boss":

Which one?보고 (report)결재 (formal approval)
What it isTelling a superior what is happening, in person or in writingA document formally approved up the chain, usually in an electronic approval system (전자결재)
Key verbs보고하다, 보고드리다, 중간보고 (progress report)결재를 올리다 (submit), 결재를 받다 (get approved)
Possible outcomesFeedback, questions승인 (approved) or 반려 (returned / rejected)

A good habit Korean managers value highly is the 중간보고: telling your boss how things are going before the deadline, especially if there's a problem. Bad news reported early is forgiven; bad news discovered late is not.

지은팀장님, 잠깐 보고드릴 게 있는데 지금 시간 괜찮으세요?Team leader, I have something to report. Do you have a moment?
팀장네, 괜찮아요. 뭔데요?Sure. What is it?
지은지난주에 말씀하신 시장 조사 결과가 나왔습니다. 그런데 거래처 자료가 늦게 오는 바람에 보고서가 하루 늦어질 것 같습니다.The market research results you mentioned last week are in. But because the partner's data came late, the report looks like it'll be a day late.
팀장그래요? 미리 말해 줘서 고마워요. 내일 오전까지는 돼요?Really? Thanks for telling me in advance. Can it be done by tomorrow morning?
지은네, 내일 오전까지 결재 올리겠습니다.Yes, I'll submit it for approval by tomorrow morning.

Notice the register split: the junior speaks 하십시오체 (보고드리겠습니다, 올리겠습니다) with 해요체 for softer moments, while the team leader speaks polite 해요체 downward. Many managers also drop to 반말 with juniors; you should not do the reverse.

✉️ Business Email

Korean business emails follow a predictable shape. Get the frame right and the content can be simple.

PartKoreanEnglish
제목 (subject)[요청] 3월 마케팅 회의 일정 변경[Request] Change of March marketing meeting date
인사 (greeting)박지훈 과장님, 안녕하십니까.Mr Park Jihun, hello. (literal)
자기소개 (self)마케팅팀 김민수 대리입니다.This is assistant manager Kim Minsu of Marketing.
용건 (purpose)다름이 아니라 12일 오후 2시 회의 일정 변경을 요청드리고자 연락드렸습니다.I'm writing to request a change to the 2 p.m. meeting on the 12th.
이유 (reason)당일 오후에 고객사 미팅이 잡히는 바람에 참석이 어렵게 되었습니다.A client meeting has come up that afternoon, so I won't be able to attend.
요청 (request)혹시 13일 오전으로 변경이 가능한지 확인 부탁드립니다.Could you check whether the morning of the 13th would be possible?
첨부 (attachment)관련 자료는 첨부 파일로 보내 드리니 참고 부탁드립니다.I've attached the related materials for your reference.
마무리 (closing)바쁘신 와중에 번거롭게 해 드려 죄송합니다. 감사합니다.Sorry to trouble you when you're busy. Thank you.
서명 (sign-off)김민수 드림Kim Minsu (lit. "given by")

다름이 아니라 ("it's nothing other than…") is the classic way to move from greeting to business. 참조 (CC) and 회신 (reply) are the other email words you'll see daily: 회신 부탁드립니다, "Please reply."

Which sign-off?Nuance
드림The safe default for almost anyone, inside or outside the company
올림More deferential; to someone clearly senior. Some consider it overly formal
Just your name + 감사합니다Common in quick internal emails and chat

⚠️ Honest Note on "Over-Honorific" Email Phrases

Phrases such as 요청드리다, 안내드리다 and 확인 부탁드립니다 are everywhere in real offices. Some language purists argue that nouns like 요청 don't need 드리다 (요청하다 is enough), but in practice these forms are standard business Korean and you will sound normal using them. 수고 많으십니다 as an email opener is also common, although, as you learned in the beginner course, some people feel 수고 expressions shouldn't be aimed at superiors. When unsure, 안녕하십니까 or 안녕하세요 is never wrong.

Disagreement in Korean meetings is usually softened: -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (Lesson 4), -기는 하지만/-긴 한데 (Lesson 7) and questions like 혹시 … 어떨까요? do the work that a blunt "I disagree" does in English.

🍻 회식

You know from the beginner course that 회식 (team dinners) matter and that the culture is changing. Here is the language you need on the night.

KoreanEnglish

🇰🇷 Culture note — drinking etiquette, then and now. Traditional etiquette is still widely followed: receive and pour with two hands (or one hand supporting the other wrist) when a senior pours, and turn your head slightly away when you drink in front of a senior. What has changed is the pressure. Lunch 회식, activity-based 회식 and shorter evenings are increasingly common, and declining alcohol with a polite reason is widely accepted, especially at larger or younger companies. Still, office cultures vary a lot: watch your team, and ask a trusted colleague if unsure.

Look at how often reported speech appears. A huge part of a junior employee's day is relaying: what the boss said, what the client asked for, what HR announced. If Lessons 2 and 3 felt abstract, this is where they pay off.

Korean

English translation

These days the team-dinner culture of Korean companies is changing. In the past there were many late drinking sessions that ran on to a second and third round, but recently more and more companies are replacing 회식 with lunch gatherings or activities such as going to a film or bowling. The aim is to let staff who don't drink attend without feeling pressured. Forms of address are changing too. Some companies have started attaching 님 to people's names, or using titles like "manager" or "pro", instead of ranks. The idea is to share opinions freely in a flatter atmosphere. Of course, many companies still use traditional rank titles. So when you join a new company, it's a good idea to observe its atmosphere first.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 저는 김민수 대리님입니다. Never 님 on your own title: 김민수 대리입니다.

✗ 과장 씨 / 민수 과장 씨. 씨 doesn't combine with titles. Use 과장님 or 김 과장님.

✗ Calling a 과장 who leads your team 과장님 when everyone says 팀장님. Not rude, but it sounds as if you don't know the structure. Follow the team's usage.

✗ Using 결재 for any feedback. 결재 is formal document approval. Casual "can you take a look?" is 검토해 주시겠어요? or 한번 봐 주시겠어요?

✗ Leaving a 회식 by just disappearing. Say 먼저 들어가 보겠습니다 to the most senior person present; slipping away unannounced is noticed.

❓ Common Questions

My company calls everyone "name + 님". Do I still need to learn the ranks?

Yes. Even flat companies deal with clients, partners and government offices that use traditional titles, and ranks appear in email signatures, org charts and news. You need to recognise them even if you rarely say them.

Should I speak 하십시오체 all day?

No. Office Korean mixes 하십시오체 and 해요체. Use 하십시오체 for formal reports, meetings, phone greetings and emails (보고드리겠습니다, 알겠습니다), and polite 해요체 for everyday chat with superiors. 반말 only with peers who have agreed to it (Lesson 1), usually 동기.

Do I have to drink at 회식?

Increasingly, no. A polite reason (health, medication, driving, early start, religion) is generally accepted, and holding a soft drink lets you join the toasts. Norms still vary by company, industry and generation, so watch what others do and ask a trusted colleague.

What's the difference between 드림 and 올림?

Both mean roughly "offered by". 드림 is the neutral default for colleagues and clients. 올림 ("offered up") is more deferential, for someone clearly far above you, and some people find it stiff in everyday email. If unsure, use 드림.

How do I disagree with my boss politely?

Acknowledge first, then offer an alternative as a question: 좋은 방법이긴 한데요, 혹시 일정을 조금 늦추면 어떨까요? The -긴 한데 (Lesson 7) and the question form soften it; a flat 아니에요, 틀렸어요 would sound confrontational.

🌟 Going Further

1. Write an email (about 250 characters). Your client has asked you to move a meeting. Write to your 팀장님 to report this and ask whether the new date is possible. Use 다름이 아니라, -는 바람에, -(으)ㄴ/는지 and 드림.

2. Relay chain. Your boss says: "내일 회의 자료를 오늘까지 보내 주고, 회의실도 예약해 주세요." Pass this on to a colleague in two versions: full reported speech (Lesson 2) and spoken contractions (Lesson 3).

Workplace Korean is where many learners discover that "knowing the grammar" and "sounding right" are two different skills. If you feel clumsy on your first calls and emails, that is completely normal. Native speakers spend their first year at a company learning these exact conventions too.
