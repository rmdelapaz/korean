🇰🇷 Lesson 18: Hobbies & Daily Life

"What do you do for fun?" is the conversation that turns a stranger into a friend. In Korea it often leads straight to an invitation: to a 노래방, a hike, a baseball game or a riverside picnic. This lesson gives you the hobby vocabulary and the verbs that go with it. Then it puts Lesson 17's wants, plans and "shall we?" to work, so you can not only say what you like but make plans to do it together.

💡 Where's the daily routine?

The everyday-routine verbs (일어나다, 씻다, 출근하다, 퇴근하다, 청소하다, 자다…) live in Lesson 12: Home, Household & Daily Routine. This lesson is about free time. If those verbs feel rusty, a quick flashcard review of Lesson 12 before you start will pay off.

🎸 Hobby Verbs: Which Verb Goes With Which Hobby?

English "play" covers guitar, tennis and games. Korean picks a different verb depending on how you do the activity, a bit like English "ride a bike" vs "play a sport". These are worth learning as pairs.

💡 Two irregular hobby verbs to hold for now

듣다 (to listen) → 음악을 들어요, and 부르다 (to sing) → 노래를 불러요. Both are irregular; Lesson 21 explains the ㄷ→ㄹ and 르 patterns. Until then, learn 들어요 and 불러요 as chunks, or simply say 노래해요 (I sing).

❤️ Saying What You Like

The easiest pattern uses a noun and the object particle from Lesson 7: noun + 을/를 좋아해요.

KoreanEnglish
취미가 뭐예요?What's your hobby?
제 취미는 등산이에요.My hobby is hiking.
제 취미는 요리예요.My hobby is cooking.
저는 야구를 좋아해요.I like baseball.
음악을 정말 좋아해요.I really like music.
운동은 별로 안 좋아해요.I don't really like exercise.

For "I like doing X", Koreans use a chunk: verb stem + 는 것을 좋아해요. In speech it shrinks to -는 걸 좋아해요 or -는 거 좋아해요. The -는 is a noun-modifier ending you'll study properly in Lesson 25; for now, just plug in stems.

KoreanEnglish
음악 듣는 것을 좋아해요.I like listening to music.

💡 Culture Note: Hiking (등산) is close to a national pastime. On a weekend morning, trails around Seoul fill with middle-aged hikers in full professional gear, and sharing snacks at the summit is part of the ritual. Hobbies are also social in Korea: people join a 동호회 (hobby club) and meet regularly, and a first 노래방 visit with coworkers or classmates is a classic bonding moment. Nobody expects you to sing well. They expect you to sing!

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 기타를 해요 ❌ → 기타를 쳐요 ✅. Instruments you strike or pluck take 치다.
  • 춤을 해요 ❌ → 춤을 춰요 ✅. 춤 has its own verb, 추다.
  • 자전거를 해요 ❌ → 자전거를 타요 ✅. You ride (타다) a bike, just like a bus.
  • 수영하러 해요 ❌ → 수영하러 가요 ✅. -(으)러 only pairs with movement verbs: 가다, 오다, 다니다.
  • 가끔 안 가요 for "rarely" ❌ → 거의 안 가요 ✅. 가끔 is positive ("sometimes").

If you mixed up 치다 and 하다 today, good: that's the mix-up that makes the right pair stick. Everyone who speaks Korean well once said 기타를 해요.

❓ Common Questions

제 취미는 등산이에요 or 등산을 좋아해요: which is more natural?

Both are fine. 제 취미는 … 이에요/예요 answers the question 취미가 뭐예요? directly and sounds a little formal, like an introduction. In casual conversation Koreans more often say what they like or do: 등산을 좋아해요, 주말에 자주 등산해요.

Why is it 기타를 쳐요 and not 기타를 치어요?

That's the contraction rule from Lesson 7: stem 치 + 어요 → ㅣ and ㅓ merge into ㅕ → 쳐요. The same thing happens in 그리다 → 그려요 and 마시다 → 마셔요.

Can I use 게임하다 and 게임을 하다 interchangeably?

Yes. Many activity nouns can attach 하다 directly (게임하다, 요리하다, 운동하다) or take 을/를 before 하다 (게임을 해요). The 을/를 version lets you add words: 게임을 많이 해요.

Is 대박 polite?

It's casual slang: fine with friends, classmates and people your age, but not something to say to your boss or a teacher. With them, try 와, 정말 좋아요!
