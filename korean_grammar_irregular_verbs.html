🇰🇷 Lesson 21: Irregular Verbs

By now you've probably met a few words that refused to behave. You'd expect 덥다 ("to be hot") to become 덥어요, but everyone says 더워요. 듣다 ("to listen") should be 듣어요, but it's 들어요. In Lesson 20 you said 아파요 ("it hurts") from 아프다, and in Lesson 19 you asked for help with 도와주세요 from 돕다. Today we explain every one of them.

First, the reassuring news: Korean "irregular" verbs are not random. English irregulars are a list you just memorise (go/went, eat/ate, buy/bought). Korean irregulars fall into seven small families, and every member of a family changes in exactly the same way. Learn the rule once, and it works for dozens of words.

💡 The one question that unlocks everything

Almost every irregular change happens only when the stem meets an ending that starts with a vowel — -아요/어요, -았어요/었어요, -(으)세요, -(으)면, -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요. Before an ending that starts with a consonant — -고 싶어요, -지 마세요, -습니다 — the stem usually stays exactly as it is (the ㄹ family is the one exception). So always ask: what sound does the ending start with?

Why does Korean do this? Mostly for smooth pronunciation. A final ㅂ or ㄷ squeezed against a vowel was softened over centuries into 우 or ㄹ — the spelling simply records how people actually speak.

🔥 1. The ㅂ family: ㅂ → 우

When a stem ending in ㅂ meets a vowel, the ㅂ turns into 우. Then 우 + 어 contracts to 워, just as 배우 + 어요 became 배워요 in Lesson 7.

💡 Formula

덥 + 어요 → 더우 + 어요 → 더워요 · 덥 + (으)세요 → 더우세요 · 덥 + (으)면 → 더우면

The 오 exception: only 돕다 (to help) and 곱다 (to be fine, lovely) take 오 → 도와요, 고와요 — the 오 in the stem pulls the ending into -아 harmony. Every other ㅂ-irregular uses 워.

덥다 (hot)더워요더웠어요더우세요더우면
춥다 (cold)추워요추웠어요추우세요추우면
돕다 (help)도와요도왔어요도우세요도우면
맵다 (spicy)매워요매웠어요—매우면
쉽다 / 어렵다쉬워요 / 어려워요쉬웠어요 / 어려웠어요—쉬우면 / 어려우면
가깝다 (near)가까워요가까웠어요—가까우면
눕다 (lie down)누워요누웠어요누우세요누우면

Other everyday members: 귀엽다 → 귀여워요 (cute), 무겁다 → 무거워요 (heavy), 가볍다 → 가벼워요 (light), 반갑다 → 반가워요 (glad — yes, the 반갑습니다 you learned in Lesson 4!), 고맙다 → 고마워요 (thankful). A handy rule of thumb: almost every adjective ending in ㅂ is irregular, while most verbs ending in ㅂ are regular (see "look-alikes" below). "—" in the table means the honorific form isn't something you'd naturally say about a person.

Notice 반갑습니다 and 고맙습니다: before the consonant ending -습니다, the ㅂ stays. That's the "what does the ending start with?" rule in action.

👂 2. The ㄷ family: ㄷ → ㄹ

A handful of common verbs ending in ㄷ change the ㄷ to ㄹ before a vowel.

DictionaryPresentPastHonorific-(으)ㄹ 수 있어요
듣다 (listen)들어요들었어요들으세요들을 수 있어요
걷다 (walk)걸어요걸었어요걸으세요걸을 수 있어요
묻다 (ask)물어요물었어요물으세요물을 수 있어요

Before consonants, the ㄷ is back: 음악을 듣고 싶어요 (I want to listen to music), 걷지 마세요 (don't walk). Also note that the new ㄹ is a real consonant, so the 으 in -(으)세요 and -(으)면 stays: 들으세요, 걸으면.

😷 3. The ㅡ family: ㅡ disappears

When a stem ending in the vowel ㅡ meets -아/어, the ㅡ simply drops out. Then you choose 아 or 어 by looking at the vowel one syllable further back:

💡 Formula

바쁘 + 아/어 → the syllable before 쁘 is 바 (ㅏ) → 바빠요

예쁘 + 아/어 → the syllable before 쁘 is 예 (not ㅏ/ㅗ) → 예뻐요

One-syllable stems (쓰다, 크다, 끄다) have nothing further back → always 어: 써요, 커요, 꺼요

DictionaryPresentPastHonorific-(으)면
바쁘다 (busy)바빠요바빴어요바쁘세요바쁘면
아프다 (hurt, be sick)아파요아팠어요아프세요아프면
예쁘다 (pretty)예뻐요예뻤어요예쁘세요예쁘면
슬프다 (sad)슬퍼요슬펐어요슬프세요슬프면
쓰다 (write, use)써요썼어요쓰세요쓰면
크다 (big)커요컸어요크세요 (tall)크면

✅ An honest note: is this really "irregular"?

Many Korean grammars — including the school grammar Korean students learn — classify ㅡ-dropping as regular (규칙 활용). The reason: it happens to every ㅡ-stem (the 르 stems below add their own twist), so it's an automatic sound rule rather than a quirk of certain words. The same is true of the ㄹ family below. Learner textbooks list them with the irregulars because the written form visibly changes and learners need to practise them. Either way, the practical takeaway is identical: whenever you see ㅡ before -아/어, drop it.

🎵 4. The 르 family: 르 → ㄹㄹ

Stems ending in 르 lose their ㅡ and grow an extra ㄹ: the ㄹ jumps onto the previous syllable as a final consonant. Then 라 or 러 follows the usual harmony.

💡 Formula

모르 + 아요 → 몰 + 라요 → 몰라요 (모 has ㅗ → 아)

부르 + 어요 → 불 + 러요 → 불러요 (부 has ㅜ → 어)

DictionaryPresentPastHonorific-(으)면
모르다 (not know)몰라요몰랐어요모르세요모르면
부르다 (sing, call)불러요불렀어요부르세요부르면
빠르다 (fast)빨라요빨랐어요—빠르면
다르다 (different)달라요달랐어요—다르면
고르다 (choose)골라요골랐어요고르세요고르면

Also: 자르다 → 잘라요 (cut), 누르다 → 눌러요 (press), 배부르다 → 배불러요 (full). A few 르 stems, such as 따르다 → 따라요 (follow, pour) and 들르다 → 들러요 (drop by), don't double the ㄹ — they just follow the ㅡ rule. They're rare at this level; if you meet one, a dictionary will show it.

🏠 5. The ㄹ family: ㄹ drops before ㄴ, ㅂ, ㅅ

This family is the mirror image of the others: with -아/어 endings it's perfectly regular (살다 → 살아요). It changes before consonants. Two rules:

💡 Formula

Rule 1: the stem's ㄹ drops before an ending beginning with ㄴ, ㅂ or ㅅ: 살 + 세요 → 사세요, 살 + ㅂ니다 → 삽니다, 살 + 는 → 사는.

Rule 2: an ㄹ-stem never takes the extra 으: 살 + (으)면 → 살면, 만들 + (으)ㄹ 수 → 만들 수 (you met this in Lesson 19).

DictionaryPresentPastHonorificFormal -ㅂ니다-(으)면
살다 (live)살아요살았어요사세요삽니다살면
만들다 (make)만들어요만들었어요만드세요만듭니다만들면
알다 (know)알아요알았어요아세요압니다알면
놀다 (play, hang out)놀아요놀았어요노세요놉니다놀면
열다 (open)열어요열었어요여세요엽니다열면

So that's why Lesson 19 had 창문을 여세요 from 열다. And here's a fun trap: 사세요 can come from 살다 ("Where do you live?" 어디에 사세요?) or from 사다 ("Please buy it!"). Context always settles it. As mentioned above, many grammars count this ㄹ-dropping as a regular, automatic rule — but it's still something you must actively apply.

🩹 6. The ㅅ family: ㅅ disappears (and nothing contracts)

A small group of verbs lose their final ㅅ before a vowel. The unusual part: after the ㅅ disappears, the vowels do not contract. 나 + 아요 stays 나아요 (not 나요), and 지 + 어요 stays 지어요 (not 져요). The 으 of -(으)세요 also stays.

DictionaryPresentPastHonorific-(으)면
낫다 (get better, recover)나아요나았어요나으세요나으면
짓다 (build, make up)지어요지었어요지으세요지으면
붓다 (swell, pour)부어요부었어요부으세요부으면

낫다 is the star of this family after Lesson 20: 감기가 다 나았어요. ("My cold is completely better.") 짓다 appears in 집을 짓다 (build a house) and 이름을 짓다 (choose a name). 붓다 describes swelling: 얼굴이 부었어요 (my face is puffy).

🎨 7. The ㅎ family: colour & "how/so" adjectives

Adjectives ending in ㅎ — mostly colours and the 이렇다/그렇다/저렇다/어떻다 set — lose the ㅎ before a vowel, and the vowel + 아/어 fuses into ㅐ (ㅑ fuses into ㅒ). Before 으, the ㅎ and the 으 both vanish.

DictionaryPresentPastHonorific-(으)면 / modifier
어떻다 (be how)어때요어땠어요어떠세요어떤 (what kind of)
그렇다 (be so)그래요그랬어요그러세요그러면 (then, if so)
빨갛다 (red)빨개요빨갰어요—빨가면 / 빨간
노랗다 (yellow)노래요노랬어요—노라면 / 노란
하얗다 (white)하얘요하얬어요—하야면 / 하얀

You've been using this family since your first week: 어때요? ("How is it?") and 그래요? ("Really? / Is that so?") are ㅎ-irregulars! And 어떻게 ("how") keeps its ㅎ because -게 starts with a consonant. (Modifier forms like 빨간 are previewed here; you'll learn modifiers properly in Lesson 25.)

🚫 Regular Look-alikes — the ones that don't change

This is where learners slip: not every word ending in ㅂ, ㄷ, ㅅ or ㅎ is irregular. These common words are completely regular:

💡 How do I know which is which?

Honestly: for ㄷ and ㅅ you learn each word as you meet it — the irregular groups are small, so the table above covers the ones you'll use most. For the other two, rules of thumb help: ㅂ adjectives are almost all irregular (좁다 is the famous exception), and ㅎ is irregular only in adjectives — every ㅎ verb (넣다, 놓다, 낳다) and 좋다 is regular. Good dictionaries mark irregular verbs, so when in doubt, look it up. There's even a well-known pair: 묻다 "to ask" is irregular (물어요), but 묻다 "to bury" is regular (묻어요).

🇰🇷 Cultural note — weather talk and 고와요. Korean small talk runs on ㅂ-irregulars: 오늘 정말 더워요! in the humid August heat, 많이 추워요? in January, and 안 매워요? as a caring question to a foreign guest at dinner. And 곱다 — one of only two words with the 와 form — has a gentle, slightly old-fashioned warmth. Koreans use 고와요 to praise a grandmother's hanbok, soft skin, or a kind heart (마음이 고와요): a beautiful word to know.

Did you catch them? ㅅ: 나았어요 · ㅡ: 바빠요 · ㅂ: 추워요, 쉬워요, 고마워요, 가까워요, 도와줄게요 · ㄷ: 들을, 걸을, 걸어요 · ㄹ: 만들 · 르: 몰라요. And two look-alikes: 맵지 (consonant ending, so ㅂ stays) and 입으세요 (입다 is regular).

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 덥어요 / 듣어요 ✗ → 더워요 / 들어요 ✓. The most common irregular slip of all.
  • 돕다 → 도워요 ✗ → 도와요 ✓. 돕다 and 곱다 take 와. Every other ㅂ-irregular takes 워.
  • 나요 / 져요 ✗ → 나아요 / 지어요 ✓. ㅅ-irregulars never contract after the ㅅ drops.
  • 몰아요 ✗ → 몰라요 ✓. 르 verbs need the doubled ㄹ.
  • 살으세요 / 만들으면 ✗ → 사세요 / 만들면 ✓. ㄹ-stems never take 으, and lose ㄹ before ㅅ.
  • Over-applying the rules: 입다 → 이워요 ✗, 받다 → 발아요 ✗, 웃다 → 우어요 ✗. These are regular: 입어요, 받아요, 웃어요.

Irregular verbs are one of the places where every learner — and plenty of Korean children — makes mistakes for a while. That's fine. Each time you catch yourself saying 덥어요 and correct it to 더워요, the right form gets a little more automatic. You're not bad at irregulars; you just haven't said them enough times yet.

❓ Common Questions

Do I have to memorise which words are irregular?

Partly. The ㅡ, 르 and ㄹ families are predictable from the spelling alone. For ㅂ, "adjective = irregular" is right almost every time. For ㄷ and ㅅ, the irregular groups are small — learn 듣다, 걷다, 묻다, 낫다, 짓다 and 붓다 now, and add others as you meet them.

Why is 좋다 regular when 빨갛다 isn't?

The ㅎ-irregular pattern belongs to adjectives ending in -앟다/-엏다/-얗다 — mostly colours plus 이렇다, 그렇다, 저렇다, 어떻다. 좋다 has a different shape and stays regular (좋아요, 좋은). Words with a double final like 많다 and 괜찮다 are also regular.

Is 바빠요 really "irregular"? My textbook disagrees.

Your textbook may well be right! Korean school grammar treats ㅡ-dropping (and ㄹ-dropping) as regular sound rules because they apply to every such stem with no exceptions. Other textbooks group them with irregulars because the form changes visibly. The label differs; the forms don't.

Why is it 반갑습니다 but 반가워요?

Because -습니다 starts with a consonant, so the ㅂ stays. -어요 starts with a vowel, so the ㅂ becomes 우. It's the same rule, applied consistently.
