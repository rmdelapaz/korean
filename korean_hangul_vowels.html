🇰🇷 Lesson 2: Hangul Vowels & Syllable Blocks

🎯 What You'll Learn

Master all 21 Korean vowels (10 basic + 11 compound) and understand how consonants and vowels combine into syllable blocks — the fundamental unit of written Korean.

Estimated Time: 50–60 minutes

📖 How Vowels Work in Hangul

Korean vowels are built from three elements that King Sejong based on Neo-Confucian philosophy:

  • ㆍ (dot) — represents the sky/heaven (양, yang)
  • ㅡ (horizontal line) — represents the earth (음, yin)
  • ㅣ (vertical line) — represents a person standing

All vowels are combinations of these elements. Vowels are either vertical (written to the right of a consonant) or horizontal (written below a consonant).

✅ Pattern: Plain → "Y" Version

Adding an extra stroke turns a vowel into its "y" version:

ㅏ (a) → ㅑ (ya)  ·  ㅓ (eo) → ㅕ (yeo)  ·  ㅗ (o) → ㅛ (yo)  ·  ㅜ (u) → ㅠ (yu)

⚠️ ㅐ vs ㅔ

In modern Korean, ㅐ and ㅔ are pronounced nearly identically (both sound like "eh"). Young Koreans often can't distinguish them by sound alone — they rely on context and spelling. Don't stress about hearing the difference!

🧱 Building Syllable Blocks

Korean is written in syllable blocks. Each block is one syllable, combining a consonant + vowel (and optionally a final consonant). Every block must start with a consonant — if a syllable starts with a vowel sound, use ㅇ as a silent placeholder.

Block Patterns

PatternStructureExampleReading
CV (horizontal vowel)Consonant on top, vowel below누nu
CV (vertical vowel)Consonant left, vowel right나na
CVC (horizontal vowel)Consonant, vowel, final consonant below눈nun (snow/eye)
CVC (vertical vowel)Consonant, vowel right, final consonant below남nam (south)

💡 The ㅇ Placeholder

Since every block needs a consonant, vowel-initial syllables use ㅇ:

아 = ㅇ + ㅏ = "a"  ·  오 = ㅇ + ㅗ = "o"  ·  우 = ㅇ + ㅜ = "u"

🎯 Summary

🎉 Key Takeaways

  • Korean has 21 vowels: 10 basic + 11 compound
  • Vowels are either vertical (right of consonant) or horizontal (below consonant)
  • Adding a stroke creates the "y" version: ㅏ→ㅑ, ㅓ→ㅕ, ㅗ→ㅛ, ㅜ→ㅠ
  • Korean is written in syllable blocks (consonant + vowel + optional final consonant)
  • Vowel-initial syllables use ㅇ as a silent placeholder
