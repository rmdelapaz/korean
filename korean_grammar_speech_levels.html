🇰🇷 Lesson 13: Speech Levels & Honorifics

In English, you can say "Where are you going?" to your best friend, your boss and your grandmother, and only your tone changes. In Korean, the verb itself changes. It's the part of Korean that feels most foreign at first, and it's also one of the most rewarding to learn. Once it clicks, you'll hear respect, closeness and distance in every sentence a Korean speaker says.

The good news is that you already speak one level well. Every -아요/어요 sentence since Lesson 7 has been in 해요체, the polite style that is safe with almost anyone. This lesson adds the pieces around it.

🎛️ The Mental Model: Two Separate Dials

Beginners often mix up two different systems. Picture two dials on a mixing desk.

💡 Dial 1 = the LISTENER · Dial 2 = the SUBJECT

Dial 1: speech level (the sentence ending). It shows your relationship with the person you are talking to: formal 합니다, polite 해요 or casual 반말.

Dial 2: honorific -(으)시- (inside the verb). It shows respect for the person doing the action, who is often but not always the listener.

The dials turn independently. You can talk casually to a friend about your respected grandmother, or politely to your teacher about your little brother.

Talking TO…ABOUT…KoreanEnglish
a close friendthe friend어디 가?Where are you going?
a close friendyour grandmother할머니 어디 가셔?Where's Grandma going?
your teacheryour teacher선생님, 어디 가세요?Where are you going, teacher?
your teacheryour little brother동생이 학교에 가요.My little brother is going to school.

Traditional grammars list six or more speech levels, but only three are used in everyday modern speech. Here is the same verb at each one.

🏛️ 합쇼체: the formal -(스)ㅂ니다 style

You already know several 합쇼체 words by heart: 감사합니다, 반갑습니다, 처음 뵙겠습니다. Now you'll learn how they're built. Unlike 해요, this ending does not care about vowel harmony. It only cares whether the stem ends in a vowel or a consonant.

✅ Formula: -(스)ㅂ니다 / -(스)ㅂ니까?

Vowel stem + ㅂ니다: 가 → 갑니다 · 하 → 합니다 · 마시 → 마십니다

Consonant stem + 습니다: 먹 → 먹습니다 · 있 → 있습니다 · 좋 → 좋습니다

ㄹ stem: drop ㄹ, then + ㅂ니다: 살 → 삽니다 · 만들 → 만듭니다 · 알 → 압니다

Noun + 입니다 (for any noun): 학생입니다 · 의사입니다

Questions: swap 다 for 까: 갑니까? · 먹습니까? · 학생입니까?

KoreanEnglish
저는 캐나다 사람입니다.I am Canadian.
회의는 세 시에 시작합니다.The meeting starts at three.
저는 서울에 삽니다.I live in Seoul.
질문이 있습니까?Are there any questions?
이번 역은 시청역입니다.This stop is City Hall Station. (subway announcement)

💡 Pronunciation: it's "-mnida", not "-bnida"

You met nasalization in Lesson 3. ㅂ before ㄴ is pronounced ㅁ, so 합니다 is said [함니다] hamnida, and 먹습니다 is said [먹씀니다] meokseumnida. Revised Romanization writes the sound, which is why you see "gamsahamnida".

😊 해요체: your trusty default

You already know how to build it: stem + -아요/어요/해요, with 이에요/예요 for nouns. It is polite but warm, and it's what Koreans use with shop staff, colleagues, neighbours and new acquaintances. In real conversation, even people who open with 합니다 usually slide into 해요 within a few minutes. When in doubt, use 해요. You'll rarely go wrong with it.

🤝 반말: casual speech (for recognising)

반말 literally means "half speech", the opposite of 존댓말 (polite speech). The basic rule is short: take the 해요 form and drop the 요.

해요체반말Note

먹어요 / 마셔요먹어 / 마셔just drop 요
해요해just drop 요
학생이에요 / 친구예요학생이야 / 친구야이에요 → 이야, 예요 → 야
아니에요아니야"it isn't"
네 / 아니요응 (or 어) / 아니yes / no

In 반말 you call friends by name plus 야 (after a vowel) or 아 (after a consonant): 민지야! 준호야! 민석아! For "you", use 너, which is only for 반말. In polite speech Koreans avoid "you" altogether and use the person's name or title instead.

⚠️ Recognise first, use by invitation

Using 반말 uninvited with an adult you don't know is genuinely rude, and far worse than an English speaker skipping "please". Learn to understand it now, since you'll hear it constantly in dramas and among friends. Only use it when someone offers: 말 편하게 하세요 ("Please talk comfortably with me").

🙇 Dial 2: The Honorific -(으)시-

The honorific infix -(으)시- slides in between the stem and the ending. It lifts up the subject of the sentence: a teacher, a boss, a parent, a grandparent, a customer, or the older stranger you're speaking to. In 해요체, -시- + -어요 fuses into -세요, which is the form you'll use most.

✅ Formula: stem + (으)세요 / (으)십니다

Vowel stem + 세요: 가 → 가세요 · 오 → 오세요 · 하 → 하세요 · 보 → 보세요

Consonant stem + 으세요: 읽 → 읽으세요 · 앉 → 앉으세요 · 받 → 받으세요

ㄹ stem: drop ㄹ + 세요: 살 → 사세요 · 알 → 아세요 · 만들 → 만드세요

Noun + (이)세요: 선생님이세요 · 누구세요? · 어디세요?

Formal version: -(으)십니다 / -(으)십니까?: 가십니다 · 읽으십니까?

💡 One form, three jobs

Honestly, -(으)세요 is busy. 가세요 can be a statement ("[He] goes"), a question ("Are you going?"), or a polite request ("Please go"). Intonation and context sort it out. You'll study the request use properly in Lesson 19. For now, just know that 앉으세요 ("please have a seat") is the same ending.

🔭 Preview: honorific past -(으)셨어요

You'll learn the past tense in Lesson 15. As a sneak peek, -시- + past becomes -(으)셨어요: 가셨어요 ("went", honorific), 하셨어요 ("did"). You'll hear the greeting 식사하셨어요? ("Have you eaten?") every day. Just recognise it for now.

👑 Special Honorific & Humble Verbs

A handful of very common verbs don't just add -시-. They switch to a completely different word, a bit like English "dine" instead of "eat". They come in two types. Honorific verbs lift up the person doing the action. Humble verbs lower yourself when your action goes toward a respected person.

⚠️ 계세요 vs 있으세요

계시다 is only for a respected person being somewhere: 선생님이 교실에 계세요. When a respected person has something, use 있으세요: 선생님은 차가 있으세요. So "Do you have time?" is 시간 있으세요?, never 시간 계세요? The negative follows the same split: 안 계세요 ("isn't in") vs 없으세요 ("doesn't have").

Two honest notes. First, 께서 is fairly formal. In relaxed speech 아버지가 오세요 is perfectly fine, as long as the verb carries -시-, and the verb matters most. Second, 말씀 많이 들었어요 is in the past tense. Learn it as a set phrase for now, and Lesson 15 will explain it.

🚫 The golden rule: never honour yourself

-(으)시- and the honorific verbs are only for other people. When you talk about yourself, you go the other way and use the humble 저 (I), 제 (my) and 저희 (we, our). "저는 선생님이세요" sounds like you're bowing to yourself. Say 저는 선생님이에요 or 저는 선생님입니다. One exception is worth knowing: Koreans say 우리나라 ("our country") even in humble speech, because a nation isn't lowered before anyone.

💡 Culture Note: why Koreans ask your age. A new acquaintance may ask your age, or your birth year, within minutes: 몇 년생이에요? It isn't nosiness. Age decides who is 선배 (senior) and who is 후배 (junior), who pours drinks for whom, and whether you can switch to 반말. Two people born in the same year are 동갑, and 동갑 friends often agree to drop formalities right away.
☕ Culture Note: "Your coffee has honourably come out." In cafés and shops you'll hear staff honour objects: 주문하신 커피 나오셨습니다, 만 원이세요. This "over-honorification" (사물 존대) comes from wanting to sound extra polite. The National Institute of Korean Language calls it non-standard, because coffee doesn't deserve -시-. The correct forms are 커피 나왔습니다 and 만 원입니다. Understand it when you hear it, but don't copy it.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 저는 학생이세요 ✗ → 저는 학생이에요 ✓. Never use -시- about yourself.
  • 시간 계세요? ✗ → 시간 있으세요? ✓. 계시다 is only for a person's location.
  • 할머니가 자요 (too plain) → 할머니가 주무세요 ✓. Special verbs replace the plain verb completely. Don't add -시- to the plain one (자세요 is wrong here).
  • 먹습니다 ≠ 먹읍니다. Consonant stems take 습니다. ("읍니다" is an old spelling abolished in 1988.)
  • 너 to a stranger. 너 is 반말 only. Politely, use the person's name + 씨 or their title, e.g. 선생님.
  • Mixing up the dials. 합니다 is not "more honorific" than 해요 in the -시- sense. It is more formal. 가요 and 가세요 differ in respect for the subject, while 가요 and 갑니다 differ in formality of the situation.

This system is a lot to hold at once, and Korean children take years to master it too. Every time someone gently corrects your 반말 or your 계세요, that correction is the lesson working. Mistakes here are just feedback.

❓ Common Questions

If I only learn one level, which should it be?

해요체, plus -(으)세요 for the person you're talking to. "어디 가세요?", "뭐 하세요?" and "시간 있으세요?" will carry you politely through almost any situation as an adult learner. Learn 합니다 for set phrases, presentations and formal writing, and 반말 for recognition.

Are 합니다 and 해요 different in politeness?

Not really. They differ in formality. 합니다 sounds official, crisp and public, and 해요 sounds warm and personal. A newsreader uses 합니다, and a kind neighbour of the same status uses 해요. Both are fully polite. Many speakers mix them in the same conversation, often opening and closing with 합니다 and chatting in 해요.

Will Koreans be offended if I get honorifics wrong?

Almost never, if you're clearly a learner and clearly trying. Leaving out -시- with an older person sounds slightly blunt, not insulting. What does offend people is uninvited 반말 with a stranger or an elder. If you stay in 해요, you're safe.

What should I call people instead of "you"?

Use their name + 씨 (민지 씨) for peers, or a title for anyone senior: 선생님, 교수님, 사장님, or 사모님 for a respectful older woman. You'll also hear family words used for strangers, such as 이모 for the auntie at a restaurant. 당신 exists, but it is mostly used between spouses or in arguments, so avoid it.

🌟 Going Further

  • Three-way rewrite. Take five sentences from your Lesson 12 routine (e.g. 일곱 시에 일어나요) and rewrite each twice. First make it formal (일곱 시에 일어납니다). Then make it about your grandmother (할머니는 일곱 시에 일어나세요). Watch for 자다 → 주무시다 and 먹다 → 드시다!
  • Drama detective. Watch a scene from any K-drama with Korean subtitles. Count how many lines end in -요 and how many don't, and notice who switches level with whom. The switches often mark the story's turning points.
