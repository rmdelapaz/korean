🇰🇷 Lesson 8: Family & Personal Descriptions

In English, "brother" covers a lot of ground. In Korean, the word you use for a sibling depends on who is older and on your own gender, and a relative's title can tell you which side of the family they come from. That isn't extra complication for its own sake: Korean family words carry the information Korean grammar cares about most — age and relationship. Learn them here and you'll already understand half of the politeness system you meet in Lesson 13.

⚠️ Sibling Terms Depend on YOUR Gender

A male calls his older brother 형 and his older sister 누나. A female calls her older brother 오빠 and her older sister 언니. These words are also used with close friends and acquaintances who are a little older — which is why fans call a male idol 오빠. Younger siblings are all simply 동생; you add 남- or 여- only when gender matters.

🌳 Mother's Side, Father's Side

Korean often marks which side of the family someone belongs to. The prefix 외- (literally "outside") marks the mother's side; 친- can mark the father's side when you need to be specific.

In real families you'll also hear 큰아버지 and 작은아버지 for the father's married older and younger brothers. You don't need them yet — just know the system is precise on purpose.

🌏 Culture Note — "Our" Mom: Koreans usually say 우리 엄마 ("our mom") rather than "my mom", even when they are an only child. Family, home, school and country are things you share, so 우리 (our) feels warmer and more natural than 제 (my). When being humble to someone senior, 우리 becomes 저희. Family words also stretch far beyond family: a friendly restaurant worker may be called 이모, and older neighbours become 아저씨 and 아주머니.

🎨 Describing People

You met the key idea in Lesson 7: Korean adjectives conjugate exactly like verbs. There is no separate word for "is" — 커요 already means "is big/tall". Here are the words you'll use most for people:

💡 Two Forms Worth Noticing

커요, 예뻐요, 바빠요: when a stem ends in ㅡ, the ㅡ drops before -아요/어요. Memorise these three now; Lesson 21 gives you the full rule.

잘생겼어요: Koreans describe good looks with what is technically a past-tense ending ("has turned out handsome"). It means "is handsome" right now. Learn it as a set phrase; past endings come in Lesson 15.

From Lesson 5: the "double subject" 은/는 … 이/가

To describe a feature of a person, Korean puts the person in 은/는 (the topic — "as for my brother") and the feature in 이/가 (the subject of the adjective). English does something similar with "My brother, he's tall."

✅ The Formula

Person + 은/는  +  Feature + 이/가  +  Adjective

형은 키가 커요. = As for my older brother, (his) height is big → "My brother is tall."

KoreanEnglish

할머니는 마음이 따뜻해요.My grandmother is warm-hearted.
누나는 머리가 길어요.My older sister has long hair.
아빠는 목소리가 커요.My dad has a loud voice.

💡 A Note on Grandparents

할아버지는 신문을 읽어요 is grammatically correct, but Korean speakers talking about grandparents or parents usually add a respectful -(으)시- to the verb: 할아버지는 신문을 읽으세요. You'll learn exactly how in Lesson 13. For now, the plain polite form is fine — people will understand you perfectly.

And from Lesson 6, counting family members: 가족이 몇 명이에요? — 다섯 명이에요. To say you have a sibling, use the set phrase 형이 있어요 ("I have an older brother") or 동생이 없어요 ("I have no younger siblings"); 있어요/없어요 are taught fully in Lesson 9.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 오빠 used by a male speaker → males say 형 for an older brother.
  • 제 부인 for "my wife" → say 제 아내 (or casually 와이프); 부인 is respectful and used for other people's wives.
  • 저의 엄마 → natural Korean says 우리 엄마 (or 제 어머니).
  • 잘생겨요 for "is handsome" → the natural form is 잘생겼어요.
  • 형은 커요 is understood, but for height Koreans say 형은 키가 커요.
  • 동생가 → 생 has a final consonant, so it's 동생이.

Family words take time — even Koreans sometimes stop to work out what to call a cousin's spouse. If you pick the wrong sibling word today, you've just found the one to review tomorrow.

❓ Common Questions

Can I call a friend 오빠 or 언니 if we're not related?

Yes — that's very common between friends with a small age gap, once you're close. Using 오빠/언니/형/누나 signals warmth. With a new acquaintance, wait until the older person suggests it, or use their name + 씨.

Why is it 우리 엄마 when I have no siblings?

우리 here isn't about counting people — it expresses belonging to a group. Your mother, house, school and country all "belong" to your group, so 우리 is simply the natural possessive.

What if I don't know if the person is older than me?

Use a neutral option: their name + 씨, or a title such as 선생님. This uncertainty is exactly why Koreans often ask your age (몇 살이에요?) soon after meeting.

Is 동생 male or female?

Either. 동생 just means "younger sibling". Add 남 (male) or 여 (female) when you need to specify: 남동생, 여동생.
