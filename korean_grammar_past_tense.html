🇰🇷 Lesson 15: The Past Tense (-았/었어요)

Up to now you could say what you do: 가요, 먹어요, 공부해요. But most conversations start with what already happened — "How was your weekend?", "Did you eat?", "I went to Busan last year." Good news: if you can build the present tense from Lesson 7, you already know 90% of the past tense. Korean uses the same vowel-harmony rule and the same contractions. You just add one extra piece.

🧠 The Mental Model: One Extra Block

Think of a Korean verb as a train of blocks: stem + (honorific) + (tense) + ending. In Lesson 7 you built stem + 아요/어요. The past tense slides a tense block, 았 / 었, between the stem and the ending, and after that ㅆ the ending is 어요 every single time. So the whole job is choosing 았 or 었 — using the very same vowel check you already do for 아요 and 어요.

English does something similar: walk → walked. The difference is that English "‑ed" has dozens of exceptions (go → went, eat → ate). Korean's past marker is almost perfectly regular. The only "exceptions" are the irregular verb classes you'll meet in Lesson 21 — and even those follow their own clear rules.

Shortcut: take the present form you already know, drop the 요, and add ㅆ어요. 가요 → 가 + ㅆ어요 → 갔어요. 먹어요 → 먹어 + ㅆ어요 → 먹었어요. 해요 → 해 + ㅆ어요 → 했어요. It works for every regular verb.

Pronunciation tip: the ㅆ carries over into the next syllable, so 갔어요 sounds like [가써요] and 먹었어요 like [머거써요]. That strong "ss" sound is the audible signal of the past — listen for it.

💡 Contracted vs. Full Forms

Some verbs have a "full" written form too: 보았어요, 주었어요, 되었어요 are correct and appear in careful writing and songs. In speech, the contracted forms (봤어요, 줬어요, 됐어요) are what you'll hear almost every time. 오다 is always 왔어요, and 가다 is always 갔어요 — there is no "가았어요".

🎩 Past + Honorific + Formal

Lesson 13 gave you the honorific block -(으)시- and the formal 합니다 style. They combine with the past block in a fixed order: stem + 시 + 었 + ending. 시 + 었 merges into 셨.

Style가다하다먹다
Polite past (해요)갔어요했어요먹었어요
Formal past (합니다)갔습니다했습니다먹었습니다
Honorific polite past가셨어요하셨어요드셨어요
Honorific formal past가셨습니다하셨습니다드셨습니다

Because the past block ends in ㅆ (a consonant), the formal ending is always -습니다, never -ㅂ니다: 갔습니다, 왔습니다, 이었습니다, 였습니다. Formal questions end in -습니까?: 식사하셨습니까? "Have you had your meal?" And don't forget the special honorific verbs: 먹다 → 드시다 → 드셨어요, 자다 → 주무시다 → 주무셨어요.

Notice 잘했어요 at the end — literally "you did well". It's the all-purpose past-tense compliment: "Well done!", "Good call!", "Nice work!" You'll hear it constantly in classrooms, too, so every time you get something wrong in the exercises below, remember that the mistake is just the step before 잘했어요.

🌏 Culture Note — 밥 먹었어요? (Did you eat?): One of the most common greetings in Korea is a past-tense question: 밥 먹었어요? It isn't really about food — it's a way of saying "Are you doing okay?" that grew out of harder times when having a meal wasn't guaranteed. A simple 네, 먹었어요. 수진 씨는요? is the perfect answer, even if you only had a coffee. With an elder or your boss, the honorific version is 식사하셨어요?

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to contract: ✗ 가았어요, ✗ 오았어요 → ✓ 갔어요, ✓ 왔어요.
  • Treating 하다 as a normal ㅏ-verb: ✗ 공부하았어요 → ✓ 공부했어요.
  • Mixing up 이었어요 and 였어요: ✗ 학생였어요 → ✓ 학생이었어요 (consonant ending); ✓ 의사였어요 (vowel ending).
  • Adding 에 to 어제: ✗ 어제에 갔어요 → ✓ 어제 갔어요. But ✓ 지난주에 갔어요.
  • Putting 안 in front of a whole 하다-verb: ✗ 안 공부했어요 → ✓ 공부 안 했어요.
  • Using -ㅂ니다 after the past: ✗ 갔ㅂ니다 / ✗ 갔니다 → ✓ 갔습니다.

❓ Common Questions

Does -았/었어요 mean "did" or "have done"?

Both. Korean doesn't split the simple past and the present perfect the way English does. 밥 먹었어요 can mean "I ate" or "I've eaten"; 숙제 했어요? can mean "Did you do your homework?" or "Have you done your homework?" One past form covers both English tenses, and context does the rest. (A separate pattern for "have you ever…?" arrives in Lesson 25.)

Why is 결혼했어요 "I'm married" and not "I got married"?

Some Korean verbs describe a change of state, and the past tense describes the finished change — which is still true now. 결혼했어요 is "I (have) married" → "I'm married". Likewise 늦었어요 is "I'm late" (I've become late), and 알았어요 is "Got it" (I've come to understand). When you hear a past form describing something clearly true right now, this is usually why.

What about verbs like 듣다, 춥다 or 바쁘다?

Those are irregular verbs: 듣다 → 들었어요, 춥다 → 추웠어요, 바쁘다 → 바빴어요. The past block itself is perfectly regular; what changes is the stem, in the same way it changes in the present (들어요, 추워요, 바빠요). You'll learn all seven irregular classes in Lesson 21 — until then, learn these few as whole words.

I've seen 갔었어요 with two ㅆ blocks. What is that?

That's the "double past" -았었/었었-. It stresses that something happened and is no longer the case: 부산에 갔었어요 "I had been to Busan (and came back)". It's real and common in speech, but subtle. At A1–A2, the single past is always a safe choice.
