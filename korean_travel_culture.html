🎎 Korean Cultural Customs

Bowing (인사)

Bowing is the standard greeting in Korea. A slight nod (15°) for casual greetings, a deeper bow (45°) for formal situations or showing respect to elders. When giving or receiving objects (especially to elders), use both hands or support your right arm with your left hand.

Shoes Off (신발)

Always remove shoes when entering Korean homes, many traditional restaurants, and temples. Look for a shoe rack at the entrance — that's your cue!

Age & Hierarchy

Age matters deeply in Korea. One of the first questions Koreans ask new acquaintances is their age (나이가 어떻게 되세요?) to determine the appropriate speech level. The older person leads: they eat first, drink first, and you use polite speech with them.

Drinking Etiquette

When drinking with elders, turn your head away while taking a sip. When someone older pours you a drink, hold your glass with both hands. You pour for others, never for yourself.

💡 설날 & 추석

These are Korea's two biggest holidays. Traffic is extreme (everyone goes to their hometown), and most businesses close. During 설날, children perform 세배 (deep formal bow to elders) and receive 세뱃돈 (money in envelopes). During 추석, families visit ancestors' graves and make 송편 together.
