🎎 Korean Cultural Customs

Bowing (인사)

Bowing is the standard greeting in Korea. A slight nod (about 15°) for casual greetings, a deeper bow (about 45°) for formal situations or to show respect to elders. When giving or receiving something, especially with elders, use both hands or support your right arm with your left hand.

Shoes Off (신발)

Always remove your shoes when entering Korean homes, many traditional restaurants, some guesthouses and temple halls. A shoe rack or a step up at the entrance is your cue.

Age & Hierarchy

Age matters deeply in Korea. New acquaintances often ask your age (나이가 어떻게 되세요?) early on, because it decides who uses which speech level — exactly the system you learned in Lesson 13. The older person leads: they start eating first, and you use polite speech with them.

Drinking Etiquette

When drinking with elders, turn your head slightly away while you take a sip. When someone older pours for you, hold your glass with both hands. Traditionally you pour for others, not for yourself — so keep an eye on your neighbours' glasses.

💡 설날 & 추석

These are Korea's two biggest holidays. Traffic is extreme (everyone heads to their hometown), and many businesses close. On 설날, children perform 세배 (a deep formal bow to elders) and receive 세뱃돈 (New Year's money). On 추석, families visit ancestors' graves and make 송편 together. The usual greetings are 새해 복 많이 받으세요! for New Year and 추석 잘 보내세요! for Chuseok.

⚠️ Common Mistakes (a final checklist)

  • 비가 와서 우산을 가져가세요 ✗ → 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요 ✓. -아서/어서 can't be followed by a command; -(으)니까 can (Lesson 23).
  • 어제 도착했어서 피곤했어요 ✗ → 어제 도착해서 피곤했어요 ✓. No past tense before -아서/어서 — the final verb carries the tense.
  • Answering 어디에서 오셨어요? with 미국에서 오셨어요 ✗ → 미국에서 왔어요 ✓. Never use honorific -(으)시- about yourself.
  • 여기서 사진 찍으면 돼요? is okay, but for "May I…?" the standard is 찍어도 돼요?
  • 한글날 = Korean Thanksgiving ✗ — that's 추석. 한글날 is Hangul Day.

If one of these still trips you up, that's not failure — it's the next thing to polish. Every fluent speaker you'll ever meet made these same mistakes on the way.

❓ Common Questions

Do I need to speak Korean to travel in Korea?

No — signs in cities are bilingual and many young people speak some English. But even the phrases in this lesson change the experience completely: staff relax, people help more readily, and you understand what's going on around you.

What is TOPIK, and am I ready for it?

TOPIK (한국어능력시험) is the official Korean proficiency test. TOPIK I covers levels 1–2 with listening and reading sections; this course targets exactly that range (NIKL grades 1–2). If you can do the exercises in Lessons 21–28 comfortably, a TOPIK I practice test is a great next step to see where you stand. TOPIK II (levels 3–6) adds writing.

I've finished the course but still feel slow when I speak. Is that normal?

Completely. Knowing a pattern and producing it in real time are different skills, and the second only grows with use. Read the course readings aloud, shadow Korean audio, and speak with a partner — speed follows repetition.

Should I learn 반말 now?

You can recognise it already (Lessons 13 and 26). Actively using it with friends is a natural B1 step — just wait until a Korean friend invites you (말 편하게 하세요) before switching.
