🇰🇷 Lesson 17: Wants, Plans & the Future

In Lesson 15 you learned to look back: 갔어요, 먹었어요, 재미있었어요. Now we look forward. This lesson gives you the endings for everything that hasn't happened yet: what you want, what you plan, what you promise, what you suggest, and why you're going somewhere. That sounds like a lot, but every pattern here is built the same way — stem + ending — and most of them share one small helper sound, (으). Master that helper and the whole lesson falls into place.

🧠 The Mental Model: The (으) Cushion

Most endings in this lesson start with a consonant sound: -ㄹ 거예요, -ㅂ시다, -러, -려고. Korean doesn't like to pile a consonant straight onto a stem that already ends in a consonant, so it slips in a soft "cushion" vowel, 으. Think of it like English adding "a" vs. "an" to keep sounds smooth.

💡 The (으) Rule

Stem ends in…What happensExample with -(으)ㄹ 거예요
a vowelno 으 — attach directly가다 → 갈 거예요
a consonantadd 으먹다 → 먹을 거예요
ㄹno 으 — the ㄹ is already there만들다 → 만들 거예요

The ㄹ-stem row is the one to watch. Verbs like 살다, 만들다, 놀다 and 열다 never take the cushion (✗ 만들을 거예요). Before ㅂ they even drop their ㄹ: 놀다 → 놉시다. You'll get the full story of ㄹ-stems in Lesson 21; for now, just remember "ㄹ-stems: no 으".

The only pattern in this lesson without the cushion is the first one, -고 싶다, because -고 attaches straight to every stem.

💭 1. Wanting: -고 싶어요

Attach -고 싶어요 to any verb stem to say "I want to…". The stem never changes.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
비빔밥을 먹고 싶어요.bibimbabeul meokgo sipeoyoI want to eat bibimbap.
한국에 가고 싶어요.hanguge gago sipeoyoI want to go to Korea.
오늘은 집에서 쉬고 싶어요.oneureun jibeseo swigo sipeoyoToday I want to rest at home.
뭐 하고 싶어요?mwo hago sipeoyoWhat do you want to do?
그 영화를 정말 보고 싶었어요.geu yeonghwareul jeongmal bogo sipeosseoyoI really wanted to see that movie.
오늘은 가고 싶지 않아요.oneureun gago sipji anayoI don't want to go today.

싶다 is itself an adjective, so it conjugates like one: 싶어요 (present), 싶었어요 (past — Lesson 15!), 싶지 않아요 (negative — Lesson 10). Note that -고 싶다 is only for verbs. To want a thing, use a different word: 커피를 원해요 or, more naturally, 커피 마시고 싶어요 / 커피 주세요.

Other people's wishes: -고 싶어해요

Korean is careful about claiming to know what's inside someone else's head. You can say "I want" directly, and you can ask "do you want?", but for a third person you describe the wish from the outside with -고 싶어해요 — roughly "shows that they want".

KoreanEnglish
저는 강아지를 키우고 싶어요.I want to raise a puppy.
수진 씨도 강아지를 키우고 싶어요?Do you want to raise a puppy too, Sujin?
동생이 강아지를 키우고 싶어해요.My little brother wants to raise a puppy.

This is the everyday future. It means "I'm going to…" for plans, and "it'll probably…" for predictions.

Pronunciation: after -(으)ㄹ, the ㄱ of 거 is tensed, so 갈 거예요 sounds like [갈 꺼예요], and 거예요 itself is often pronounced [거에요]. But the spelling is always 거예요. Negatives work as usual: 안 갈 거예요 "I'm not going to go". With someone you honor, add -(으)시-: 언제 가실 거예요? "When will you go?"

🤝 3. Promises & Offers: -(으)ㄹ게요

-(으)ㄹ게요 is a future with a relationship built in. It means "I'll… (for you / since you mention it / I promise)". It is a response to the listener and the situation.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
제가 할게요.jega halgeyoI'll do it (leave it to me).
이따가 전화할게요.ittaga jeonhwahalgeyoI'll call you later.
여기서 기다릴게요.yeogiseo gidarilgeyoI'll wait here (for you).
잘 먹을게요!jal meogeulgeyoI'll enjoy the food! (thanking the host)
제가 낼게요.jega naelgeyoI'll pay.

💡 거예요 or 게요?

-(으)ㄹ 거예요-(으)ㄹ게요
States a plan or prediction as information.Commits to the listener, often in response to them.
Any subject: 저, 친구, 비…Only "I" (or "we") as the subject.
Works in questions: 갈 거예요?Not used for questions.
Works with adjectives (predictions).Only action verbs.
내일 갈 거예요. "I'm going tomorrow." (my schedule)내일 갈게요. "I'll go tomorrow, then." (agreeing with you)

🙋 4. Suggestions: -(으)ㄹ까요? and "Let's"

-(으)ㄹ까요? literally asks "shall…?" With 우리 or 같이 it means "shall we?"; with 제가 it offers "shall I?"; and about the world it wonders aloud, "do you think…?"

KoreanEnglishUse
같이 갈까요?Shall we go together?shall we
우리 점심 먹을까요?Shall we have lunch?shall we
몇 시에 만날까요?What time shall we meet?shall we
제가 창문을 열까요?Shall I open the window?shall I (offer)
내일 비가 올까요?Do you think it'll rain tomorrow?I wonder

To say "let's" there are two tools:

  • -아요/어요 with 같이 or 우리 — the plain present you already know, used as a suggestion: 같이 가요! "Let's go!" 우리 커피 마셔요. "Let's have coffee." It's warm and works with almost anyone. Answering 같이 갈까요? with 네, 같이 가요! is perfect.
  • -(으)ㅂ시다 — a formal "let's": 갑시다! 먹읍시다! 시작합시다! "Let's begin!" It's used by a leader to a group, in announcements, or between equals. Because it sounds like you are directing people, don't use it to your boss, teacher or elders.

🎯 5. Going Somewhere to Do Something: -(으)러 가다/오다

English says "I'm going to buy a book" — and that "to" is ambiguous. Korean marks purpose with -(으)러, and it is only used with movement verbs: 가다, 오다, 다니다 (and their family, like 나가다, 들어오다).

KoreanEnglish
책을 사러 서점에 가요.I'm going to the bookstore to buy a book.
밥을 먹으러 식당에 가요.I'm going to a restaurant to eat.
친구를 만나러 명동에 갈 거예요.I'm going to Myeongdong to meet a friend.
한국어를 배우러 한국에 왔어요.I came to Korea to learn Korean.
주말에 친구 집에 놀러 갔어요.On the weekend I went over to a friend's place to hang out.

놀러 가다 "go to hang out / go on an outing" is one of the most common phrases in Korean — and a nice ㄹ-stem example: 놀다 → 놀러, not ✗ 놀으러.

🧭 6. Intentions: -(으)려고 해요

-(으)려고 해요 means "I intend to / I'm thinking of / I'm planning to". It's softer than -(으)ㄹ 거예요 — the plan is in your head, not necessarily on the calendar.

KoreanEnglish
내일은 집에서 쉬려고 해요.I'm planning to rest at home tomorrow.
내년에 태권도를 배우려고 해요.I'm thinking of learning taekwondo next year.
이 책을 이번 달에 읽으려고 해요.I intend to read this book this month.
서울에서 살려고 해요.I'm planning to live in Seoul.
어제 운동하려고 했어요. 그런데 비가 왔어요.I was going to work out yesterday. But it rained.

That last one is especially useful: -(으)려고 했어요 = "I was going to (but…)". It combines this lesson with Lesson 15's past tense.

💬 Dialogue: Weekend Plans

Sujin and Minjun are chatting on Thursday afternoon.

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
수진민준 씨, 이번 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?Minjun, what are you doing this weekend?
민준토요일에 친구를 만나러 명동에 갈 거예요. 수진 씨는요?On Saturday I'm going to Myeongdong to meet a friend. How about you, Sujin?
수진저는 집에서 쉬려고 해요. 요즘 너무 피곤해요. 그래도 영화는 보고 싶어요.I'm planning to rest at home. I've been so tired lately. I do want to see a movie, though.
민준그럼 일요일에 같이 영화 볼까요?Then shall we see a movie together on Sunday?
수진좋아요! 몇 시에 만날까요?Great! What time shall we meet?
민준두 시에 영화관 앞에서 만나요.Let's meet in front of the cinema at two.
수진네. 표는 제가 살게요.Okay. I'll buy the tickets.
민준고마워요. 그럼 팝콘은 제가 살게요!Thanks. Then I'll get the popcorn!

Count the patterns: 할 거예요 (plan question), 만나러 갈 거예요 (purpose + plan), 쉬려고 해요 (intention), 보고 싶어요 (want), 볼까요 / 만날까요 (suggestion), 만나요 (let's), 살게요 (promise). Seven tools in eight lines — that's what a real conversation about the future looks like.

🌏 Culture Note — "언제 밥 한번 먹어요!": You'll often hear 언제 밥 한번 먹어요 — "Let's have a meal sometime." It uses the "let's" -아요 you just learned, but it's usually a friendly way to end a conversation, like "We should get together sometime!" in English, rather than a real invitation. If someone actually wants to meet, they'll follow up with a day: 다음 주 금요일에 시간 있어요? And when you're treated to a meal, 잘 먹을게요 before eating (or the more formal set phrase 잘 먹겠습니다) and 잘 먹었습니다 afterwards are the expected thank-yous.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Using -고 싶어요 for someone else: ✗ 동생이 가고 싶어요 → ✓ 동생이 가고 싶어해요.
  • Adding 으 to ㄹ-stems: ✗ 만들을 거예요, ✗ 놀으러 → ✓ 만들 거예요, ✓ 놀러.
  • Misspelling 거예요: ✗ 갈 거에요 → ✓ 갈 거예요 (even though many people say [거에요]).
  • Using -(으)ㄹ게요 for other people or in questions: ✗ 친구가 올게요 → ✓ 친구가 올 거예요.
  • -(으)러 with a non-movement verb: ✗ 책을 사러 공부해요 → -(으)러 must be followed by 가다/오다/다니다.
  • Saying 갑시다 to your boss: it sounds like an order. Use 같이 가요 or 같이 가실까요?

Six patterns in one lesson is a lot, and mixing up 거예요 and 게요 for a while is completely normal — even intermediate learners do. Every mix-up you notice is your ear getting sharper.

❓ Common Questions

Can I say 물이 마시고 싶어요 with 이/가 instead of 을/를?

You will hear it. Because 싶다 is an adjective, the object sometimes gets marked with 이/가 in speech, especially to emphasize the thing you want. Both 물을 마시고 싶어요 and 물이 마시고 싶어요 are acceptable, but 을/를 is always correct, so use it while you're learning.

What about -겠어요? I've heard 잘 먹겠습니다.

-겠- is another future/intention marker, more formal or used for strong intention and conjecture (날씨가 좋겠어요 "The weather looks like it'll be nice"). At A2 you should recognize set phrases like 잘 먹겠습니다 and 알겠습니다 "I understand"; for your own sentences, -(으)ㄹ 거예요 and -(으)ㄹ게요 cover almost everything.

Is -(으)려고 해요 the same as -(으)ㄹ 거예요?

They overlap. -(으)ㄹ 거예요 sounds more settled ("I'm going to"), while -(으)려고 해요 foregrounds your intention ("I'm thinking of / I mean to"). The difference is clearest in the past: 가려고 했어요 means "I was going to go" — usually implying you didn't.

Can I just use the present tense for the future?

Yes, for scheduled or near-certain events, just like English "I leave tomorrow": 내일 부산에 가요. The future forms add the sense of plan, prediction or promise.
