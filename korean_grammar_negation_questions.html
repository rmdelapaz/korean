🇰🇷 Lesson 10: Negation & Asking Questions

🧭 The Big Idea: Two Kinds of "Not"

English uses one small word for almost every negative: "I don't go", "I can't go". Korean splits this into two separate ideas, each with its own word:

  • 안 — "don't / won't / isn't". A plain fact or a choice.
  • 못 — "can't". Something stops you: ability, circumstances, rules.

The good news: neither one changes the verb ending you learned in Lesson 7. You conjugate the verb exactly as before (가요, 먹어요, 공부해요) and simply put the negative word in front — or use a longer ending that means the same thing. Questions are even easier: in 해요 style, a statement and a question have identical words. Only your voice changes.

💡 Mental Model: A Switch in Front of the Verb

Picture 안 and 못 as switches mounted directly in front of the verb. They don't go at the start of the sentence like "not" can in English — they sit right before the verb they cancel: 저는 고기를 안 먹어요 (I meat not eat).

🚫 Short Negation: 안 + Verb

📐 Formula

안 + conjugated verb/adjective → 안 가요, 안 먹어요, 안 비싸요

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
저는 고기를 안 먹어요.jeoneun gogireul an meogeoyo.I don't eat meat.
오늘은 학교에 안 가요.oneureun hakgyoe an gayo.I'm not going to school today.
커피를 안 마셔요.keopireul an masyeoyo.I don't drink coffee.
이 가방은 안 비싸요.i gabangeun an bissayo.This bag isn't expensive.
동생이 안 와요.dongsaengi an wayo.My younger sibling isn't coming.

안 is the everyday form — short, quick and used constantly in conversation. It works with both verbs and adjectives.

📜 Long Negation: Stem + -지 않아요

The same meaning can be built with an ending instead. Take the stem (dictionary form minus 다), add -지 않아요. It sounds a little more careful and is common in writing, news and polite explanations, but it is not a different meaning.

📐 Formula

Stem + 지 않아요 → 먹다 → 먹지 않아요 · 가다 → 가지 않아요 · 비싸다 → 비싸지 않아요

Pronunciation: 않아요 sounds like [아나요] — the ㅎ goes silent.

Short (안)Long (-지 않아요)English
안 먹어요먹지 않아요don't eat
안 가요가지 않아요don't go
안 마셔요마시지 않아요don't drink
안 비싸요비싸지 않아요isn't expensive
공부 안 해요공부하지 않아요don't study

🧩 Where Does 안 Go with 하다 Verbs?

Many verbs are really noun + 하다: 공부하다 = 공부 (study) + 하다 (do). With these, 안 goes between the noun and 하다 — you "don't do study".

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
주말에는 공부 안 해요.jumareneun gongbu an haeyo.I don't study on weekends.
저는 운동 안 해요.jeoneun undong an haeyo.I don't exercise.
일요일에는 일 안 해요.iryoireneun il an haeyo.I don't work on Sundays.
집에서 요리 안 해요.jibeseo yori an haeyo.I don't cook at home.

⚠️ The Honest Exception

This split only works when the part before 하다 is a real action noun. Two groups keep 안 in front:

  • 하다 adjectives (describing states): 피곤하다 → 안 피곤해요 (not tired), 깨끗하다 → 안 깨끗해요 (not clean), 조용하다 → 안 조용해요.
  • 좋아하다 (to like): 좋아 isn't a noun, so it's 안 좋아해요, never "좋아 안 해요".

Not sure? The long form never has this problem: 공부하지 않아요, 좋아하지 않아요, 피곤하지 않아요 — all correct.

🔒 Can't: 못 + Verb / Stem + -지 못해요

못 works exactly like 안 — same position, same 하다 rule — but means "can't / unable to". The long form is stem + 지 못해요.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
오늘은 못 가요.oneureun mot gayo.I can't go today.
김치를 못 먹어요.gimchireul mot meogeoyo.I can't eat kimchi.
밤에 못 자요.bame mot jayo.I can't sleep at night.
오늘은 숙제를 못 해요.oneureun sukjereul mot haeyo.I can't do my homework today.
저는 운전 못 해요.jeoneun unjeon mot haeyo.I can't drive.
내일은 오지 못해요.naeireun oji motaeyo.I can't come tomorrow. (long form)

💡 Sound Changes Around 못 (from Lesson 3)

The final ㅅ of 못 is pronounced [ㄷ], and it reshapes the next sound: 못 가요 → [몯까요], 못 자요 → [몯짜요], 못 먹어요 → [몬머거요] (nasalization), 못 해요 → [모태요] (aspiration). Spelling stays the same; only the sound shifts.

⚖️ 안 vs 못: Choice or Circumstance?

KoreanWhat it implies
술을 안 마셔요.I don't drink alcohol. (my choice / habit)
술을 못 마셔요.I can't drink alcohol. (allergy, medication, can't handle it)
파티에 안 가요.I'm not going to the party. (decided not to)
파티에 못 가요.I can't make it to the party. (something prevents me)

못 is normally used with action verbs. Adjectives describe states you don't control, so they take 안: 안 비싸요 ✓, 못 비싸요 ✗.

🇰🇷 Cultural note — declining gracefully. When you turn down an invitation, 못 가요 ("I can't go") sounds much softer than 안 가요 ("I'm not going"), because it suggests circumstances rather than unwillingness. Koreans value keeping harmony (눈치 — reading the room — matters a lot), so a refusal usually comes as 미안해요. 못 가요. plus a short reason. A flat 안 가요 to an invitation can sound like you simply don't want to be there.

❔ Asking Yes/No Questions

In the polite 해요 style, a question is the statement with rising intonation. Nothing is added, nothing moves. In writing, just add a question mark.

KoreanRomanizationEnglish
학교에 가요.hakgyoe gayo.I'm going to school. ↘
학교에 가요?hakgyoe gayo?Are you going to school? ↗
시간 있어요?sigan isseoyo?Do you have time? ↗
고기 안 먹어요?gogi an meogeoyo?You don't eat meat? ↗

Answering Negative Questions — Korean Agrees with the Words

This is where English speakers stumble. In Korean, 네 means "what you just said is right" and 아니요 means "what you said is wrong". English answers the fact; Korean answers the question.

QuestionYour realityKorean answerEnglish answer
커피 안 마셔요?you don't drink it네, 안 마셔요.No, I don't.
커피 안 마셔요?you do drink it아니요, 마셔요.Yes, I do.
오늘 학교에 안 가요?you aren't going네, 안 가요.No, I'm not.

✅ Trick That Always Works

Don't think about 네 or 아니요 first — think about the verb. If your answer contains 안 or 못, the answer to a negative question starts with 네. If it's positive, start with 아니요. You'll also hear 아뇨, the relaxed spoken form of 아니요.

💡 Small Details That Matter

  • 누구 + 가 = 누가. "누구가" is wrong; the subject form contracts to 누가.
  • 뭐 is the spoken form of 무엇. 뭐를 often shortens to 뭘: 뭘 먹어요?
  • 몇 always needs a counter or unit after it: 몇 시 (what time), 몇 살 (how old), 몇 명 (how many people), 몇 개 (how many things).
  • 언제 takes no 에 — it behaves like 오늘 and 내일.
  • 무슨 vs 어느 vs 어떤: 어느 picks from a known set (어느 쪽? which side?), 무슨 asks for an identity (무슨 영화? what movie?), 어떤 asks for a description or type (어떤 사람? what kind of person?). They overlap a lot — don't panic if you mix them up at first.
  • 왜요? on its own is a common reply: "Why?" / "Why do you ask?"

SpeakerKoreanEnglish

지훈좋아요! 수진 씨는 팝콘 안 먹어요?Great! You don't eat popcorn, do you?

Notice how Sujin declines with 못 가요 plus a reason, and how she answers the negative question with 네, 안 먹어요 — "Yes (you're right), I don't." 어때요? ("how is it / how about…?") is a set phrase for now; its verb 어떻다 is irregular and returns in Lesson 21.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 안 공부해요 ✗ → 공부 안 해요. 안 goes between an action noun and 하다.
  • 피곤 안 해요 ✗ → 안 피곤해요. 하다 adjectives keep 안 in front.
  • 안 알아요 ✗ → 몰라요. 안 있어요 ✗ → 없어요.
  • 못 비싸요 ✗ → 안 비싸요. 못 is for actions, not states.
  • 커피 안 마셔요? — 아니요, 안 마셔요 ✗ → 네, 안 마셔요. Agree with the question's words.
  • 누구가 와요? ✗ → 누가 와요? 언제에 가요? ✗ → 언제 가요?
  • 뭐 당신은 먹어요? ✗ → 뭐 먹어요? Don't move question words to the front, and skip 당신 ("you") — Koreans use the person's name + 씨 or simply leave "you" out.

The 네/아니요 flip may feel backwards for weeks — that's completely normal. You haven't failed to get it; you just haven't got it yet. Each time you catch yourself, the pattern gets a little more automatic.

❓ Common Questions

Should I use 안 or -지 않아요?

In conversation, 안 is more common and sounds natural. -지 않아요 feels a little more careful or written, and it's handy when 안 placement is tricky (long 하다 verbs, adjectives). Both are polite in 해요 style; you won't offend anyone with either.

Is 못 ever rude?

Quite the opposite — when declining, 못 is the polite choice because it implies you would if you could. The one thing to avoid is 못 with adjectives, which simply sounds wrong.

Why can't I just say "you" (당신) in questions?

당신 exists but is used between spouses, in songs, or in arguments — it can sound cold or confrontational. Korean usually drops "you" (뭐 해요? = What are you doing?) or uses the person's name or title: 수진 씨, 선생님.

Do question words change with formality?

No — 뭐, 어디, 언제 and the others stay the same; only the verb ending changes with politeness level. In very formal speech you may hear 무엇 instead of 뭐. You'll see the formal 합니다/합니까 endings in Lesson 13.
