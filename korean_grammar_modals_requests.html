🇰🇷 Lesson 19: Can, Must & May: Ability, Obligation, Requests

So far you can say what you do, what you did, and what you want or plan to do. But real life is full of can, can't, have to, may I…? and please…. Can you drive? Do you have to work tomorrow? May I take a photo here? Could you speak slowly? This lesson gives you the handful of patterns that answer all of those questions.

Here is the mental model to hold on to: English uses little helper words (can, must, may) in front of the verb. Korean attaches an ending to the verb stem and then adds a small "support verb" after it. The meaning lives in the ending plus that support verb — 있다 (there is), 하다/되다 (do / be OK), 안 되다 (not OK). Once you see that each pattern is literally built from words you already know, they stop feeling like new vocabulary and start feeling like logic.

English ideaKorean patternLiteral picture
can / can't-(으)ㄹ 수 있다 / 없다"a way to do it exists / doesn't exist"
have to / must-아야/어야 하다 (되다)"only if you do it, it works"
may / it's OK to-아도/어도 되다"even if you do it, it's fine"
must not-(으)면 안 되다"if you do it, it's not OK"
please do / please don't-(으)세요 / -지 마세요polite command / polite "stop"
please do (for me)-아/어 주세요"do it and give it (to me)"

💪 Part 1 — Ability: -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 / 없다

The word 수 means "way, means, possibility". So 갈 수 있어요 is literally "a way to go exists" → I can go. Swap 있어요 for 없어요 and you get I can't go. You already know 있어요/없어요 from Lesson 9 — the only new piece is the -(으)ㄹ ending that hooks the verb onto 수.

💡 Formula: -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 / 없어요

Stem ends in a vowel → add ㄹ: 가다 → 가 → 갈 수 있어요

Stem ends in a consonant → add 을: 먹다 → 먹 → 먹을 수 있어요

Stem already ends in ㄹ → add nothing: 만들다 → 만들 수 있어요 (not 만들을!)

Dictionary formKoreanEnglish
수영하다저는 수영할 수 있어요.I can swim.
운전하다동생은 아직 운전할 수 없어요.My younger sibling can't drive yet.
먹다회를 먹을 수 있어요?Can you eat raw fish?
읽다한글을 읽을 수 있어요.I can read Hangul.
만들다김치를 만들 수 있어요.I can make kimchi.
오다내일 파티에 올 수 있어요?Can you come to the party tomorrow?
가다 (past)어제는 갈 수 없었어요.I couldn't go yesterday.

Tense goes on the final 있다/없다, not on the verb in front: 갈 수 있었어요 "I was able to go", 갈 수 없었어요 "I couldn't go". The verb before 수 never changes.

💡 Pronunciation tip

After the -ㄹ ending, the ㅅ of 수 is pronounced tense: 할 수 있어요 sounds like [할 쑤 이써요]. Revised Romanization still writes it hal su isseoyo — the spelling doesn't change, only the sound.

수 없다 vs 못 — what's the difference?

In Lesson 10 you met 못 ("can't"). 못 가요 and 갈 수 없어요 both mean "I can't go", and in most everyday sentences they are interchangeable. As a rough guide, 못 is short and conversational, while 수 없다 sounds a little fuller and is common for circumstances and rules ("You can't park here"). Neither is wrong.

잘하다 / 못하다 — being good or bad at something

For skills, Koreans very often skip "can" and talk about how well you do it. 잘하다 = to be good at, 못하다 = to be bad at / unable to do. The skill takes the object particle 을/를.

KoreanEnglish
민수 씨는 요리를 잘해요.Minsu is good at cooking.
저는 노래를 못해요.I'm bad at singing. / I can't sing.
한국어를 조금 할 수 있어요.I can speak a little Korean.
아직 잘 못해요.I'm not very good at it yet.
한국어를 잘하세요!You speak Korean well! (honorific)

⚠️ Watch the space: 잘 못해요 vs 잘못해요

잘 못해요 (with a space) = "I'm not good at it." 잘못해요 (no space) = "I do it wrong / I make a mistake" — 잘못 is a separate word meaning "wrongly, by mistake". In speech the difference is subtle, so context carries it, but in writing keep the space.

🇰🇷 Cultural note — the modesty reflex. If you say even two words of Korean, expect to hear 한국어를 잘하세요! ("You speak Korean well!"). The expected reply is not "thank you" but polite modesty: 아니에요, 아직 잘 못해요. ("Not at all, I'm not good yet.") Accepting a compliment too directly can sound a little boastful. Deflecting it is a social ritual, and now you have the grammar to play along.

📌 Part 2 — Obligation: -아야/어야 하다 (되다)

The ending -아야/어야 means "only if (you) do". Follow it with 해요 or 돼요 and you get have to / must: 가야 해요 = "(only if I go, it works) → I have to go".

💡 Formula: stem + -아야/어야 해요 (돼요)

Choose 아 or 어 exactly as you do for the polite present (Lesson 7). The easy trick: take the -아요/어요 form, remove 요, add 야.

가요 → 가야 해요 · 먹어요 → 먹어야 해요 · 공부해요 → 공부해야 해요 · 마셔요 → 마셔야 해요

KoreanEnglish
내일 일찍 일어나야 해요.I have to get up early tomorrow.
숙제를 해야 돼요.I have to do my homework.
약을 먹어야 해요.You have to take the medicine.
물을 많이 마셔야 돼요.You have to drink a lot of water.
버스를 기다려야 해요.We have to wait for the bus.
어제는 늦게까지 일해야 했어요.Yesterday I had to work late.

하다 or 되다? They mean the same thing. 해야 돼요 is extremely common in everyday speech; 해야 해요 is slightly more neutral and is what you'll see more in writing. Use whichever sounds natural to you — both are correct.

"Don't have to": -지 않아도 되다 / 안 -아도 되다

Here's a trap for English speakers: "you must not go" and "you don't have to go" are very different. Korean keeps them apart with different patterns. "Don't have to" is literally "even if you don't do it, it's fine":

KoreanEnglish
내일은 학교에 안 가도 돼요.You don't have to go to school tomorrow.
오늘은 요리하지 않아도 돼요.You don't have to cook today.
걱정 안 해도 돼요.You don't need to worry.

That "even if…, it's fine" shape is the permission pattern — which is exactly Part 3.

✅ Part 3 — Permission & Prohibition

May I…? -아도/어도 되다

-아도/어도 means "even if (you) do". Add 돼요 ("it's OK") and you get "it's OK to / you may". Turn it into a question for "May I…?" Same trick as before: -아요/어요 form, drop 요, add 도.

💡 Formula: stem + -아도/어도 돼요(?)

앉아요 → 앉아도 돼요? (May I sit?) · 찍어요 → 찍어도 돼요? · 해요 → 해도 돼요?

Answers: 네, 돼요. / 네, 앉으세요. — or — 아니요, 안 돼요.

KoreanEnglish
여기 앉아도 돼요?May I sit here?
사진을 찍어도 돼요?May I take a photo?
창문을 열어도 돼요?Is it OK if I open the window?
들어가도 돼요?May I come in?
네, 먹어도 돼요.Yes, you can eat it.

Must not: -(으)면 안 되다

-(으)면 means "if". So -(으)면 안 돼요 is "if you do it, it's not OK" → you must not / you're not allowed to. You will see this on signs everywhere in Korea.

💡 Formula: -(으)면 안 돼요

Vowel stem → -면: 가다 → 가면 안 돼요

Consonant stem → -으면: 먹다 → 먹으면 안 돼요

ㄹ stem → -면, no 으: 만들다 → 만들면 안 돼요

KoreanEnglish
여기서 사진을 찍으면 안 돼요.You can't take photos here.
여기서 담배를 피우면 안 돼요.You must not smoke here.
수업에 늦으면 안 돼요.You mustn't be late for class.
도서관에서 전화하면 안 돼요.You can't make phone calls in the library.
교실에서 음식을 먹으면 안 돼요.You can't eat in the classroom.

✅ The four-way map

PatternKorean (가다)Meaning

🙏 Part 4 — Asking Others: Requests & Commands

-(으)세요 — "please do"

Good news: you already know this one. In Lesson 13 you learned the honorific -(으)시- and its polite form -(으)세요. Said to someone about their own action, it becomes a gentle, respectful instruction: 앉으세요 "Please sit." It's what shop staff, teachers and hosts say all day long.

KoreanEnglish
여기 앉으세요.Please sit here.
잠깐만 기다리세요.Please wait a moment.
안으로 들어오세요.Please come inside.
맛있게 드세요.Enjoy your meal. (먹다 → 드시다)
안녕히 주무세요.Good night. (자다 → 주무시다)

Remember the special honorific verbs from Lesson 13: you say 드세요, never 먹으세요, and 주무세요, never 자세요, to someone you're being polite to.

-지 마세요 — "please don't"

To ask someone not to do something, attach -지 to any stem and add 마세요 (from 말다, "to stop, refrain"). No vowel harmony, no 으 — the easiest ending in this lesson.

KoreanEnglish
걱정하지 마세요.Don't worry.
가지 마세요.Please don't go.
만지지 마세요.Please don't touch.
늦지 마세요.Please don't be late.

-아/어 주세요 — "please do it (for me)"

주다 means "to give". -아/어 주세요 literally says "do it and give (the favour) to me". It turns a command into a request for a favour, so it feels warmer than a bare -(으)세요. Build it from the -아요/어요 form again: drop 요, add 주세요.

KoreanEnglish
천천히 말해 주세요.Please speak slowly.
다시 한 번 말해 주세요.Please say it one more time.
사진 좀 찍어 주세요.Could you take a photo (of us), please?
잠깐만 기다려 주세요.Please wait just a moment (for me).
도와주세요!Please help me! / Help! (from 돕다 — Lesson 21)

-아/어 줄래요? — "will you…?"

Turning the request into a question makes it softer still. -아/어 줄래요? ("will you do it for me?") is friendly and polite — ideal with friends, classmates and colleagues around your age. For strangers and elders, the extra-polite -아/어 주시겠어요? ("would you kindly…?") is useful to recognise: 창문 좀 열어 주시겠어요?

PolitenessKoreanEnglish
Direct instruction창문을 여세요.Open the window.
Favour request창문 좀 열어 주세요.Please open the window.
Friendly question창문 좀 열어 줄래요?Will you open the window?
Very polite question창문 좀 열어 주시겠어요?Would you mind opening the window?

(열다 is an ㄹ-stem, which is why its -(으)세요 form is 여세요 — you'll see exactly why in Lesson 21.)

🇰🇷 Cultural note — the magic of 좀. Notice the little word 좀 ("a bit") in so many requests. It doesn't really mean "a little" here; it's a softener, like English "just" in "Could you just…". Koreans also often open a request with 죄송하지만 ("I'm sorry, but…") (a set phrase — the -지만 'but' ending comes in Lesson 23) or 저기요 ("excuse me") to get attention. A request with 좀 and 주세요 sounds considerate; a bare command can sound blunt even when it's grammatically polite.

💬 Dialogue — At the Palace Museum

Emma is visiting a museum in Seoul and talks to a staff member (직원).

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
Emma저기요, 여기서 사진을 찍어도 돼요?Excuse me, may I take photos here?
직원밖에서는 찍어도 돼요. 그런데 전시실 안에서는 찍으면 안 돼요.You can take them outside. But you can't take them inside the exhibition hall.
Emma아, 알겠어요. 가방도 맡겨야 해요?Ah, I see. Do I have to leave my bag too?
직원아니요, 가방은 안 맡겨도 돼요.No, you don't have to check your bag.
Emma죄송하지만, 조금 천천히 말해 주세요. 한국어를 잘 못해요.Sorry, but please speak a little slowly. I'm not very good at Korean.
직원아니에요, 한국어를 잘하세요! 이쪽으로 들어오세요. 그리고 작품은 만지지 마세요.Not at all, you speak Korean well! Please come in this way. And please don't touch the artworks.
Emma네, 감사합니다. 혹시 영어 안내서를 받을 수 있어요?OK, thank you. Could I possibly get an English guide?
직원네, 잠깐만 기다려 주세요.Yes, please wait a moment.

Count them: permission, prohibition, obligation, "don't have to", a favour request, a polite command, a "please don't", and ability — the whole lesson in eight lines.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 만들을 수 있어요 ✗ → 만들 수 있어요 ✓. An ㄹ-stem already has its ㄹ; don't add 을. Same with 살 수 있어요, 놀 수 있어요.
  • Mixing up "must not" and "don't have to". 가면 안 돼요 = you must not go. 안 가도 돼요 = you don't need to go. Opposite messages!
  • 안 가세요 for "please don't go" ✗. 안 + -세요 is a statement about someone ("he doesn't go"). For a negative request, use 가지 마세요.
  • 먹으세요 to a guest ✗ → 드세요 ✓. Honorific requests use the special verbs from Lesson 13.
  • Putting tense on the wrong verb. "I couldn't go" = 갈 수 없었어요, not 갔을 수 없어요. "I had to go" = 가야 했어요.
  • Answering 돼요? with 네, 있어요. Answer with the same verb: 네, 돼요 / 아니요, 안 돼요.

If these endings blur together at first, that's normal — they all reuse the same -아/어 stem you already know, so your brain is sorting similar shapes. Each mix-up you notice is feedback that the sorting is happening. You don't have it "wrong"; you just don't have it yet.

Exercise 1 — Can or can't: -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 / 없어요

  1. 수영하다 → 저는 _____ 있어요. (I can swim) (Answer: 수영할 수)
  2. 먹다 → 김치를 _____ 있어요. (I can eat kimchi) (Answer: 먹을 수)
  3. 만들다 → 불고기를 _____ 있어요. (I can make bulgogi) (Answer: 만들 수)
  4. 읽다 → 한자를 _____ 없어요. (I can't read Chinese characters) (Answer: 읽을 수)
  5. 운전하다 → 저는 아직 _____. (I can't drive yet — use 수 없다) (Answer: 운전할 수 없어요)
  6. 가다 → 어제는 _____. (I couldn't go yesterday — use 수 없다) (Answer: 갈 수 없었어요)
  7. 'Minsu is good at cooking' → 민수 씨는 요리를 _____. (Answer: 잘해요)

Exercise 2 — Have to / don't have to

  1. 가다 → 지금 _____ 해요. (I have to go now) (Answer: 가야)
  2. 먹다 → 약을 _____ 해요. (You have to take medicine) (Answer: 먹어야)
  3. 공부하다 → 내일 시험이 있어요. 오늘 _____ 돼요. (I have to study) (Answer: 공부해야)
  4. 기다리다 → 버스를 _____ 해요. (We have to wait) (Answer: 기다려야)
  5. 마시다 → 물을 많이 _____ 돼요. (You have to drink) (Answer: 마셔야)
  6. 일하다 → 어제 늦게까지 _____. (I had to work — use 하다, past) (Answer: 일해야 했어요)
  7. 가다 → 내일은 학교에 _____ 돼요. (You don't have to go — use 안) (Answer: 안 가도)

Exercise 3 — May I? / Must not

  1. 앉다 → 여기 _____ 돼요? (May I sit?) (Answer: 앉아도)
  2. 찍다 → 사진을 _____ 돼요? (May I take a photo?) (Answer: 찍어도)
  3. 들어가다 → 지금 _____ 돼요? (May I go in now?) (Answer: 들어가도)
  4. 먹다 → 네, _____ 돼요. (Yes, you may eat it) (Answer: 먹어도)
  5. 찍다 → 여기서 사진을 _____ 안 돼요. (You must not take photos) (Answer: 찍으면)
  6. 피우다 → 여기서 담배를 _____ 안 돼요. (No smoking here) (Answer: 피우면)
  7. 늦다 → 수업에 _____ 안 돼요. (You mustn't be late) (Answer: 늦으면)

Exercise 4 — Requests

  1. 앉다 → 여기 _____. (Please sit — -(으)세요) (Answer: 앉으세요)
  2. 기다리다 → 잠깐만 _____. (Please wait — -(으)세요) (Answer: 기다리세요)
  3. 걱정하다 → _____ 마세요. (Don't worry) (Answer: 걱정하지)
  4. 가다 → 아직 _____. (Please don't go yet) (Answer: 가지 마세요)
  5. 말하다 → 천천히 _____ 주세요. (Please speak slowly) (Answer: 말해)

❓ Common Questions

Is 해야 돼요 less correct than 해야 해요?

No. Both are standard and mean exactly the same thing. 해야 돼요 is extremely common in conversation; 해야 해요 is a touch more neutral. In formal speech you'll hear 해야 합니다 or 해야 됩니다.

Should I use 못 or -(으)ㄹ 수 없다?

In everyday speech they overlap almost completely. 못 is shorter and very common for personal inability ("I can't swim"). -(으)ㄹ 수 없다 sounds slightly more formal and is common for rules, circumstances and possibility ("You can't enter here", "It can't be true"). If in doubt, either will be understood.

Isn't -(으)세요 the honorific, not a command?

It's both — the same form. 선생님이 가세요 is a statement ("The teacher goes", honorific). Said directly to someone about their own action — 여기 앉으세요 — it's understood as a polite request. Context and intonation tell you which.

Can I use -아/어 줄래요? with my boss?

It's polite 해요체, but it has a friendly, peer-to-peer feel. With a boss, a teacher or an elder, choose -아/어 주시겠어요? or simply -아/어 주세요 with 좀 — those are safer.

🌟 Going Further

  1. House rules: Write five rules for your home or classroom using -아야 해요, -아도 돼요 and -(으)면 안 돼요 (e.g. 집에서 신발을 신으면 안 돼요 — "you mustn't wear shoes in the house").
  2. Skills inventory: List three things you can do, one thing you can't do yet, and one thing you're good at, using -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요, 아직 못해요 and 잘해요. Then say them aloud.
