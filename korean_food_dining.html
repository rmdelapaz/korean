🇰🇷 Lesson 11: Food & Dining

Food is where your Korean gets used the fastest. A Korean meal is shared: rice and soup for each person, and a table full of 반찬 (side dishes) in the middle for everyone. This lesson gives you the words for what's on the table, the phrases for ordering, and — the real payoff — a chance to use everything from Lessons 7, 9 and 10 to say what you eat, where, with whom, and what you don't (or can't) eat.

🗣️ Ordering at a Restaurant

Most of these are set phrases: learn them whole for now. The -세요 and -아 주세요 endings inside them are explained properly in Lesson 19.

KoreanMeaning

메뉴 주세요.The menu, please.

물 좀 주세요.Some water, please.

포장돼요?Can I get it to go?

Two counters from Lesson 6 do most of the work when ordering: dishes are usually counted with Native numbers (하나, 둘 — 김밥 두 줄 for gimbap rolls, or just 두 개), while barbecue meat is ordered by portion with Sino numbers + 인분 (삼 인분 = three servings).

💡 Pronunciation and Irregular Forms

맛있어요 is pronounced [마시써요] and 맛없어요 [마덥써요] — the final ㅅ links to the next syllable differently in each. 매워요 and 싱거워요 are ㅂ-irregular (the ㅂ turns into 워); memorise them now and Lesson 21 explains the whole family.

From Lesson 10: 안 vs 못, and questions

This is where negation really matters at the table. 안 means you don't (by choice); 못 means you can't (you're unable — allergy, religion, too spicy for you). Telling a host "I can't eat it" is gentler than "I don't eat it".

KoreanEnglish
저는 고기를 안 먹어요.I don't eat meat. (choice — e.g. vegetarian)
저는 해산물을 못 먹어요.I can't eat seafood. (e.g. allergy)
오늘은 술을 안 마셔요.I'm not drinking today.
김치찌개는 안 매워요?Isn't the kimchi stew spicy?
뭐 먹어요?What are you eating / having?
어디에서 먹어요?Where are we eating?
무슨 음식을 좋아해요?What kind of food do you like?
이거 얼마예요?How much is this?

💡 Answering a Negative Question

From Lesson 10: Korean 네/아니요 answer "is what you said true?". If someone asks 안 매워요? (Isn't it spicy?) and it's not spicy, answer 네, 안 매워요 — "Right, it isn't spicy."

💬 Dialogue 1: At a Korean Restaurant

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
Server어서 오세요. 몇 분이세요?Welcome. How many people?
Customer두 명이에요.Two.
Customer저기요! 비빔밥 하나하고 된장찌개 하나 주세요.Excuse me! One bibimbap and one doenjang stew, please.
Server네, 알겠습니다. 음료는요?Certainly. Anything to drink?
Customer물만 주세요. 그리고 된장찌개는 안 매워요?Just water, please. And the doenjang stew isn't spicy?
Server네, 안 매워요.Right, it's not spicy.
Server여기 있습니다.Here you are.
Customer잘 먹겠습니다! … 아, 맛있어요!Thank you for the meal! … Ah, it's delicious!

Notice the server's 몇 분이세요? — the honorific counter 분 from Lesson 6 plus a respectful ending you'll study in Lesson 13. You answer about yourself with the neutral 명.

💬 Dialogue 2: What Do You Like?

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
지민무슨 음식을 좋아해요?What food do you like?
Tom불고기를 좋아해요. 그런데 매운 음식은 잘 못 먹어요.I like bulgogi. But I can't really eat spicy food.
지민아, 그래요? 저는 떡볶이를 자주 먹어요.Oh, really? I often eat tteokbokki.
Tom떡볶이는 너무 매워요!Tteokbokki is too spicy!
지민그럼 오늘 저녁은 삼계탕 어때요? 안 매워요.Then how about samgyetang tonight? It's not spicy.
Tom좋아요! 어디에서 먹어요?Great! Where do we eat?

(매운 음식 "spicy food" uses a noun-modifier ending you'll learn in Lesson 25, and 어때요 comes from an irregular verb in Lesson 21 — treat both as set phrases for now.)

🌏 Culture Note — Table Manners: Koreans say 잘 먹겠습니다 before a meal, thanking whoever cooked or paid, and 잘 먹었습니다 afterwards. Wait for the oldest person to start eating. Rice and soup are eaten with the spoon (숟가락); chopsticks (젓가락) are for side dishes — and never stand them upright in your rice, which resembles a funeral offering. When drinking with elders, pour for others rather than yourself, receive a glass with two hands, and turn slightly away when you drink. 반찬 are shared and usually refillable for free, and calling 저기요! to a server is completely normal.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 밥를 먹어요 → 밥 ends in a consonant: 밥을 먹어요.
  • 식당에 먹어요 → eating is an action, so the place takes 에서: 식당에서 먹어요.
  • 안 먹어요 when you mean an allergy → use 못 먹어요 ("can't").
  • 맛있어요 pronounced "mat-it-eoyo" → it's [마시써요].
  • 맵어요 → ㅂ-irregular: 매워요.
  • 잘 먹었습니다 before the meal → that's the after phrase; before is 잘 먹겠습니다.

Restaurants are the friendliest classroom there is: if your order comes out a little wrong, you've still eaten, and you've learned exactly which word to fix next time.

❓ Common Questions

Is it rude to shout 저기요! at a server?

No. Many Korean restaurants expect it, and some tables even have a call button. Say it clearly and politely — that's all. 여기요! works the same way.

Why do so many phrases end in 주세요?

주세요 means "please give (me)". Put any noun in front and you have an order: 김밥 주세요, 물 주세요. With a verb it becomes "please do … for me" (계산해 주세요). Lesson 19 shows how to build that pattern yourself.

How do I say I'm vegetarian?

저는 채식주의자예요 ("I'm a vegetarian"), then be specific: 고기를 안 먹어요. Many soups and 반찬 use meat or fish stock, so it's worth asking 고기 있어요? ("Is there meat in it?").

Should I leave a tip?

No. Tipping is not customary in Korea, and a tip left on the table may even be returned to you.
