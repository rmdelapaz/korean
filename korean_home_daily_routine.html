🇰🇷 Lesson 12: Home, Household & Daily Routine

Where do you live, what is in your room, and what does your day look like? These are some of the very first questions a Korean friend, teacher or TOPIK exam will ask you. This lesson gives you the words for Korean homes, rooms, furniture and chores, and a full day's routine from 일어나요 (I get up) to 자요 (I go to sleep). You already have the grammar to talk about all of it. Lessons 5, 7, 9 and 10 gave you the tools, and this lesson gives you a house to use them in.

🏢 Types of Housing in Korea

Housing words in Korea don't always mean what their English look-alikes mean. A Korean 빌라 is not a luxury villa, and a 아파트 is not just any flat. Think of these words as Korean words that happen to sound English.

KoreanRomanizationWhat it really is
집jiphome, house (the general word)
아파트apateua flat in a high-rise complex (단지), the most common Korean home, often owned
빌라billaa low-rise block of flats (usually 4–5 floors), cheaper than a 아파트
원룸wolluma studio: one room with a kitchenette and a bathroom, typical for students
오피스텔opiseutel"office + hotel": a studio in a mixed office/residential building

💡 Culture Note: more than half of Korea lives in a 아파트. Apartments make up about two-thirds of all Korean homes, and more than half of households live in one. A complex (단지) is a small town of its own, with numbered buildings (동) and unit numbers (호). An address like 101동 1203호 means building 101, unit 1203, which is the 12th floor. Many elevators label the 4th floor "F" instead of 4, because 사 (four) sounds like 死 (death).

💡 Memory trick: 장 means "cabinet"

옷 (clothes) + 장 = 옷장, a wardrobe. 책 (book) + 장 = 책장, a bookshelf. The same logic runs through the appliances. 세탁 (washing) + 기 (machine) = 세탁기, and 청소 (cleaning) + 기 = 청소기. Once you see these pieces, you can often guess a new word.

📍 Where Is It? Position Words in Use

In Lesson 9 you learned that Korean says "the bed's top" where English says "on the bed". The object you are using as a landmark comes first, then the position word, then 에. Put 있어요 or 없어요 after that.

✅ Formula: Thing이/가 + Landmark + position + 에 있어요

고양이가 + 침대 + 위 + 에 있어요 → 고양이가 침대 위에 있어요. (The cat is on the bed.)

Positions: 위 (on/above) · 아래 / 밑 (under) · 옆 (next to) · 안 (inside) · 밖 (outside) · 앞 (in front) · 뒤 (behind)

KoreanEnglish
책상 위에 컴퓨터가 있어요.There is a computer on the desk.
침대 아래에 가방이 있어요.There is a bag under the bed.
소파 옆에 책장이 있어요.There is a bookshelf next to the sofa.
냉장고 안에 우유가 없어요.There's no milk in the fridge.
현관 앞에 신발이 있어요.There are shoes in front of the entrance.
부엌은 거실 옆에 있어요.The kitchen is next to the living room.
리모컨이 어디에 있어요? 소파 뒤에 있어요.Where's the remote? It's behind the sofa.

Look at the last two sentences in the table. Use 은/는 when the thing is already the topic of conversation ("The kitchen, it's next to the living room"). Use 이/가 when you are introducing it or answering "where is it?" This is the same contrast you learned in Lesson 5.

⏰ A Day in the Life: Your Routine Timeline

Now let's walk through a whole day. Remember the rule from Lesson 6: hours use native numbers (일곱 시), minutes use Sino numbers (삼십 분), and 반 means "half past". Time takes the particle 에 (Lesson 9).

💡 Three verbs worth a closer look

씻다 means "to wash (yourself)". "씻어요" is the everyday word for washing up, whether that's your face, your hands or a quick shower. 출근하다 / 퇴근하다 literally mean "to go in to work" and "to leave work". English has no single word for them, and Koreans use them every day. 자다 is "to sleep", so 자요 can mean "I sleep" or "I go to bed" depending on context.

⚠️ 에 or 에서? Ask "exist or do?"

With 있다/없다 (being somewhere), use 에: 집에 있어요. With an action verb (doing something somewhere), use 에서: 집에서 쉬어요. One honest exception: with 살다 (to live), both 서울에 살아요 and 서울에서 살아요 are correct and mean the same thing.

🏮 Korean Home Culture

👟 Shoes off at the 현관: Every Korean home has a 현관, a small entry area a step lower than the rest of the floor. Shoes stay down on that lower level and never go past the step. Many homes keep 슬리퍼 (slippers) for guests. Wearing shoes inside is one of the few things that will genuinely shock a Korean host.
🔥 온돌, the warm floor: Traditional Korean houses were heated by flues running under stone floors. Today the same idea, 온돌, is done with hot-water pipes, and almost every home has it. This is why Koreans often sit, eat and even sleep on the floor on an 이불. In winter the warmest spot is the floor, not the sofa.
🔑 전세 and 월세, renting the Korean way: With 월세 (monthly rent) you pay a deposit (보증금) plus rent every month, which is familiar to most foreigners. 전세 is unique to Korea. You hand the landlord a very large lump-sum deposit, often more than half the flat's value, and pay no monthly rent at all. At the end of the contract, usually two years, you get the whole deposit back. The landlord earns money by investing it in the meantime.
♻️ 분리수거, recycling is not optional: Korean households sort plastic, cans, glass, paper and styrofoam into separate bins. Food waste (음식물 쓰레기) goes into its own bin. General trash must go in official pay-per-volume bags (종량제 봉투) bought at the convenience store. Apartment complexes often have fixed recycling days, and neighbours do notice when you get it wrong.

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 위 책상에 ✗ → 책상 위에 ✓. The landmark comes first and the position word second: "desk's top".
  • 집에서 있어요 ✗ → 집에 있어요 ✓. 있다 takes 에. Save 에서 for actions: 집에서 쉬어요.
  • 칠 시에 일어나요 ✗ → 일곱 시에 일어나요 ✓. Hours are native Korean numbers. Minutes are Sino: 일곱 시 십 분.
  • 안 청소해요 ✗ → 청소 안 해요 ✓. With noun + 하다 verbs, 안 goes between the noun and 하다.
  • 아파트 ≠ any apartment. A small studio is a 원룸 or an 오피스텔, not a 아파트.

❓ Common Questions

안 means "inside" AND "not"? How do I tell them apart?

They are two unrelated words that happen to be spelled the same. Position 안 is a noun, so it comes after another noun and takes a particle: 방 안에, 냉장고 안에. Negative 안 comes directly before a verb or adjective: 안 먹어요, 안 추워요. If it is followed by 에, it's "inside".

What's the difference between 화장실 and 욕실?

화장실 is the everyday word for "bathroom/toilet", both at home and in public. It's the word to use when you're asking where it is. 욕실 specifically means the room with the bath or shower. You'll see it in flat listings and on shop signs, but in a Korean home it's usually the same room as the 화장실.

Should I say 집에 가요 or 집에 와요?

It's the same logic as English "go" and "come". 가다 is movement away from where the speaker is, and 오다 is movement toward it. At the office you say 집에 가요 (I'm going home). If your roommate is already at home and texts you, they'd ask 언제 와요? (When are you coming?)

Is 부엌 or 주방 more common?

Both are fine. 부엌 is the native Korean word and feels homey. 주방 is Sino-Korean and a little more modern or formal. It's the word you'll see in restaurants ("주방" = the kitchen staff area) and in estate-agent listings.
