🇰🇷 Lesson 23: Connecting Clauses

Up to now, most of your Korean sentences have been one idea long: It's hot. I'm tired. I eat lunch. Real speech strings ideas together: It's hot, so I'm tired. I eat lunch and then go to class. If it rains, let's stay home. This lesson gives you the glue — and once you have it, your Korean suddenly starts to sound like conversation instead of a list of facts.

🧠 The Mental Model: Glue on the Stem

English connects clauses with separate words placed between them: "I was hungry so I ate." Korean usually does it differently: it takes the stem of the first verb or adjective and attaches a connecting ending to it. The first clause loses its normal -아요/어요 ending and becomes a "hook" that the second clause hangs on.

💡 The Formula

[Clause 1: stem + connector] + [Clause 2: fully conjugated verb]

배가 고프다 + -아서 + 밥을 먹었어요 → 배가 고파서 밥을 먹었어요 — "I was hungry, so I ate."

Notice where the tense lives: at the very end of the sentence. For many connectors, the final verb carries the tense and politeness for the whole sentence.

Because connectors are endings, everything you learned about stems still applies. That includes the irregular verbs from Lesson 21 — and there is one simple rule that tells you when they wake up:

✅ The Irregular-Verb Rule of Thumb

Endings that start with a consonant (-고, -지만, -기 전에) leave the stem alone: 덥고, 듣지만, 바쁘기 전에.

Endings that start with a vowel (-아서/어서, -(으)니까, -(으)면, -(으)ㄹ 때, -(으)ㄴ 후에, -(으)면서) trigger the irregular changes: 더워서, 들으니까, 바빠서.

ㄹ-stems are special: they never take the extra 으 (살면, 만들 때), and they drop ㄹ before ㄴ (사니까, 만든 후에).

➕ -고: "and" / "and then"

-고 is the simplest connector. Attach it to any stem — no vowel harmony, no 받침 worries. It has two jobs:

UseKoreanEnglish
Listing (and)이 식당은 싸고 맛있어요.This restaurant is cheap and tasty.
Listing, different subjects저는 커피를 마시고 동생은 주스를 마셔요.I drink coffee and my sibling drinks juice.
Sequence (and then)아침을 먹고 학교에 가요.I eat breakfast and then go to school.
Sequence, past숙제를 하고 잤어요.I did my homework and then slept.

For a sequence, keep -고 in its plain form and let the last verb show the past: 숙제를 하고 잤어요, not 숙제를 했고 잤어요. For simple listing of two past facts, -았고/었고 is fine: 어제는 비가 왔고 바람도 불었어요 ("Yesterday it rained, and it was windy too").

↔️ -지만: "but"

-지만 also attaches straight to any stem. It works just like English "but", and it happily takes the past tense.

KoreanEnglish
한국어는 어렵지만 재미있어요.Korean is hard, but it's fun.
그 옷은 예쁘지만 너무 비싸요.Those clothes are pretty, but too expensive.
어제는 추웠지만 오늘은 따뜻해요.Yesterday was cold, but today is warm.
음악을 듣지만 노래는 안 불러요.I listen to music, but I don't sing.

🔗 -아서/어서: Two Meanings

-아서/어서 follows the same vowel-harmony rule as -아요/어요: take the polite form, swap 요 for 서. 가요 → 가서, 먹어요 → 먹어서, 해요 → 해서, 더워요 → 더워서. If you can make the polite present, you can make -아서. It has two quite different meanings.

Meaning 1: Cause — "because / so"

KoreanEnglish
배가 고파서 라면을 먹었어요.I was hungry, so I ate ramyeon.
너무 더워서 에어컨을 켰어요.It was too hot, so I turned on the AC.
요즘 바빠서 운동을 못 해요.I'm busy these days, so I can't exercise.
길을 몰라서 친구한테 물어봤어요.I didn't know the way, so I asked a friend.
음악을 많이 들어서 귀가 아파요.I listened to a lot of music, so my ears hurt.
늦어서 죄송해요.Sorry I'm late. (lit. "Because I'm late, sorry.")

Look at the tense in those examples: 배가 고파서, not 고팠어서, even though the hunger was in the past. That's the first big rule of -아서.

⚠️ Two Restrictions on Cause -아서

1. No past tense before -아서. Never say -았어서/었어서. The final verb tells us when it happened: 어제 늦게 자서 피곤해요 ("I went to bed late yesterday, so I'm tired"), not 잤어서.

2. No command or suggestion after cause -아서. If the second clause is "please do…" (-(으)세요), "let's…" (-(으)ㅂ시다, -(으)ㄹ까요?) or "don't…" (-지 마세요), -아서 sounds wrong. Use -(으)니까 instead (below).

✗ 추워서 창문을 닫으세요. → ✓ 추우니까 창문을 닫으세요. "It's cold, so please close the window."

With nouns, "because it's a …" is 이라서 after a 받침 and 라서 after a vowel: 학생이라서 할인을 받았어요 ("I'm a student, so I got a discount"), 친구라서 도와줬어요 ("Because they're a friend, I helped").

Meaning 2: Linked Sequence — "do A and (there/with it) do B"

The second meaning has nothing to do with cause. -아서 can link two actions where the first sets up the second: you go somewhere and then do something there, or you make something and then do something with it. The same person does both.

KoreanEnglish
도서관에 가서 공부해요.I go to the library and study (there).
김밥을 만들어서 먹었어요.I made gimbap and ate it.
친구를 만나서 영화를 봤어요.I met a friend and saw a movie (with them).
여기 앉아서 기다리세요.Please sit here and wait.
일찍 일어나서 운동했어요.I got up early and exercised.

Notice 여기 앉아서 기다리세요 — a command after -아서! That's fine, because this is the sequence meaning, not cause. The command restriction only applies to "because" -아서. (The past-tense rule applies to both meanings.)

💡 -아서 vs -고 for Sequences

친구를 만나서 영화를 봤어요 — I met my friend and we watched a movie together.

친구를 만나고 영화를 봤어요 — I met my friend, and then (maybe on my own) I watched a movie. Two separate events.

Movement and posture verbs (가다, 오다, 앉다, 서다, 일어나다, 만나다) usually take -아서 because the next action happens there. Wearing and riding verbs take -고: 코트를 입고 나갔어요 ("I put on a coat and went out"), 버스를 타고 가요 ("I go by bus").

🗣️ -(으)니까: "because" (and it's okay to ask for things)

-(으)니까 also means "because/so", but it foregrounds the speaker's reason for what they are saying. Add -니까 after a vowel stem or ㄹ-stem (ㄹ drops: 살다 → 사니까) and -으니까 after a consonant stem (먹으니까). Irregulars change as usual: 추우니까, 들으니까.

KoreanEnglish
비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요.It's raining, so take an umbrella.
추우니까 창문을 닫을까요?It's cold — shall we close the window?
시간이 없으니까 택시를 탑시다.We don't have time, so let's take a taxi.
어제 많이 걸었으니까 오늘은 쉬어요.I walked a lot yesterday, so I'm resting today.
학교가 머니까 일찍 출발하세요.School is far, so leave early.
-아서/어서 (cause)-(으)니까
Past before it?✗ Never (늦어서, not 늦었어서)✓ Yes (늦었으니까)
Command / "let's" after it?✗ No✓ Yes — its specialty
Thanks & apologies✓ 늦어서 죄송해요, 도와주셔서 감사합니다✗ Sounds like an excuse
FeelNeutral, natural cause-and-effectSpeaker's reason, often "so (you/we) should…"

An honest subtlety: in plain statements both are often possible (바빠서 못 가요 / 바쁘니까 못 가요). -아서 is the safer, softer default for explaining yourself; -(으)니까 can sound a little like "…obviously" if you use it when someone asks you 왜요? So reply 좀 바빠서요 ("Because I'm a bit busy") rather than 바쁘니까요 to a boss. You'll also hear -(으)니까 meaning "when I did X, I found…": 집에 가니까 아무도 없었어요 ("When I got home, nobody was there") — just recognize it for now.

⏰ Conditions and Time: -(으)면, -(으)ㄹ 때, -기 전에, -(으)ㄴ 후에, -(으)면서

ConnectorMeaningKoreanEnglish
-(으)면if / when(ever)시간이 있으면 같이 가요.If you have time, let's go together.
-(으)면if / when(ever)더우면 창문을 여세요.If it's hot, open the window.
-(으)ㄹ 때when (at the time)한국에 살 때 김치를 많이 먹었어요.When I lived in Korea, I ate a lot of kimchi.
-았/었을 때when (completed event)작년에 한국에 갔을 때 부산에도 갔어요.When I went to Korea last year, I went to Busan too.
-기 전에before …ing자기 전에 이를 닦아요.I brush my teeth before sleeping.
-(으)ㄴ 후에after …ing수업이 끝난 후에 만나요.Let's meet after class ends.
-(으)면서while (same person)음악을 들으면서 공부해요.I study while listening to music.

With nouns, 때, 전에 and 후에 attach directly: 방학 때 ("during vacation"), 수업 전에 ("before class"), 식사 후에 ("after the meal"). -기 전에 never takes the past (자기 전에, not 잤기 전에), and -(으)ㄴ 후에 is already "after", so the final verb does the tense work. You may also hear -(으)ㄴ 다음에 — it means the same as -(으)ㄴ 후에.

💡 A Sneak Peek at Lesson 25

The ㄹ in -(으)ㄹ 때 and the ㄴ in -(으)ㄴ 후에 are modifier endings — the same pieces you'll use in Lesson 25 to say "the food I ate" and "the place I'll go". Learn these two as set phrases now; they'll make perfect sense soon.

🧷 Sentence Connectors: 그리고, 그래서, 그런데, 하지만

Sometimes you want two full sentences with a linking word at the start of the second. These are the standalone cousins of the endings:

WordMeaningKoreanEnglish
그리고and / and then저는 커피를 좋아해요. 그리고 차도 좋아해요.I like coffee. And I like tea too.
그래서so / therefore비가 왔어요. 그래서 집에 있었어요.It rained. So I stayed home.
하지만but / however이 옷은 예뻐요. 하지만 너무 비싸요.These clothes are pretty. But they're too expensive.
그런데but / by the way어제 영화를 봤어요. 그런데 그 영화 봤어요?I saw a movie yesterday. By the way, have you seen it?

그런데 (often shortened to 근데 in speech) is softer than 하지만 and is also the classic way to change the subject. 그래서 matches -아서, and there's also 그러니까 ("so, therefore") which matches -(으)니까.

💬 Dialogue: Planning a Hike

SpeakerKoreanEnglish
민수지나 씨, 주말에 뭐 할 거예요?Jina, what are you doing this weekend?
지나토요일에는 친구를 만나서 영화를 볼 거예요. 그런데 일요일에는 시간이 있어요.On Saturday I'm meeting a friend to see a movie. But on Sunday I'm free.
민수그럼 일요일에 같이 등산할까요?Then shall we go hiking together on Sunday?
지나음… 요즘 너무 더워서 등산은 좀 힘들어요.Hmm… it's so hot these days that hiking is a bit hard.
민수아침에는 시원하니까 일곱 시에 만날까요?It's cool in the morning, so shall we meet at seven?
지나좋아요! 등산하기 전에 김밥을 사서 가요.Great! Before the hike, let's buy gimbap and take it along.
민수네, 산에 올라가서 같이 먹어요.Yes, let's climb the mountain and eat it together up there.
지나내려온 후에 냉면도 먹어요!After we come down, let's have naengmyeon too!
민수좋아요. 비가 오면 다음 주에 가요.Sounds good. If it rains, let's go next week.
지나네, 일요일 아침에 날씨를 보고 연락할게요.Okay, I'll check the weather Sunday morning and get in touch.

Spot the pattern choices: 더워서 explains a feeling (neutral cause), but 시원하니까 is followed by a suggestion (만날까요?), so it must be -(으)니까. 올라가서 같이 먹어요 is the sequence -아서 — "go up and eat there" — so a "let's" after it is fine.

🌏 Culture Note — the reason instead of the "no": Koreans often decline an invitation by simply giving a reason and letting the sentence trail off: 오늘은 좀 바빠서요… ("It's just that I'm a bit busy today…"). The listener understands the "no" without anyone having to say it. The same softening appears in apologies and thanks, which almost always use -아서: 늦어서 죄송합니다, 도와주셔서 감사합니다, and of course 만나서 반가워요 — a phrase you've been using since Lesson 4 without knowing it contained a connector!

⚠️ Common Mistakes

✗ 어제 늦게 잤어서 피곤해요. → ✓ 어제 늦게 자서 피곤해요. (No past before -아서.)

✗ 비가 와서 우산을 가져가세요. → ✓ 비가 오니까 우산을 가져가세요. (Command after a reason needs -(으)니까.)

✗ 늦으니까 죄송해요. → ✓ 늦어서 죄송해요. (Apologies and thanks use -아서.)

✗ 더워니까 / 덥으니까 → ✓ 더우니까. (ㅂ-irregular: ㅂ → 우 before a vowel ending.)

✗ 살으면, 만들으면서 → ✓ 살면, 만들면서. (ㄹ-stems don't take 으.)

✗ 잤기 전에 → ✓ 자기 전에. (-기 전에 never takes past.)

If -아서 vs -(으)니까 still feels slippery, that's completely normal — even intermediate learners mix them up. Each mistake you catch is your ear being trained. You haven't "failed" at it; you just haven't got it yet.

❓ Common Questions

Is -아서 or -(으)니까 more polite?

Neither is ruder in itself, but -아서 is the gentler default when you explain your own situation ("I'm sick, so I can't come"). -(으)니까 highlights your reasoning and pairs naturally with requests and suggestions. Answering a senior's 왜요? with -니까요 can sound like "because, obviously…", so -아서요 is safer there.

What's the difference between -(으)면 and -(으)ㄹ 때?

-(으)면 is a condition — "if" or "whenever it happens": 비가 오면 집에 있어요 ("If/Whenever it rains, I stay home"). -(으)ㄹ 때 points to the time or period itself: 비가 올 때 우산을 써요 ("When it's raining, I use an umbrella"). Often both work; -(으)면 leans toward "if", -(으)ㄹ 때 toward "at the time when".

Can the two clauses have different subjects?

With -고 (listing), -지만, -아서 (cause), -(으)니까, -(으)면 and -(으)ㄹ 때, yes: 비가 와서 제가 집에 있었어요. But sequence -아서 and -(으)면서 need the same person doing both actions.

Why does 만나서 반가워요 use -아서?

It's the cause meaning: "Because I'm meeting you, I'm glad." Emotions, thanks and apologies with a cause are classic -아서 territory — which is also why they never use -(으)니까.
