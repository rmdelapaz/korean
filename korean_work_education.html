🇰🇷 Lesson 24: Work & Education

"What do you do?" and "Where did you study?" are among the first questions people ask when they meet you in Korea. The good news is that you already have almost all the grammar you need to answer them. This lesson gives you the words for jobs, offices and schools, and then uses them to practise the grammar from Lessons 5–23: particles, the past and future, honorifics, "must", and connecting clauses. Think of it as bringing your toolkit to work.

To say your own job, use the sentence frame from Lesson 5: 저는 회사원이에요. (after a consonant) or 저는 의사예요. (after a vowel). To ask someone else politely, the standard question is 무슨 일 하세요? — literally "What work do you do?" with the honorific -(으)세요 from Lesson 13.

💡 선생님 vs 교사

선생님 is how you address or refer to a teacher — it already contains the honorific 님. 교사 is the neutral job title. So when a teacher introduces herself formally, the careful choice is 저는 교사예요. In casual speech you will hear teachers say 선생님 about themselves to children, but you should not attach 님 to your own title.

🏢 Workplace Hierarchy

Korean companies have clear ranks, and people are usually addressed by their title rather than their name. Here is a typical ladder (exact titles vary by company, and many firms have flattened them in recent years):

⚠️ 님 Is for Other People

When you address or talk about someone by title, add 님: 부장님, 과장님, 사장님. Calling your boss just "부장" to their face sounds rude. But 님 honours the other person, so never use it for yourself: say 저는 과장이에요., not 저는 과장님이에요. (This is the same logic as Lesson 13: you never use honorific -(으)시- about yourself.)

Notice the two ways to say where you work. 일하다 is an action, so the place takes 에서 (Lesson 9): 은행에서 일해요. 다니다 means "to go regularly / attend", so it takes 에, like any destination: 회사에 다녀요, 대학교에 다녀요. Koreans use 다니다 constantly for both jobs and schools.

💡 Culture Note — 수고하셨습니다 and leaving the office: 수고하셨습니다 ("you worked hard") is said to colleagues when a job or the day is finished — a warm way to thank people for their effort. The person who leaves first usually says 먼저 가 보겠습니다. Be careful with the present-tense version 수고하세요 ("keep up the hard work"): many Koreans feel it sounds like an instruction, so it is best not said to a superior.
💡 Culture Note — 회식 (Hoesik): After-work team dinners (회식) are a big part of Korean work culture. They typically involve food and drinks (소주!) and are seen as important for team bonding. Declining too often used to be frowned upon, though this culture is changing fast — younger workers and many companies now favour shorter dinners, lunches, or no alcohol at all.

💡 수능 (Suneung) — The Big Exam

Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test (수능) is held every November and is one of the most important days in a Korean student's life. Flights are halted during the English listening section, police give late students rides to the exam hall, and younger students cheer outside the gates. Many students spend evenings at 학원 (private academies) for years beforehand — a sign of how deeply education is valued.

✅ Pattern: N이/가 되다 — "to become N"

Noun + 이/가 + 되다 means "to become". It uses the subject particle, not the object particle — you don't "become" something the way you "eat" something; the noun is what you turn into. 되다 conjugates 돼요 (present), 됐어요 (past), 될 거예요 (future).

의사 → 의사가 돼요 · 선생님 → 선생님이 됐어요 · 대리 → 대리가 될 거예요

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • 회사에서 다녀요 ✗ → 회사에 다녀요 ✓. 다니다 takes 에; 일하다 takes 에서.
  • 의사를 되고 싶어요 ✗ → 의사가 되고 싶어요 ✓. "Become" uses 이/가.
  • 저는 선생님이에요 in a formal self-introduction → prefer 저는 교사예요. Never 님 on your own title.
  • 공부 안 해요 ✓, not 안 공부해요 — with 하다-verbs, 안 goes right before 하다 (Lesson 10).
  • 수고하세요 to your boss can sound bossy; use 수고하셨습니다 or 먼저 가 보겠습니다.

Job and school sentences can get long — if one comes out wrong, that is simply feedback telling you which piece to adjust. Fix the particle, try again, and it will be yours.

❓ Common Questions

What is the difference between 일하다 and 다니다?

일하다 is the activity of working: 은행에서 일해요 ("I work at a bank"). 다니다 is regularly going somewhere, so 회사에 다녀요 means "I'm employed at a company" and 학교에 다녀요 means "I attend school". Both are natural answers to 무슨 일 하세요?, and 다니다 is extremely common in everyday speech.

Should I call my coworker by name or by title?

In most Korean workplaces, by title + 님 (과장님, 대리님), or full name + title (김민수 과장님) for clarity. Some start-ups and tech companies have switched to English names or name + 님 to feel less hierarchical. When in doubt, follow what your colleagues do.

Is 학원 the same as a university?

No. 학원 is a private academy for extra lessons — English, maths, piano, coding, even driving. Students go after regular school, and adults use them for language or job skills. A university is 대학교.

Why does 먼저 가 보겠습니다 have 보다 in it?

It's a fixed polite phrase, so learn it as a whole for now. The -아/어 보다 ending (roughly "try doing") is taught in Lesson 25, and the -겠- inside it expresses humble intention, which you'll meet beyond A2.
