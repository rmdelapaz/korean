⚠️ Always Add 님 (nim)!

When addressing someone by title, always add 님 (nim) — it's the honorific suffix. 선생님 (teacher), 사장님 (CEO). Dropping 님 is disrespectful. In Korean offices, people are often addressed by title rather than name.

💡 Culture Note — 회식 (Hoesik): After-work team dinners (회식) are a big part of Korean work culture. They typically involve food and drinks (소주!) and are considered important for team bonding. Declining too often can be seen negatively, though this culture is slowly changing with younger generations.

💡 수능 (Suneung) — The Big Exam

Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test (수능) is held every November and is one of the most important days in a Korean student's life. Planes are grounded during the listening section, police escort late students, and younger students cheer outside exam halls. Education is deeply valued in Korean culture.

🎯 Summary

🎉 Key Takeaways

  • 무슨 일 하세요? = "What do you do for work?"
  • Always use 님 when addressing someone by title
  • 수고하셨습니다 = "You've worked hard" — said when leaving the office
  • Korean workplace culture includes 회식 (team dinners) and strict hierarchy
  • Education is highly valued — 학원 (cram schools) and 수능 are central to Korean life
